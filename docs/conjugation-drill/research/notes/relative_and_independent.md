## 1. Relative clauses: antecedent type, negation, questions, quantifiers, superlatives, free relatives, -quiera forms

### Takeaway
Mood in restrictive (especificativa) relative clauses depends on the antecedent:
- Indicative when it is specific, known, or presented as existing.
- Subjunctive when it is nonspecific, hypothetical, desired, denied (nadie/nada/ningún/no hay), questioned, or presented as scarce (pocos).
- Non-restrictive (explicativa) relatives essentially take the indicative.

Free relatives (lo que, quien, donde, como, cuando) follow the same logic: indicative for a known referent, subjunctive for an unknown or future one. They are NOT always subjunctive. The -quiera forms take que + subjunctive. The exception is the causal conjunction comoquiera que, which takes the indicative.

### Cited Findings

**General rule**
- Subjunctive when the antecedent is "indeterminado, desconocido o inexistente"; indicative when it is concrete. Examples: "Necesito una secretaria que sepa japonés" vs "Conozco una chica griega que sabe japonés" — [todoele, relativos.pdf](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/relativos.pdf)
- The subjunctive is triggered by the nonspecific character of the antecedent. The mood contrast applies to especificativas; explicativas generally do not take the subjunctive — [USC Verba article](https://revistas.usc.gal/index.php/verba/article/view/8511/14101), as summarized by search tool
- RAE glossary entry "sintagma nominal inespecífico" cross-references NGLE §15.9–15.10 for the underlying concept — [RAE GTG](https://www.rae.es/gtg/sintagma-nominal-inespecífico) (via search snippet)
- Princeton "Contextos": no subjunctive with specific antecedents ("Tengo un perro que…"). Subjunctive with a nonexistent, nonspecific, or not-concrete-in-the-speaker's-mind antecedent ("quiero un trabajo que…", "Me gustaría vivir en una ciudad donde…") — [Princeton Contextos](https://commons.princeton.edu/contextos/?p=3980)

**buscar / necesitar / querer + definite vs indefinite article**
- With buscar, necesitar, querer: a definite NP (el/la) usually takes the indicative, an indefinite NP (un/una) usually the subjunctive.
  - "Busco al hombre que habla japonés" (indicative)
  - "Busco un hombre que hable japonés" (subjunctive)
  - — [SpanishDict guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/subjunctive-in-relative-clauses)
- Personal "a" is often omitted when the person is unidentified — [SpanishDict guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/subjunctive-in-relative-clauses)

**Nonexistent or denied antecedent**
- Negated tener, conocer, haber, existir → subjunctive.
  - "Hay alguien por aquí que te puede ayudar" (indicative)
  - "No hay nadie por aquí que te pueda ayudar" (subjunctive)
  - "No hay nada que odie más que las arañas"
  - "Nadie que yo conozca tiene una serpiente como mascota"
  - — [SpanishDict guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/subjunctive-in-relative-clauses)
- Subjunctive for an antecedent of unknown or uncertain existence, and for a negated antecedent (no, nadie, nada, ninguno). Example: "No hay nadie en clase que entienda árabe" — [Units (Trieste) handout, oraciones adjetivas](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/463420/mod_resource/intro/oraciones%20adjetivas.pdf), as summarized by search tool
- A Czech university text says to distinguish negation of the main verb from negation of the antecedent — [Masaryk Univ., Oraciones de relativo](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/podzim2018/BPJ_JI3S/Oraciones_de_relativo.pdf?lang=cs), as summarized by search tool

**Questions about existence**
- "¿Conoces a alguien que vive en Fiji?" (indicative: the speaker is confirming a fact) vs "¿Conoces a alguien que viva en Fiji?" (subjunctive: antecedent not identifiable). Affirmative indefinites (algo, alguien, alguno) trigger the subjunctive mainly in questions — [SpanishDict guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/subjunctive-in-relative-clauses)
- Question/answer pattern:
  - "¿Hay alguien en la clase que tenga dos hijos?"
  - "Sí, hay alguien… que tiene dos hijos" (indicative: existence now known)
  - "No, no hay nadie… que tenga dos hijos" (subjunctive)
  - — [LibreTexts, Redes 2 (Warnock), Lección 18](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Languages/Spanish/Second-year_Spanish_Bookshelf/Redes_2%3A_Curso_de_espanol_intermedio_(Warnock)/04%3A_Modulo_Cuatro._Un_mundo_sin_fronteras./4.02%3A_Leccion_18._El_presente_de_subjuntivo_en_clausulas_relativas_(adjetivales))

**Scarce antecedent (pocos)**
- Subjunctive when existence is denied or described as scarce. Examples: "Hay pocas personas que hagan la paella como Rafa"; exercise "Hay pocos que ___ (saber) tanto como él" — [Charles Univ. handout](https://dl1.cuni.cz/mod/resource/view.php?id=575914), as summarized by search tool. Classroom material only; no NGLE text retrieved.

**Superlatives / el único / el primero / el último**
- Learner guide: superlatives (más, mejor, peor, único) are evaluative and can take the subjunctive. Example: "Es la mejor película que haya visto". Indicative "Es el mejor libro que he leído" is "not wrong" — [Sublearn, subjunctive with superlatives](https://sublearn.com/learn/es/grammar/using-subjunctive-with-superlatives) (learner site)
- RAE glossary defines the relative superlative construction, citing NGLE §45.13 (no mood rule in the snippet) — [RAE GTG, construcción superlativa](https://www.rae.es/gtg/construcción-superlativa)

**-quiera forms**
- Nueva gramática básica (2010: 133): quienquiera, comoquiera, dondequiera, adondequiera, cuandoquiera are relativos inespecíficos (relative + grammaticalized -quiera). Cualquiera belongs to the group when specified: "cualquiera que venga" — [UDEP Castellano Actual, Relativos inespecíficos](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-relativos-inespecificos/)
- Learner grammar on the -quiera forms (que + subjunctive) — [elon.io free relatives](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-latin-america/complex/free-relatives) (learner site):
  - Examples: "Quienquiera que sea, no abras la puerta"; "Dondequiera que vas" is an error (should be vayas).
  - Past frame shifts to the imperfect subjunctive: "Quienquiera que fuera, no quiso identificarse".
  - Colloquial equivalents: quien sea que, donde sea que, como sea que.
  - Frequency: dondequiera is the most frequent; cuandoquiera is rare; adondequiera and quienesquiera are literary or legal.
  - Spelling: quienquiera (one word, 'whoever') differs from quien quiera ('whoever wants').
  - Note: the same page claims free relatives are "always subjunctive". This is contradicted by its own contrast ("la persona que vino ayer") and by the general rule; do not adopt it.
- **comoquiera que (causal) + indicative:** per the DPD, comoquiera que is a cultured causal conjunctive locution meaning 'dado que, puesto que', "habitualmente" with indicative and set off by a comma — [DPD, comoquiera](https://rae.es/dpd/comoquiera) (via search snippet; page 403 on direct fetch)
- The NGLE treats it among the anteposed explanatory causals, followed by indicative, "que no debe confundirse con el relativo indefinido comoquiera" — [RAE, Las causales explicativas](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/las-causales-explicativas) (via search snippet)

### Inferences

**Personal "a" is not a reliable signal.** Alguien, nadie, alguno and ninguno referring to people keep "a" even when nonspecific: "Busco a alguien que hable ruso" (subjunctive). With an ordinary indefinite NP, "a" correlates with specificity (busco a una secretaria que habla… = a particular one) but is not a rule. The app should key mood on (in)definiteness and existence, not on "a". (Standard grammar knowledge, consistent with the SpanishDict "often omitted" wording.)

**Negated main verb with a definite antecedent stays indicative.** "No conozco al chico que vive aquí" (constructed). The subjunctive comes from the existence of the antecedent being denied, not from "no" itself.

**Superlatives, el único, el primero, el último que.** Treat the indicative as the neutral default for a factual experience ("Es el mejor libro que he leído"; "Fue el primero que llegó"). Allow the subjunctive as an emphatic or hypothetical option ("Es lo mejor que haya visto nunca"; "el único que pueda ayudarnos" when the person is not identified). The el único que rule is the search tool's own inference, not a sourced rule.

**Free relatives (lo que, quien, el que, donde, como, cuando, cuanto)** (constructed examples):
- Indicative for a known or past referent: "Dime lo que quieres" (you want something specific; tell me); "Hice lo que me dijiste".
- Subjunctive for an unknown or future referent: "Haz lo que quieras"; "Lo que digas" ('whatever you say'); "Quien llegue primero gana"; "Iremos donde tú digas"; "Cuanto más estudies, mejor" (cuanto in a correlative with future reference).
- The elon.io contrast "como tú digas" (concessive) vs "como tú dices" (as you say) illustrates the same split.

**todo el que / todos los que** (constructed):
- Future or generic-open reference → subjunctive: "Todo el que quiera venir, que venga"; "Todos los que lleguen tarde pagarán".
- Factual or habitual-known reference → indicative: "Todos los que vinieron ayer se divirtieron".

**comoquiera que has two uses; generate both carefully** (constructed examples):
- (a) Concessive or indefinite relative + subjunctive: "Comoquiera que lo hagas, hazlo bien".
- (b) Formal causal conjunction + indicative: "Comoquiera que no llegaba, nos fuimos".

- The NGLE text on relatives and mood (cap. 25 on mood in relatives; §22 relatives) could not be retrieved (403).
- No authoritative (RAE or Butt & Benjamin) source was found on superlatives, el único que, el primero que or el último que. Learner sites only.

- "Pocos/poco que + subjunctive" is attested only in classroom handouts.

## 2. Independent subjunctive with ojalá and epistemic adverbs (quizá, tal vez, acaso, -mente adverbs, a lo mejor, igual, lo mismo, capaz que, puede que); position effects

### Takeaway
- **Ojalá** always takes the subjunctive. Tense signals the type of wish:
  - present: possible, present or future
  - perfect: possible, recent past, outcome unknown
  - imperfect: unlikely or impossible, present or future
  - pluperfect: impossible, past (counterfactual)
- **Ojalá que** is fully standard; **ojalá y** is mainly Mexican, Central American and Caribbean.
- **Quizá(s), tal vez, acaso, probablemente, posiblemente** take either mood when placed before the verb. Placed after the verb, they take the indicative only.
- **A lo mejor, igual, lo mismo** take the indicative.
- **Puede (ser) que** takes the subjunctive.
- **Capaz (que)** (Latin America, colloquial) mostly takes the indicative, except in Chile, where the subjunctive is usual.

### Cited Findings

**Ojalá: mood and tenses**
- DPD: interjection of wish; can stand alone or introduce a subjunctive clause, optionally preceded by que — [DPD, ojalá](https://www.rae.es/dpd/ojal%C3%A1) (via search snippet; page 403 on direct fetch)
- DPD: ojalá y (with y) occurs "sobre todo en México, Centroamérica y el área caribeña"; in Spain it is now restricted to popular speech in some areas — [DPD, ojalá](https://www.rae.es/dpd/ojal%C3%A1) (via search snippet)
- Secondary report of the RAE "que" entry: que after ojalá is optional; cultured language tends to omit it — [SpanishDict answers forum](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/290129/ojal-que-vs-ojal-solo) (forum; unverified against the RAE)
- RAE Libro de estilo: avoid combining ojalá with infinitives and nouns — [RAE Libro de estilo, letra O](https://www.rae.es/libro-estilo-lengua-espa%C3%B1ola/o) (via search snippet)
- Ojalá always takes the subjunctive (vaya, fuera, haya ido). DLE: "denota vivo deseo de que suceda algo". The page calls "Ojalá Dios quiera que…" redundant and recommends "Ojalá que no llueva hoy" or "Dios quiera que no llueva hoy" — [UDEP, Ojalá](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/ojala/)
- Tense meanings — [Univ. Milano-Bicocca handout, Usos del subjuntivo en oraciones independientes](https://elearning.unimib.it/pluginfile.php/1380444/mod_resource/content/1/USOS%20DEL%20SUBJUNTIVO%20EN%20ORACIONES%20INDEPENDIENTES.pdf):
  - "Que/Ojalá + presente": possible wishes about the present or future
  - "Ojalá + pretérito perfecto de subjuntivo": possible wishes about a recent past
  - "Ojalá + imperfecto": wishes that are hard to fulfil, present or future
  - "Ojalá + pluscuamperfecto": impossible wishes about the past
- Usage by region: ojalá without que is somewhat more frequent in Spain; ojalá que is very common in Latin America, including Mexico — [Kwiziq Q&A](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/ojala-que-1) / [Lawless Spanish Q&A](https://progress.lawlessspanish.com/questions/view/ojala-que-1) (tutor answers, not normative)

**Quizá / quizás: position and mood**
- Quizá(s) takes subjunctive or indicative.
  - Adverb before the verb: subjunctive, though the indicative is also possible ("Quizás sea (es) un poco tarde para actuar").
  - Adverb after the verb: indicative ("Se olvidó, quizás, de la cita").
  - The subjunctive marks greater uncertainty ("Quizás él te haya engañado"); the indicative marks a weaker doubt ("Quizás él te engañó").
  - Quizá and quizás are interchangeable.
  - — [UDEP, Quizás, quizás, quizás](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/quizas-quizas-quizas/)
- UDEP answer citing NGLE (2010): when the verb is subjunctive, quizá must obligatorily precede it, with no pause between them. "Quizá habló" / "Hablara quizá" are cited (the latter with -ra in an indicative-like value) — [UDEP, Quizás y el tiempo verbal](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-quizas-y-el-tiempo-verbal/), as summarized by search tool. The NGLE section (search tool suggested 25.5.2c) was not verified.
- Many contexts show modal neutralization (either mood with no appreciable difference) — [Alonso Aldeguer, REDELE](https://redined.educacion.gob.es/xmlui/bitstream/handle/11162/72184/00820083000304.pdf?sequence=1), as summarized by search tool. Position matters but speaker intention also plays a role — [Aurová 2013, EER](https://eer.cz/pdfs/eer/2013/01/02.pdf), as summarized by search tool

**Grouping of probability expressions** — [Univ. Milano-Bicocca handout](https://elearning.unimib.it/pluginfile.php/1380444/mod_resource/content/1/USOS%20DEL%20SUBJUNTIVO%20EN%20ORACIONES%20INDEPENDIENTES.pdf)
- + INDICATIVO: a lo mejor, igual, lo mismo (es que), seguramente, seguro que, estoy seguro/a de que, supongo que, lo más seguro es
- + QUE + SUBJUNTIVO: es posible, puede (ser), dudo, no es seguro
- + INDICATIVO o SUBJUNTIVO: tal vez, quizá(s), acaso, probablemente, posiblemente
- Also: future of probability for present hypotheses; conditional for past hypotheses
- Conflict: the Lodz paper groups seguramente with probablemente and posiblemente as able to take indicative, "aproximativo" (= its label for future/conditional of probability; not a third mood) and/or subjunctive. A lo mejor, igual and lo mismo (with tal vez in one list) take indicative or "aproximativo"; it says those adverbs "cannot select the subjunctive" — [Univ. Łódź press paper](https://press.uni.lodz.pl/index.php/wul/catalog/download/412/1828/862?inline=1)

**A lo mejor**
- A lo mejor takes the indicative despite meaning 'maybe' — [Kwiziq Q&A](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/quiza-tal-vez-or-a-lo-mejor); [SpanishDict compare](https://www.spanishdict.com/compare/subjuntivo/a%20lo%20mejor)

**Capaz (que)**
- DPD: colloquial speech of many American countries uses capaz as a doubt adverb 'probablemente o quizá', often in capaz que. Generally with indicative, except Chile, where it is used almost always with subjunctive — [DPD, capaz](https://www.rae.es/dpd/capaz) (via search snippet; page 403 on direct fetch)
- UDEP: es capaz que (impersonal, 'it is probable') admits both moods.
  - Examples: "Es capaz que consigue boletos"; "Es capaz que la arquitecta obtenga la casa…"; "Capaz mañana venga a visitarnos mi abuela"; "No me percaté de tu error, si no capaz te reprendo"
  - Restrictions reported as RAE's: present only ("Es capaz que los invitados se fueron temprano", not *"Fue capaz que…"); no quantifying adverb (*"Es muy capaz que…")
  - Choice of mood depends on speaker intention
  - — [UDEP, Es capaz que…](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/es-capaz-que-en-el-dia-del-padre-ningun-papa-se-queda-en-casa/)
- Conflict: DPD "generally indicative (Chile subjunctive)" vs UDEP "both moods" for es capaz que.

### Inferences
- **Generation rule for quizá, tal vez, acaso, probablemente, posiblemente:**
  - Before the verb: allow both moods. The subjunctive conveys more doubt.
  - After the verb or parenthetical: indicative only.
  - Constructed examples: "Quizá venga mañana" / "Quizá viene mañana"; "Viene mañana, quizá"; "Tal vez tengas razón"; "Probablemente llegue tarde" / "Probablemente llegará tarde".
- **A lo mejor, igual (mainly Spain, colloquial), lo mismo (Spain, colloquial):** indicative only. Constructed: "A lo mejor llueve", "Igual no viene", "Lo mismo se ha perdido".
- **Seguramente:** default to indicative ("Seguramente está en casa"). The subjunctive is attested (Lodz) but not a safe teaching default.
- **Puede (ser) que + subjunctive** (constructed): "Puede que llueva"; "Puede que ya hayan llegado"; past "Puede que lo supiera". Because puede que is an impersonal matrix + que, it is not an adverb, and the subjunctive is not optional.
- **Ojalá examples for the app** (constructed):
  - "Ojalá llueva mañana" (possible)
  - "Ojalá haya aprobado" (possible past, unknown)
  - "Ojalá tuviera más tiempo" (unlikely or contrary to present fact)
  - "Ojalá hubiera estudiado" (past, impossible)
  - Ojalá + imperfect can also be a merely less-probable future wish: "Ojalá ganáramos mañana".
- **Capaz que:** for a Mexico/Colombia-oriented app, generate capaz que + indicative ("Capaz que llueve") and mark it colloquial Latin American. Avoid it in normative or formal register.

## 3. Que + subjunctive in main clauses (wishes, orders, echo commands), ¡Viva!, ¡Vaya!, Así + subjunctive

### Takeaway
Bare "que + present subjunctive" is the standard independent optative/jussive frame. It covers wishes (¡Que te vaya bien!, ¡Que aproveche!, ¡Que descanses!) and third-person or relayed orders (¡Que pase!, Que te calles). Frozen optatives include ¡Viva…!, en paz descanse, así sea and Dios mediante. "Así + subjunctive" is used for curses and oaths (así me muera).

### Cited Findings
- "Que + presente de subjuntivo" expresses possible wishes for the present or future — [Univ. Milano-Bicocca handout](https://elearning.unimib.it/pluginfile.php/1380444/mod_resource/content/1/USOS%20DEL%20SUBJUNTIVO%20EN%20ORACIONES%20INDEPENDIENTES.pdf)
- Standalone optative que — [elon.io fixed expressions](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/complex/subjunctive-fixed-expressions) (learner site):
  - "¡Que tengas un buen día!"
  - "¡Que aproveche!"
  - "Que descanses"
  - "Que te mejores pronto"
  - "Que sepas (que…)" ('just so you know', often with an edge)
- Frozen forms, same page:
  - "así sea" ('so be it', formal or religious)
  - "en paz descanse" (formal)
  - "Dios mediante" (slightly archaic)
  - "así me muera" ('I swear', informal, emphatic)
- The handout's "Expresar mandato o prohibición" heading lists the subjunctive in negative imperatives — [Univ. Milano-Bicocca handout](https://elearning.unimib.it/pluginfile.php/1380444/mod_resource/content/1/USOS%20DEL%20SUBJUNTIVO%20EN%20ORACIONES%20INDEPENDIENTES.pdf)
- UDEP references "el subjuntivo sin 'que'" as a topic (frozen independent subjunctives); not fetched — [UDEP](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/?p=7155)

### Inferences
All examples below are constructed (standard grammar knowledge, not verified this session).
- **Third-person order or relayed command:** "¡Que pase el siguiente!"; "Dile que venga" vs bare "¡Que venga!". Echo command (repeating an order the listener ignored): "¡Que te calles!" — que here marks repetition or reporting.
- **¡Viva…! / ¡Vivan los novios!** Frozen optative that agrees in number. "¡Vaya!" is a frozen subjunctive of ir, now an interjection ('wow / well'); "¡Vaya día!" has no mood choice.
- **Así + subjunctive curse:** "¡Así te mueras!", "¡Así te parta un rayo!" (colloquial, strong).
- **¡Dios quiera que + subjunctive!** = ojalá: "Dios quiera que llegue bien". UDEP prefers this or plain ojalá over "ojalá Dios quiera que".

### Gaps
- No authoritative source retrieved for echo-command que, ¡Viva!/¡Vaya!, or "Así + subjunctive" curses (beyond elon.io's "así me muera").
- NGLE §42 (independent subjunctive and imperative) was not retrieved.

## 4. Commands (formal usted/ustedes, negative tú/vos/vosotros, nosotros "let's", third person)

### Takeaway
Spanish uses the present subjunctive for:
- all negative commands
- all usted/ustedes commands
- nosotros ("let's") commands

Affirmative nosotros + -nos drops the final -s (sentémonos, vámonos). Ir in the affirmative "let's" uses vamos (vámonos), not vayamos, in normal usage.

### Cited Findings
- NGLE (2009, p. 3133, §42.3m, as quoted): final /s/ drops before nos (callémonos, vámonos); vayámonos, not *vayámosnos — via search snippet citing [123teachme](https://123teachme.com/learn_spanish/imperative_nosotros_commands) / [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/vamanos-vs-vamos-vs-vayamos)
- Negative nosotros: the pronoun precedes, nothing drops: "no nos vayamos" — [Kwiziq Q&A](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/vamos-vayamos)
- Forum quotation attributed to the DPD (unverified): vamos keeps an old subjunctive value, and vayamos in the 'let's go' sense is nearly confined to literary language — [SpanishDict answers](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/284321/vayamos-vs-vamos) (forum)

### Inferences
All forms below are standard paradigm knowledge, not verified this session.
- **usted/ustedes:** hable/hablen, coma/coman; with pronouns attached in the affirmative ("Siéntese", "Dígamelo") and before the verb in the negative ("No se siente").
- **Negative tú:** no hables. Negative vos: no hablés is attested colloquially, but the normative form in voseo regions is no hables. Negative vosotros (Spain): no habléis.
- **Affirmative vosotros + os** drops the -d: sentaos, idos (the exception keeps the -d).
- **Nosotros:**
  - Affirmative: hablemos, comamos; vamos for ir.
  - With -nos: sentémonos, vámonos.
  - With -se-: "Digámoselo" (the -s drops before se as well: *digámosselo is wrong).
  - Negative: no vayamos, no nos sentemos.
  - "Vamos a + infinitive" is the common colloquial 'let's' alternative.
- **Third-person indirect command:** "¡Que lo haga él!" (see section 3).

### Gaps
- No authoritative source retrieved for the vos negative-command norm, the vosotros -d drop, or "Digámoselo" -s drop. The DPD vamos/vayamos statement is forum-quoted only.

## 5. Fixed formulas and reduplicative structures

### Takeaway
- **Reduplicatives** ("V lo que V", "V + interrogative + V", "V o no V") are concessive ('no matter…'). They use present subjunctive for present or future frames and imperfect subjunctive for past frames, with the same person, number and tense in both verbs.
- **"Que yo sepa / que yo recuerde"** are frozen epistemic hedges.
- **"Que digamos"** is a negative-emphatic tag.
- **"Digamos", "o sea"** are discourse markers with no live mood choice.

### Cited Findings
- **"X lo que X" family** — [elon.io fixed expressions](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/complex/subjunctive-fixed-expressions):
  - pase lo que pase ("Pase lo que pase, mañana cojo el tren a Madrid")
  - diga/haga/piense lo que diga/haga/piense
  - cueste lo que cueste
  - venga lo que venga
  - Past frame: pasara lo que pasara, hiciera lo que hiciera
  - Rule: both verbs must match in person, number and tense
- **"sea + interrogative" family**, same page: sea quien sea, sea cuando sea, sea donde sea, sea como sea, sea cual sea; plurals sean quienes sean, sean cuales sean; past fuera como fuera / fuese como fuese.
- **Epistemic hedges**, same page:
  - "que yo sepa" ('as far as I know'), "que yo recuerde", "que yo vea", "que él sepa", "que nosotros sepamos"
  - Past frame: "que yo supiera"
  - Example: "El museo está cerrado los lunes, que yo sepa"
- elon.io lists "quieras (o) no quieras" ('whether you want it or not') — [elon.io](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/complex/subjunctive-fixed-expressions)
- "o sea" ('that is, I mean') is informal, very common, and frozen — [elon.io](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/complex/subjunctive-fixed-expressions)
- "como tú digas" (concessive, 'whatever you say') vs "como tú dices" ('as you say') — [elon.io](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/complex/subjunctive-fixed-expressions)
- "digamos", "no digamos", "que digamos" result from fixation and idiomatization into discourse markers — [Grande Alija 2010, Onomázein](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/1345/134513546004.pdf), as summarized by search tool
- "Que yo sepa"-type segments are not standard subordinates; they are semi-lexicalized, at the edge of grammar — [Asterisco, Univ. Salamanca 2023](https://revistas.usal.es/dos/index.php/asterisco/article/download/29622/29053/115442), as summarized by search tool

### Inferences
All examples below are constructed (standard knowledge).
- **"V o no V"** with the same verb or its antonym: "quieras o no", "te guste o no", "llueva o no llueva"; past "quisiera o no".
- **"venga quien venga"**: "Venga quien venga, no abriremos".
- **"digan lo que digan"**: "Digan lo que digan, lo haré".
- **"que digamos"** follows a negative statement to understate: "No es muy listo, que digamos".
- **"digamos"** means 'let's say': "Llegaremos, digamos, a las ocho".
- **"es decir", "como quien dice"** are indicative or frozen, not subjunctive triggers: "como quien dice" = 'so to speak'.
- **"¡Dios quiera que…!"** see section 3.
- **"siquiera"** (historically 'si quiera') today means 'at least' or 'even' (ni siquiera). Its old concessive use "siquiera + subjunctive" ('even if') is literary: "Dame algo, siquiera sea un vaso de agua".

### Gaps
- No source retrieved for: "es decir", "como quien dice", "siquiera" (concessive), "te guste o no", "venga quien venga", "digan lo que digan" (beyond the generic pattern).

## 6. Polite softening with -ra (quisiera, debiera, pudiera) and "Me gustaría que + imperfect subjunctive"

### Takeaway
The -ra imperfect subjunctive of querer, deber and poder works in main clauses as a polite or attenuated equivalent of the conditional: quisiera ≈ querría; debiera ≈ debería; pudiera ≈ podría. A conditional or past matrix of wanting/liking ("Me gustaría que…", "Querría que…", "Quisiera que…") takes the imperfect subjunctive in the subordinate clause.

### Cited Findings
- No source retrieved this session addresses -ra softening directly. The closest is the UDEP note citing "Hablara quizá" as -ra after an anteposed epistemic — [UDEP, Quizás y el tiempo verbal](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-quizas-y-el-tiempo-verbal/), as summarized by search tool

### Inferences
All examples below are constructed (standard knowledge).
- "Quisiera un café, por favor" (more polite than quiero; ≈ querría). "Debieras descansar" (≈ deberías). "¿Pudiera ayudarme?" is possible but more frequent in parts of Latin America (Mexico, Venezuela) than in Spain, where podría is the default.
- Only querer is fully general in this use. For deber and poder, the conditional is the safer default for generation.
- "Me gustaría que vinieras", "Quisiera que me ayudaras": imperfect subjunctive, not present (*me gustaría que vengas is heard colloquially in parts of Latin America but is non-normative by sequence of tenses).
- -se forms (quisiese) are NOT used for this polite main-clause value.

- No source found for the regional frequency of "pudiera" as a polite form.
