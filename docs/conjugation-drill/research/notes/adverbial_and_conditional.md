## Final / purpose clauses

### Takeaway
Purpose connectors always take the subjunctive, or the infinitive when the subject is the same. *De modo/manera/forma que* is the one connector that splits: subjunctive when it means purpose ("so that"), indicative when it means result ("so"). None of this could be checked against a primary source this session.

### Inferences
Reference knowledge, not verified this session (NGLE §46.5-46.7; Butt & Benjamin §16.12).

| Connector | Mood | Condition / note | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| para que | S | Always. Different subjects. Same subject → *para* + Inf | Te lo explico **para que** lo **entiendas**. / Lo expliqué **para que** lo **entendieran**. / Estudio **para aprobar** (same subject). |
| a fin de que | S | Formal. Same subject → *a fin de* + Inf | Se modificó la ley **a fin de que** nadie **quedara** excluido. / **a fin de evitar** problemas |
| con el fin de que / con objeto de que / con la intención de que / con el propósito de que | S | Formal/written. Same subject → *con el fin de* + Inf | Lo llamé **con el fin de que** me **aclarara** las dudas. |
| de modo que / de manera que / de forma que (purpose) | S | Means "in such a way that / so that" (an intended goal) | Habla alto, **de modo que** todos te **oigan**. / Lo coloqué **de manera que** se **viera** bien. |
| de modo que / de manera que / de forma que (result) | I | Means "so / therefore" (a fact that follows). Often after a comma | Llovía, **de modo que** nos **quedamos** en casa. / No me avisaste, **de manera que** no **sabía** nada. |
| no sea que / no vaya a ser que / no fuera a ser que | S | Negative purpose, "lest / just in case". Present tense after a present or future main clause, *fuera* after a past one | Llévate el paraguas, **no sea que llueva**. / Lo apunté, **no fuera a ser que** se me **olvidara**. |
| a que (after verbs of motion: venir, ir, salir, bajar…) | S | Purpose. Same subject → *a* + Inf | **Vengo a que** me **ayudes**. / Fue **a que** le **cortaran** el pelo. / **Vengo a ayudarte**. |
| que (purpose, after an imperative; colloquial) | S | Colloquial | Ven aquí, **que** te **vea**. |
| porque (purpose, = para que; less common) | S | With the subjunctive *porque* can mean purpose | Hice lo posible **porque** no se **enterara**. |

- **Sequence of tenses:** after a present, future or imperative main clause → present subjunctive. After a past or conditional main clause → imperfect subjunctive (*-ra* or *-se*; both are standard).
- **Same subject:** *para que* + subjunctive with the same subject is ungrammatical: *Estudio para que (yo) apruebe → Estudio para aprobar*. The app should never generate the same-subject subjunctive for final connectors. Exception: when the main verb is passive or impersonal, the subjects count as different.
- The *de modo que* split is a classic learner trap. App explanations should present it as a meaning difference: purpose → subjunctive, consequence → indicative.

## Temporal clauses

### Takeaway
Temporal connectors take the indicative for habitual or past events and the subjunctive for events still in the future (pending) relative to the main clause. *Antes de que* is always subjunctive, even in the past. *Después de que* in past contexts takes the indicative but also accepts the imperfect subjunctive. *Desde que* (since) is indicative.

### Cited Findings
- A Todoele ELE worksheet gives the core rule: temporal clauses take the INDICATIVE when they refer to a habitual action in the present or an action that happened in the past, and the SUBJUNCTIVE when they refer to a future action. It lists cuando, en cuanto, tan pronto como, mientras, hasta (que), después de que. — [Todoele, "Oraciones temporales I-II" (A. Flores Álamo)](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf)
- Same source: when the subject of the *hasta* clause is the same as that of the main clause, the verb is in the INFINITIVE. — [Todoele](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf)
- Same source: when a sentence in the past is introduced by DESPUÉS DE QUE, it requires a past indicative tense "pero también acepta el pretérito imperfecto de subjuntivo". Worksheet example: *Yo sabía que Bill era una buena persona, pero me lo confirmó después de que (donar…)*. — [Todoele](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf)
- Worksheet items illustrating the rule (answers are not printed; the expected moods follow from the rule): *Tan pronto como terminaron de cocinar, se pusieron a comer* (past → I); *En cuanto le cuentes la verdad a tus padres, podrás descansar tranquilo* (future → S); *No se lo digas a Carlos, en cuanto le cuento algo, se lo dice a su novia* (habitual → I); *No nos iremos antes de que llegues* (S); *Antes de que él me lo dijera, yo ya había visto…* (past, still S). Also expletive negation: *No recibiremos a los conferenciantes hasta que no nos lo pida el director*. — [Todoele](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf)
- (Search-result summary, full text not read.) A Zagreb thesis on mood after *después de que* says that, unlike *antes de que*, which always takes the subjunctive, *después de que* can appear with both moods. — [FFZG repository thesis](https://repozitorij.ffzg.unizg.hr/islandora/object/ffzg%3A6380/datastream/PDF/view)
- (Search-result summary, full text not read.) A Czech bachelor's thesis gives examples of *después de que* with either indicative or subjunctive. — [Doubková, theses.cz](https://theses.cz/id/f3ecd3/Bakalsk_prce-_Kristna_Doubkov.pdf)
- (Search-result summary of the DPD entry *después*; the page itself returned 403.) The DPD says *después de que* arose from crossing *después que* and *después de*. It was once criticised as dequeísmo but is now considered valid, and *Después que te fuiste* and *Después de que te fuiste* are equally correct. — [DPD, "después"](https://www.rae.es/dpd/después)

| después de que / después que | I/S | Future → S. Past → I is normal; the imperfect subjunctive (*-ra/-se*) is also accepted (Todoele). | antes de que | S | **Always S**, even in past or habitual contexts, because the event has not yet happened at the reference point. Same subject → *antes de* + Inf | Sal **antes de que llueva**. / Salimos **antes de que lloviera**. / Siempre llamo **antes de salir**. |
| desde que (= since) | I | Normally I (a starting point, which is a fact). S only marginally, for future or hypothetical starting points | **Desde que** la **conocí**, soy feliz. / No lo veo **desde que** se **mudó**. |
| siempre que / cada vez que / todas las veces que (= whenever) | I/S | I for habitual or past. S for future. Note that *siempre que* = "provided that" is conditional and always S (see Conditionals) | **Siempre que** viene, trae flores. / **Cada vez que** **vengas**, te invitaré. |
| una vez que | I/S | Same split | **Una vez que termines**, avísame. / **Una vez que terminó**, se fue. |
| a medida que / conforme / según (= as, progressively) | I/S | I for facts or habits. S for future, pending events | **A medida que** **avanzaba**, todo se aclaraba. / **Conforme vayan** llegando, siéntenlos. / **Según** **lleguen**, que pasen. |
| al + Inf | Inf | Simultaneous or immediately prior. The subject may differ: *al llegar yo* | **Al salir**, cerró la puerta. |

- **Why *después de que* allows the subjunctive in past contexts.** The NGLE explanation, as I recall it, is that the subordinate event is presented as a backdrop rather than asserted, together with analogy with *antes de que*. The construction is often described as frequent in journalistic and formal registers in Spain and the Americas. Not verified this session; see Gaps. App recommendation: accept both *llegó* and *llegara* in past *después de que* clauses, but generate the indicative as the default.
- **The "pending" test** is the most reliable algorithm for the app: is the subordinate event future relative to the main-clause reference time? If so → S. This also covers *Dijo que vendría cuando terminara* (future in the past → imperfect subjunctive).
- **No future indicative and no conditional** in temporal clauses with *cuando* and the like: *cuando tendré → cuando tenga*, and *cuando tendría → cuando tuviera*.

## Conditional clauses

### Takeaway
*Si* takes the indicative for real or open conditions, the imperfect subjunctive for potential or unreal present conditions, and the pluperfect subjunctive for unreal past conditions. It never takes the present subjunctive or the future or conditional indicative in standard Spanish. Every other conditional connector (*en caso de que, a menos que, con tal de que, siempre que/siempre y cuando, a condición de que, a no ser que, salvo que, como*) takes the subjunctive. *Por si* behaves like *si*.

### Cited Findings
- **Conditional *como*:** *como* introducing a condition allows only the subjunctive, not the indicative, so *"Te vas a arrepentir como no vienes ahora mismo"* is incorrect. With the indicative, *como* means "because": *Como salí del trabajo tarde, no pude ir al cine*. — [Kwiziq, "Using como + subjunctive as 'if'"](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/my-languages/spanish/view/7267)
- **Conditional *como*** is typically used for a warning, threat or prediction: *Como no apruebes el examen, no te llevo a McDonalds*; *Voy a llamar a mi hermano mayor como no me dejéis tranquilo*; *Como llueva ahora, toda la ropa… se mojará*. With the imperfect subjunctive: *Me iba a enfadar con ella como se lo dijera a Paco*. — [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/my-languages/spanish/view/7267)
- (Search-result summary.) A Masaryk University handout says sentences with conditional *como* "tienen un matiz de advertencia, amenaza". — [MUNI, "Oraciones condicionales"](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/podzim2018/BPJ_JI3S/Oraciones_condicionales.pdf)
- ***Por si / por si acaso*** (= in case): use the indicative (present or past) for events seen as fairly likely, and the imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive for less likely ones. "El Presente de Subjuntivo is **never used** after por si/por si acaso." Examples: *Me voy a llevar el paraguas por si llueve* / *…por si lloviera*; *Llevaré un abrigo por si hace frío* / *…por si hiciera frío*; ✗ *por si haga frío*. Past main clause: *Llamé al médico por si la fiebre subía*; *Había guardado una copia por si el archivo se hubiera perdido*. — [Kwiziq, "por si / por si acaso"](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/my-languages/Spanish/view/7526)

**A. *Si* patterns**

| Type | Protasis (si-clause) | Apodosis (result) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real / open (present, future) | present indicative | present, future, imperative, *ir a* + Inf | **Si llueve**, no salgo / no saldré / no salgas. |
| Real (habitual or past) | imperfect, preterite or perfect indicative | indicative | **Si llovía**, nos quedábamos en casa. / **Si lo dijo**, se equivocó. / **Si has terminado**, puedes irte. |
| Potential / unreal present | imperfect subjunctive (*-ra* or *-se*) | conditional | **Si tuviera** dinero, **viajaría**. / **Si fuese** rico, **compraría** una casa. |
| Unreal past | pluperfect subjunctive (*hubiera/hubiese* + participle) | conditional perfect, or *hubiera* + participle | **Si lo hubiera sabido**, **habría venido** / **hubiera venido**. |
| Mixed: past condition, present result | pluperfect subjunctive | simple conditional | **Si hubiera estudiado**, ahora **tendría** trabajo. |
| Mixed: present (permanent) condition, past result | imperfect subjunctive | conditional perfect | **Si fuera** más prudente, no **habría tenido** el accidente. |

- **Forbidden after conditional *si*:** the present subjunctive (*si tenga ✗*), the future indicative (*si tendrá ✗*) and the conditional (*si tendría ✗*). The last one is a stigmatised regional feature, heard in the Basque Country, Navarre, La Rioja, Cantabria and parts of the Americas (Argentina, Paraguay), and the DPD rejects it. *Si* + future or conditional is fine when *si* means "whether" (an indirect question): *No sé si vendrá*, *Me preguntó si iría*. The app should never treat this as a conditional.
- **Pluperfect subjunctive *-ra* in the apodosis:** *hubiera venido* = *habría venido* is fully standard. *Hubiese* in the apodosis is less accepted. A simple *-ra* in the apodosis with modal verbs (*quisiera, debiera, pudiera*) is standard: *Debieras descansar*. The NGLE regards simple *-ra* for *-ría* with other verbs (*si pudiera, lo hiciera*) as archaic or literary, though still found in some American varieties. Don't generate it.
- **Colloquial *si* + imperfect indicative:** spoken Spanish uses it for an unreal condition, with an imperfect indicative in the apodosis: *Si lo sabía, no venía* (= *si lo hubiera sabido, no habría venido*); *Yo que tú, me iba*. This is colloquial, not normative for writing. Accept it in listening material at most; don't produce it.
- **Colloquial mixing:** *si tendría* (regional, censured), and *si habría sabido* (censured).
- **Future subjunctive** (*si hubiere, si fuere*): archaic, surviving in legal and administrative language (*Si el arrendatario no pagare…*) and fixed phrases (*sea lo que fuere*, *donde fueres, haz lo que vieres*). The present indicative is the modern equivalent. Don't generate it.
- ***Si bien*** is not an exception to "no present subjunctive after *si*": it is a concessive connector (= *aunque* for facts) and takes the indicative (see Concessives).
- ***Como si*** is a manner or comparison connector (see Modal clauses).
- **"If not" / alternatives:** *si no* (written as two words) = "if not". *Sino* is a different word, "but rather".
- **Implicit conditions:** *de* + Inf: *De haberlo sabido, habría venido* (= si lo hubiera sabido); *De seguir así, suspenderás* (= si sigues así). A gerund: *Estudiando más, aprobarías* (= si estudiaras más). A participle: *Bien mirado, no es tan grave*. *Con* + Inf: *Con llamar basta*. Imperative + *y*: *Insiste y lo conseguirás*. *Yo que tú / yo en tu lugar* + conditional (or colloquial imperfect indicative).

**B. Other conditional connectors**

| Connector | Mood | Note | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| como (conditional) | S | Usually a threat or warning, with the clause placed first. Indicative *como* = causal (see Causals). (Kwiziq, cited) | **Como no vengas**, me enfado. / **Como lo vuelvas a hacer**, te castigo. |
| en caso de que | S | Same subject: *en caso de* + Inf. The present subjunctive is fine here, unlike *si* | **En caso de que llueva**, se suspende. / **En caso de necesitar** ayuda, llame. |

## Concessive clauses

### Takeaway
*Aunque* and similar connectors take the indicative for a fact the speaker asserts and the subjunctive for a hypothesis or for a fact that is granted but dismissed as irrelevant (the "polemic" subjunctive: *aunque sea tu hermano*). *Por más/mucho/poco que* and *por* + adj + *que* lean strongly to the subjunctive. *Si bien* and *y eso que* are indicative only. *Mal que* is subjunctive (*mal que le pese*).

### Cited Findings
- (Search-result summary.) A DELE C1 prep site lists *por más que*, *por mucho que* and *por muy que* as concessive structures used with the subjunctive. — [A por el DELE](https://www.aporeldele.com/en/blog/estructuras-con-subjuntivo-para-la-prueba-oral-del-dele-c1)
- (Search-result summary.) A Brazilian grammar page lists "por mucho que, por poco que + verbo en subjuntivo", with *Por poco que me paguen, me encanta mi trabajo*. — [Prepara ENEM, "Conjunciones subordinantes"](https://www.preparaenem.com/amp/espanhol/conjunciones-subordinantes-conjuncoes-subordinadas-em-espanhol.htm)

|---|---|---|---|

| por más que / por mucho que | I/S | S by default ("however much"). I for an asserted fact, which is possible but less common | **Por más que** **insistas**, no cambiaré. / **Por mucho que** **estudió**, suspendió (fact). |

| por + noun + que (por más dinero que…) | S | | **Por más dinero que tenga**, no es feliz. |
| si bien | I | Concessive for facts, formal register. Never S. Not the conditional *si* | **Si bien** es caro, merece la pena. |
| y eso que | I | Placed after the main clause, colloquial, emphatic ("and that's despite…") | Suspendió, **y eso que estudió** mucho. |
| mal que | S | Fixed phrase: *mal que le/me/te pese* (whether you like it or not) | **Mal que te pese**, tiene razón. |
| así (= even if) | S | Concessive *así*, said to be frequent in Latin America (Colombia, Mexico, Andes), standard but not usual in Spain. **Unverified, see Gaps** | No voy **así me paguen**. / **Así llueva**, salimos. |

| siquiera / ni aunque | S | Literary, or the negative *ni aunque* | **Ni aunque** me lo **pidas** de rodillas. |
| con + Inf / con lo que (concessive) | Inf / I | Colloquial | **Con lo que** **trabaja**, y no le suben el sueldo. |

- **App explanation template for *aunque*:** indicative → "you're stating it as a fact the listener may not know". Present subjunctive → "either you don't know whether it's true, or you both know it and are saying it doesn't matter". Imperfect subjunctive → "unlikely or contrary to fact".
- **The polemic subjunctive is where generated content goes wrong.** *Aunque es tu hermano* and *aunque sea tu hermano* are both correct with the same facts; they differ in communicative intent. The app should accept both unless the context forces a hypothesis (for example a future main clause about an unknown event → S).

## Causal clauses

### Takeaway
Causal connectors take the indicative, because a cause is asserted as fact. The exception is a negated or denied cause (*no porque + S*, *no es que + S*), which takes the subjunctive. Causal *como* must come first in the sentence and takes the indicative; *como* + subjunctive is conditional.

### Cited Findings
- *Como* + indicative means cause, not condition: *Como salí del trabajo tarde, no pude ir al cine*. — [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/my-languages/spanish/view/7267)

### Inferences

| porque | I | Ordinary cause | No vino **porque estaba** enfermo. |
| no porque … sino porque | S in the denied cause, I in the real cause | The negated cause is not asserted | Lo hago **no porque** me **guste**, **sino porque** **es** necesario. / No lo dejé **porque** **fuera** difícil, sino porque me aburría. |

| ya que / puesto que / dado que / visto que / debido a que / en vista de que / a causa de que / considerando que | I | Known or presupposed cause. *Ya que* and *puesto que* often come first | **Ya que estás** aquí, ayúdame. / **Puesto que** no **contestas**, me voy. / **Dado que** **subieron** los precios… |
| como (causal) | I | Must precede the main clause. After the main clause, *como* reads as manner | **Como no llegabas**, me fui. |
| pues (causal) | I | Formal or literary in Spain, more common in Latin America | No salgo, **pues** **está** lloviendo. |

- **Archaic or regional:** *como* + imperfect subjunctive as a cause (*Como fuese tarde, se marchó*) is literary or archaic. Recognize it, don't generate it.

## Consecutive (result) clauses

### Takeaway
Result connectors take the indicative. Intensive consecutives (*tan/tanto … que*) take the indicative unless the main clause is negated, a question, or an imperative, in which case they take the subjunctive. *De modo que* in the purpose sense takes the subjunctive (see Final clauses).

| así que / de modo que / de manera que / de forma que (result) / conque / luego / por (lo) tanto / por consiguiente / por eso / así pues | I | Result of a fact. A main clause in the imperative or a question is fine: *así que cállate* | Estaba cansado, **así que me fui**. / No has estudiado, **conque** no te quejes. / Pienso, **luego existo**. |
| tan + adj/adv + que / tanto/a/os/as + noun + que / verb + tanto que / tal + noun + que / de tal modo que | I (affirmative) | Intensive consecutive | Es **tan** alto **que toca** el techo. / Comió **tanto que se enfermó**. |
| no … tan … que / no … tanto … que | S | Negated intensive: the result is denied | **No es tan** rico **que pueda** comprarlo todo. / No hace **tanto** frío **que haya** que encender la calefacción. |

## Modal / manner clauses

### Takeaway
*Como*, *según* and *donde* take the indicative for a known or specific manner, place or source, and the subjunctive for an unknown or open one. *Sin que* and *como si* are always subjunctive. *Como si* takes only the imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive.

| conforme / tal (y) como / tal cual / de la manera que / del modo que | I/S | Same split | Lo dejé **tal como** lo **encontré**. / Déjalo **tal como** lo **encuentres**. |

## Comparative and locative clauses

### Takeaway
*Donde*, *adonde* and *por donde* follow the specificity rule: indicative for a known, specific place and subjunctive for an unknown or undetermined one. Comparative clauses are normally indicative, with the subjunctive when the compared quantity is open or hypothetical.

## Sin + infinitive vs sin que (and the same-subject rule generally)

### Takeaway
For *sin, para, antes de, después de, hasta, a fin de, con tal de, a condición de, en caso de, a pesar de, con el fin de*: same subject → preposition + infinitive. Different subject → *que* + subjunctive (or the indicative where the connector allows it). The Todoele worksheet confirms this for *hasta*.

### Cited Findings
- When the subject of a clause introduced by *hasta* is the same as that of the main clause, the verb is in the infinitive. — [Todoele](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf)
- Worksheet item with the same subject and *después de* + infinitive: *María perdonó a su pareja después de hablar con él seriamente*. — [Todoele](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf)

### Inferences
Reference knowledge, not verified this session.

| Same subject (Inf) | Different subject (que + verb) |
|---|---|
| Se fue **sin despedirse**. | Se fue **sin que** nadie lo **notara**. |
| Estudio **para aprender**. | Te enseño **para que aprendas**. |
| Llamé **antes de salir**. | Llamé **antes de que** **salieras**. |
| Lo haré **después de comer**. | Lo haré **después de que** **comas** / Lo hice **después de que** **comiste** (or **comieras**). |
| No pararé **hasta conseguirlo**. | No pararé **hasta que** lo **consigas**. |

- **Colloquial and Latin American:** an infinitive with an explicit different subject (*antes de yo llegar*, *para yo entender*, *al llegar yo*) is common in Caribbean Spanish (Puerto Rico, Dominican Republic, Cuba, Venezuela), less so in Mexico and Colombia. *Al* + Inf + subject is standard everywhere. *Para* + subject + Inf (*para yo hacerlo*) is regional or colloquial; don't generate it in neutral material. Unverified this session.
- **App rule:** when generating a sentence, check subject identity before choosing *que* + subjunctive. A same-subject subjunctive after *sin/para/antes de* (*\*Me fui sin que (yo) me despidiera*) is ungrammatical.
