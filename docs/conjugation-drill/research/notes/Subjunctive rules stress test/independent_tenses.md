## 1. Ojalá (5.1)

### Takeaway
The rule holds, with three caveats:
- The imperfect is not only "unlikely or untrue": it also covers a possible future you are less hopeful about (*Ojalá ganáramos mañana*).
- *Ojalá* + indicative shows up in learner error lists. No dialect study documenting it was found.
- "Avoid *ojalá* + infinitive" matches the RAE style book. The style book says the same about *ojalá* + noun, and the app shouldn't flag the standalone *¡Ojalá!*, which is fully standard.

Verdict: **holds with caveat**.

### Cited Findings
- DPD: *ojalá* introduces a subjunctive clause, optionally after *que*; *ojalá y* is found "sobre todo en México, Centroamérica y el área caribeña" and in Spain is limited to popular speech. — [DPD, ojalá](https://www.rae.es/dpd/ojal%C3%A1) (SN, via earlier notes; 403 again this session)
- RAE *Libro de estilo*: avoid *ojalá* with infinitives and with nouns. — [RAE Libro de estilo, O](https://www.rae.es/libro-estilo-lengua-espa%C3%B1ola/o) (SN, earlier notes)
- UDEP tense table: present = possible present/future; perfect = possible recent past; imperfect = hard to fulfil (present/future); pluperfect = impossible past. It calls *Ojalá Dios quiera que…* redundant. — [UDEP, Ojalá](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/ojala/) (F, earlier notes)
- Learner sources list *Ojalá viene pronto* as an error. No corpus or dialect study of *ojalá* + indicative turned up in two searches. — [Wikipedia, Subjunctive mood in Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjunctive_mood_in_Spanish); [Fasla 2025, Variación (ojalá on both sides of the Atlantic)](https://revistaseug.ugr.es/index.php/Variacion/article/view/31786) (SN; the article body wasn't read)
- A university handout contrasts *Ojalá apruebe* (I studied, it seems possible) with *Ojalá aprobara* (presented as hard, I didn't study). Both are about the future, so the imperfect means "less likely", not "untrue". — [MUNI handout, pretérito imperfecto de subjuntivo](https://is.muni.cz/el/sci/podzim2018/JSP01/um/20b_preterito_imperfecto_de_subjuntivo_final.pdf) (SN)
- *Ojalá que* vs bare *ojalá*: bare *ojalá* is somewhat more frequent in Spain; *ojalá que* is very common in Latin America. — [Kwiziq Q&A](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/ojala-que-1) (L)

### Inferences
- Better wording for the imperfect row: "less likely, or contrary to fact now (*Ojalá tuviera más tiempo*; *Ojalá ganáramos mañana*: possible, but I'm not counting on it)". The row currently says "unlikely or untrue", which is close enough. Just don't teach the imperfect as "impossible".
- Examples: *Ojalá que no llueva*; *Ojalá y te vaya bien* (Mexico); *¡Ojalá!* (standalone, standard).
- Adversarial check that found nothing: no dialect is documented where *ojalá* + indicative is accepted. Keep "always subjunctive". Grade the existence of indicative uses as "learner error / nonstandard" (L).

### Gaps
- Whether some American varieties use *ojalá* + imperfect or pluperfect for a past whose outcome is unknown (*Ojalá que le fuera bien ayer*, meaning "I hope it went well"). I believe this exists (RK). A targeted search found nothing, so it is not verified. If it is real, the "pluperfect = impossible" row has an exception.
- The exact DPD wording on *ojalá* + indicative wasn't retrieved (403).

## 2. "Maybe" words (5.2)

### Takeaway
The position rule holds, and the NGLE states it strongly: subjunctive requires the adverb before the verb with no pause. Four rows need fixes:
- *seguramente*: about 5% subjunctive in CORPES (*Seguramente sea el catalán…*), so it is "indicative, subjunctive possible", not a hard indicative.
- *acaso* in questions takes the indicative (rhetorical *¿Acaso crees que…?*).
- *tal vez* takes the subjunctive more often than the indicative in Spain.
- *a lo mejor* + subjunctive is attested, mainly in American Spanish (NGLE).

### Cited Findings
- NGLE 2010 §25.5.2c, quoted by UDEP: when the verb is subjunctive, *quizá* "debe preceder, obligatoriamente, al verbo" and must not be separated from it by a pause. Examples listed: *Quizá habló* / *Habló quizá* (indicative), *Quizá hablara* / *Hablara quizá*. — [UDEP, Quizás y el tiempo verbal](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-quizas-y-el-tiempo-verbal/) (F: secondary source quoting the NGLE). **Apparent counterexample:** a verbatim refetch shows the list exactly as "Quizá habló (indicativo) / Quizá hablara (subjuntivo) / Habló quizá (indicativo) / Hablara quizá (subjuntivo)", with **no asterisk** on *Hablara quizá*. So the source, as quoted, accepts a postposed *-ra* labelled subjunctive, right next to a rule that says the subjunctive must precede. Whether that *-ra* is really subjunctive or the literary indicative *-ra* is unresolved. The NGLE original wasn't seen. (F for what the page says; the interpretation is open.)
- Before the verb, either mood: *Quizás sea (es) un poco tarde*. After the verb or set off by commas, indicative: *Se olvidó, quizás, de la cita*. — [UDEP, Quizás, quizás, quizás](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/quizas-quizas-quizas/) (F, earlier notes)
- García Pérez 2024 (Philologica Canariensia 30, 241–257), CORPES XXI, adverb sentence-initial with the verb immediately after it, questions excluded:

  | Adverb | Indicative | Subjunctive |
  |---|---|---|
  | *a lo mejor* | 98.3% (1434) | 1.7% (25) |
  | *seguramente* | 95% (1286) | 5% (66) |
  | *probablemente* | 70% | 30% |
  | *tal vez* | 60% | 40% |
  | *acaso* | 55.3% | 44.7% |
  | *quizá(s)* | 55% | 45% |
  | *posiblemente* | 51.2% | 48.8% |

  — [García Pérez 2024](https://ojsspdc.ulpgc.es/ojs/index.php/PhilCan/article/download/1706/1783/7953) (F, PDF text read)
- Same paper: *Seguramente sea el catalán el más normalizado* (CORPES) is cited as not a mere exception. It calls Kovacci's "seguramente → indicative" only a tendency. — same source (F)
- Same paper: with *a lo mejor* "el uso del subjuntivo no está excluido, especialmente en América (NGLE 2009, p. 1956)". Example: *a lo mejor sea una idea reprimida* (Nelson Camacho, 2012, CORPES). — same source (F; the NGLE claim is secondary, as quoted by this paper)
- Same paper: *tal vez* + subjunctive outnumbers the indicative in peninsular Spanish but not in American Spanish. The NGLE says the subjunctive is slightly more frequent when the information is not focal. — same source (F)
- Same paper: in questions *acaso* is a rhetorical marker (NGLE p. 3189), and questions were excluded from the counts. Outside questions, the indicative still wins. — same source (F)
- DPD: *a lo mejor* / *a la mejor* almost always take the indicative, but the subjunctive is possible "especially in American Spanish"; it cites a Colombian example. — [DPD, mejor](https://www.rae.es/dpd/mejor) (SN, earlier notes)
- DPD: *capaz (que)* = "probablemente o quizá" in the colloquial speech of many American countries, generally with the indicative, but in Chile almost always with the subjunctive. — [DPD, capaz](https://www.rae.es/dpd/capaz) (SN, earlier notes). UDEP says *es capaz que* takes both moods (*Es capaz que consigue boletos* / *…obtenga la casa*; *Capaz mañana venga…*). — [UDEP, Es capaz que](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/es-capaz-que-en-el-dia-del-padre-ningun-papa-se-queda-en-casa/) (F, earlier notes)
- *de pronto* = "maybe" in Colombia. — [kaikki/Wiktionary, de pronto](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Spanish/meaning/d/de/de%20pronto.html) (F, earlier notes). Its mood isn't sourced.

### Inferences (verdicts and corrected wording)
- **Before the verb → both moods**: **holds**. Add "subjunctive = less sure, or not the main point". The NGLE's non-focal remark supports the second half.
- **After the verb or between commas → indicative**: **holds**, and it is the NGLE's own rule.
  - Correct: *Viene, quizás, mañana*.
  - Wrong: *Venga quizás mañana*. *Venga, quizás…* only works if the subjunctive comes from somewhere else, as in *Le pido que venga, quizás, mañana* (RK).
  - Caveat: the NGLE, as quoted by UDEP, allows a postposed *-ra* (*Hablara quizá*, labelled "subjuntivo"). Whether its value is subjunctive or indicative is unresolved. Suggested wording: "after the verb → indicative (literary *-ra* aside)". The app shouldn't generate it.
- ***acaso***: **holds with caveat**. Add: "in questions (*¿Acaso no lo sabías?*) → indicative; it is rhetorical, not 'maybe'".
- ***seguramente***: **holds with caveat**. Change to "indicative (subjunctive possible, about 5%, especially in Spain: *Seguramente sea así*)". The app shouldn't mark *seguramente sea* wrong.
  - *Seguro que* + subjunctive: no data found; keep it indicative.
- ***a lo mejor / igual / lo mismo***: **holds**, with "don't mark the subjunctive harshly". Note that *igual* and *lo mismo* = "maybe" are mainly colloquial Spain (RK). In the Southern Cone *igual* usually means "anyway" (RK).
- ***puede (ser) que, es posible que* → always subjunctive**: **holds**. No counterexample was found. *Puede que tienes razón* is nonstandard (RK).
- ***capaz (que)***: **holds with caveat**. "Mostly indicative; subjunctive also found (*Capaz mañana venga*), and nearly always in Chile."
- ***de pronto***: mood unsourced. RK: Colombians use both moods (*De pronto llueva / llueve*). Keep it "like *quizás*" at RK.
- Examples for the app: *Quizá venga / viene mañana*; *Viene, quizás, mañana*; *A lo mejor llueve*; *Puede que llueva*; *Capaz que llueve*.

### Gaps
- The NGLE original for §25.5.2c and p. 1956 wasn't seen (rae.es 403). The *Hablara quizá* labelling is unresolved.
- No mood data for *de pronto* or *seguro que*.

## 3. Wishes and orders with *que*, commands, polite forms, *me gustaría que* (5.3)

### Takeaway
Most of this holds. Three claims are overstated:
- **"Only -ra, never -se" in the polite use** is the NGLE's position. It still finds -se examples, especially with *querer*, so make it "-ra; -se is treated as unacceptable but turns up".
- ***Vayamos* "nearly literary"** is too strong: *Vayamos por partes* is an everyday phrase, and *no vayamos* is the normal negative.
- **Independent *que* + verb** is not always subjunctive: *¡Que viene el lobo!* and *¡Que no lo sé!* are indicative.

### Cited Findings
- Iglesias (Crisol 26, Sorbonne Nouvelle; cites 2022 data): most authors (Alarcos 1994, Ridruejo 1999, Rojo & Veiga 1999, NGLE 2009, Blas Arroyo 2015) treat -ra and -se as strict synonyms in subjunctive uses. Only -ra works with indicative value (*el que fuera presidente del CD Málaga*, Cadena Ser 2022) and with conditional value for *querer, haber, deber, poder* (*Quisiera / querría decirte una cosa*; *Me hubiera / habría gustado hablar contigo*). — [Iglesias, "Si pudiera o pudiese"](https://crisol.parisnanterre.fr/index.php/crisol/article/download/552/627) (F, PDF text read)
- Same source: in those conditional uses the NGLE (p. 1804) considers -se unacceptable, "bien qu'on puisse trouver des témoignages avec cette forme, notamment avec querer". It gives the NGLE example *¡Qué más {quisieras ~ querrías ~ quisieses} tú!* — same source (F: secondary source quoting the NGLE)
- A USC paper (Rojo) is reported as saying -ra alternates with *debería / podría / querría* but not with *debiese / pudiese / quisiese* (citing NGLE §24.2). — [Rojo, "El libro que publicase"](https://gramatica.usc.es/~grojo/Publicaciones/El_libro_que_publicase.pdf) (SN)
- Learner sources call *quisiese un café* wrong or forced. — [Elon.io, quisiera](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/verbs/subjunctive/imperfect/quisiera) (SN/L)
- *Me gustaría que vengas*: a forum native says it occurs when the event is still ahead and acceptance is expected. — [Tomísimo forum](https://forums.tomisimo.org/archive/index.php/t-24370.html) (SN/L). The RAE grammar page rejects the conditional inside subjunctive slots (*Deseamos que *irían*) and recommends the imperfect subjunctive. — [RAE, el condicional simple](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/el-condicional-simple-cantar%C3%ADa) (SN)
- *Vamos* vs *vayamos* for "let's go": learner sources say *vamos* is normal and *vayamos* is formal. — [Kwiziq, vámonos vs vamos vs vayamos](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/vamanos-vs-vamos-vs-vayamos) (L, earlier notes)
- Colombian voseo, present subjunctive with final stress: *que vos tengás / podás*. — [Wikipedia, Voseo](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voseo) (F, earlier notes)

### Inferences (verdicts)
- ***que* + subjunctive wishes and passed-on orders**: **holds**.
  - Add a caveat: independent *que* + **indicative** exists for warnings and emphatic repetition, as in *¡Que viene el lobo!* and *—¿Qué? —¡Que no lo sé!* (RK; NGLE "que enunciativo"). A checker shouldn't force the subjunctive after every sentence-initial *que*.
  - Examples: *¡Que te mejores!*; *Que pase el siguiente*; *¡Que te calles!*
- **Frozen formulas**: **holds** (RK/L). *¡Viva el rey! / ¡Vivan los novios!*, *Así sea*, *Que en paz descanse*. *Dios mediante* is an absolute construction, not a subjunctive. Remove it from the list or label it differently (RK).
- **Negative commands and *usted / ustedes* → present subjunctive**: **holds**.
  - *No + infinitive* is also used for impersonal or general orders (*No fumar*; colloquial Spain *¡No gritar!*) (RK).
  - Rioplatense negative voseo is normally *no cantes* (tú form). Colombian voseo uses *no cantés / no digás* (RK; consistent with the Voseo article).
- ***Nosotros* commands**: *sentémonos* and *vámonos* hold. "*Vayamos* nearly literary" is **overstated, on RK alone**: this verdict overturns an L-sourced claim with unverified reference knowledge.
  - Affirmative "let's go" is *vamos*.
  - *vayamos* is normal in *no vayamos*, in *que vayamos*, and in set phrases like *Vayamos por partes* and *vayamos al grano* (RK).
- **Polite -ra, "only -ra, never -se"**: **holds with caveat**. Corrected: "Only *-ra* is standard here (*quisiera = querría*). The NGLE treats *quisiese* as unacceptable in this use, though it is occasionally attested." Accept nothing else in the drill, but don't call it impossible.
  - "*pudiera* more common in Mexico": **unsourced** (RK). No frequency data was found. Drop it or keep it at RK.
- ***Me gustaría que* + imperfect**: **holds**. *Me gustaría que vengas* is attested informally, but no regional source was found (L). Keep "heard, not standard; accept leniently if the app wants".

### Gaps
- The NGLE text on conditional + present subjunctive in American Spanish wasn't retrieved.
- No frequency data on *pudiera* (polite) in Mexico.

## 4. Fixed phrases (6)

### Takeaway
All the listed phrases are real. "Both verbs match" needs *sea lo que fuere* as an exception (present + future). The past forms also take -se (*pasase lo que pasase*). Verdict: **holds with caveat** (mostly L/RK; no new primary source).

### Cited Findings
- Fixed subjunctive expressions (*pase lo que pase*, *que yo sepa*, etc.). — [Elon.io, fixed expressions](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-spain/complex/subjunctive-fixed-expressions) (L, earlier notes)
- The future subjunctive survives in set phrases and legal language. — [RAE GTG, futuro simple de subjuntivo](https://www.rae.es/gtg/futuro-simple-de-subjuntivo) (SN, earlier notes)

### Inferences
- The reduplicative pattern (*diga lo que diga, digan lo que digan, sea como sea*) holds. *Sea lo que fuere* and *sea como fuere* mix a present with a future subjunctive, so "both verbs match" has to exclude them.
- *Quieras o no* and *te guste o no* hold. *Que yo sepa* and *que yo recuerde* hold. *Que yo vea* is rare (RK).
- *Como tú digas* ("whatever you say") vs *como tú dices* ("as you say"): holds (RK).

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for this section.

## 5. Tense sequence (7)

### Takeaway
The table is right as a default. Two claims need fixing:
- **"After a feeling only *hubiera*"**: *hubiese* works just as well. The real point is "subjunctive, never conditional".
- **Past main verb + present subjunctive** is probably not only Latin American. I believe it also occurs in Spain when the event is still pending or still true (RK). The doc's own *Esbozo* example *Mandaron que estudie* supports it being generally acceptable.

### Cited Findings
- *Me hubiera / habría gustado hablar contigo*: *hubiera = habría* in the conditional slot. Only -ra works there. — [Iglesias](https://crisol.parisnanterre.fr/index.php/crisol/article/download/552/627) (F)
- Sequence-of-tenses data and the Latin American relaxation (Jujuy 63% etc.; Colombian style guides prefer *pidió que se anulara*) are in the earlier notes. — [Ámbito Jurídico, consecutio temporum](https://www.ambitojuridico.com/noticias/etcetera/educacion-y-cultura/consecutio-temporum) (F, earlier notes); [WordReference, me pidió que pongamos/pusiéramos](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/verbo-en-pasado-presente-del-subjuntivo-me-pidi%C3%B3-que-pongamos-pusi%C3%A9ramos.2447359/) (L)
- The RAE rejects the conditional in subjunctive slots (*Deseamos que *irían*). — [RAE, condicional simple](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/el-condicional-simple-cantar%C3%ADa) (SN)

### Inferences (verdicts)
- **Present → present/perfect subjunctive; past → imperfect/pluperfect; conditional → imperfect**: **holds** as the default.
  - Counterexample check: *Quiero que vinieras* is ungrammatical, but *Me alegro de que vinieras* (past event) is fine. That is the documented relaxation.
- **Present + imperfect subjunctive for a past event** (*No creo que mintiera*): **holds** (RK).
- **Past + present subjunctive for a pending event**: **holds**. Suggested wording: "acceptable when the event is still ahead or still true (*Me pidió que venga mañana*; the *Esbozo* allows *Mandaron que estudie*); more frequent in Latin America; resisted in Colombian formal style." The claim that it is also common in Spain is RK.
- **"After a feeling only *hubiera*, never *habría*"**: **holds with caveat**. Corrected: "after a feeling the subjunctive is required: *hubiera* or *hubiese*, never *habría* (*Lamentó que no le hubiera / hubiese gustado*)". Check that the app also accepts *hubiese* there.
- ***hubiera* = *habría* in unreal past results**: **holds** (F).
  - Examples: *Si lo hubiera sabido, te habría / hubiera llamado*.
  - Never *Si lo *habría sabido…* in standard Spanish. It occurs in northern Spain and the River Plate area as nonstandard (RK).

### Gaps
- The present perfect row (Spain present subjunctive, Latin America imperfect) still rests only on L sources.

## 6. Forms: -ra / -se and the future subjunctive (8)

### Takeaway
All claims hold. One addition: the -ra indicative use isn't only pluperfect; journalism uses it as a plain preterite (*el que fuera presidente*). The future subjunctive survives in law, set phrases, and historically in the Caribbean, Venezuela and Panama area and the Canaries. No current speech data was found.

### Cited Findings
- -ra and -se are strict synonyms in subjunctive uses. Only -ra has indicative value (rare, literary or journalistic, e.g. *Fallece el que fuera presidente…*, with preterite rather than pluperfect value, NGLE 24.21). Only -ra has conditional value with modals. — [Iglesias](https://crisol.parisnanterre.fr/index.php/crisol/article/download/552/627) (F)
- Old survival zone for the future subjunctive: the Canaries, the Spanish-speaking Caribbean (Santo Domingo, Puerto Rico, Cuba), and Atlantic South America from Panama to Venezuela (Granda 1978). Today *hubiere cantado* appears mainly in legal texts. — [Revista de Investigación Lingüística (Murcia), "Acerca de la eliminación de «hubiere cantado» del español de América"](https://revistas.um.es/ril/article/view/481551) (SN)
- Some authors say the form survives in parts of America and in legal language, without recent survey data. — [Kwartalnik Neofilologiczny 2022](https://czasopisma.pan.pl//Content/126692/PDF/2022-04-KNEO-03.pdf) (SN)
- -se is about 9% in an Ecuadorian corpus. — earlier notes (SN/F there)

### Inferences
- **"-ra = -se as subjunctive"**: **holds** (F).
- **"Only -ra as past indicative and conditional"**: **holds** (F). Add the preterite value.
- **"Future subjunctive only in laws, set phrases, some American regions"**: **holds with caveat**. "Some American regions" is historical (Caribbean and Venezuela); in modern speech it is essentially gone.
  - Modern equivalents are right: *fuere → sea*; *si fuere necesario → si es necesario*.

## 7. Mexico and Colombia (9)

### Takeaway
*Ojalá y*, *a la mejor*, *capaz que*, *de pronto* and voseo are supported at SN/F level. *Hubiera … hubiera* is standard everywhere. Its regional frequency is unverified (RK); it may be more frequent in Latin America. *Qué bueno que* + indicative is documented across Latin America as indicative after reactions to known facts, not only in Mexico. *No sé qué haga* is unverified for Mexico; the NGLE notes the infinitive now predominates generally.

### Cited Findings
- *Ojalá y* is mainly Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean. — [DPD, ojalá](https://www.rae.es/dpd/ojal%C3%A1) (SN)
- *A la mejor* is the colloquial form. — [DPD, mejor](https://www.rae.es/dpd/mejor) (SN)
- An NRFH (El Colegio de México) study of indicative for subjunctive after expressions of personal reaction uses data from Mexico City, Lima, Santiago and San Juan. It marks *Me alegro de que *has venido* as ungrammatical. — [NRFH, "Indicativo por subjuntivo"](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/1943/1934/) (SN; fetch 403, no per-city figures obtained)
- In River Plate Spanish the indicative replaces the subjunctive after emotion verbs (*Lamento mucho que te lastimaste la pierna*). — [Gingins 2021, UNRN](https://rid.unrn.edu.ar/bitstream/20.500.12049/10828/1/ArticuloICNEA.GinginsOmar.2021.pdf) (SN)
- Forum: *¡Qué bueno que vienes!* is indicative for a real, known event. — [SpanishDict, qué bueno que](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/194398/qu-bueno-que) (L)
- The NGLE contrasts *No sé qué hago / hice / haré* with *No sé qué hacer*. In these subordinates the subjunctive peaked in Classical Spanish, and today the infinitive predominates (*no sé qué decir* rather than *no sé qué diga*). — [ULL article citing NGLE §25.5o](https://www.ull.es/revistas/index.php/filologia/article/download/7656/5256/) (SN)

### Inferences (verdicts)
- *ustedes* with no *vosotros*, and -ra rather than -se: **holds**.
- *Ojalá y*, mainly Mexico: **holds** (SN).
- *A la mejor* + indicative: **holds** (SN).
- *Capaz que* + indicative: **holds**, but say "except Chile, where the subjunctive is usual".
- *De pronto* = maybe in Colombia: **holds** (F). Its mood is unsourced.
- Voseo (Antioquia, Valle, Nariño): **holds** (F).
- Past + present subjunctive: **holds**. It isn't only Latin American (section 5 above).
- *Qué bueno que* + indicative: **holds with caveat**. Not just Mexico; it is documented in several Latin American cities and in River Plate Spanish (SN). The app should keep generating the subjunctive and accept the indicative leniently in Latin American mode.
- *No sé qué haga*, Mexico: **unverified** (RK). Its existence is plausible and historically grounded (SN); its Mexican currency isn't sourced. "Not flagged" is a safe app choice.
- *Hubiera … hubiera* in both halves, Mexico and Colombia: **reword** to "standard everywhere; possibly more frequent in Latin America; regional frequency unverified" (RK). If anything regional belongs here, it is colloquial imperfect indicative in conditions (*Si lo sabía, te avisaba*), which is RK and unsourced.

### Gaps
- NRFH per-city percentages (403).
- Mexican data on *no sé qué haga*.
- Mood data for Colombian *de pronto*.
