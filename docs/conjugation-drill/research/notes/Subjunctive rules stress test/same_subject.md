## 0.1 Wanting/needing/preferring with one person → infinitive is a must

### Takeaway
**Holds.** With volition verbs (*querer, desear, preferir, necesitar*), a same-subject *que* + subjunctive is ungrammatical (obviation), not just clumsy. Examples: *Quiero ir* / \**Quiero que (yo) vaya*; *Juana quiere venir* / \**Juana quiere que (ella) venga* (meaning Juana herself). Sentences that look like exceptions (*Quiero que me operen*, *No quiero que me vean*, *Espero que me den el trabajo*) have a different subject, an impersonal 3rd plural. The speaker is only the object, so they obey the rule. The only genuine same-subject cases are focus clefts (*Quiero que sea yo quien lo diga*) and marginal uses with *poder*.

Corrected wording: "One person who both wants and does → infinitive. If you are only on the receiving end (*que me operen, que me llamen*), someone else is the subject, so *que* + subjunctive is normal."

### Cited Findings
- Luján states that in *Juan quiere que {él/Ø} venga* the embedded subject must be read as disjoint from the main subject ("Obviación (anti-correferencia oblig.)"). In *Juan quiere Ø venir* it must be coreferent ("Control (correferencia oblig.)"). — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- Luján marks \**Juana quiere que (ella) venga* (coindexed) as ungrammatical. She says the obviation effect "se restringe al complemento de verbos de volición (como querer/desear) y de verbos factivos (como lamentar/deplorar)". — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- Luján describes the infinitive and subjunctive forms after *querer* as being in "distribución complementaria", depending on whether the subordinate subject is the same as or different from the main subject (*Quieres venir* / *Quieres que venga*, with \**Quieres que vengas*). — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- A UAM master's thesis (snippet only) uses *Juan quiere que apruebe* to illustrate the disjoint-reference effect, i.e. pronominal obviation. — [UAM TFM](https://libros.uam.es/tfm/catalog/download/905/1583/1543?inline=1) (SN)
- Learner sites call "Yo quiero que yo vaya a Valencia" incorrect. One SpanishDict answer disagrees and calls this kind of sentence "grammatically correct" but "highly unnatural". — [Kwiziq/Lawless](https://progress.lawlessspanish.com/questions/view/subjunctive-clause-with-same-subject) (L); [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/279517/subjunctive-with-no-change-of-subject) (L). The academic source (Luján) sides with "ungrammatical".

### Inferences
- *Quiero que me operen / No quiero que me vean / Espero que me den el trabajo / Quiero que me quieran*: the subject of the embedded verb is an unspecified 3rd person plural ("they"), not *yo*, so obviation isn't violated. They aren't exceptions to 0.1. Learners should be told that "me" as an object doesn't count as the same person. (RK, consistent with Luján's subject-based definition)
- Same-subject alternative for a passive: *Quiero ser operado* is grammatical but stilted. *Quiero que me operen* is the natural form. (RK)
- Focus/cleft: *Quiero que sea yo quien lo diga* and *Quiero ser yo quien lo diga* are both fine. In the first, the subject of *sea* is the cleft, not *yo* directly, which sidesteps obviation. A bare emphatic \**Quiero que yo sea el primero* stays bad for most speakers; the natural forms are *Quiero ser yo el primero* and *Quiero ser el primero*. (RK) Spanish allows an overt emphatic pronoun inside the infinitive (*Quiero hacerlo yo*), and Herbeck's paper on overt subjects in Spanish control infinitives explains these as "emphatic pronouns" whose reference is overridden by the anaphoric link. — [Herbeck 2011](https://www.unige.ch/lettres/linge/syntaxe/journal/Volume7/Herbeck_GGG11.pdf) (F for the general claim; the example *Quiero hacerlo yo* is RK)
- *Queremos que vayamos* (nosotros = nosotros) is exact coreference, not partial, so it's as bad as \**quiero que yo vaya*. Use *Queremos ir*. (inference from Luján, F-based)
- Mexico/Colombia: I found no evidence that obviation differs regionally. (gap)

## 0.2 Feelings → same subject takes the infinitive

### Takeaway
**Holds.** Factive-emotive verbs (*lamentar, sentir, alegrarse, temer*) show the same obviation as *querer*. Examples: *Siento llegar tarde* / \**Siento que (yo) llegue tarde* (meaning I am late); *Me alegro de estar aquí* / *Me alegro de que estés aquí*. *Siento que llegue tarde* is only fine with a different subject (he/she/you-usted is late). Said about yourself, *Me alegro de que esté aquí* gets read as "that he/she/usted is here".

Caveat: *sentir que* + **indicative** means "to sense/feel that", and it can be same-subject: *Siento que estoy perdiendo el tiempo* (I feel I'm wasting time). That isn't an exception to 0.2; it's the perception meaning of *sentir* (see 1.5 in the doc).

### Cited Findings
- Luján gives \**(Tú) lamentas que Ø vengas* (same subject) as ungrammatical and groups factive verbs (*lamentar/deplorar*) with volition verbs as the only ones that show obviation. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- Luján shows that *alegrar(se)* alternates with an infinitive or *que*: *le alegra que vengas* / *le alegra verte* / *(ellos) se alegran de que vengas* / *se alegra de verte*. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)

### Inferences
- *Tengo miedo de perderme* / *Tengo miedo de que me pierdas* fits the same pattern. (RK)
- *Me alegro de que me hayan elegido*: "me" is the object and "they" chose, so it's fine (same reasoning as 0.1). (RK)
- *Temo que no llegue a tiempo* with a same-subject reading is heard in speech, especially with non-agentive predicates, but it's not standard. *Temo no llegar a tiempo* is preferred. (RK)

### Gaps
- No corpus or authority source was found for how acceptable same-subject *temer que* / *sentir que* + subj is in speech.

## 0.3 Para, sin, antes de, a fin de: same subject → infinitive

### Takeaway
**Holds as the norm.** Same-subject *para que / sin que* is strongly dispreferred. Same-subject *antes de que / después de que / hasta que* is softer: it's attested, and acceptable mostly when the event is non-agentive, or when the conjunction-plus-finite form is needed to mark tense or reference. I found no source that calls it ungrammatical, so the evidence supports "avoid", not "wrong". Examples: *Lo terminé antes de irme* (preferred) vs. *Lo terminé antes de que me fuera* (odd, reads as if someone else left unless context forces it); *Estudio para aprobar* / *Estudio para que apruebes*; *Salió sin decir nada* / *sin que nadie lo viera*.

Caribbean (and some other American) Spanish uses an overt preverbal subject in the infinitive: *para yo poder descansar, antes de yo llegar*. This is the dialectal alternative to same-subject *para que yo pueda*. *Lo hago para que yo pueda descansar* is rejected by standard norms; use *para poder descansar*.

### Cited Findings
- The DPD says *antes de que* and *antes que* are both valid before a finite verb (*Llegará antes (de) que anochezca*), and that *antes de* is required before an infinitive. The snippet doesn't mention the same-subject condition. — [DPD "antes"](https://www.rae.es/dpd/antes) (SN)
- University handouts: infinitive with the same subject, subjunctive when the subject changes (*El Greco vivió en Venecia antes de mudarse a Roma*). — [Masaryk Univ. "Oraciones temporales"](https://is.muni.cz/el/law/podzim2020/MV504Z/um/Oraciones_temporales__1_.pdf) (SN); [EdTech Books, "El subjuntivo en cláusulas adverbiales"](https://edtechbooks.org/docx/4525) (SN)
- Herbeck, discussing overt preverbal subjects in adjunct infinitives (*para/antes de* + pronoun + infinitive), cites Lipski (1991): such subjects are "not fully integrated into the grammar of peninsular Spanish speakers". He says pronouns are strongly preferred over full noun phrases in that position. — [Herbeck 2011](https://www.unige.ch/lettres/linge/syntaxe/journal/Volume7/Herbeck_GGG11.pdf) (F)
- A Colmex (NRFH) article notes that in *para sacarme la placa del pecho* the subject of the infinitive can be either the main subject or the object, i.e. *para* + infinitive doesn't always force the main subject. — [NRFH/Colmex](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/729/729) (SN)

### Inferences
- The doc's line "*Para, sin, antes de* still want the infinitive" is right as a teaching rule. The checker shouldn't flag same-subject *antes de que / hasta que* as an error, because it's dispreferred, not wrong. (RK)
- *Para* + infinitive can be controlled by the object (*Te lo doy para leer* = for you to read), so "same subject" means the controller, not strictly the grammatical subject. (RK, consistent with the Colmex snippet)
- *Para yo poder* / *antes de yo llegar* is Caribbean (PR, DR, Cuba, coastal Colombia and Venezuela) and is heard elsewhere in Latin America. It's uncommon in Mexico City or Bogotá norms. (RK; Lipski cited only via Herbeck)

### Gaps
- No NGLE or DPD statement on whether same-subject *antes de que / para que* is ungrammatical or merely dispreferred was retrieved.
- No corpus frequency was retrieved for *antes de que me fuera*-type sentences.

## 0.4 Impersonal phrases: infinitive = general; que + subj = particular person; que is fine even about yourself

### Takeaway
**Holds.** Impersonal expressions have no subject of their own to cause obviation, so *que* + subjunctive about oneself is grammatical. Examples: *Es mejor que me vaya* = *Es mejor irme* (both natural; *Es mejor que me vaya* is probably the more common in speech); *Es importante que (yo) termine hoy* (fine); *Me conviene salir temprano* (the dative controls the infinitive).

### Cited Findings
- Luján restricts obviation to volition and factive complements. Impersonal *es mejor / es importante* has no main-clause subject to be disjoint from. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F; applying it to impersonals is my inference)

### Inferences
- *Es mejor irme* is natural. The clitic on the infinitive tells you who's meant, and *Es mejor ir* without the clitic would be read generically. (RK)
- *Es importante que yo...*: grammatical. Overt *yo* adds contrast (*que yo, no tú*). (RK)
- With a dative experiencer (*Me conviene / Me hace falta / Me es imposible*), the infinitive is controlled by the dative: *Me hace falta dormir*. *Me hace falta que duerma* is odd as same-subject but fine with a 3rd person (*que él duerma*). (RK)

### Gaps
- No authority quote was retrieved on whether *es mejor que me vaya* is more frequent than *es mejor irme*.

## 0.5 Gustar/encantar/importar: the "same person" is the experiencer

### Takeaway
**Holds.** The dative experiencer controls the infinitive subject. Examples: *Me gusta bailar* (I dance) / *Me gusta que bailes*; *¿Te importa esperar?* (you wait) / *¿Te importa que espere?* (I wait); *Le alegra verte* / *Le alegra que vengas*.

### Cited Findings
- Luján gives the pairs *le alegra que vengas* / *le alegra verte*, where the infinitive is understood with the dative's reference and the *que* clause has a different subject. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- Felíu Arquiola studies infinitive subjects of *gustar*-type verbs, including non-standard plural agreement (*Me gustan más estudiar otras asignaturas*). — [Felíu Arquiola, Dialnet](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8881629.pdf) (SN)
- Biezma Garrido argues that with Spanish psych verbs, control isn't always fixed to the experiencer: either argument may control in some examples. — [Biezma Garrido 2005](https://cla-acl.artsci.utoronto.ca/pdfs/actes-2005/Biezma-Garrido.pdf) (SN)

### Inferences
- Caveat for learners: *Me molesta esperar* = I wait. *Me molesta que esperes* = you wait. A same-subject *Me molesta que (yo) tenga que esperar* is heard in speech for non-agentive situations, though *Me molesta tener que esperar* is standard. (RK)
- Biezma's point suggests rare readings where a non-experiencer controls (e.g. *A Juan le preocupa llegar tarde a la reunión de María*). These don't matter for teaching. (SN-based inference)

### Gaps
- Biezma's specific examples weren't read in full.

## 0.6 Overlapping people are different subjects (partial coreference)

### Takeaway
**Holds for the *que* + subjunctive side, and the user's examples need correcting.**
1. When *yo* ⊂ *nosotros*, *que* + subjunctive is fully grammatical, because obviation only blocks exact identity: *Quiero que vayamos juntos*, *Me alegro de que hayamos ganado*, *Espero que nos vaya bien*.
2. **The mood is set by the trigger, not by overlap.** After a volition verb, partial overlap never allows the indicative: \**Quiero que vamos juntos*. This holds even though *Quiero que vamos* is heard in some casual Latin American speech as a calque or error. (RK) After *creer*, overlap keeps the indicative as normal: *Creo que ganamos*.
3. **Whether an infinitive is possible with overlap depends on agreement.** If the infinitive clause contains something marked plural that must refer to the group (*juntos, nos, nuestro, entre todos*), the infinitive is bad or marginal: ?\**Quiero ir juntos*, \**Quiero reunirnos*. Without such a marker, the infinitive can be read loosely with the speaker as part of a group: *Me alegro de haber ganado* (team member speaking) is acceptable and common in sports talk. *Prefiero hacerlo juntos* is heard colloquially but is non-standard; standard is *Prefiero que lo hagamos juntos*. (all RK)
4. ***Queremos que vayamos*** is not partial coreference. It's exact (nosotros = nosotros) and therefore ungrammatical; use *Queremos ir*.

Corrected wording for 0.6: "If 'I' is only part of 'we', use *que* + subjunctive (*Quiero que vayamos*). The infinitive can work only when nothing in it says 'we' (*Me alegro de haber ganado* is fine; \**Quiero ir juntos* is not). Overlap never makes the indicative possible after *querer*."

### Cited Findings
- Landau's typology: obligatory control is exhaustive or partial. In partial control the understood subject must include the controller, but can be larger than it. — [Biezma Garrido 2005, summarizing Landau](https://cla-acl.artsci.utoronto.ca/pdfs/actes-2005/Biezma-Garrido.pdf) (SN)
- Spanish has "Inverse Partial Control" (Rodrigues 2007): *No sabemos si firmar los lingüistas la carta* ("(We) don't know whether to sign, the linguists, the letter"). The overt infinitive subject's "reference set must be included in the reference set of the matrix controller", which shows that Spanish infinitives tolerate subjects that only partially overlap with the controller. — [Herbeck 2011](https://www.unige.ch/lettres/linge/syntaxe/journal/Volume7/Herbeck_GGG11.pdf) (F)
- Obviation is defined as disjoint reference with the main subject, restricted to volition and factive verbs. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F). Luján doesn't discuss overlap, so treating overlap as "not identical, therefore allowed with *que*" is my inference.
- Sheehan (2014), "Partial Control in Romance Languages: The Covert Comitative Analysis", exists and is relevant, but only as a bibliography listing. — [search listing](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/la.270.09her) (SN, content not seen)

### Inferences
- Inverse partial control (subset inside the infinitive) is documented. Ordinary partial control (*Quiero reunirme a las seis* meaning "we meet") is the Landau type, and Spanish speakers vary in accepting it. Teach *que* + subjunctive for "we". (RK)
- *Espero que me vaya bien*: the subject of *ir* is impersonal, and *me* is a dative. It's correct, and it's a case of "different subject", as the doc says. (RK)
- The doc's statement that "I is part of we → treat as the same person" (if the learner was told this) is **wrong for *que*-avoidance**, and the doc's own 0.6 contradicts it. The user should be told 0.6 is right: overlap counts as different. (inference)

### Gaps
- The NGLE text on "control parcial" wasn't retrieved, and no Spanish-specific judgments for *quiero ir juntos* or *me alegro de haber ganado* were found in fetched sources. Points 3–4 above are RK.
- Sheehan 2014 and Landau 2015 weren't read.

## 0.7 Verbs with an object (dejar, permitir, prohibir, hacer, mandar, impedir, aconsejar) allow both; pedir/decir prefer que

### Takeaway
**Holds with caveats.**
- (a) The two forms aren't fully equivalent. Only the infinitive is controlled by the object (Luján). The *que* clause can even have a third subject: *Te permito que tus niños jueguen en mi jardín*.
- (b) With a reflexive object, only the infinitive works: *Ana no se permite salir* / \**Ana no se permite que salga*; *No te permites descansar* / \**No te permites que descanses*.
- (c) Don't put *prometer* on this list. It's controlled by the subject (*Ana le promete salir* = Ana goes out), and its *que* form takes the future indicative (*le promete que saldrá*).
- (d) *Hacer* + infinitive is the norm when the object is the doer (*Me hizo reír*). *Hacer que* + subj is used more for non-human causes (*La lluvia hizo que cancelaran el partido*). (RK)
- (e) *Pedir*: *Te pido que vengas* is standard. *Pido venir / Pidió salir* means the asker does it ("asked to leave"). *Decir* + infinitive for commands (*Me dijo venir*) is regional (some Latin American usage) or non-standard; the standard is *Me dijo que viniera*. (RK)

Examples: *Te dejo salir = Te dejo que salgas*; *Me prohibieron fumar / Me prohibieron que fumara*; *Le prometí volver / Le prometí que volvería*.

### Cited Findings
- *Ana le permite salir* (object control, Ø = le) vs *Ana le promete salir* (subject control, Ø = Ana). The *que* versions are *le permite que salga* and *le promete que saldrá*. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- Luján argues that the infinitive and subjunctive forms of *permitir* "no son equivalentes": only the infinitive's null subject is controlled by the indirect object. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- Ungrammatical with a reflexive: \**Ana no se permite que (ella) salga*, \**No te permites que (tú) descanses*; grammatical: *Ana no se permite salir*. A subjunctive subject different from the dative is possible: *Te permito que tus niños jueguen en mi jardín*, vs \**Tus niños te permito a ellos jugar en mi jardín*. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)

### Inferences
- Perception verbs (*ver, oír, sentir* + infinitive) work like causatives: *Te vi salir*, *La oí cantar*. They never take subjunctive *que*; *ver que* + indicative is a fact report (*Vi que salías*). (RK)
- *Impedir*: *Me impidió entrar / Me impidió que entrara*, both standard. *Aconsejar*: *Te aconsejo descansar / que descanses*; the *que* form is more common. (RK)

### Gaps
- No source compared the frequency of infinitive vs *que* per verb, or confirmed regional *decir* + infinitive.

## 0.8 Where the same person with que is allowed

### Takeaway
**Partly overstated, and the doc contradicts itself.**
- *Esperar*: *Espero poder ir* is standard. Same-subject *Espero que pueda ir* is reported by teachers as possible and widely heard, especially with *poder* or outcomes you don't control. A cleaner analysis: *esperar* behaves less strictly than *querer*. **But section 0's checker note says it flags *espero que yo…***, which conflicts with 0.8. Either drop *espero* from the checker or limit the flag to non-*poder* verbs.
- Time words: *Trabajé hasta que me cansé* is fine. It's indicative because the event happened, so the infinitive question doesn't arise, though *Trabajé hasta cansarme* is equally standard. *Te llamo cuando llegue* is fine. **"*cuando* has no infinitive form" is wrong as stated**: *al* + infinitive covers it (*Te llamo al llegar*). The accurate point is that *cuando* itself can't take an infinitive.
- *Para/sin/antes de*: see 0.3. They want the infinitive; same subject with *que* is dispreferred.
- Add: *dudar* and negated *creer/decir* allow the same subject with subjunctive (*Dudo que pueda llegar a tiempo*; *No creo que (yo) pueda*). See 0.9.

### Cited Findings
- Spanishland School: with no change of subject, use the infinitive (*Espero ir*), but "al usar poder, sí puedes usar el subjuntivo"; *Espero que (yo) pueda ir* is possible, though "suena mejor decir: 'Espero poder ir'". — [Spanishland School 324](https://spanishlandschool.com/324) (L)
- Kwiziq's Head of Spanish (native teacher) says there is "some flexibility" in colloquial Spanish, giving *Dudo que llegue a tiempo* and *Duda mucho que pueda asistir a la reunión* (same subject) as alternatives to *Dudo (poder) llegar a tiempo*. — [Kwiziq/Lawless](https://progress.lawlessspanish.com/questions/view/when-subjects-are-the-same-subjunctive-errors) (L)
- Luján lists *esperar*-type predicates among neither volition (*querer/desear*) nor factive. Her obviation list includes only volition and factive verbs. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F; whether *esperar* counts as volition isn't stated, so this is inference)

### Inferences
- Why *esperar* is looser: it straddles "hope" (weaker volition) and "expect". *Espero que me salga bien* and *Espero que no me pase nada* (impersonal subject) are extremely common and not same-subject. *Espero que pueda ir* is the borderline type, accepted in speech in Spain, Mexico and Colombia. (RK)
- *Hasta que* same-subject with a past indicative (*hasta que me cansé*) is unremarkable. With a future subjunctive (*Trabajaré hasta que me canse*), it's also common, alongside *hasta cansarme*. (RK)

### Gaps
- No corpus frequency was retrieved for *espero que pueda* (same subject) vs *espero poder*.

## 0.9 Believing/saying keep que + indicative for the same person; creer + infinitive is formal

### Takeaway
**Overstated.** It's true for **affirmative** *creer/decir/pensar* (*Creo que tengo razón*; *Dice que está cansado*). But when negated or doubted, these verbs take the subjunctive, and **the same subject is allowed** because there's no obviation: *No creo que (yo) pueda ir*; *No digo que (yo) lo haga bien*; *Ana no cree que (ella) venga*; *Dudo que pueda llegar a tiempo*. *Creer* + infinitive (*Creo tener razón*, *No creo ser egoísta*) is formal or written but fully correct.

Corrected wording: "Believing and saying don't cause obviation. Same subject: affirmative → *que* + indicative (*creo que tengo razón*); negated or doubted → *que* + subjunctive is still fine with the same subject (*no creo que pueda*). The infinitive (*creo tener razón*) is formal."

Also, *prometer* and *jurar* take *que* + indicative with the same subject (*Prometo que volveré*) or an infinitive (*Prometo volver*).

### Cited Findings
- Luján contrasts obviative volition and factive verbs with *creer/decir*: *Ana no cree que (ella) venga* and *(Tú) no dijiste que Ø vinieras* are grammatical with coreference. The absence of obviation also holds in *No digo que (yo) lo haga bien*. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
- A Kwiziq tutor: with negated *creer*, both *Yo no creo ser muy egoísta* and the subjunctive version are accepted, and the subjunctive is more common. — [Kwiziq (via search)](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/subjunctive-clause-with-same-subject) (L/SN)
- *Le promete que saldrá* (indicative with subject control). — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)

### Inferences
- The checker must never flag *no creo que (yo)…* or *dudo que (yo)…* as same-subject errors. (inference from F)

### Gaps
- No DPD/NGLE statement on the register of *creer* + infinitive was retrieved.

## 0.10 Something already done: haber + participle

### Takeaway
**Holds.** The perfect infinitive expresses a prior event with the same subject: *Me alegro de haber venido*; *Siento no haber llamado*; *Después de haber comido, salimos*. The different-subject counterpart uses the perfect subjunctive: *Me alegro de que hayas venido*. With *después de*, the simple infinitive is equally common (*después de comer*). (RK)

### Inferences

## Cross-cutting: is "Quiero que yo vaya" ever acceptable, and other edge cases

### Takeaway
\**Quiero que yo vaya* stays bad even with emphasis. The fixes are *Quiero ir yo* (overt emphatic pronoun in the infinitive) or the cleft *Quiero que sea yo quien vaya*. Sentences with "me" as an object (*Quiero que me operen / me vean / me den*) are not exceptions, because their subject is different. Real same-subject *que* + subjunctive occurs only:
- with non-obviative triggers (impersonals, *dudar*, negated *creer/decir*, time conjunctions),
- marginally with *esperar* + *poder*,
- in colloquial speech.

### Cited Findings
- Overt nominative pronouns are licit in Spanish control infinitives (the emphatic pronoun type). — [Herbeck 2011](https://www.unige.ch/lettres/linge/syntaxe/journal/Volume7/Herbeck_GGG11.pdf) (F)
- Obviation is limited to volition and factive complements. — [Luján 1996](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/295/284) (F)
