## Q1. The big idea: indicative = asserted; subjunctive = not asserted (nobody claims it, or it is presupposed and only reacted to). Is this the NGLE's position, and what breaks it?

### Takeaway
**Holds, with a caveat.** The two-way test matches the main line of the literature: the NGLE ties the indicative to *aserción*, and Ruiz Campillo's *declarar/no declarar* says the same. But the second half ("presupposed → subjunctive") is the weak point that grammarians themselves flag. Being known or presupposed does **not** by itself bring the subjunctive (*Ya sé que trabajas*). Feeling and evaluation predicates **do** allow the indicative when the speaker also reports the fact as news. The NGLE describes this as occasional and more frequent in American Spanish. The doc should say "presupposed **and the sentence only comments on it**", and should admit that feeling predicates are a real area of alternation, not an exceptionless rule.

### Cited Findings
- The NGLE (2009, §25.6h–l) is cited as saying that the indicative expresses the content of an assertion — [search summary of secondary articles](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CLAC/article/download/82539/4564456569142/4564456709300) (SN).
- The NGLE (§40.1d) defines *información temática* as information the speaker presupposes is known to the hearer. That definition sits in the information-structure chapter, not in ch. 25 — [same search summary](https://digitum.um.es/digitum/bitstream/10201/74303/1/2266-6304-1-PB.pdf) (SN). **I found no snippet showing that NGLE ch. 25 explains mood after feeling verbs as "temático". The doc's "(the NGLE's *aserción*)" is safe. Attributing the "presupposed" half to the NGLE is not verified.**
- Ruiz Campillo defines the indicative as declaration ("lo que un sujeto sabe o piensa sobre el mundo") and the subjunctive as non-declaration. On feelings, he says that commenting on something may presuppose that it is true, but it "nunca implica la responsabilidad del hablante sobre la declaración de esa supuesta verdad". His examples: *Me alegra que ESTÉS aquí* / *Siento mucho que FRACASARAS* (true facts, subjunctive) against *Yo creo que ESTÁ en casa* (uncertain, indicative) — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F).
- Ruiz Campillo credits the discourse view (indicative = rheme/new information; subjunctive = theme/shared information) with the best explanation of "matrices de comentario" (*Me alegra que lo HAYAS DECIDIDO*: "no informo de que 'lo has decidido' porque esta información es compartida"). He also says it has clear limits. In a footnote he notes that Bybee & Terrell had to "renunciar a la elegancia de una hipótesis aserción / no aserción" and add presupposition. In other words, the assertion-only theory needs a second clause, exactly as the doc's "two ways" says — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F).
- **Counterexample to "known → subjunctive":** Aletá shows that the rule "subordinada en indicativo = información nueva; en subjuntivo = información ya conocida" predicts the wrong *Ya sé que \*trabajes en la Opel* and *Te pido que \*vienes mañana*. Known information after *saber* stays indicative — [Aletá 2004](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/dam/jcr:7b24dd06-2d99-4e89-9cbc-05761cd6b92c/2004-redele-2-01aleta-pdf.pdf) (F).
- **Counterexample: feelings with the indicative, in the NGLE.** As quoted on hispanoteca: "Aunque los sujetos y los complementos oracionales de los verbos de afección se construyen en subjuntivo, algunos de ellos se usan en ocasiones en indicativo, más frecuentemente en el español americano que en el europeo: *Me alegro de que terminaron ya el trabajo*; *Me preocupa que lo agarraron a él*. El uso del indicativo en estos casos revela la intención de los hablantes de resaltar el contenido informativo de la subordinada." The same passage gives the alternation with *quejarse de, reprochar, echar en cara, censurar, presumir de, jactarse de, vanagloriarse de* (*Se quejó de que … había propalado infundios*) — [NGLE Manual §25.3.2–25.3.3b via hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- Porto Dapena (1991, pp. 117–118), quoted: "Los verbos de emoción, pese a exigir normalmente subjuntivo, permiten la alternancia: *Lo que nos preocupa es que no escribe* / *… no escriba*. Con indicativo, el hablante adopta una postura informativa". He also rejects "new information → indicative" as a hard rule: *Lo que más me molestaba es que se reían / se riesen de mí* are both fine as an answer to a question about something the hearer does not know — [hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- Borrego, Asencio & Prieto (1987), quoted: feeling verbs sometimes "llevan dentro un verbo de comunicación" and then take the indicative: *Me preocupa que la Bolsa ha bajado* (I react **and** inform) vs *…haya bajado* (I take a known fact and react). Same with *Me quejo de que mi hijo estudia/estudie poco* and *Tengo la satisfacción de que todos me han/hayan ayudado* — [hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- An NGLE example with an evaluative *lo + adj*: *Lo raro es que no {vi ~ viera} huellas* (NGLE 2009 p. 3587); also *Era raro que alguien denunciase a un compañero* (p. 3547) — [search summary of secondary articles](https://digitum.um.es/digitum/bitstream/10201/74303/1/2266-6304-1-PB.pdf) (SN).
- *El hecho de que* + indicative is about 36% of CORPES XXI cases (see Q8) — [Nowikow](https://press.uni.lodz.pl/index.php/wul/catalog/download/412/1846/880?inline=1) (F).
- *Es lógico que* is listed by Ruiz Campillo as an evaluation (subjunctive), set against *Opino que* — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F). I found no attested *es lógico que* + indicative. RK: it occurs in speech when the speaker is really arguing a conclusion ("es lógico que no vino: estaba enfermo"), but treat that as unverified.

### Inferences
- Suggested wording for the doc: "Indicative: the speaker puts the clause forward as information. Subjunctive: the clause is not put forward, either because nobody claims it, or because it is taken as given and the sentence only reacts to it or judges it. With feelings and judgements the indicative is possible when the speaker **also** wants to report the fact (*Me preocupa que la Bolsa ha bajado*). This is more frequent in Latin America; the subjunctive is the safe default."
- "Known" is not the test. Being only reacted to is. Keep the word *only* and add Aletá's *Ya sé que trabajas* as the warning example.

### Gaps
- Exact NGLE ch. 25 wording on *aserción* vs *presuposición* for feeling predicates. rae.es could not be fetched; check §25.3 and §25.6 directly.

## Q2. Negated belief: *Juan no cree que la Tierra es/sea redonda*; *No creía que eras/fueras*; first-person *\*No creo que la Tierra es redonda*

### Takeaway
**The third-person claim holds (F).** **The first-person-present claim is slightly overstated.** Ruiz Campillo accepts *No pienso que estoy enfermo* with a marked reading ("X is true, but I don't think about it"). So "only the subjunctive works" should become "the subjunctive is normal; the indicative is very marked".

### Cited Findings
- "the negation of a verb of belief in the main clause triggers the subjunctive in the next clause, but it is also not wrong to use the indicative." *Michael no cree que Panamá sea…* is neutral; with *es* the speaker asserts their own view (Pountain 2016, pp. 116–117) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjunctive_mood_in_Spanish) (F).
- Ruiz Campillo's table: *Ella no piensa que esté enfermo* = "ella no cree X" (S); *Ella no piensa que estoy enfermo* = "X es verdad, aunque ella no lo piensa" (I). *No pienso que esté enfermo* = "no creo X" (S); *No pienso que estoy enfermo* = "X es verdad, pero no pienso en esa verdad" (I). *Yo no veía que estaba enferma* = "X es verdad, yo no me daba cuenta antes… ahora sí" (I) — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F). This directly supports the doc's *No creía que eras tan simpático* row (the past self doubts, the present speaker asserts).
- Aletá gives *No cree que viniera ayer* / *No cree que vino ayer* as a real difference that teaching rules wrongly call "neutralización" — [Aletá](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/dam/jcr:7b24dd06-2d99-4e89-9cbc-05761cd6b92c/2004-redele-2-01aleta-pdf.pdf) (F).

### Inferences
- Examples: *Juan no cree que la Tierra es redonda* (speaker: it is) / *…sea redonda* (neutral). *No creía que eras tan simpático* (and you are). *No creo que venga* (normal); *No creo que viene* is heard colloquially but is not standard (RK).
- Corrected doc wording: "With *yo* in the present, the subjunctive is normal. The indicative (*no pienso que estoy enfermo*) is possible only in the odd sense 'it's true, I just don't dwell on it'. The app should not generate it."

### Gaps
- No corpus figure for *no creo que* + indicative in Mexico or Colombia.

## Q3. Knowing negated: *No sabía que estabas* vs *No sabía que estuvieras*; *No esperaba que vinieras*

### Takeaway
**Holds with a caveat.** The indicative is the default and vouches for the fact. *No sabía que estuvieras / fueras* is also attested and accepted. It presents the content from the ignorant past viewpoint without the speaker vouching for it (it may not be true, or the speaker isn't committing). The doc's table calls *no sabía que* simply "a *no* with no subjunctive". Add that the subjunctive is possible and changes the meaning. *No esperaba que vinieras* holds, and is better explained as *esperar* = expect, a non-assertive verb, than as a "reaction".

### Cited Findings
- A Foro de ELE article (Univ. València) says *saber* "no necesariamente debe cambiar su modo" and gives *No sabía que eras/fueras fan de Amaral* as both possible — [search summary](https://ojs.uv.es/index.php/foroele/article/download/9189/8707) (SN; the page is behind a bot wall).
- A Kwiziq/Lawless teacher: *No saber que* doesn't "normally" trigger the subjunctive. The indicative shows the speaker fully accepts the fact (*No sabía que estabas tan enfermo*); the subjunctive signals an unconfirmed state of knowledge — [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/no-saber-trigger-subjunctive), [Lawless](https://progress.lawlessspanish.com/questions/view/subjuntivo-pasado) (L, via search summary).
- Ruiz Campillo: *Yo no veía que estaba enferma* (indicative) = it's true, I didn't notice then, I do now — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F).

### Inferences
- *No sabía que estabas aquí* (and you are; default). *No sabía que estuvieras aquí* (correct; it puts less weight on the fact, or the speaker doesn't vouch for it). *No sé que esté aquí* = "as far as I know, he isn't" (RK; this is the present first-person case, where the subjunctive is normal: *No sé que haya ninguna regla así*).
- Same pattern (RK): *no recordar que*: *No recuerdo que me lo dijeras* (I doubt you did) / *No recordaba que tenías clase* (you did). *Ignorar que* alternates the same way.
- *No esperaba que vinieras* (subjunctive although true). Expectation is non-assertive with or without *no*, like *esperar* (hope). The indicative *No esperaba que vendrías* is also attested in the formal "expect" sense (RK).

### Gaps
- No NGLE wording found on negated factives with the subjunctive (believed to be in §25.5); not verified.

## Q4. Questions and negative commands: *¿No crees que es tarde?*, *¿Crees que venga?*, *No creas que es fácil*

### Takeaway
*¿No crees que…?* + indicative **holds (F)**. *No creas que* + indicative **holds (L/SN)**. *¿Crees que venga?* being "more common in Mexico" is **unverified**: I found only a forum claim (L). Wording should stay hedged: "the subjunctive adds doubt; it is reported to be more common in Mexico".

### Cited Findings
- Ruiz Campillo: *¿No crees que es un poco tarde?* = "Que yo pienso X, y pido confirmación de esa creencia (→ I)" — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F).
- Neutral *¿Crees que…?* usually takes the indicative. A forum user says the subjunctive after affirmative *¿crees que?* depends on region; another says they read it is "most common in Mexico" (no data) — [Tomisimo forum](https://forums.tomisimo.org/showthread.php?p=161027), [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/usine-creer-as-a-question) (L, via search summary).
- A 2023 LSRL abstract on Yucatec Spanish reports bilingual speakers accepting the subjunctive after *creer* where other dialects would not — [Bove, LSRL 49 abstract](https://www.linguistics.uga.edu/sites/default/files/LSRL49-abstracts/BOVE_22.pdf) (SN). This is a contact variety, not general Mexican Spanish.
- WordReference: after *no creas que* the clause "admite muy mal el subjuntivo" — [WordReference thread](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/no-creas-que-indicativo-o-subjuntivo.3406967/) (SN, from the prior research run).

### Inferences
- Examples: *¿No crees que ya es tarde?* (I think it is; agree?). *¿Crees que va a llover?* (neutral) / *¿Crees que llueva?* (doubtful; common in Latin American speech, RK). *No creas que es fácil* (= it isn't easy, I'm telling you). *No pienses que te voy a esperar.*
- RK: *¿Crees que venga?* is grammatical across the Spanish-speaking world (questions are a non-assertive context). The regional skew is plausible but unsourced. The app should accept both and generate the indicative.

### Gaps
- No corpus study found comparing *¿crees que* + subjunctive by country.

## Q5. Doubt and denial: *dudar*, *no dudar*, *negar*, *no negar*, *es mentira que*, *¿Niegas que lo dijiste?*

### Takeaway
**Holds.** *Dudar/negar* → subjunctive. *No dudar/no negar* → indicative preferred, subjunctive also correct (it holds back commitment). *¿Niegas que lo dijiste?* + indicative is supported only by teaching sites. RK: the subjunctive is also fine there (*¿Niegas que lo dijeras?*): the indicative presupposes that you said it, the subjunctive just asks.

### Cited Findings
- *No dudo de que fuera belga* (García Márquez): a teacher says grammarians "still differ"; the indicative declares, the subjunctive avoids full commitment — [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/subjuntivo-indicativo) (L, prior run).
- *Dudo/Niego que sea* vs *No dudo/No niego que es*; *¿Niegas que Juan dice la verdad?* — [ver-taal](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm) (F, prior run).
- A CREA corpus study of *no dudo que* / *no crees que* found variation that textbook rules don't capture (abstract only) — [Lu & Pai 2004](https://www.uv.es/ajl/pdf/94.pdf) (SN, prior run).

### Inferences
- Examples: *Dudo que sea verdad.* / *No dudo que es / sea verdad.* / *No niego que me equivoqué / me equivocara.* / *Es mentira que lo haya dicho.* / *No es mentira que lo dijo* (RK).
- In written Spain usage *no dudo que* + subjunctive is very common (RK). Accepting both is right.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on *no dudo que* + subjunctive by region.

## Q6. Verbs whose meaning changes with the mood

### Takeaway
**Holds**, and the list is incomplete. The NGLE passage adds *quejarse de, reprochar, echar en cara, censurar, presumir de, jactarse de, vanagloriarse de*. These are feeling or judgement verbs that also report: indicative = report plus judgement; subjunctive = judgement only. The two-meaning verbs already listed match the teaching literature.

### Cited Findings
- *Se quejó de que el citado individuo había propalado infundios* / *Se queja de que la impiedad pretenda corromper…*; the alternation covers *presumir (de), vanagloriarse (de), jactarse (de), censurar, echar en cara, reprochar* — [NGLE Manual §25.3.3b via hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- *Les reprocho que no me hacen caso* (I tell them, and judge it) / *…que no me hagan caso* (judge only); same with *Me quejo de que…* (Borrego et al. 1987) — [hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- Borrego et al.: verbs with two clearly different meanings belong to two groups and take the mood of each one — [hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- The *decir, decidir, comprender, pensar, suponer, sentir, temer(se)* pairs were confirmed in the prior run — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/), [ihdemu](https://ihdemu.com/verbos-doble-significado-espanol/) (F, prior run).

### Inferences
- Add to the "mood changes meaning" tag: *quejarse de, reprochar, lamentarse de* (RK for *lamentarse*), *presumir de, jactarse de*.
- *Esperar que* + future (*Espero que vendrá*) is correct but formal or rare. RK: many speakers hear it as "I expect". Fine to accept, wrong to generate.
- *Sentir que* + indicative also means "feel / have the impression that" (*Siento que no me escuchas*). That is an opinion reading, not only physical perception (RK).
- *Insistir en que*, *recordar*, *avisar*, *advertir*, *acordar/establecer*: no new source; still RK.

### Gaps
- *Convencer de que* + subjunctive (persuade to do) vs + indicative (persuade that): only the prior-run F source; not re-verified.

## Q7. Feelings take the subjunctive even for facts; exceptions; regional indicative

### Takeaway
**Holds as the norm. "It isn't standard" is too strong.** The NGLE (as quoted) describes the indicative after feeling verbs as occasional, more frequent in America, and used to highlight the information. It does not condemn it in the passage read. For Mexico, Lope Blanch's examples are now sourced (F), restricted to past or present real facts. *Me temo que* + indicative and *menos mal que* + indicative: no new source (RK), uncontroversial.

### Cited Findings
- NGLE Manual §25.3.2 (quoted above): *Me alegro de que terminaron ya el trabajo*; *Me preocupa que lo agarraron a él*; "más frecuentemente en el español americano que en el europeo" — [hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- Lope Blanch (1990, pp. 181–182): "El indicativo regido por verbos de emoción se produce solo cuando se trata de acciones pretéritas o presentes, es decir, de hechos ciertos cuya realidad se afirma." Examples: *Estoy muy satisfecho de que supo terminarlo solo. Tengo miedo de que tu hermano fue el que se lo dio. Me da coraje que lo hizo sin permiso. Mucho me alegra que no ha caído en el vacío mi escrito* — [hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- An NRFH (El Colegio de México) article on "indicativo por subjuntivo" cites Lope Blanch (1958) for Mexico and Hispanoamérica, with corpus examples from Lima, Santiago, Bogotá and San José (*Me agrada que esa tesis ha sido comentada…*). Vogel (1979) calls it inadmissible — [NRFH](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/1943/1934/) (SN; 403 on fetch).
- Rioplatense: *Me sorprendió que vino (< viniera)* is documented in Gingins 2021 — [UNRN](https://rid.unrn.edu.ar/bitstream/20.500.12049/10828/1/ArticuloICNEA.GinginsOmar.2021.pdf) (SN).
- *Me alegro de que* is the standard form (RAE on X) — [RAE on X, via search summary](https://x.com/RAEinforma/status/1614593518395170816) (SN).

### Inferences
- Corrected wording: "Feelings take the subjunctive. In Latin America (Mexico, Bogotá, Lima and elsewhere are documented) speakers sometimes use the indicative for a past or present fact they want to report as news: *Qué bueno que viniste*, *Me da coraje que lo hizo sin permiso*. The NGLE records it. Prescriptive grammars reject it. The app generates the subjunctive and may accept the indicative only for past/present facts." Never for future: there is no *\*me alegra que vendrás*, per Lope Blanch's restriction.
- *Qué bueno que viniste* is very common in Mexican speech (RK). It is not in the sources, beyond a SpanishDict forum (*¡Qué bueno que vienes!*, [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/194398/qu-bueno-que), L).
- Exceptions that take the indicative: *me temo que* (= I suspect, F prior run), *menos mal que* (RK), *lo malo / lo bueno es que* stating a fact (RK; see Q8 for *lo + adj*).

### Gaps
- No frequency data for Colombia or Mexico specifically.

## Q8. Impersonals, *lo + adj es que*, *el hecho de que*, a preposed *que* clause, *el que*, *lo de que*

### Takeaway
- Impersonals: **hold** (RK; *puede que* F prior run).
- ***Lo + adj es que* "follows the adjective": wrong as a strict rule.** Evaluative *lo raro / lo que me preocupa / lo importante es que* allow **both** moods (NGLE and Porto Dapena examples).
- ***El hecho de que*: holds**, but the indicative is far from marginal: 36% of CORPES cases, near 50% in Mexico, only the indicative in the Andean sample.
- **Preposed *que* clause "always subjunctive, whatever comes after": overstated.** It strongly favours the subjunctive, but with a fact or knowledge predicate, especially in a left dislocation with *lo*, the indicative is normal (RK).

### Cited Findings
- *Lo raro es que no {vi ~ viera} huellas* (NGLE p. 3587) — [search summary](https://digitum.um.es/digitum/bitstream/10201/74303/1/2266-6304-1-PB.pdf) (SN).
- *Lo que nos preocupa es que no escribe / no escriba*; *Lo que más me molestaba es que se reían / se riesen de mí* — [Porto Dapena 1991 via hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- *El hecho de que*: "en el español moderno las construcciones con *el hecho de que* admiten tanto el modo indicativo como el subjuntivo". The subjunctive "rebasa el del IND en el habla más cuidada". Examples: *El hecho de que escribió el libro impresionado por la pérdida de su madre explica…* (IND) / *El hecho de que el desempleo sea una prioridad … explica…* (SUBJ) — [Nowikow](https://press.uni.lodz.pl/index.php/wul/catalog/download/412/1846/880?inline=1) (F).
- CORPES XXI 2001–2016, *ser/estar/tener* after *el hecho de que*: 157 cases, subjunctive 100 (63.7%), indicative 57 (36.3%). (The paper's conclusion transposes the percentages; the table gives these.) Spain: 51 subjunctive / 18 indicative. Mexico and Central America: 14 / 14. Río de la Plata: 15 / 10. Andean zone (Bolivia, Ecuador, Peru): 0 subjunctive / 6 indicative. Caribe continental (Colombia, Venezuela): 3 / 1 — [Nowikow](https://press.uni.lodz.pl/index.php/wul/catalog/download/412/1846/880?inline=1) (F). The counts are small.
- Another corpus analysis: the indicative appears in factive clauses "cuando éstas contienen proposiciones de alto valor informativo" — [search summary](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CLAC/article/download/82539/4564456569142/4564456709300) (SN).
- Ruiz Campillo, on *Es bonito que Ana sea mi amiga*: the indicative would be declaring two things at once ("declarar que … ES bonito, y declarar además que Ana ES mi amiga") — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F).

### Inferences
- *Lo + adj es que*:
  - *Lo importante es que estás bien* (very common: "the important thing is the fact that you're fine", asserting it).
  - *Lo importante es que estés bien* (what matters, as a goal or wish).
  - Both are correct. The app should accept both for *lo importante / lo raro / lo bueno / lo malo / lo peor*.
  - Generate the indicative for a stated fact and the subjunctive for a goal (RK).
  - *Lo cierto / lo malo es que* + fact → indicative (RK).
- *El hecho de que*: keep "subjunctive by default, indicative accepted". In the doc, drop "when the fact is new information" as a condition for **accepting**; say that the indicative highlights the fact. Note Mexico ~50/50 and Andean indicative.
- Preposed clause (RK, no source found):
  - Usual: *Que sea rico no significa nada. Que estés aquí me alegra.*
  - But indicative: *Que la Tierra es redonda lo sabe todo el mundo. Que tienes razón está claro* (subjunctive also possible). Corrected wording: "A *que* clause at the start strongly favours the subjunctive; with knowing or truth predicates the indicative is also normal."
- *El que* + clause = *el hecho de que* (RK). *Lo de que* (RK, no source).
- Impersonals (RK unless noted):
  - *Es posible / probable / necesario / importante / mejor / lógico que* + subjunctive (*es lógico* F as an evaluation, Ruiz Campillo).
  - *Es verdad / cierto / evidente / obvio / claro* + indicative.
  - *No es verdad que lo haya dicho*.
  - *¿No es verdad que lo dijiste?* (asking for agreement → indicative).
  - *Puede que* + subjunctive. *Puede que tienes razón* is nonstandard (RK).
  - *A lo mejor* + indicative. *A lo mejor* + subjunctive is nonstandard but heard (RK).

### Gaps
- NGLE treatment of preposed subject clauses with indicative predicates, and of *lo de que*: not found.

## Q9. *No es que*, *no porque … sino porque*, indirect questions, nouns and adjectives

### Takeaway
**Holds.** *No sé qué haga* is better than RK now: an article (SN) documents the indirect-question subjunctive growing in 20th–21st-century Latin America, especially Mexico, not tied to formal style, and not synonymous with *qué hago*. On nouns, the NGLE-quoted examples confirm *el temor / la esperanza / miedo de que* + subjunctive. *Tener la satisfacción de que* alternates.

### Cited Findings
- Indirect questions: subjunctive use grew "en el siglo XX, y con más intensidad en el XXI, en Hispanoamérica, especialmente en algunas áreas como México". *No sé qué hago* and *no sé qué haga* are not equivalent: the subjunctive expresses uncertainty about what one should do. With the same subject the infinitive (*no sé qué decir*) predominates — [ULL Revista de Filología](https://www.ull.es/revistas/index.php/filologia/article/download/7656/5256/) (SN; fetch failed). DeMello (1995) is cited as finding the subjunctive variant predominant in Mexico City — [Gerards abstract](https://ilg.usc.gal/sites/default/files/pdfs/resumo_gerards.pdf) (SN).
- *Alba perdió el temor de que su madre la abandonara; Entonces me entró miedo de que nos viera alguien; Había tenido la esperanza de que … Felipe estuviera de lado* — [NGLE Manual §25.3.3b via hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- *Tengo la satisfacción de que todos me han / hayan ayudado* — [Borrego et al. via hispanoteca](http://hispanoteca.eu/Foro/ARCHIVO-Foro/Subjuntivo%20o%20indicativo%20con%20verbos%20de%20afecci%C3%B3n.htm) (F, quoted).
- *Estoy convencido de que oculta algo* — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/) (F, prior run).

### Inferences
- Examples (RK unless marked):
  - *No es que no quiera, es que no puedo.*
  - *Lo hizo no porque quisiera, sino porque tenía que.*
  - *No sé si viene / qué hacer / qué haga* (Mexico).
  - *La noticia de que había dimitido.*
  - *La posibilidad de que llueva.*
  - *Tengo ganas de que llegue el verano.*
  - *La idea de que la Tierra es plana* (belief) / *La idea de que vayamos juntos* (proposal).
- Feeling nouns (*la satisfacción / la alegría / la pena de que*) follow the feeling-verb pattern: subjunctive by default, indicative when reporting.

### Gaps
- No source found for *no porque … sino porque* or *lo de que*.

## Q10. Trigger tags: does every word marked "subjunctive with no" behave that way? Are there belief verbs that keep the indicative when negated, or feeling verbs that don't take the subjunctive?

### Takeaway
**Mostly holds, with three problems:**
1. **"*ver que*" tagged "+ indicative" is wrong for the negated present.** *No veo que estés enferma* takes the subjunctive (F). Perception and knowledge verbs negated in the present with *yo* behave like *no creer*. Only past or third-person negation keeps the indicative (*no veía que estaba*). The same RK caution applies to *no digo que sea malo* (negated *decir* → subjunctive) and *no me di cuenta de que…* (indicative, fact).
2. ***suponer, imaginar, considerar*** negated are uncommon. *No supongo que* is odd. The natural forms are *no me imagino que* + subj and *no considero que* + subj (RK). The tag is fine but should be presented as rare.
3. **Not every feeling verb is subjunctive-only.** *Quejarse, reprochar, presumir, jactarse* alternate (NGLE, F). *Temerse* → indicative. *Sentir* = perceive → indicative.

### Cited Findings
- *Yo no veo que estés enferma* (S) within the pattern "Pensar, ver, creer… que + Ind. / No pensar, no ver, no creer… que + Subj."; *Yo no veía que estaba enferma* (I) = true fact — [Ruiz Campillo](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (F).
- Negated *advertir, darse cuenta, saber* in the present are "constatación negativa" with the indicative: *Pedro no se da cuenta de que comete muchos errores* — [search summary of a conference paper](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/dam/jcr:86face80-098b-4cd7-be7b-364c86728809/2005-esp-05-22difranco-pdf.pdf) (SN).
- Feeling verbs that alternate: NGLE Manual §25.3.3b (see Q6) (F, quoted).
- *no decir que* + subjunctive — [ver-taal](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm) (F, prior run).

### Inferences
- Change the "+ indicative" trap for *ver que* to "indicative; negated with *yo* in the present → subjunctive (*no veo que haga falta*)". Keep *saber que* and *darse cuenta de que* as indicative traps, with the note that *no sé que + subj* (= not aware of any) exists.
- Third-person negated knowledge (*Pedro no se da cuenta de que comete errores*) is indicative because the speaker asserts the fact. This is the same mechanism as *Juan no cree que la Tierra es redonda*.

### Gaps
- No source on *no suponer que* or *no imaginar que* frequency.
