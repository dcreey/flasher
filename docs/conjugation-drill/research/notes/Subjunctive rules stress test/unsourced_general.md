## 1. Rejected cause: *no porque … sino porque*, *no es porque*

### Takeaway
**Partly confirmed.** In a rejected cause the subjunctive is the usual form and is preferred, but it is not required. The indicative also occurs and is grammatical; it tends to present the rejected cause as true. "Subjunctive in the rejected cause" overstates the rule.

### Cited Findings
- J. A. Blesa, "Las causales inefectivas" (*Cuadernos de Investigación Filológica*, Univ. de La Rioja, early 1980s; I read the PDF text). This is a dedicated study (Blesa himself notes the topic is under-studied); its examples come from 1960s–70s Peninsular texts, so there is no Mexico/Colombia data. For *No me caso con Juan porque esté embarazada*, *No porque esté embarazada me caso con Juan* and *Porque esté embarazada no me caso con Juan*, Blesa says the normal way to deny that a cause produces the effect is the subjunctive: "lo propio para expresar tales ideas es el uso de secuencias como la de (2), esto es, con verbo en alguna forma de subjuntivo". He lists the subjunctive as one of the features that set these clauses apart from ordinary causal clauses. **F** — [Blesa, Las causales inefectivas (PDF)](https://publicaciones.unirioja.es/ojs/index.php/cif/article/download/1462/1367)
- Blesa also gives an attested indicative example, *Nadie se vuelve loco porque un hijo se va de casa* (Buero Vallejo), and says the indicative "no es usual en este tipo de construcción, aunque tampoco imposible". **F** — same source
- For the *no porque … (sino)* type (*Me caso con Juan no porque esté embarazada*): "el uso de una forma de indicativo sirve precisamente para indicar que, si bien el embarazo no determina la boda, el embarazo es real. Los tiempos de subjuntivo, por su parte, sirven bien para indicar que no hay tal embarazo, bien para velar la auténtica razón". With the subjunctive, the rejected cause can be either true or false. **F** — same source
- A UNAM *Decires* paper contrasts *Vino no porque era obligatorio, sino porque le interesó el tema* (indicative, cause true) with the subjunctive version, where the negation does affect the mood. **SN** (the fetch failed with connection refused) — [Decires, UNAM](https://decires.cepe.unam.mx/index.php/decires/article/download/58/49/97)
- KwizIQ's head of Spanish, a native speaker, says both moods are acceptable: *Voy al gimnasio, no porque me guste / me gusta, sino porque debo perder peso*. In her account the subjunctive presents the reason as background and the indicative as new information. She withdraws her earlier "with *no porque* you use the subjunctive". A claim that the Instituto Cervantes requires the subjunctive is repeated in the thread without a citation. **L** — [KwizIQ: clarification no porque with the indicative](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/clarification-no-porque-with-the-indicative)
- A Slovak university handout gives the rule as subjunctive when the negation targets the cause, not the main verb: *No viene porque quiera verte, sino porque le han invitado*. **SN** — [unipo.sk, Subjuntivo 5 (doc)](https://www.unipo.sk/public/media/files/docs/ff_katedry/svk/dokument_692_153.doc)

### Inferences
- The doc's existing caveat in section 13 (*No lo dejé porque era difícil* is grammatical but ambiguous; the subjunctive removes the ambiguity) agrees with Blesa. The rule line in sections 1.8 and 3.4 should be softened to match it.
- App behaviour: prefer the subjunctive in drills, but don't mark the indicative wrong in *no porque … sino porque*.
- No source covered *no es porque* specifically. By analogy with *no es que* + subjunctive, it should behave the same way (subjunctive usual, indicative possible). This is RK.

### Gaps
- I found no NGLE quote; NGLE §46 (causal clauses) is the place to look, but rae.es is blocked. I found no frequency data for indicative vs subjunctive in negated *porque*, and no Mexico- or Colombia-specific data.

**Corrected wording (1.8 / 3.4):** *no porque … sino porque*, *no es porque* → subjunctive is the normal choice in the rejected cause: *No lo dejé porque fuera difícil, sino porque me aburría.* The indicative also occurs, less often ("no es usual… aunque tampoco imposible", Blesa), and presents the rejected cause as true (*no porque era difícil* = "it was difficult, but that wasn't why"); don't mark it wrong. Grade: F (Blesa) / L.

## 2. *Lo de que*

### Takeaway
**Partly confirmed, as a tendency rather than a rule (L only).** The one source found (a learner site) says *lo de que* takes either mood with no change in meaning, but its examples mostly match the doc's split: *¿es verdad?* takes the indicative and *me parece genial* the subjunctive. The one exception is *Aquello de que se casaba… me sonaba raro*, an evaluation with the indicative. The mood seems to follow the main predicate, as with *el hecho de que*: reports and questions about truth go with the indicative, evaluations allow the subjunctive.

### Cited Findings
- KwizIQ lesson: "'Lo de que' can be followed by the indicative or subjunctive, without changing the meaning of the sentence." It gives the pair *Lo de que yo pago / pague todo era una broma*. Other examples: *Lo de que María se va a vivir a Francia, ¿es verdad?* (indicative, truth question), *Lo de que tengas un trabajo nuevo me parece genial* (subjunctive, evaluation), *Aquello de que se casaba por amor me sonaba un poco raro* (indicative with an evaluation), *Eso de que no tienes dinero no me lo creo* (indicative). **L** (fetched) — [KwizIQ: Using lo de que / lo de](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/my-languages/Spanish/view/6182)
- In a KwizIQ Q&A, a teacher says either mood works and the difference is at most indicative = plain statement vs subjunctive = background, adding that there's no clear difference in the case discussed. **SN/L** — [KwizIQ: choice of subjunctive or indicative](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/choice-of-subjunctive-or-indicative)
- Searches for an academic or NGLE treatment of *lo de que* found nothing; the results were about dequeísmo. *Lo de que* is not dequeísmo, since *de* is required after *lo*. That last point is my own analysis (RK).

### Inferences
- The source's own example *Aquello de que se casaba por amor me sonaba raro* has an evaluating predicate with the indicative, so the doc's example *Me molesta lo de que no nos avisaran* should not be presented as the only option. *Me molesta lo de que no nos avisaron* is also fine (RK, consistent with the doc's finding that *el hecho de que* is indicative 36% of the time).
- With a truth question (*¿es verdad?*, *no me lo creo*) the indicative is the natural choice in every example found.

### Gaps
- No Mexico/Colombia data.
- No NGLE, Bosque or corpus data. The NGLE treats *el hecho de que* and similar noun-plus-clause constructions in §25, but no quote was obtainable.

**Corrected wording (1.7):** *lo de que* ("the business about…") → either mood. The indicative is the norm when the content is reported or questioned (*Lo de que te vas, ¿es verdad? Eso de que no tienes dinero no me lo creo*). With a reaction both work, as with *el hecho de que*: *Me molesta lo de que no nos avisaran* (L); *avisaron* is probably also fine (RK, no source).

## 3. Colombian *de pronto* = "maybe"

### Takeaway
**Still no reliable evidence on mood.** The "maybe" meaning is well attested for Colombia. The only signals on mood are anecdotal and point to the indicative (*De pronto sí, De pronto nos vemos mañana, O de pronto vamos a jugar bolos*). I found no example or judgment of the subjunctive (*de pronto llueva*), either way.

### Cited Findings
- The DLE marks a sense of *de pronto* as "Col. posiblemente", as quoted by a forum poster. **L** — [SpanishDict: so many ways to say maybe](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/199158/so-many-ways-to-say-maybe-in-spanish)
- A Colombian-Spanish learner dictionary says that in Colombia *de pronto* "mainly means 'maybe' rather than 'all of a sudden'". **SN/L** — [colombianspanish.co learner's dictionary](https://colombianspanish.co/blog/colombian-spanish-learners-dictionary)
- Forum examples of the "maybe" sense all use the indicative: *¿Va a llover hoy? — De pronto sí*, *O de pronto vamos a jugar bolos*. **SN/L** — [Tomisimo forum thread](https://forums.tomisimo.org/archive/index.php/t-1329.html)
- A search snippet attributed to a SpanishDict thread says the phrase "almost always (maybe always, but I am not sure)" takes the indicative. The fetched page did not show that sentence, so I could not verify it. **SN, unverified** — [SpanishDict thread](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/199158/so-many-ways-to-say-maybe-in-spanish)
- A ProZ contributor reports a well-educated Colombian using *de pronto* = "maybe" without the subjunctive. **SN/L** — [ProZ: de repente in this context](https://nor.proz.com/kudoz/spanish-to-english/slang/2484458-de-repente-in-this-context.html?paging=y)

### Inferences
- "Like *quizás*" (both moods) is unproven. The weak evidence puts it closer to *a lo mejor* (indicative-led). Since *de pronto* comes from a temporal phrase, like *a lo mejor* and *igual*, an indicative preference is plausible (RK).
- App behaviour: show the indicative in examples, and accept the subjunctive without teaching it.

### Gaps
- No academic study of *de pronto* as an epistemic adverb in Colombian Spanish turned up in two targeted searches (PRESEEA-Medellín work covers *dizque*, not *de pronto*).
- No corpus counts. A query of Corpus del Español (Web/Dialects, Colombia section) or CORPES XXI for "de pronto" + present subjunctive vs indicative would settle this. Native Colombian judgments on *De pronto llueva mañana* vs *De pronto llueve mañana* are also missing.

**Corrected wording (5.2 row):** *de pronto* = maybe (Colombia; also reported for Venezuela) | mostly indicative in the examples found (*De pronto nos vemos mañana*) | the subjunctive isn't documented either way; accept it | SN/L.

## 4. *Puede que* + indicative

### Takeaway
**No evidence that it's acceptable.** Nothing found documents *puede que* + indicative as standard, regional, or even common colloquially. The DPD treats *puede que* as a locution "de sentido análogo a *quizá*" and its example uses the subjunctive. Keep "subjunctive, always" as the norm and mark *puede que tienes* wrong.

### Cited Findings
- DPD, entry *poder*: when *ser* is dropped, *poder* stays in the present indicative and *puede que* "se comporta en la práctica como una locución adverbial de sentido análogo a *quizá*". The example given (*me arrepienta*) is subjunctive, and the entry advises against other tenses of *poder* in this use. **SN** (rae.es blocked) — [DPD: poder](https://www.rae.es/dpd/poder)
- A Hispadoc article says that for unverified or probable events (*puede que hayan ocurrido / ocurran*) speakers "utiliza[n] mayormente modo subjuntivo". "Mayormente" leaves room for a small residue, but no indicative example was shown. A snippet from the same search also says that in many uses, "sobre todo en la lengua coloquial o familiar, el subjuntivo convive con el indicativo". It's unclear whether that refers to *puede que* specifically. This is the only signal pointing the other way, and it doesn't change the verdict. **SN** — [Hispadoc 6069254](https://hispadoc.es/descarga/articulo/6069254.pdf)
- A 2024 Zagreb BA thesis analyses *puede que, quizá, tal vez, acaso, a lo mejor* in CORPES XXI. The fetch timed out, so I don't have its figures. **gap** — [FFZG repository](https://repozitorij.ffzg.unizg.hr/islandora/object/ffzg:11159/datastream/PDF/download)
- Searches for an Andalusian, Rioplatense, Caribbean or anglicism-driven *puede que* + indicative found nothing.

### Inferences
- The DPD's "analogous to *quizá*" doesn't license the indicative after *puede que*: unlike *quizá*, *puede que* is formally a main verb + *que* clause, and every normative and teaching source gives the subjunctive. Sporadic *puede que + indicative* in speech probably exists, as with any rule (RK), but I found no attestation.

### Gaps
- No corpus count of *puede que* + indicative (the CORPES XXI figure in the Zagreb thesis is unread). Nothing on Mexico or Colombia.

**Corrected wording (5.2 row):** keep "*puede (ser) que, es posible que* → subjunctive, always". Add: no source records *puede que* + indicative as standard or regional; *puede que tienes* is wrong (DPD SN; no counter-evidence found).

## 5. *No creas que es fácil* (negative command of *creer* + indicative)

### Takeaway
**Confirmed, with a strong corpus source.** After the negative imperative *no creas / no pienses que*, conversational Spanish uses the indicative. This holds in Spain and in Mexico City: 2/2 cases in CORLEC and 24/24 in the CSCM. Meanwhile *no creo que* in Spain is 21/21 subjunctive. One theoretical account (Kempchinsky 2016) claims the opposite, that the subjunctive is "la única opción" with *no creas*; the corpus data contradict it.

### Cited Findings
- Torres Cacoullos, LaCasse, Johns & De la Rosa Yacomelo, "El subjuntivo: hacia la rutinización", *Moenia* 23 (2017): 73–94. In CORLEC (spoken Peninsular Spanish, about 240,000 words), "si bien *no creo que* aparece exclusivamente con el subjuntivo (21/21), contrariamente *no creas que* aparece con el indicativo (2/2)". Example: *Pero... no creas que allí se comportan[IND] igual.* Footnote 3: "En el CSCM, *no creas que* tiene la subordinada en indicativo (24/24)". The CSCM is the Corpus Sociolingüístico de la Ciudad de México. **F** — [Moenia 23, Torres Cacoullos et al. 2017](https://revistas.usc.gal/index.php/moenia/article/view/3984/5536)
- The same paper cites Quer (2001) and Kempchinsky (2016: 69) as predicting the subjunctive as "la única opción" with first-person *no creo* and second-person imperative *no creas*. It then reports the corpus contrast above. **F** — same source
- Universidad de Piura's *Castellano Actual*, citing Luis Aragonés, *Gramática de uso del español* (2010): "con imperativos negativos y con determinadas interrogativas negativas se mantiene el indicativo", e.g. *No finjas que no me conoces*; "el hablante presenta el contenido subordinado como un hecho real". **F** (grammar via a university column; no NGLE citation) — [UDEP: Creo tener razón / No creo que tenga razón](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/creo-tener-razon-creo-que-tengo-razon-no-creo-que-tenga-razon/)
- A Masaryk University handout lists "Verbo Imperativo Negativo + QUE + Indicativo: *No creas que tienes la razón*". **SN** (the fetch returned an HTML shell) — [MUNI handout](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/podzim2023/BPJ_JI3S/um/gramatica/explicaciones/Indicativo_subjuntivo_verbos_de_entendimiento.pdf)

### Inferences
- The indicative fits the assertion account in the doc. With *no creas que X* the speaker asserts X ("it is like that over there, don't think otherwise"), so X is asserted. The imperative's own form, *creas*, is subjunctive, which is a source of learner confusion worth flagging.
- *No creas que sea fácil* is probably possible for a pure "don't assume" reading without the speaker vouching for X, as Quer/Kempchinsky predict (RK). It does not occur in either corpus sample, so the app should default to the indicative and at most tolerate the subjunctive.
- The Mexico City data (24/24) directly supports a Mexico note.

### Gaps
- I found no direct NGLE quote; the gtg pages are 403. The samples are small for Spain (2 tokens) and larger for Mexico City (24). No Colombian data.

**Corrected wording (1.4 or 1.3):** negative command of a believing verb → indicative: *No creas que es fácil. No pienses que no me importa. No finjas que no me conoces.* The speaker is asserting the clause. Spoken corpora: *no creas que* + indicative 2/2 in Spain (CORLEC) and 24/24 in Mexico City (CSCM), against *no creo que* + subjunctive 21/21 (Torres Cacoullos et al. 2017). The subjunctive (*no creas que sea*) is predicted by some theorists but not found in these corpora. Grade: F.
