### Mexico and Colombia notes (all RK unless marked)
- ***hasta* without *no* = "not until"** in Mexico, Central America, Colombia and Ecuador: *Abren hasta las diez* = they don't open until ten; *Comemos hasta que llegue* = we won't eat until he arrives (no negation needed). This matters for the *hasta que no = hasta que* rule: learners in those areas will hear bare *hasta que* with the "not until" meaning. Mood is unaffected (pending → subj).
- **Concessive *así* + subj** (*Así llueva, vamos*) is everyday spoken Spanish in Colombia and Mexico, not literary.
- **Regional *si* + conditional** is not a Mexican or Colombian norm. Standard usage there follows the strict rule.
- ***después de que* + -ra in the past** is common in Latin American press writing as well as Spanish.
- *Por si* + present subj: no regional norm found. Treat it as nonstandard everywhere.

## 1. Relative clauses: known / unknown / nonexistent / questioned / scarce / superlative

### Takeaway
The core rule (specific known antecedent → ind; nonspecific, wanted, denied → subj) holds. Two claims are too strong. *Pocos … que* takes the subjunctive only when *pocos* means "almost none"; otherwise the indicative is normal and often preferred. The superlative rule leaves out the main reason for the subjunctive: a future or unidentified referent ("el primero que llegue"). It is not only emphasis.

### Cited Findings
- **Known vs unknown (holds, F in earlier notes).** A known antecedent takes the indicative and an unknown one the subjunctive: *Busco al hombre que habla japonés* / *Necesito una secretaria que sepa japonés* — [Princeton "Contextos" handout](https://commons.princeton.edu/contextos/?p=462) (SN this session); [SpanishDict guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/subjunctive-in-relative-clauses) (L, from earlier notes).
- **Question about existence (holds with caveat).** Both moods are grammatical. *¿Conoces a alguien que viva en Fiji?* asks openly and doesn't assume anyone exists. *¿Conoces a alguien que vive en Fiji?* assumes or confirms such a person exists ("the speaker is confirming a fact") — [SpanishDict guide](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/subjunctive-in-relative-clauses) (L). The search tool came to the same reading (its own synthesis, SN) — [Princeton handout](https://commons.princeton.edu/contextos/?p=462).
- **Scarce *pocos* (wrong as an absolute).** Borrego et al., quoted on a forum, say *poco* and *apenas* "exigen subjuntivo" in the relative clause only when they mean *casi ninguno / casi nadie / casi nada*. Examples: *Sé de pocos que hayan venido*; *Disponemos de pocos folletos que puedan servirte*. When *poco* has a determiner and doesn't mean "almost none", it "admite indicativo o subjuntivo": *Los pocos invitados que han llegado están cenando* / *…que hayan llegado estarán cenando* — [Tomísimo forum thread quoting Borrego](https://forums.tomisimo.org/archive/index.php/t-7577-p-2.html) (F for the quote; the book itself was not seen).
- Same thread: a native speaker said *Hay muy pocos que quieran participar* "would be felt somewhat strange" and preferred *quieren*. Another poster said that without a focus on scarcity it is *Hay unos pocos que quieren participar* — [Tomísimo](https://forums.tomisimo.org/archive/index.php/t-7577-p-2.html) (F, L-level authority).
- Classroom handouts teach only the subjunctive: *Hay pocas personas que hagan la paella como Rafa*; exercise *Hay pocos que ___ (saber) tanto como él* — [Charles Univ. handout](https://dl1.cuni.cz/mod/resource/view.php?id=575914) (SN).
- **Superlatives / *el único que*.** Learner sources say the indicative is the default (*Es el mejor libro que he leído*) and the subjunctive is evaluative or emphatic (*Es la mejor película que haya visto*) — [Sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/es/grammar/using-subjunctive-with-superlatives) (L, earlier notes). No RAE text on mood with *único/primero* was retrieved; RAE GTG only defines the relative superlative — [RAE GTG](https://www.rae.es/gtg/construcción-superlativa) (SN).
- **Missing case (RK, high confidence).** With a future or unidentified referent the subjunctive is required, not optional or emphatic: *El primero que llegue abre la puerta*; *El único que pueda ayudarnos es un abogado* (= whoever turns out to be able). Compare the indicative for a known fact: *Fue el primero que llegó*; *Es el único que me ayudó* (RK).
- **Personal *a* (holds with caveat, RK).** *Busco a alguien que hable ruso* is standard with the subjunctive, so *a* doesn't force a specific reading. With a definite or specific noun, *a* leans toward a specific reading and the indicative: *Busco a una secretaria que habla ruso* (a particular one I have in mind) vs *Busco una secretaria que hable ruso*. The NGLE describes the personal *a* as favouring, not forcing, the specific reading (RK).
- **Nonexistent (holds, RK).** *No hay nadie que pueda ayudarte*; *No tengo nada que te interese*. The indicative after denied existence (*no hay nadie que sabe*) is nonstandard. Caveat: negation that doesn't deny the antecedent leaves the indicative: *No vi al hombre que estaba allí* (RK).

### Inferences
- Rewrite §2 "Scarce" as: "*pocos/apenas … que* meaning 'almost none' → subjunctive (*Hay pocos que sepan tanto*); stating a fact about an existing small group → indicative also fine (*Hay pocas personas que saben eso*; *los pocos que vinieron*)." The checker should accept both moods after *pocos*.
- Rewrite "Superlative": "indicative for a known fact (*el único que vino*); subjunctive for a future or unidentified referent (*el primero que llegue*) and, optionally, for emphasis (*lo mejor que haya visto*)."
- Asking whether it exists: accept both. Subj is the open question; ind means "I think there is such a person".

- No NGLE ch. 25 text retrieved on *pocos*, *único*, *primero*, or the personal *a*. rae.es blocked fetching.
- No corpus frequencies for *pocos que* + ind vs subj.

## 2. Free relatives and -quiera forms

### Takeaway
All the app's examples hold. The indicative/subjunctive contrast in free relatives works like the headed-relative rule: known or past → ind; open or future → subj. *Como tú digas* (whatever you say) vs *como tú dices* (as you say, I agree) is a real minimal pair.

### Cited Findings
- Future or unknown referent → subj: *Haz lo que quieras*; *Lo que digas*; *Quien llegue primero gana*; *Iremos donde tú digas*; *Cuanto más estudies, mejor* — [Elon free relatives (LatAm)](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-latin-america/complex/free-relatives) (L, earlier notes).
- NGLE quoted in a secondary article: in free relatives the subjunctive comes from the nonspecific antecedent, and a specific antecedent favours the indicative — [ULL article](https://ull.es/revistas/index.php/filologia/article/download/4798/3262) (SN).
- *Como tú digas* = deferring to an unknown future wish; *como tú dices* = "as you are saying" (agreeing with a known statement) (RK).
- *Cuanto más estudias, más aprendes* (generic fact, ind) vs *Cuanto más estudies, más aprenderás* (future, subj). Both are correct (RK).
- *Todo el que*: future/open → subj (*Todo el que quiera venir, que venga*); generic or known fact → ind (*Todo el que lo prueba repite*; *Todos los que vinieron se divirtieron*) (RK).
- *Quienquiera que / cualquier … que / dondequiera que* → subj, past included: *Quienquiera que fuese, sabía mi nombre* (RK). Literary indicative after *cualquiera que* exists but is rare (RK, low confidence).

### Inferences
- No changes needed. Optionally add the indicative counterpart for *todo el que* and *cuanto* (generic present), so the checker doesn't mark *Todo el que lo prueba repite* wrong.

### Gaps
- No RAE source fetched for *todo el que* or *cuanto más … más*.

## 3. Time clauses

### Takeaway
The pending → subj / habit-or-past → ind test holds for *cuando, en cuanto, hasta que, mientras, tan pronto como*. "*Antes de que* always subj" holds. "*Después de que* past: ind, impf subj also accepted" holds. "*desde que* → indicative" is overstated: a future starting point takes the subjunctive (*desde que aterrices hasta que llegues*).

### Cited Findings
- Core rule: temporal clauses take the indicative for habitual or past events and the subjunctive for future ones. In the past with *después de que*, both ind and the -ra/-se subjunctive appear — [Todoele worksheet](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/oraciones_temporales_i_ii.pdf) (F in earlier notes).
- DPD *después*: in past contexts the indicative is the normal choice; the -ra subjunctive is used and accepted (journalistic register) — [DPD *después*](https://www.rae.es/dpd/después) (SN only; page returned 403).
- ***desde que* + subj exists.** A KwizIQ lesson uses the example *Mañana, desde que aterrices hasta que llegues al hotel habrán pasado un par de horas* (present subjunctive after *desde que* for a future starting point). A C1 contributor questioned the lesson's emphases, not the subjunctive, and no one answered — [KwizIQ question "Desde que + subjuntivo"](https://progress.lawlessspanish.com/questions/view/desde-que-subjuntivo) (F, L-level).
- Normal *desde que* is indicative because it marks a real starting point: *Conozco a Sonia desde que estudiaba medicina* — [search summary of Colanguage "temporales avanzadas"](https://app.colanguage.com/spanish/grammar/temporales-avanzadas) (SN; the page returned 403).
- *Antes de que* takes the subjunctive in every tense, past included: *Antes de que él me lo dijera, ya lo sabía*. *Antes de que* + indicative is nonstandard (RK, high confidence; DPD *antes* not fetched).
- *Hasta que no* (expletive *no*): same meaning as *hasta que*. It is common, especially in Spain, and accepted, usually after a negative main clause: *No me iré hasta que (no) vuelvas* (RK; DPD *hasta* not fetched).
- Never *cuando* + future or conditional in a time clause: *Dijo que vendría cuando pudiera* (not *cuando podría*); *Cuando tenga tiempo* (not *cuando tendré*) (RK). Exempt: the question word *¿cuándo…?* and indirect questions *No sé cuándo vendrá*, *Me preguntó cuándo llegaría*. These are not time clauses (RK, high confidence).

### Inferences
- Fix §3.2 *desde que* row: "indicative for a real starting point (*desde que la conocí*); subjunctive for a future starting point, often paired with *hasta que* (*desde que llegues hasta que te vayas*)." The checker should accept a present subj after *desde que* when the frame is future.
- Add to the *cuando* note: "indirect questions with *cuándo* (*No sé cuándo vendrá*) take any tense." The section has the direct-question case but not the indirect one, and learners mix them up.
- *Después de que* + -ra in the past: keep it accepted; flag it as journalistic/formal.

### Gaps
- No RAE text on *desde que* + subj. Older or regional conditional *desde que* (= "provided that") was not researched or verified, so don't teach it.
- DPD *antes* and *hasta* entries could not be fetched.

## 4. *Mientras* and *siempre que*

### Takeaway
The meaning pairs (while vs as long as; whenever vs provided) hold. The shortcut "subjunctive = conditional meaning" is wrong, because both connectors also take the subjunctive in their time sense when the event is still to come.

### Cited Findings
- *Mientras* + ind = while (fact/habit): *Mientras cocino, escucho la radio*. *Mientras* + subj = as long as: *Mientras me paguen, sigo* (F in the rules doc; earlier notes).
- **Counterexample (RK, high confidence).** Future time *mientras* also takes the subj with no conditional meaning: *Mientras estés en casa, no fumes* (= during the time you are…). Future *siempre que* (= whenever) likewise takes the subj: *Siempre que vengas, tráeme pan* (= every time you come). Context decides between "whenever" and "provided": *Te ayudo siempre que me lo pidas* is ambiguous.
- *Mientras que* (contrast, "whereas") → indicative: *Él trabaja, mientras que tú duermes* (RK).
- *Siempre que* + ind = whenever (habit/past): *Siempre que viene, trae flores* (RK).

### Inferences
- Reword the §3.2 and §4.2 notes: "*siempre que / mientras* + ind = whenever/while (fact or habit). + subj = either a future whenever/while **or** provided/as long as; the context decides." The drill shouldn't present subj after *siempre que* as proof of "provided".

### Gaps
- No fetched authority on the future-time vs conditional ambiguity.

## 5. Purpose

### Takeaway
All purpose rules hold: *para que, a fin de que, con el fin de que, no sea que, no vaya a ser que*, *a que* after movement verbs, and *que* after a command (*Ven, que te vea*) take the subjunctive. *De modo/manera/forma que* is subj for purpose and ind for result.

### Cited Findings
- *Para que* and other purpose connectors always take the subjunctive. Same subject → infinitive (*para* + inf) (RK, high confidence; listed in DELE C1 prep materials — [aporeldele](https://www.aporeldele.com/en/blog/estructuras-con-subjuntivo-para-la-prueba-oral-del-dele-c1), SN in earlier notes).
- *Vengo a que me ayudes*; *Ven, que te vea*; *Llévate el paraguas, no sea que llueva*: all standard (RK).
- *De modo que* purpose → subj (*Habla alto, de modo que te oigan*). Result → ind (*Llovía, de modo que nos quedamos*). Also manner after commands: *Hazlo de manera que no se note* (subj) (RK).
- Caveat (RK): *que* after a command can also be causal with the indicative: *Abrígate, que hace frío* (= because it's cold). Only the purpose reading (*Ven, que te vea* = so that I can see you) takes the subj. The app should not treat *imperative + que* as automatically subjunctive.

### Inferences
- Add the *Abrígate, que hace frío* contrast to §3.1, since post-command *que* is ambiguous between purpose (subj) and cause (ind).

### Gaps
- No fetched RAE text for this section.

## 6. Result

### Takeaway
Result → ind holds (*así que, de modo que, conque, por eso, tan/tanto … que*). Denied result → subj holds. *De ahí que* + subj is the standard rule. The indicative after *de ahí que* turns up in real writing but no source accepts it, so keep subj as the taught answer.

### Cited Findings
- A Zagreb thesis states that *de ahí que / de aquí que* are the only consecutive connectors that require the subjunctive — [Zagreb thesis](https://repozitorij.ffzg.unizg.hr/islandora/object/ffzg%3A8733/datastream/PDF/view) (SN, academic secondary).
- A learner site says *de ahí que* "almost always" triggers the subjunctive, and the "almost" suggests indicative cases exist — [Sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/es/grammar/es-connectors-of-consequence-por-consiguiente-de-ahi-que) (SN, L).
- RK (medium confidence): the NGLE calls the subjunctive after *de ahí que* the norm and the indicative occasional, mostly American and journalistic (*de ahí que es importante…*). Not verified this session.
- Denied result: *No es tan rico que pueda comprarlo todo*; *No comió tanto que se enfermara* (RK). Also *tan … como para que* → subj (RK).

### Inferences
- Keep *de ahí que* → subj as the taught answer. If the checker sees an indicative, the feedback could say "subjunctive expected (indicative heard, nonstandard)" rather than a hard error, if the app has a soft-error tier.

### Gaps
- NGLE wording on indicative after *de ahí que* not retrieved. No Fundéu or StackExchange answer found.

## 7. Concession

### Takeaway
The *aunque* three-way rule (fact → ind; possibility or waved-away fact → present subj; unreal → impf/pluperf subj) holds. "*Por más/mucho que* → subjunctive" is **wrong as an absolute**. Like *aunque*, it takes the indicative for a fact the speaker knows: *Por más que estudio, no aprendo* is correct. *Si bien* and *y eso que* → ind holds. *Así* = even if → subj holds.

### Cited Findings
- *Aunque* + ind = a real objection (*Aunque llueve, iré*); + subj = a possible one (*Aunque llueva, iré*) — [Flexiclasses](https://flexiclasses.com/grammar/even-though-in-spanish/) (SN, L).
- ***Por más que* + indicative.** A Masaryk Univ. exercise: *¡Pobre Antonio! Por más que estudia, no aprueba* ("Sé o considero cierto que estudia") — [Masaryk concesivas handout](https://is.muni.cz/el/fss/jaro2020/CJVS402/um/1_oraciones_concesivas.pdf?kod=SPp411) (SN).
- Colanguage (search summary): subj for a habitual or ongoing attempt (*Por más que lo analice, no entiendo*), ind for a completed past attempt (*Por más que lo analicé, no lo entendí*). It also claims ind for an unsuccessful attempt is an error, which contradicts the Masaryk example — [Colanguage las concesivas](https://app.colanguage.com/spanish/grammar/las-concesivas) (SN; 403 on fetch). **Conflict noted**: both sources accept the past indicative; they disagree on the present indicative.
- Academic work on concessives: the indicative goes with experience and known facts, the subjunctive with unknown or doubtful ones, "though these assumptions are not always met" — [hispadoc article](https://hispadoc.es/descarga/articulo/893007.pdf); [Zagreb repository](https://repozitorij.ffzg.unizg.hr/islandora/object/ffzg%3A4557/datastream/PDF/view) (SN).
- *Por (muy) + adj + que* (*Por difícil que sea*) is almost always subjunctive. The indicative is rare but possible for facts (*Por listo que es, no lo entiende*) (RK, medium confidence).
- ***Así* = even if.** *Así llueva, truene o relampaguee, vamos a ir*; *Así me paguen el doble, no acepto ese trabajo* (Elon LatAm page) — [Elon concesivas avanzadas](https://elon.io/grammar/spanish-latin-america/complex/concessive-advanced) (SN, L). Concessive *así* is a recognised function — [USC Minerva paper](https://minerva.usc.gal/bitstreams/c878a64e-00a2-4e89-b083-75e52cbb4032/download) (SN). RK: very common in Colombia, Mexico, Venezuela and the Andes; also used, less often and in literary style, in Spain. Always subjunctive.
- *Si bien* → ind; *y eso que* → ind (*Suspendió, y eso que estudió*); *con lo que* (exclamative) → ind (RK, high confidence).
- *A pesar de que / pese a que / aun cuando* follow *aunque*; *ni aunque* → subj; *mal que te pese* (fixed) (RK).

### Inferences
- Fix §3.3 row: "*por más / mucho / poco que*: like *aunque*. Subjunctive for a hypothetical or generic effort (*Por más que insistas…*), indicative for an effort that really happens or happened (*Por más que estudio, no aprendo*; *Por más que lo intenté, no pude*)." The checker should accept both when the main clause is factual.
- Keep *así* + subj; change "not confirmed" to "everyday in Colombia, Mexico, Venezuela and the Andes".

### Gaps
- No RAE text on *por más que* + ind or concessive *así* by region.

## 8. Cause

### Takeaway
Holds. Cause connectors take the indicative. A rejected cause (*no porque … sino porque*) takes the subjunctive. Caveat: plain *no … porque* is ambiguous between the two readings, and the indicative is possible.

### Cited Findings
- *Como* (clause-first) → cause, ind: *Como salí tarde, no pude ir* (F in rules doc). *Como* + subj (clause-first) → warning condition (*Como no apruebes, no te llevo*) (RK; see §10).
- RK (high confidence): *No lo dejé porque fuera difícil, sino porque me aburría* (subj: rejected cause). *No lo dejé porque era difícil* (ind) is grammatical too. It reads "because it was hard, I didn't quit" (negation inside the main clause) or, in speech, can carry the rejected-cause reading through intonation. The subjunctive removes the ambiguity.
- RAE "causales explicativas" (earlier notes): *ya que, puesto que, como* introduce explanatory causes with the indicative — [RAE gramática](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/las-causales-explicativas) (SN in earlier notes).
- Same-structure trap: *que* after a command in the cause sense takes the ind (*Date prisa, que llegamos tarde*) (RK).

### Inferences
- §3.4 is fine. Optionally note that *no … porque* + ind is not an error. It is just ambiguous.

### Gaps
- None critical.

## 9. *Si* conditions

### Takeaway
The four-type table holds, and "never present subj after *si*" holds. "Never future after *si*" fails for **emphatic/exclamative *si*** with the future of guessing (*¡Si será tonto!*). It also fails for *si* = whether, which the app already exempts. *Si* + conditional is a regional, stigmatized feature (northern Spain: Basque Country, Navarre, La Rioja, Cantabria, and parts of Argentina and other American areas). There is also an accepted **echo** use (*si tanto te gustaría, ve*).

### Cited Findings
- Standard pattern: real → *si* + present ind; unreal now → *si* + impf subj → conditional; unreal past → pluperf subj → *habría* or *hubiera* + participle — [Masaryk "Oraciones condicionales"](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/podzim2018/BPJ_JI3S/Oraciones_condicionales.pdf) (F in earlier notes).
- DPD *si*: *si* + conditional is not acceptable in standard Spanish and is regional. The -ra form is accepted in the result clause (*hubiera venido*) — [DPD *si*](https://www.rae.es/dpd/si) (SN in earlier notes; 403 this session).
- RK (medium-high confidence): in the result clause, *hubiera* + participle is accepted, but *hubiese* + participle there (*Si lo hubiera sabido, hubiese venido*) is criticized by the NGLE as less recommended/nonstandard, though it is frequent. The -se form belongs in the *si* clause.
- **Counterexample: emphatic *si*** (RK, high confidence). In exclamations of surprise or protest, *si* + future/conditional of conjecture is standard colloquial Spanish: *¡Si será tonto!*; *¡Si serán las tres y aún no ha llegado!* This is not a condition.
- **Counterexample: echo/contrastive *si*** (RK, medium confidence; NGLE ch. 47). The conditional is accepted after *si* when it repeats someone's words or an assumption: *Si tanto te gustaría ir, ¿por qué no vas?* Also *si* + future of probability in contrastive use: *Si ahora tendrá 50 años, entonces tenía 20* (= if, as is probably the case…).
- Present subj after *si*: unacceptable in modern Spanish. Old Spanish used the future subjunctive (*si hubiere*), which survives in legal texts (*Si alguien hiciere…*) (RK; future subj in legal register — [UDEP Castellano Actual on futuro de subjuntivo](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-el-futuro-del-subjuntivo/), SN).
- Colloquial unreal *si* + impf ind: *Si lo sabía, no venía* (spoken). *Yo que tú, me iba* is colloquial; the standard is *yo que tú, me iría* (RK).
- *Si* = whether (indirect question) takes any tense: *No sé si vendrá*; *Me preguntó si iría* (RK, high confidence).

### Inferences
- Reword §4.1: "Never present subj after conditional *si*. Never future or conditional after **conditional** *si* in standard Spanish (*si* + conditional is regional). Exceptions that are not conditions: *si* = whether; exclamative *¡Si será…!*; echoing *si tanto te gustaría…*." The checker should keep the strict rule for drill cards, which are all real conditions.
- Note under "Unreal past": prefer *habría* or *hubiera*. *Hubiese* in the result clause is common but not recommended.

### Gaps
- DPD *si* not fetched this session. The regional extent of *si* + conditional comes from RK and earlier snippets only.

## 10. Other condition connectors

### Takeaway
All hold. *Por si* + present subj (*por si llueva*) is treated as wrong by mainstream teaching sources; one forum dissents without a source. *En caso de que, a menos que, a no ser que, con tal (de) que, siempre y cuando, a condición de que* → subj. *Salvo que / excepto que* + ind = "except (the fact) that" holds. *Como* + subj = warning condition holds.

### Cited Findings
- *Por si*: "the present subjunctive is never used" after *por si / por si acaso*. *Voy a llevarme el paraguas por si llueva* is marked incorrect; correct are *por si llueve* or *por si lloviera* — [KwizIQ lesson 7526](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/my-languages/Spanish/view/7526) (SN this session; F in earlier notes).
- Dissent: a forum post offers *Por si acaso llueva, lleva un paraguas* and says *por si acaso* "takes mostly the subjunctive", with no authority cited — [Tomísimo forum](https://forums.tomisimo.org/showthread.php?p=189389) (SN, L).
- RK: *por si acaso* as a standalone adverb ("just in case") followed by a separate clause is fine. *Por si* + present subj is heard in some American speech but is nonstandard. Pluperf subj is fine for the past: *Lo llevé por si hubiera llovido* (rare).
- *En caso de que, a menos que, a no ser que, con tal (de) que, a condición de que, siempre y cuando* → always subj (RK, high confidence; no source fetched this session).
- *Salvo que / excepto que* + subj = unless (*Iré, salvo que llueva*). + ind = except that, stating a fact (*Todo salió bien, salvo que llegamos tarde*) (RK, high confidence).
- *Como* + subj, clause-first, = threat/warning condition: *Como no vengas, me enfado* (RK). Caveat: *como* + subj in the clause-second position can also be a manner relative (*Hazlo como quieras*), not a condition.
- Infinitive and other non-clause conditions: *De haberlo sabido, habría venido*; *De seguir así, suspenderás* (RK).

### Inferences
- Keep *por si* + present subj as wrong, with the KwizIQ ruling as the source. Mention that it is heard but nonstandard.
- In §4.2, separate *salvo/excepto que* from *a menos que / a no ser que*. The last two have no indicative reading.

- No RAE text on *por si* + present subj. The DPD *si* entry, which reportedly covers *por si*, returned 403.
- No corpus data on how often *por si llueva* appears in Mexico or Colombia.
