## 1. Volition / influence verbs; infinitive vs. subjunctive

### Takeaway
Verbs of wanting, ordering, requesting, advising, permitting, forbidding, and causing take the subjunctive when the subordinate subject differs from the main subject. They take the infinitive when the subject is the same. With object-taking influence verbs (*dejar, hacer, mandar, permitir, prohibir, impedir, obligar a, invitar a, aconsejar*), the infinitive is also possible when the object pronoun refers to the subordinate subject. With *hacer* and *dejar* the infinitive is the preferred option.

### Cited Findings
- Verbs of volition, exhortation, demand, or fear generally take the subjunctive: *Quiero que venga a verme mañana* — [Wikipedia (en), Spanish subjunctive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- When both clauses have the same subject, an infinitive replaces the subjunctive clause — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- Verbs of prohibition, request, or advice can take either the subjunctive or an infinitive: *Te dejo que me invites* / *Te dejo invitarme* — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- Verbs of causation take the subjunctive: *La lluvia hizo que los ríos se desbordaran* — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- Example of desire with the indicative in the main clause vs. a subjunctive complement: *Tú te comes eso ahora mismo* vs. *Quiero que te comas eso ahora mismo*; necessity: *Necesito que te comas eso* — [Wikipedia (es)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modo_subjuntivo)
- *Convencer de que* + subjunctive means persuading someone to do something: *Me convenció de que comprara la moto* (vs. indicative *Estoy convencido de que oculta algo*) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Trigger list (subjunctive, different subject):** querer, desear, preferir, necesitar, pedir, rogar, suplicar, exigir, mandar, ordenar, decretar, disponer, aconsejar, recomendar, sugerir, proponer, insistir en (demand sense), permitir, dejar, consentir, tolerar, aceptar (= agree to), autorizar, prohibir, impedir, evitar, oponerse a, hacer (cause), lograr, conseguir, obtener, procurar, intentar/tratar de (rarely with a different subject), animar a, invitar a, obligar a, forzar a, ayudar a, incitar a, empujar a, esforzarse por, luchar por, contribuir a, conducir a, provocar, causar, motivar, encargar, aprobar, votar por, esperar (hope), ansiar, anhelar, soñar con (wish), estar de acuerdo en, acordar (decide), decidir (impose), pensar (plan), elegir/escoger que, merecer.
- **[RK] Same subject: the infinitive is obligatory.** *Quiero ir* (not *\*Quiero que (yo) vaya*). *Logré terminar* vs. *Logré que terminaran*. *Prefiero quedarme* vs. *Prefiero que te quedes*. *Evité caerme* vs. *Evité que se cayera*.
- **[RK] Object-controlled verbs: the infinitive is optional.** The subordinate subject must equal the main-clause object.
  - *Te prohíbo salir* = *Te prohíbo que salgas*.
  - *Le permitieron entrar* = *Le permitieron que entrara*.
  - *Me mandó callar* = *Me mandó que me callara*.
  - *Les aconsejo descansar* = *Les aconsejo que descansen*.
  - *Nos impidieron pasar* = *Nos impidieron que pasáramos*.
  - *Lo obligaron a firmar* = *Lo obligaron a que firmara*.
- **[RK] *hacer* and *dejar*** strongly prefer the infinitive with a clitic: *Me hizo reír*, *Déjame pensar*, *No lo dejan salir*. The subjunctive is used especially with non-pronominal or inanimate causers, or for emphasis: *La lluvia hizo que se cancelara el partido*; *Deja que te ayude*.
- **[RK] *pedir, rogar, exigir, sugerir, proponer, recomendar*** with a different subject normally take the subjunctive (*Te pido que me escuches*). The infinitive is common only with an indirect-object pronoun (*Te ruego guardar silencio* — formal style), or with the same subject (*Pidió salir antes* = he asked to leave).
- **[RK] *insistir en que*:** subjunctive = demand (*Insiste en que vengamos*). Indicative = assertion (*Insiste en que es inocente*).
- **[RK] *Esperar a que* (wait until)** always takes the subjunctive: *Espera a que llegue tu padre*.
- **[RK] Tense sequence:** a past or conditional main verb takes the imperfect subjunctive (*Quería que vinieras*; *Me pidió que lo llamara*). A present or future main verb takes the present subjunctive (*Te pediré que lo hagas*). *-ra* and *-se* forms are interchangeable in Spain. *-se* is much rarer in Latin America — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive).

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave the NGLE's exact list of verbs allowing infinitive/subjunctive alternation with an object (the NGLE treats this under infinitive constructions, ch. 26). The *pedir* + infinitive restriction, the formal *Te ruego guardar silencio* style, and regional preferences (e.g., *dejar que* vs. *dejar* + infinitive in Mexico/Colombia) are [RK] only.

## 2. Emotion / evaluation predicates

### Takeaway
Normatively, the subjunctive is used even when the subordinate content is a known fact (*Me alegra que estés aquí*). The indicative after emotion verbs exists but is non-standard: it is described as surviving in some Latin American speech. *Temer(se) que* with the indicative is standard when it means "suspect / I'm afraid that (polite)". *Menos mal que* is a fixed evaluative expression that takes the indicative.

### Cited Findings
- The subjunctive also marks perfectly objective, real, and confirmed facts: *Me alegra que estés aquí*, set against the indicative *Me alegra que estás aquí* — [Wikipedia (es)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modo_subjuntivo)
- Many Latin American speakers keep the indicative with emotion verbs, though it is not the standard — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- *Temía que lo supieran* (genuine fear, subjunctive) vs. *Temía que no vendríais* (conventional or polite "fear" = expectation, indicative) — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- *Temer* + subjunctive = be afraid: *Temo que mi mujer deje de quererme*. *Temerse* + indicative = suspect something bad: *Me temo que voy a suspender el examen* — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- *Sentir* + subjunctive = regret: *Siento que te hayas roto la muñeca*. *Sentir* + indicative = perceive: *Siento que hace frío* — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- Fear can also be expressed through a pseudo-cleft with the subjunctive: *Lo que me da miedo es que tenga una pistola* — [Wikipedia (es)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modo_subjuntivo)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Emotion verb triggers (subjunctive):**
  - Gustar-type verbs: gustar, encantar, molestar, fastidiar, irritar, enfadar, enojar (LatAm), preocupar, sorprender, asombrar, extrañar, alegrar, entristecer, doler, importar, interesar, indignar, avergonzar, dar pena / rabia / miedo / igual / lástima / vergüenza.
  - Pronominal verbs: alegrarse de, sorprenderse de, quejarse de (complaint sense), lamentar, sentir, temer, tener miedo de / a, odiar, detestar, soportar / aguantar, agradecer, celebrar, extrañarse de, conformarse con, contentarse con, estar harto de.
- **[RK] Evaluative impersonal expressions (subjunctive):** es una pena / lástima / vergüenza, es raro / extraño / curioso, es normal / lógico / natural / comprensible, es bueno / malo / mejor / peor, es increíble, es absurdo, es una suerte, es injusto / justo, es triste, es ridículo, es interesante, me parece bien / mal / lógico / injusto que, qué pena / lástima / raro / bien que, lo normal / lo lógico / lo raro es que.
- **[RK] Indicative exceptions:**
  - ***menos mal que*** + indicative (*Menos mal que llegaste*).
  - ***lo bueno / malo / peor es que*** + indicative when stating a fact (*Lo malo es que no tenemos dinero*). Compare evaluative or prescriptive *Lo importante es que estés bien* (subjunctive).
  - ***quejarse de que*** used as a report of a complaint can take the indicative (*Se queja de que nadie lo ayuda*).
- **[RK] Regional, Mexico (usage-based, non-normative):** ***Qué bueno que viniste*** with the indicative is very common colloquially. Standard is *Qué bueno que hayas venido / que vinieras*.
- **[RK] Prepositions (DPD):** pronominal *alegrarse **de** que* requires *de*. Dropping it ("queísmo", *me alegro que*) is widespread and the DPD tolerates it as colloquial. Non-pronominal *me alegra que* takes no *de*.
- **[RK] App rule:** generate subjunctive for all emotion triggers. Accept the indicative only for *temerse* (suspect/regret to say), *menos mal que*, factual *lo malo es que*, and *sentir* = perceive.

## 3. Doubt / denial, and negated certainty

### Takeaway
Doubt and denial (*dudar, negar, no creer, no estar seguro, es dudoso*) take the subjunctive. Negated doubt (*no dudar, no negar*) is taught as taking the indicative, but both moods are attested and accepted. The subjunctive softens commitment. Fixed certainty phrases (*no cabe duda de que, no hay duda de que, sin duda*) take the indicative.

### Cited Findings
- Verbs of doubt take the subjunctive: *Dudo que alcancen la victoria* — [RAE GTG "modo subjuntivo", via search snippet](https://www.rae.es/gtg/modo-subjuntivo)
- *Dudar que* and *negar que* take the subjunctive (*Dudo que sea un problema*; *Niego que sea un problema*). *No dudar que* and *no negar que* take the indicative (*No dudo que es un problema*; *No niego que es un problema*) — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm)
- A Kwiziq teacher says grammarians "still differ" on *no dudo de que*. It accepts both moods: the indicative "declares", while the subjunctive avoids full commitment (García Márquez: *no dudo de que fuera belga*) — [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/subjuntivo-indicativo)
- A corpus study (CREA, RAE) of *no dudo que*, *no crees que*, and *no basta que* found real-usage variation that traditional textbook rules cannot easily explain. Only the abstract was readable — [Lu & Pai 2004, AJL XIX Encuentro, U. València](https://www.uv.es/ajl/pdf/94.pdf)
- *¿Niegas que...?* is said to take the indicative, by the same logic as *¿No crees que...?*: *¿Niegas que Juan dice la verdad?* — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm)
- A forum-quoted RAE note says *dudar que* is the usual construction but *dudar de que* is also correct. This was not verified on rae.es — [SpanishDict Answers](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/200832/no-dudo-de-que)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Subjunctive triggers:** dudar (de) que, negar que, no creer / no pensar / no opinar / no considerar que, no parecer que, no estar seguro / convencido de que, no es cierto / verdad / seguro / evidente / obvio / claro que, es dudoso que, es mentira que, no es que, desconfiar de que, sospechar (only when it means "doubt" — usually indicative), rechazar que, descartar que, ignorar que (= not know; both moods, see section 4).
- **[RK] Indicative after negated doubt or denial (preferred, subjunctive also accepted):**
  - *No dudo que viene / venga*: the indicative is more frequent in speech and textbooks.
  - *No niego que es / sea verdad*: the subjunctive is common because *no niego* often concedes without full endorsement.
- **[RK] Always indicative:** no cabe duda de que, no hay duda de que, sin duda, está claro que, estoy seguro de que, es indudable que, es innegable que.
- **[RK] *negar que* + indicative** is possible when the speaker presupposes the content is true: *Negó que lo había robado* (and he did). The default is the subjunctive.
- **[RK] App rule:** *dudar / negar / no creer* → subjunctive only. *No dudar / no negar* → generate the indicative, accept both. *No cabe / hay duda* → indicative only.

## 4. Belief, perception, communication: affirmed, negated, questioned, negative imperative

### Takeaway
- Affirmed belief, perception, and communication verbs take the **indicative**.
- Negated ones normally take the **subjunctive**. The indicative remains possible, and signals that the *speaker* holds the content true.
- In questions both moods occur. The negative question *¿No crees que...?* (seeking agreement) takes the indicative.
- The negative imperative *No creas / pienses que...* typically takes the indicative.
- The NGLE says thinking verbs admit the indicative because they are close to verbs of knowledge and so to assertion.

### Cited Findings
- Affirmative *creer que* / *decir que* take the indicative (*Creo que tiene razón*). Negative *no creer que* / *no decir que* take the subjunctive (*No creo que tenga razón*) — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm)
- *¿No crees que...?* takes the indicative because the fact is not questioned. It asks whether the listener believes it: *¿No crees que Juan dice la verdad?* — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm). Another teaching site gives *¿No crees que ya es tarde?*: the asker presents the content as evident and seeks agreement — [Spanish Unicorn, via search summary](https://www.spanishunicorn.com/ejercicios-subjuntivo-o-indicativo-opinion/). Whether the subjunctive is also possible here is debated — [CVC forum, via search summary](https://cvc.cervantes.es/foros/leer_asunto1.asp?vCodigo=42573)
- *No creas que* takes the indicative and rules out the opposite reading: *No creas que me gusta* (= I don't like it) — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm); a WordReference thread says the subordinate verb "admite muy mal el subjuntivo" here — [WordReference, via search summary](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/no-creas-que-indicativo-o-subjuntivo.3406967/)
- Negated belief verbs usually take the subjunctive, but the indicative is also acceptable. *Michael no cree que Panamá sea un país hispanohablante* is neutral. With *es*, the speaker asserts their own view (that Panama *is* Spanish-speaking) — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- Belief verbs also take the subjunctive when the meaning is hypothetical or hesitant — [Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_subjunctive)
- The NGLE says Spanish thinking verbs admit the indicative because they are very close to verbs of knowledge, and therefore to assertion. Bello's contrast: *Creo que Juan vendrá* vs. *No creo que Juan venga* — [search-result summary of RAE / academic sources](https://www.rae.es/gtg/oración-subordinada-sustantiva)
- The NGLE has a dedicated subsection on negated *saber*, §25.5o — [ULL article, via search summary](https://www.ull.es/revistas/index.php/filologia/article/download/7656/5256/)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Indicative when affirmed:**
  - Belief/opinion: creer, pensar, opinar, considerar, suponer, imaginar(se), sospechar, intuir, figurarse, parecer (*me parece que*), estar seguro / convencido de, tener la impresión de / la sensación de.
  - Knowledge: saber, recordar, acordarse de, darse cuenta de, descubrir, aprender, enterarse de, olvidar.
  - Perception: ver, oír, notar, observar, percibir, sentir (perceive).
  - Communication: decir, afirmar, asegurar, declarar, contar, explicar, comentar, responder, contestar, confesar, reconocer, admitir, anunciar, informar, comunicar, mencionar, prometer, jurar, repetir, escribir, gritar, avisar, advertir, demostrar, mostrar, indicar, confirmar.
- **[RK] Negated, default subjunctive, indicative possible when the speaker endorses the content:**
  - *No creo que llueva*.
  - *No pienso que sea justo*.
  - *No me parece que tengas razón*.
  - *No dijo que fuera a venir* (he did not say that) vs. *No dijo que venía* (he was coming, but didn't say so).
- **[RK] Person and tense effect (usage):**
  - First person present (*No creo que...*) strongly prefers the subjunctive.
  - Second and third person or past (*No crees que es..., No creía que era...*) allow the indicative more easily, because the speaker can hold the content true while reporting someone else's non-belief. Example: *Tú no crees que soy capaz, pero lo soy*.
- **[RK] Factive knowledge verbs keep the indicative under negation** when the content is presupposed true:
  - *No sabía que estabas aquí*.
  - *No me di cuenta de que era tarde*.
  - *No recordaba que tenías hermanos*.
  - With *no saber que* the subjunctive is possible when the content is not presupposed: *No sabía que hubiera / había otra salida*.
  - First person present *no sé que* + clause is rare. *No sé si* is the normal form (section 9).
- **[RK] Negated perception:** *No vi que hubiera / había nadie* — both moods.
- **[RK] Yes/no questions (*¿Crees que...?*):** the indicative is the neutral default (*¿Crees que va a llover?*). The subjunctive adds doubt (*¿Crees que vaya a llover?*). The subjunctive here is more frequent in Mexico and Central America — [RK, usage-based].
- **[RK] Negative imperative:** *No pienses que te voy a esperar* (indicative). The subjunctive is possible with a non-presupposed reading: *No creas que sea fácil*.
- **[RK] Fixed expressions:**
  - *Parece que* + indicative; *no parece que* + subjunctive.
  - *Parece mentira que* + subjunctive.
  - *Me parece bien / mal / lógico que* + subjunctive (evaluation, not opinion).
  - *Que yo sepa* is a fixed subjunctive.

### Gaps
- The NGLE §25.5 wording on *¿No crees que...?* and on second-person negation could not be read. Regional frequency of *¿Crees que + subjunctive?* is [RK] only.

## 5. Verbs whose meaning changes with mood

### Takeaway
Many communication and cognition verbs report or assert with the indicative, and influence or evaluate with the subjunctive. The RAE glossary's own example is *Te digo que lo haces bien* / *Te digo que lo hagas bien*.

### Cited Findings
- RAE GTG: the indicative can be an induced mood in subordinate clauses, sometimes alternating with the subjunctive: *Te digo que lo haces bien* (report) vs. *Te digo que lo hagas bien* (instruction) — [RAE GTG "modo indicativo", via search snippet](https://www.rae.es/gtg/modo-indicativo)
- *Decir*: indicative = inform (*Me dijo que había hecho los deberes*); subjunctive = ask, order, recommend (*Me dijo que hiciera los deberes*) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/); also *Dice que su marido va al supermercado* vs. *Le dice a su marido que vaya al supermercado* — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm)
- *Decidir*: indicative = reach a conclusion (*He decidido que voy a ir a la Universidad*); subjunctive = impose (*Hemos decidido que vayas en autobús*) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- *Comprender / entender*: indicative = realize (*De repente comprendí que eran novios*); subjunctive = find logical (*Comprendo que rechaces la oferta porque es mala*) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- *Pensar*: indicative = opinion (*Pienso que trabajas demasiado*); subjunctive = decide or plan (*He pensado que vayas tú al supermercado y yo cocino*) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- *Suponer*: indicative = assume (*Supongo que pasaré la Navidad con mi familia*); subjunctive = imply or entail (*Los malos resultados supondrán que haya despidos en la empresa*) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- *Sentir*, *temer / temerse*, and *convencer de*: see sections 1 and 2 — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)
- Other teaching sites add *parecer* and *soñar* to the list — [search summary of ihdemu.com / elblogdegramatica](https://ihdemu.com/verbos-doble-significado-espanol/)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Additional pairs (indicative = report or fact; subjunctive = instruction, decision, or evaluation):**
  - *insistir en* — *Insiste en que es verdad* / *Insiste en que lo hagamos*.
  - *escribir / telefonear / llamar / gritar / repetir / recordar / avisar / advertir* — *Me escribió que llegaba el lunes* / *Me escribió que lo esperara*; *Le grité que se apartaba* (rare) / *Le grité que se apartara*; *Te recuerdo que mañana hay examen* / *Recuérdale que compre pan*; *Te aviso que va a llover* / *Te aviso que no llegues tarde*; *Le advertí que era peligroso* / *Le advertí que no fuera*.
  - *acordar* — *Acordamos que la reunión era a las 5* (agreed fact) / *Acordamos que cada uno pagara lo suyo* (decision).
  - *establecer / disponer / determinar* — *La ley establece que los menores no pueden votar* (states a norm) / *La ley establece que se pague un impuesto* (mandates).
  - *esperar* — hope + subjunctive (*Espero que vengas*). Expect, mostly with the future or conditional + indicative (*Espero que vendrá*, a less common, more formal "I expect"). *Esperar a que* = wait until, always subjunctive. Same subject → infinitive (*Espero aprobar*).
  - *sentir* — perceive / regret (section 2).
  - *temer* — section 2.
  - *comprender / entender* — above.
  - *suponer* — above; also *Suponiendo que tengas razón...* (concession, subjunctive).
  - *imaginar(se)* — *Me imagino que estás cansado* (assume) / *Imagínate que te toque la lotería* (hypothesis, subjunctive possible).
  - *soñar* — *Soñé que volaba* (dream narrative) / *Sueño con que mis hijos sean felices* (wish).
  - *parecer* — *Parece que llueve* (seems) / *Me parece bien que vengas* (evaluation).
- **[RK] App rule:** for these verbs, pick the meaning first, then the mood. The explanation card should name the meaning (e.g., "decir = order → subjunctive").

### Gaps
- No fetched source covered *establecer*, *acordar*, *telefonear*, *gritar*, or indicative *esperar* ("expect"). These are [RK] only.

## 6. Impersonal expressions

### Takeaway
- **Subjunctive:** necessity, possibility, probability, importance, and value judgments.
- **Indicative:** affirmed truth or evidence (*es cierto / verdad / evidente / obvio / claro / seguro*), *está demostrado*, and event-presenters (*resulta que, sucede que, es que*).
- **Negated truth expressions** flip to the subjunctive: *no es cierto que + subj.*; *no es que + subj.*

### Cited Findings
- "No es que" introduces an explanation the speaker rejects and takes the subjunctive — [Spanish Unicorn, via search summary](https://www.spanishunicorn.com/ejercicios-subjuntivo-o-indicativo-opinion/)
- Possibility and probability can also be expressed with indicative adverbs: *Posiblemente ya lo sabe / lo sepa*; *Probablemente está / esté durmiendo* — [Wikipedia (es)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modo_subjuntivo). This applies to adverbs only, not to *es posible que*.
- Variation was found in corpus usage of *no basta que* — [Lu & Pai 2004](https://www.uv.es/ajl/pdf/94.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Subjunctive list:**
  - Necessity: es necesario / preciso / imprescindible / indispensable / esencial / fundamental / urgente / obligatorio, hace falta que.
  - Importance and advisability: es importante / conveniente / aconsejable / recomendable / preferible / mejor / peor, conviene que, importa que, más vale que, vale la pena que, ya es hora de que / es hora de que.
  - Sufficiency: basta (con) que, es suficiente que, es de esperar que.
  - Possibility and probability: es posible / imposible / probable / improbable / difícil / fácil (= likely) que, puede (ser) que.
  - Plus all the evaluative expressions in section 2.
- **[RK] Indicative list:**
  - Truth and evidence: es cierto / verdad / evidente / obvio / claro / seguro / indudable / innegable / sabido / un hecho que, está claro / demostrado / comprobado que, no cabe / no hay duda de que.
  - Event presenters: resulta que, sucede / ocurre / pasa que, es que (explanation: *Es que no tengo tiempo*).
  - Others: se ve que, se nota que, parece que.
- **[RK] Negated or questioned forms:**
  - No es cierto / verdad / evidente / seguro / obvio / claro que → subjunctive (*No es verdad que lo haya dicho*). The indicative is possible when the speaker contradicts while presupposing truth, but this is rare.
  - No es posible que → subjunctive.
  - ¿Es cierto que...? → indicative default, subjunctive for skepticism.
  - No es que → subjunctive (*No es que no quiera, es que no puedo*).
  - Es mentira que → subjunctive; no es mentira que → indicative.
- **[RK] *Lo + adjective + es que*:** follows the adjective's meaning. *Lo importante es que estés bien* (subjunctive). *Lo malo es que no hay tiempo* / *Lo cierto es que...* (indicative).

## 7. "El hecho de que", preposed *que* clauses, "lo de que"

### Takeaway
Subject clauses placed before the verb (*Que llegues tarde me molesta*; *Que sea rico no significa nada*) and *el hecho de que* clauses present presupposed or topical information. They favour the subjunctive even with predicates that would otherwise take the indicative. The indicative is possible with *el hecho de que* when the fact itself is being asserted.

### Cited Findings
- The search returned no results for "el hecho de que" in the teaching sources fetched — [search summary](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/). No cited findings are available for this question.

### Inferences
- **[RK] *El hecho de que*** takes the subjunctive by default (*El hecho de que sea joven no le impide...*; *El hecho de que no llamara me preocupó*). The indicative is accepted (NGLE) when the speaker asserts the fact as new information, especially as a verb object or with verbs of demonstrating: *El hecho de que llegó tarde demuestra que no le importa*. App: generate the subjunctive, accept both.
- **[RK] Preposed *que* clause** takes the subjunctive regardless of the predicate, because it is thematic or presupposed:
  - *Que sea rico no significa nada*.
  - *Que lo sepas tú no quiere decir que lo sepa todo el mundo*.
  - *Que estés aquí me alegra*.
  - Even with assertion predicates: *Que la situación es grave es evidente* (indicative, normal) / *Que la situación sea grave es evidente* (subjunctive possible; NGLE notes it).
- **[RK] The same predicate postposed** takes its normal mood: *Es evidente que la situación es grave*.
- **[RK] *El que* + clause** (nominalized) behaves like *el hecho de que* (*El que no hayas venido me molestó*).
- **[RK] *Lo de que*** ("the business about...") reports reported content and usually takes the indicative: *Lo de que te vas a mudar, ¿es verdad?* With an emotion predicate the subjunctive is possible (*Me molesta lo de que no nos avisaran*).

## 8. Clauses complementing nouns and adjectives

### Takeaway
The mood follows the meaning of the head noun or adjective, just as it would with the related verb. Wish, need, possibility, fear, and emotion take the subjunctive. Information, certainty, and awareness take the indicative.

### Cited Findings
- *Estoy convencido de que oculta algo* (certainty → indicative) — [Tu Escuela de Español](https://www.tuescueladeespanol.es/blogs/verbos-con-distinto-significado-indicativo-y-subjuntivo/)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Subjunctive heads:** la posibilidad / probabilidad / necesidad / esperanza / ilusión / deseo / temor / miedo (a / de) / riesgo / peligro de que; la condición de que; la orden de que; la propuesta / sugerencia / petición / exigencia de que; el objetivo / la intención de que; estar contento / feliz / encantado / harto / cansado / orgulloso / preocupado / triste / dispuesto (a) / acostumbrado (a) de que; ser partidario de que; tener ganas de que; no estar seguro de que.
- **[RK] Indicative heads:** la noticia / el rumor / la información / la prueba / la certeza / la seguridad / la convicción / la sospecha / la impresión / la sensación / la conclusión de que; estar seguro / convencido / consciente de que; darse cuenta de que.
- **[RK] Heads that allow both moods:** *la idea de que* (indicative = belief content: *la idea de que la Tierra es plana*; subjunctive = proposal or wish: *la idea de que vengas tú*); *la duda de que* (normally subjunctive); *el hecho de que* (section 7).
- **[RK] Prepositions:** mood is unaffected. Omitting *de* (queísmo: *estoy seguro que*) is common in speech. The DPD prescribes *de*.

## 9. "No es que", "no porque", "es que"; indirect questions

### Takeaway
- **"No es que" + subjunctive** rejects an explanation. **"Es que" + indicative** gives one.
- **"No porque" + subjunctive** (*no porque quiera, sino porque...*) is a causal adverbial with the same logic.
- **Indirect questions** (*si*, *qué*, *dónde*, *cuándo*...) take the **indicative**, or an **infinitive** when the subjects match and the meaning is "what to do". *No sé qué haga* (subjunctive) is a Mexican and Central American colloquial variant.

### Cited Findings
- "No es que" introduces an explanation the speaker rejects and takes the subjunctive — [Spanish Unicorn, via search summary](https://www.spanishunicorn.com/ejercicios-subjuntivo-o-indicativo-opinion/)
- Indirect questions take the indicative: *No dice cuándo va a llegar Mónica* — [ver-taal.com](https://www.ver-taal.com/gr_subjuntivo_opinion.htm)

### Inferences
- **[RK] Contrast pair:** *No es que no quiera ir, es que estoy cansado* (subjunctive, then indicative). Other examples: *No es que sea difícil*; *No era que no le gustara*.
- **[RK] *No porque*:** *Lo hago no porque me guste, sino porque es necesario* (subjunctive). Plain *porque* takes the indicative.
- **[RK] Indirect questions:**
  - *No sé si viene* (indicative). *Si* never takes the present subjunctive here.
  - Other examples: *Me pregunto si lo sabe*; *No sé dónde vive*; *¿Sabes cuándo empieza?*
  - Infinitive for deliberation: *No sé qué hacer / adónde ir / a quién llamar*.
- **[RK] Regional, Mexico (colloquial, non-normative):** *No sé qué haga* / *¿Qué hago?* — subjunctive in deliberative indirect questions. Standard is *No sé qué hacer* / *qué voy a hacer*.
- **[RK] *No sé que + subjunctive*** (rare) contrasts with *No sé si*: *No sabía que hubiera clase hoy* is possible (section 4).
