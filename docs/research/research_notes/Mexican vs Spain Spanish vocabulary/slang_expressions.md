# Mexican colloquial phrases, slang and interjections compared with Spain

How to read the source tags in this file:

The DEM's register labels are *Popular* (informal, popular speech), *Groser* (coarse or vulgar), *Ofensivo* and *Caló* (underworld or street argot).
- **[DAMER]**: checked against the ASALE *Diccionario de americanismos*, `https://www.asale.org/damer/<headword>`.

## Key question 1: the catalogue, and which expressions are mild enough for a learner to use with strangers

### Takeaway
The DEM confirms a clear split. One group is only *Popular* (informal but not coarse): qué onda, órale, ándale, híjole, neta, aguas, padre, chido, chamba, lana, chela, cuate, carnal, fresa, desmadre, chingón, hacer el oso. Another is labelled *Groser* (vulgar): pedo and therefore qué pedo, pinche, hueva and therefore qué hueva, chingar, and the whole "madre" phrase family including *a toda madre* and *qué poca madre*. A third is *Ofensivo*: chingar, naco, and güey when used as an insult. ¿Mande? is described as a courtesy formula. A learner can safely use the *Popular* set with peers and service staff. The *Groser* set is for friends only, and the *Ofensivo* set should be avoided.

### Cited findings (dictionary-verified anchors)
- **¡qué onda!** is an informal greeting. In the same section, labelled *Popular*, the DEM lists buena onda / mala onda (a nice or not-nice person or attitude), agarrar la onda (to get the hang of something), sacar de onda (to confuse or throw off), entrar en onda (to fit in), írsele la onda a uno (to lose one's train of thought) and tirar la onda (to flirt). — [DEM onda](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/onda)
- **¡mande!** is a "courtesy expression" used to answer when someone calls you or when you didn't hear. The DEM gives it no slang label. — [DEM mande](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/mande). The DAMER has no entry for *mande*. — [DAMER mande](https://www.asale.org/damer/mande)
- **¡órale!** (*Popular*) has four uses: urging someone on ("¡Órale, que ya es hora!"), accepting an offer, surprise ("¿Sacaste diez? ¡Órale!"), and telling someone to stop ("¡Órale, no te mandes, carnal!"). — [DEM órale](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/órale)
- **¡ándale! / ¡ándele!** (*Popular*) has three uses: urging, agreement in the sense of "exactly", and surprise at something already done. *¡Anda!* expresses doubt, or annoyance plus surprise. — [DEM andar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/ándale)
- **¡híjole! / ¡híjoles!** (*Popular*) is an exclamation of astonishment or emphasis. — [DEM híjole](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/híjole)
- **neta** (*Popular*) means "the truth" ("la neta es que…"). As an adverb it means seriously or truly ("Neta, yo lo vi"). *Ser la neta* means to be the best ("Esos tacos de canasta son la neta"). — [DEM neta](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/neta)
- **¡aguas!** (*Popular*) is an interjection warning of nearby danger. — [DEM agua](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/aguas)
- **padre** (adj., *Popular*) means very good, fun or of good quality: "¡Qué padre casa!", "padrísimo". — [DEM padre](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/padre)
- **chido** means pretty, or very good. The DAMER marks it Mx only, *pop.*, with the variant *shido*. — [DAMER chido](https://www.asale.org/damer/chido)
- **güey** is *Popular*. Senses: (1) an ox; (2) a stranger viewed with contempt (*ofensivo*); (3) a fool, "¿Qué me ve, güey?" (*ofensivo*); (4) *de güey*, out of foolishness; (5) "a device among young people" to hold attention and build solidarity, "¡No, güey, te aseguro que no lo supe!". — [DEM güey](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/güey). Learner guides note the spellings güey, guey and wey, and say the word went from insult to "dude". — [Tell Me In Spanish](https://tellmeinspanish.com/mexico/mexican-slang/guey)
- **cuate** (*Popular*) means a friend, a "guy" ("¿Quién es ese cuate…?"), or a warm term of address ("¡Ay, cuatito!"). *Ser cuate* means to be a decent, fair person. Its non-slang sense is "twin". — [DEM cuate](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/cuate)
- **carnal / carnala** (*Popular*) means brother or sister, and also buddy ("ya vas, carnal, vamos a pistear"). The same DEM example shows **pistear** meaning to go drinking. — [DEM carnal](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/carnal)
- **fresa** (*Popular*) has two senses: someone from a wealthy, privileged class ("un chavo fresa"), and someone straight-laced or conservative ("son bien fresas, no entran en onda"). — [DEM fresa](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/fresa)
- **naco** is labelled *Popular y Ofensivo*. Senses: (1) an Indigenous person; (2) ignorant or uneducated; (3) tacky ("¡Qué blusa tan naca!"). The first sense shows the word carries racial and class prejudice, so learners should not use it. — [DEM naco](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/naco)
- **chamba** (*Popular*) means a job: "Le consiguió una chamba en la fábrica de su tío". — [DEM chamba](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chamba). The DAMER records it in Mx, Central America, Cuba, Puerto Rico, **Co**, Pe, CR, Ve, Ec and Bo, and also marks it "unstable job" in Mx, RD and **Co**. — [DAMER chamba](https://www.asale.org/damer/chamba)
- **lana** (*Popular*) means money ("Préstame una lana"); *una buena lana* is a lot of money. — [DEM lana](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/lana). The DAMER's *lana* entry as fetched showed only Central American and Caribbean person senses, not the money sense. Treat this as a fetch or coverage gap, not a contradiction. — [DAMER lana](https://www.asale.org/damer/lana)
- **chela** (*Popular*) means a beer: "Vamos a tomarnos una chela". — [DEM chela](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chela)
- **cruda** means a hangover. The DAMER records it in Mx, Gu, Ho, ES, Ni, Bo and Ec with the label *pop.* — [DAMER cruda](https://www.asale.org/damer/cruda)
- **pedo** is *Groser*. Senses: a fart; a fight or trouble ("se armó un gran pedo"); *hacerla de pedo*, to make things difficult; *ni pedo*, too bad or no way (= ni modo); drunk (*andar pedo*, fem. *peda*); a drinking binge (*agarrar un pedo*). The DEM has no separate sub-entry for *qué pedo* or *no hay pedo*, but they belong to this *Groser* word. — [DEM pedo](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pedo)
- **pinche** (*Groser*) means contemptible or stingy ("un pinche perro"), and also cheap or poor quality. As a noun it means a kitchen assistant. — [DEM pinche](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pinche)
- **hueva** (*Groser*): *¡qué hueva!* means a lack of energy or will, laziness. *De hueva* means boring ("Estuvo de hueva la mugre película"). — [DEM hueva](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/hueva)
- **gacho** in the figurative senses is *Popular*, with some uses also tagged *Caló*. It means ugly or shabby ("un traje gacho"), or mean or dishonest ("un cuate gacho"). As an interjection it means "how awful!" — [DEM gacho](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/gacho)
- **hacer el oso / un oso** (*Popular*) means to make a fool of oneself: "Papá, no vayas a hacer el oso". *¡Qué oso!* is not a separate DEM sub-entry. — [DEM oso](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/oso)
- **desmadre** (*Popular*) means chaos or a mess ("Esto es un desmadre"). *Echar desmadre* means to have rowdy fun. The DEM labels it only *Popular*, not *Groser*, even though it contains *madre*. — [DEM desmadre](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/desmadre)
- **andar bruja** (*Popular*) means to be broke. — [DEM andar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/ándale)
- **no manches** has no DEM sense under *manchar*. — [DEM manchar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/manchar). Learner sources gloss it as "no way / come on / no kidding". — [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/?p=357505); [Vagabond Journey](https://www.vagabondjourney.com/mexican-spanish-slang-words/)
- **mamar / mamársela** in Mx means "to exaggerate" and is labelled *vulg.* The DAMER also lists *mamar* "exaggerate" for Bo. This is the base of **no mames** ("you're exaggerating / no way"), so the DAMER's vulgar label carries over to it. — [DAMER mamar](https://www.asale.org/damer/mamar). The DAMER records **mamar gallo** (to tease) for Ho, **Co** and Ve. This is a different expression and is not vulgar.
- **coger** in Mexico: the intransitive sexual sense ("¿Vamos a coger?") is *Popular*, and *cogerse a alguien* is *Groser*. Its everyday Spain senses (take a bus, grab something) also appear in the DEM but carry the *Popular* label. — [DEM coger](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/coger)

## Key question 2: the chingar / madre family map with registers, plus other vulgar items (no mames, qué pedo, pinche)

### Takeaway
In the DEM, *chingar* is both *Groser* and *Ofensivo*, and every *madre* phrase in section IV of the *madre* entry is *Groser*. That includes the positive ones (*a toda madre* = excellent) and the "don't care" ones (*me vale madre(s)*). *Pedo*, *pinche* and *hueva* are *Groser* too. *Chingón*, *desmadre*, and the mild interjections built on *onda*, *padre* and *chido* are only *Popular*.

### Cited findings
- **chingar** (*Groser, Ofensivo*) has four senses: (1) to annoy or cause serious harm, "¡No me chingues, necesito dinero!"; (2) to sexually assault, the sense behind the insult "¡Vete a chingar a tu madre!"; (3) *chingarse*, to wear oneself out ("Yo me chingo trabajando día y noche"); (4) *chingarse*, to fail ("Se chingó el negocio"). — [DEM chingar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chingar)
- **chingón** (*Popular*) is the best at one's job ("eres un verdadero chingón"). It also means very good quality, and *ponerse chingón* means to rise to the occasion. Sense II means a persistent annoyance, and *chingón del once* means a swindler. — [DEM chingón](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chingón)
- **madre**, section IV (*Groser*), lists: a toda madre (excellent); tener/no tener madre (have or lack shame); ¡qué poca madre! (how shameless); chingar alguien a su madre (insult); chingar a su madre algo (to be ruined or dead); la chingada madre; ¡(la) chingada madre! (anger, surprise or admiration); caerle de madre(s) a alguien; mandar a alguien a chingar a su madre (to cut someone off); echar madres (to curse); darse en la madre / partirse la madre (to get hurt, to fight); partirle la madre a alguien (to beat up or wreck); ¡en la madre! (what bad luck); ni madre(s) / pura madre (no way, or definitely yes); valer madre(s) (worthless); valerle madre(s) a uno (not to care); hasta la madre (fed up, or very drunk or high); oler/saber a madre(s) (to stink); (de) a madre(s) (a lot); una madre (a thingamajig). Sense I.5 is *mentarle la madre a alguien* (to insult someone's mother). — [DEM madre](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/madre)
- **desmadre** is *Popular* only: chaos, *echar desmadre*. — [DEM desmadre](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/desmadre)
- **pedo** is *Groser*: trouble, fight, drunk, binge, *ni pedo*. — [DEM pedo](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pedo)
- **pinche** is *Groser*: contemptible, stingy, lousy. — [DEM pinche](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pinche)
- **mamar / mamársela** (Mx) means to exaggerate and is *vulg.*. It is the base of *no mames*. — [DAMER mamar](https://www.asale.org/damer/mamar)
- **hueva** is *Groser*: ¡qué hueva!, de hueva. — [DEM hueva](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/hueva)

### Inferences
- Register ladder for "no way!", from mild to strong: ¿a poco? / ¡no inventes! / ¡no manches! (informal) → ¡no chingues! / ¡no mames! (vulgar) → ¡no mames, güey! (vulgar, intensely casual). The relative ordering [K] is consistent with *no manches* being widely described as the euphemism of *no mames* and with *mamar* carrying the DAMER's *vulg.* label.
- *Madre* inverts the usual pattern: *a toda madre* is very positive, *poca madre* is negative (shameless), and in recent youth speech *poca madre* is sometimes also positive ("está poca madre" = awesome) [K, unverified].
- Softened substitutes [K]: me vale gorro/queso/sombrilla/cacahuate for *me vale madre*; ¡no manches! for *no mames*; no hay bronca for *no hay pedo*; ¡qué flojera! for *qué hueva*; ¡caray! / ¡chin! / ¡chispas! for chingar-family exclamations.

### Gaps
- No DEM entry was fetched for *chingada / a la chingada / chingadera / chingadazo / un chingo / chingaquedito*. Those registers are [K].
- I found no source this session for the claim that the positive use of *poca madre* is growing among young people.

## Key question 3: Spain's typical equivalents (tío, guay, mola, vale, flipar, hostia, joder, en plan, ¿qué pasa?) and how Mexicans perceive them

### Takeaway
Learner sources agree on the core swaps: tío/tía ↔ güey, guay ↔ chido/padre, and vale ↔ sale/va/órale. They also note that *guay* sounds odd in Mexico. The DEM confirms that *coger* is sexual in Mexico, which makes the Spain sense of *coger* the best-known trap. I found no academic source this session on how Mexicans perceive Spaniards' slang or swearing.

### Cited findings
- Chido or padre is the Mexican word for "cool" and guay is Spain's. Locals in Mexico "might find it odd" to hear *guay*. — [StreetTongue Mexican vs Spain Spanish](https://streettongue-website.pages.dev/mexican-spanish-vs-spain-spanish) (secondary learner source, seen only as a search snippet)

- Mexican *coger* = to have sex (*Popular*); *cogerse a alguien* is *Groser*. — [DEM coger](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/coger)

## Key question 4: diminutives and politeness (ahorita, cafecito, ¿mande?, ¿me regalas…?, ¿me pasas…?, con permiso, provecho) vs Spain's more direct style

### Takeaway
*Ahorita* is the best-documented case. Lara (Colmex) says it works as a politeness softener, because a bare order sounds rude in Mexico. A 2021 Colmex study (Granados) finds it spreading among young speakers in Mexico City to uses that are not immediate. The DAMER contrasts *ahorita* ("in a moment, later") with *ahoritita* ("right now"). ¿Mande? is a DEM-defined courtesy formula. The other politeness habits (¿me regalas…?, provecho, con permiso, diminutives) are [K].

### Cited findings
- Luis Fernando Lara says *ahorita* is a polite, noncommittal reply to requests. It is misread as "immediately" when it really means "when convenient", and Mexican courtesy norms make a direct order such as "hazlo ahora" sound brusque. When someone answers "ahorita", the listener can push back with "ahoritita, ahorititita". — [BBC Mundo via Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=9801363)
- Pablo Fernández Christlieb (psychologist) argues that "el 'ahorita' sirve para no obedecer" ("ahorita" serves as a way not to obey), linking it to colonial history. This is cultural interpretation, not linguistic data. — [BBC Mundo via Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=9801363)
- The DAMER, as cited by the BBC, defines *ahorita* as "dentro de un momento, más tarde" (in a moment, later) and *ahoritita* as "ahora mismo" (right now). *Ahoritita* is listed for Mx, Gu, Ho, ES, Pe and Bo. — [BBC Mundo via Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=9801363)
- Granados (2021, *Cuadernos de Lingüística de El Colegio de México*) studies *ahorita* in Mexico City as "semantic attenuation" in apparent time. Its use is expanding among young speakers into contexts that are neither punctual nor close to the moment of speaking. — [Granados 2021](https://cuadernoslinguistica.colmex.mx/index.php/cl/article/view/202) (only the abstract was seen)
- ¡Mande! is a "courtesy expression" for answering a call or asking someone to repeat. — [DEM mande](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/mande)
- The DAMER records *regalar* meaning "serve or bring what someone requests" for Guatemala and central **Colombia**, and "sell" for Gu, Ho and ES. No Mexico sense is listed. — [DAMER regalar](https://www.asale.org/damer/regalar)

### Inferences
- The "¿me regalas…?" request is widely described anecdotally as common in Mexico [K], but the DAMER lists it only for Colombia and Central America. The report writer should present it as **strongest in Colombia, also heard in Mexico [K]**, and not as a defining Mexicanism.
- Spain's ordering style [K]: "Ponme una caña", "¿Me pones un café?", "Dame…". These are perfectly polite in Spain but can sound abrupt to Mexicans, who prefer "¿Me da…?", "¿Me regala…?", "¿Me puede traer…?" or "Quisiera…", plus *por favor* and diminutives (un cafecito, un momentito, una preguntita, ahorita).
- Courtesy formulas expected in Mexico [K]: *con permiso* when passing someone or leaving a table; *provecho* to people eating, strangers included; *¿mande?* instead of *¿qué?*; *joven / señorita / seño / güero* as address; *a sus órdenes* from service staff; *¿en qué le puedo servir?*. In Spain *¿qué?* or *¿cómo?* are normal, and *que aproveche* exists but is used less with strangers [K].

### Gaps
- I retrieved no corpus or sociolinguistic source on Mexican diminutive frequency (cafecito, momentito), or on *con permiso* or *provecho*, in comparison with Spain.
- The full text of Granados 2021 was not read, so I have no figures for the age distribution.

- **mamar gallo / mamarle el gallo** (to tease or pull someone's leg): Ho, **Co**, Ve. **Mamar** (to exaggerate, vulgar): Mx, Bo, not Co. — [DAMER mamar](https://www.asale.org/damer/mamar)

- **chido**: Mx only. — [DAMER chido](https://www.asale.org/damer/chido)
