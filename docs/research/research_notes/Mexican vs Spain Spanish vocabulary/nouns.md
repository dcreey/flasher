## Which differences are real today in Mexico, and which commonly listed pairs are wrong or overstated?

### Takeaway
Most of the classic pairs hold in present-day central Mexico: ordenador/computadora, móvil/celular, pajita/popote, piso/departamento, grifo/llave, bombilla/foco, acera/banqueta, maletero/cajuela, gafas/lentes, bolígrafo/pluma. Several popular lists overstate them, though. Mexico has its own uses of coche, autobús, maíz, tomate, zumo, piscina and gafas, so these Spain words are not "unknown" there. Some are used with a narrower or different meaning (zumo, tomate, maíz).

### Cited Findings
- DEM defines **zumo** only as the liquid pressed from citrus peel, plus a strip of citrus rind used as a garnish. It does not define it as fruit juice. So "zumo" exists in Mexico, but not as the everyday word for juice (that is jugo). — [DEM: zumo](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/zumo)
- DEM defines **camión** as a motor vehicle that carries people *or* goods, and names the city bus as an example. This confirms camión = bus in Mexico. In Spain, camión means truck. — [DEM: camión](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/camión)
- DEM defines **popote** as a thin hollow tube for drinking liquids. This confirms popote = straw (Spain: pajita). — [DEM: popote](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/popote)
- DEM defines **torta** first as a sandwich made with telera or bolillo bread. It also lists a *Popular* sense, "young woman, girlfriend". In Spain, torta means a flat cake or (colloquially) a slap. — [DEM: torta](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/torta)
- DEM defines **jitomate** as the round red fruit, with the "bola" and "guaje" varieties, and gives no regional mark. — [DEM: jitomate](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/jitomate)
- DEM **chavo** (*popular*): child or young person, and also boyfriend/girlfriend ("Te presento a mi chavo"). *Chavo fresa* is a well-off, well-mannered young man. — [DEM: chavo](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/chavo)
- DEM **morro/morrilla** (marked *NO* and *Popular*): child or young person ("Luisa está muy morrilla todavía"). The NO mark most likely means northern Mexico (Noroeste), but the page does not explain it. — [DEM: morro](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/morro)
- DEM **coger** has the full range of neutral senses (grab, catch an illness, take a vehicle). The sexual senses are marked *Groser* (cogerse a alguien) and *Popular*. The Spain word is therefore not unknown in Mexico, but its everyday use is risky. — [DEM: coger](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/coger)

### Commonly listed pairs that are overstated or wrong (flags)
| Claim in learner lists | Reality | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "Mexicans say jugo; zumo is unknown" | Zumo exists in DEM, but only for citrus peel liquid or rind, never for orange juice as a drink | DEM✓ [zumo](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/zumo) |
| "camión = truck" (Spain meaning) | In Mexico, camión covers both bus and truck (camión de carga, de volteo, de redilas) | DEM✓ [camión](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/camión) |
| "autobús is not used in Mexico" | Autobús is normal for intercity coaches (central de autobuses) and in formal registers. Camión is the colloquial word for the city bus | R; [DEM autobús](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/autobús) |
| "coche is Spain-only; Mexico says carro" | Coche is also used in Mexico City (often by older speakers). Auto and carro are both common. Coche is understood everywhere | R; [DEM coche](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/coche) |
| "corn = elote in Mexico" | Elote is the tender ear or cob as food. Maíz is the standard word for the plant and grain in Mexico | R; [DEM elote](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/elote) |
| "tomato = jitomate everywhere in Mexico" | Jitomate is the central and southern word. In the north and in much commerce, "tomate" means the red tomato. In central Mexico, "tomate" alone often means the green tomatillo | DEM✓ jitomate (no regional mark); Matador (LL); R |
| "Mexicans don't say gafas" | Lentes is the default. Gafas is understood and appears in advertising (gafas de sol) | R |
| "piscina is Spain-only" | Alberca is the everyday word, but piscina is understood and used in sports and formal contexts | R |
| "concha is vulgar, avoid it" | Not in Mexico. DEM gives shell, the sweet bread concha, and others. The only non-standard sense is *(Popular)* "tener/hacer concha" = to behave cynically. The vulgar sense belongs to Argentina, Uruguay, Chile and Peru (R) | DEM✓ [concha](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/concha) |
| "bicho is vulgar" | Not in Mexico. DEM: animal or insect; *(Popular)* cat; *(Popular)* a despised person or animal. The vulgar "penis" sense is Puerto Rican (R) | DEM✓ [bicho](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/bicho) |
| "coger is unusable in Mexico" | It is overstated as "unusable", but caution is right. DEM keeps the neutral senses, and *Groser* marks the sexual one. In practice Mexicans say agarrar/tomar | DEM✓ [coger](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/coger) |

### Inferences
- The safest teaching rule for learners: for these concepts, the Mexican word is what a Mexico City speaker *says*. Many Spain words are *understood* there because of TV, dubbing and Spain-made media. ## Which Spain nouns mean something different or offensive in Mexico, and vice versa?

### Takeaway
The dangerous ones for a Spain speaker in Mexico are mainly **coger** (verb, *Groser* sense in DEM), **chaqueta** (colloquial for masturbation), **madre** (and "tu madre" / "a toda madre", where madre is an all-purpose charged word), **mariquita** (slur), **paja/pajita** (masturbation, so prefer popote), **pendejo** (strong insult in Mexico, milder or "pubic hair" or "brat" in Spain) and **pinche** (kitchen helper in Spain, vulgar intensifier in Mexico). **Concha** and **bicho** are often listed as dangerous, but they are *not* vulgar in Mexico according to DEM.

### Cited Findings
- **concha** in Mexico: shell, the sweet bread, and other neutral senses. The only marked sense is *(Popular)* "tener/hacer concha" = to be cynical or irresponsible. No vulgar sense. — [DEM: concha](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/concha)
- **bicho** in Mexico: animal or insect; *(Popular)* cat; *(Popular)* a despised animal or person. No vulgar sense. — [DEM: bicho](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/bicho)
- **coger** in Mexico: the sexual senses are marked *Groser* ("cogerse a alguien") and *Popular* (intransitive "to have sex"). The neutral senses coexist. — [DEM: coger](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/coger)
- **torta**: a sandwich in Mexico, plus a *Popular* sense "young woman/girlfriend". It is not a slap or flat cake there. — [DEM: torta](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/torta)
- **zumo**: in Mexico, the citrus peel liquid or rind. — [DEM: zumo](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/zumo)
- **camión**: bus and truck in Mexico, truck only in Spain. — [DEM: camión](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/camión)
- In Mexico, **fresa** as a noun for a person equals Spain's **pijo**. — [Matador](https://matadornetwork.com/es/8-palabras-del-espanol-mexicano-que-nadie-mas-entiende/) (search summary)
- Matador says **chingar** in Spain is understood only as a sexual vulgarism, while in Mexico it is a very broadly used vulgar word. — [Matador](https://matadornetwork.com/es/10-diferencias-espanol-de-mexico-y-espana/) (search summary)
- TV Azteca notes that **pinche** in Mexico is not always a swearword and can still mean kitchen helper. Its use depends on context. — [TV Azteca](https://www.tvazteca.com/aztecauno/especiales-azteca/estas-8-palabras-solo-se-dicen-en-mexico-en-ningun-otro-pais-las-conocen) (search summary)
- Search summaries for this question mostly covered other countries (Cuba, Peru, Argentina). For example, a Matador "words to use with care" page gave papaya, chongo, paloma and gato, but not Spain vs Mexico. — [Matador](https://matadornetwork.com/es/palabras-para-usar-con-cuidado-al-hablar-en-espanol/)

## Anglicisms used in Mexico but not in Spain (and vice versa)

### Takeaway
Mexico takes US English loans for household, clothing and car words. Spain takes British/French-era loans for leisure and sports words, and uses its own calques in technology (ordenador, ratón).

### Cited Findings
- Redalyc (Moreno de Alba, per search summary) lists English loans that entered Mexican Spanish: balacera, soya, cachetes, plomero, frijol. ## Which differences also hold for Colombia?

### Takeaway
Colombia sides with Mexico on the big tech and car pairs (carro, celular, computador with masculine gender). It often has its own third word, though: esfero (pen), bombillo (bulb), pitillo (straw), arveja (pea), cobija (=MX), tinto (black coffee), parqueadero (parking lot), andén (sidewalk), and it keeps some Spain words (piscina, gafas, tiza, colegio). So "Latin American = Mexican" is wrong about half the time.

### Cited Findings
- Carro is used in Mexico and Colombia, and coche is Spain's. Celular is used in both countries, and móvil is Spain's. — [SpanishDict answers](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/286445/different-words-for-the-same-objects-in-spanish-speaking-countries); [Yabla](https://spanish.yabla.com/lesson-A-Few-Outstanding-Differences-between-Castilian-and-Latin-American-Spanish--1815) (search summary)
- Latin America has computadora or computador, and Spain has ordenador. Computador is typical of Chile and some Andean countries, while computadora is used in Mexico and Colombia among others. This conflicts with the researcher's knowledge that Colombia strongly prefers *el computador*. — search summary of the same sources; needs a check
- Colombia: esfero (pen) vs Mexico pluma; bombillo (Venezuela/Colombia) vs Mexico foco; pitillo (Venezuela, Colombia, Cuba) vs Mexico popote. — search summary of [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/286445/different-words-for-the-same-objects-in-spanish-speaking-countries)
- In Colombia "cachucha" is a cap. — [Matador](https://matadornetwork.com/es/palabras-para-usar-con-cuidado-al-hablar-en-espanol/)

### Inferences
- In the table, the CO column matches Mexico for most vehicle, tech and fruit items (carro, celular, papa, durazno, jugo, camarón, toronja, cobija). It matches Spain for piscina, gafas, tiza, colegio, ascensor and nota. Pijama/piyama is feminine in Colombia, as in Mexico. It has its own word for many household items (caneca, trapero, nevera, parqueadero, tinto, esfero).
