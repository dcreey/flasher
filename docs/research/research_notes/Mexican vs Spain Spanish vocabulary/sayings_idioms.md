# Mexican sayings (refranes, dichos, idioms, set expressions) compared with Spain

Scope: present-day usage, central Mexico by default. 88 entries in the catalogue below, plus a list of Spain idioms that sound foreign in Mexico.

**How to read the sourcing.** Each catalogue row has a Source column.
- **[S#]** means the saying (and usually its meaning) appears in a source fetched or searched in this session (key at the bottom of Question 1).
- **"unverified"** means the entry comes from the researcher's general knowledge and could not be checked against a source within the tool budget. These entries are listed because they are widely known, but the report writer should treat their meaning, register and Spain comparison as unconfirmed.
- Example sentences were written by the researcher unless they are marked "(src)". Literal translations and English equivalents are the researcher's own.
- The Spain column ("MX only" / "Shared" / "Spain: X") is the researcher's judgment unless a source is cited. Only a few rows have a sourced contrast: aguantar mecha (DLE), uña y carne, leche, and me importa un pimiento.

Register key: N = neutral, any situation; C = colloquial; V = vulgar or strong; F = familiar or older speakers.

## Which refranes/dichos and idioms are actually used by Mexicans in everyday speech now?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence of current use is a 2019 El Colegio de México study. It combined Twitter-sourced sentences, an online questionnaire, dictionaries and corpora (CREA, CORPES, CEMC, CEMD). By speaker preference, *aguantar vara* (89%), *botársele la canica* (94.6%), *haber gato encerrado* (97.8%), *ponerse trucha* (91%), *tapar el sol con un dedo* (97.4%) and *ser uña y mugre* (95.4%) are the most vital forms. Beyond that study, recurring popular-press and learner lists (TV Azteca, Mexico News Daily, Speaking Latino, UnoTV, Merca2.0) keep naming the same core of dichos. Being repeated across several independent lists is the best proxy for current use that this session could find. No frequency data was found for refranes as such.

### Cited Findings
- Fajardo Rojas (El Colegio de México, *Lingüística Mexicana* I-2, 2019, pp. 27-50) measured the "vitality" of variant verbal idioms. The measures were dictionaries (DEM 2010, DLE 2014, DMM 2011, DCTDE 2011), corpora, and an online questionnaire built on real tweets. — [Fajardo Rojas 2019](https://linguisticamexicana-amla.colmex.mx/index.php/Linguistica_mexicana/article/download/324/328/1203)
  - *Aguantar vara* ('soportar con entereza situaciones difíciles, adversas, molestas') was chosen by 89% of respondents in the frame "Chilango que no aguanta ___, no es chilango". It is documented in DEM, DMM and DCTDE, with 4 hits in CREA and 7 in CORPES. *Aguantar mecha* came second (it is also in the DLE, i.e. shared with Spain), then *aguantar bala*. Informants also supplied *aguantar carrilla*.
  - *Botársele la canica* ('perder el sentido de la realidad y hacer algo disparatado') was chosen by 94.6%. The variants *botársele la cuica* and *la cuiria* trail. Informants also offered *botársele la chaveta*.
  - *Haber gato encerrado* was chosen by 97.8%, against 1.1% for *haber chivo encerrado*.
  - *Ponerse trucha* was chosen by 91%. *Ponerse chango* is in three dictionaries but only 2% chose it. Informants also offered *ponerse buzo, ponerse avispa, ponerse (las) pilas, ponerse vivo, ponerse abusado, ponerse águila* and *ponerse verga* (vulgar).
  - *Tapar el sol con un dedo* was chosen by 97.4%. *Taparle el ojo al macho* is a synonym.
  - *Ser uña y mugre* was chosen by 95.4%, while the pan-Hispanic/DLE *ser uña y carne* got only 4.6%. This is a clear Mexico-vs-Spain split.
- TV Azteca (25 May 2025, updated Oct 2026) lists 38 "refranes y dichos mexicanos" said to be still current. They include *A falta de pan, tortillas*, *A todo puerquito le llega su San Martín*, *Ahora es cuando, chile verde, le has de dar sabor al caldo*, *Al perro más flaco se le van las pulgas*, *Camarón que se duerme, no lo contratan de velador* (a joking remix), *Del plato a la boca… nadie se equivoca* and *Del tamaño del sapo es la pedrada*. — [TV Azteca](https://www.tvazteca.com/aztecauno/especiales-azteca/lista-38-refranes-y-dichos-mexicanos-que-todos-deberian-tener-a-la-mano)
- The same article says many Mexican refranes are colonial, some have Indigenous roots, and others are adaptations of Spanish sayings. — [TV Azteca](https://www.tvazteca.com/aztecauno/especiales-azteca/lista-38-refranes-y-dichos-mexicanos-que-todos-deberian-tener-a-la-mano)
- Merca2.0's "most used" top-10 includes *Camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente*. — [Merca2.0](https://www.merca20.com/?p=12586248). El Imparcial (2024) calls it one of the most popular sayings in all of Mexico. — [El Imparcial](https://elimparcial.com/locurioso/2024/09/18/camaron-que-se-duerme-se-lo-lleva-la-corriente-conoce-el-significado-y-el-origen-de-este-refran)
- The SEP's Nueva Escuela Mexicana materials teach refranes as part of Mexican culture, including *camarón que se duerme*. — [SEP NEM](https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/coleccion/los-refranes-parte-de-la-cultura-mexicana/); [SEP NEM camarón](https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/coleccion/camaron-que-se-duerme/)
- A UNAM thesis cites Darío Rubio's *Refranes, proverbios y dichos y dicharachos mexicanos* as the most complete compilation, though not without flaws. — [search summary of UNAM repository results](https://ru.dgb.unam.mx/bitstream/20.500.14330/TES01000272142/3/272142.pdf). Herón Pérez Martínez's refranero work (El Colegio de Michoacán) exists in the repository but was not read. — [Colmich repository](https://colmich.repositorioinstitucional.mx/jspui/bitstream/1016/343/1/PerezMart%c3%adnezHer%c3%b3n2005.pdf)
- **El que no transa no avanza.** An academic article on Redalyc analyses it as one of four refranes most tied to corruption, together with *Más vale tener palancas que dinero* and *El gandalla no batalla*. — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/137/13718501002.pdf) (via search summary; not opened). Zeta Tijuana (2024) illustrates it with a *mordida* (bribe) story. — [Zeta Tijuana](https://zetatijuana.com/2024/07/la-etica-el-soborno-y-la-corrupcion/). The USC Folklore Archive records the variant *el que no trampa nunca avanza*. — [USC Folklore](https://folklore.usc.edu/el-que-no-trampa-nunca-avanza/). Spellings *transa/tranza* both occur. — [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/answers/209684/el-que-no-tranza-no-avanza)
- **Dar el avión** means ignoring or brushing someone off, and **se me fue el avión** means 'I lost my train of thought'. — [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/mexico-living/mexican-expressions-for-your-real-and-figurative-travels). **Hacerse pato** (and *dar el avionazo*) means stalling, putting off an answer to get out of an invitation politely. — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/9-expresiones-mexicanas-para-evitar-decir-no/) (via search summary)
- The DEM gives *leche* in Mexico the popular sense 'buena suerte', with the example *"¡Qué leche tienes!"* It also lists *mala leche* = 'mala intención'. — [DEM leche](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/leche). This contradicts a search-engine summary claiming that Mexican *qué leche* means 'how terrible'. Trust the DEM.

### Inferences
- The questionnaire data in [S1] shows that dictionary presence is a poor proxy for current use. *Ponerse chango* is in three dictionaries, yet only 2% of speakers chose it. Book-collected refranes in compilations like Rubio's or Pérez Martínez's should not be assumed current without corpus or speaker evidence.
- The most current material skews toward verbal idioms and short dichos (*echarle ganas, aguantar vara, dar el avión, caer el veinte*) rather than full proverbs. Full refranes survive mostly as quoted folk wisdom and in jokey remixes. TV Azteca's "camarón que se duerme, no lo contratan de velador" is one example.
- *El que no transa no avanza* should be taught with a warning: it names and criticises corruption culture. Saying it approvingly sounds cynical.

## Which idioms differ between Mexico and Spain for the same idea, and which Spain idioms sound foreign in Mexico?

### Takeaway
Many core idioms are shared (*tomar el pelo, meter la pata, ponerse las pilas, haber gato encerrado, dejar plantado*). But for many everyday ideas each country has its own form. Only a few pairs are sourced: *uña y mugre* (MX) vs *uña y carne* (ES), with Colmex showing 95.4% vs 4.6% preference in Mexico; *me vale / me importa un cacahuate* vs *me importa un pimiento*; *ser fresa* vs *ser pijo*; and *qué leche* = 'what luck' in Mexico, against *ser la leche* = 'be amazing' in Spain.

### Cited Findings
- *Ser uña y mugre* is preferred by 95.4% of Mexican respondents; the DLE form *ser uña y carne* got 4.6%. — [Fajardo Rojas 2019](https://linguisticamexicana-amla.colmex.mx/index.php/Linguistica_mexicana/article/download/324/328/1203)
- *Aguantar mecha* is in the DLE (pan-Hispanic or Spain-recognised), while *aguantar vara* is the Mexican favourite at 89%. — [Fajardo Rojas 2019](https://linguisticamexicana-amla.colmex.mx/index.php/Linguistica_mexicana/article/download/324/328/1203)
- "Me importa un pimiento" and "me la trae floja" are typical of Spain, while the Mexican default is "me vale", plus "me importa un cacahuate" and "me vale madres" (stronger). *Me importa un pepino / un comino* are used in both. — [Tell Me In Spanish](https://www.tellmeinspanish.com/vocab/i-dont-care-in-spanish/); [Tomisimo forum](https://forums.tomisimo.org/archive/index.php/t-16928.html); [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/cultura/mejores-frases-con-frutas-y-verduras-que-usan-los-mexicanos-en-su-vocabulario/)
- Inklingo tags *flipar en colores* ('be amazed'), *estar al loro* ('stay alert') and *ser la leche* ('be amazing') as Spain-specific. It tags *caer el veinte, echar carrilla, estar cañón, hazme el paro* as Mexico-specific. — [Inklingo](https://www.inklingo.app/spanish/idioms/category/mexico)
- In Spain *eres la leche* is praise and *me cago en la leche* is an angry exclamation. — [search summary, Culture Trip / The Local](https://cms.thelocal.com/?p=217573). In Mexico *leche* (popular) = 'buena suerte': "¡Qué leche tienes!" — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/leche)
- *Estar en el quinto pino* is defined by the RAE as 'a very distant place'; it originated in Madrid. — [search summary citing RAE](https://theculturetrip.com/north-america/mexico/articles/15-mexican-expressions-that-dont-make-sense-in-english) (provenance unclear; the DLE entry was not opened)
- *Fresa* (MX) corresponds to Spain's *pijo*. — [Barceló](https://www.barcelo.com/pinandtravel/es/diccionario-mexicano-palabras-mexicanas/)
- Lexical background: Spain *zumo/coche/charlar/césped* vs MX *jugo/carro/platicar/pasto*. — [52spain](https://www.52spain.com/d/79092-diferencias-y-conexiones-entre-el-espanol-mexicano-y-el-espanol-de-espana). Spain *tortilla* = potato omelette, *bocata* vs MX *torta*, and *chingar* has a sexual meaning in Spain. — [Matador, Bayliss 2018](https://matadornetwork.com/es/10-diferencias-espanol-de-mexico-y-espana/)
- Inklingo lists many Spain-flavoured idioms without region tags: *estar hasta las narices, estar como un flan, cortar el bacalao, no estar el horno para bollos, estar sin blanca, hacer el agosto, no dar palo al agua, comerse el coco, hacer la pelota, poner verde, estar como una cabra, tener mala uva*. — [Inklingo](https://www.inklingo.app/spanish/idioms/category/mexico). Whether each one sounds foreign in Mexico is NOT stated by the source. See the Inferences below.

### Inferences
- The Colmex data suggests a general pattern: when a pan-Hispanic or DLE form competes with a Mexican variant, Mexicans overwhelmingly pick the local one (*uña y mugre, aguantar vara, ponerse trucha*). Learners should be taught the Mexican form as default, with the Spain form flagged.
- Mexican food and nature idioms (atole, mole, enchiladas, tepache, pinole, chile, nopal, guaje, aguacate, cacahuate, iguana, chango) have no peninsular counterparts. Food-based idioms are the clearest marker of a Mexican saying.
- *Coger* and *chingar* are the highest-risk false friends. Spanish idioms built on *coger* sound vulgar in Mexico.

### Gaps
- No source specifically surveyed how Mexicans perceive Spain idioms such as *pillar, tela marinera, a tope, quinto pino*. The "sounds foreign" claims are researcher judgment.
- *Dar plantón* vs *dejar plantado* distribution was not resolved, and the premise may be false since both appear in both countries.
- The Valladolid TFG on Mexican verbal idioms unused in Spain (uvadoc.uva.es/bitstream/handle/10324/71937/TFG_F_2024_169.pdf) would be a primary source for contrasts, but it was blocked by bot protection.

## Food- and nature-based Mexican idioms (nopal, chile, mole, aguacate, frijoles, tortilla)

### Takeaway
Food idioms are abundant and distinctively Mexican. Mexico News Daily's dinner-table list ([S3]) and the UnoTV list ([S5]) give a sourced core: *ser el ajonjolí de todos los moles*, *echarle crema a los tacos*, *regar el tepache*, *no se puede chiflar y comer pinole*, *sale más caro el caldo que las albóndigas*, *del año del caldo*, *me importa un cacahuate*, *hacerse guaje*, *más verde que un aguacate*, *ser fresa*, *me lleva la tostada*, and *no es lo mismo Chana que Juana, ni tuna que pitahaya*. Speaking Latino adds *dar atole con el dedo*, *llegar después del atole*, *no son enchiladas*, *ni maíz* and *andar enchilado* ([S2]).

### Cited Findings
- The Mexico News Daily dinner-table list (Jamison 2023), with examples, covers items 25, 26 and 62-66 above. — [MND](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/culture/mx-expressions-inspired-by-dinner-table)
- UnoTV (Meza 2025) lists *me importa un cacahuate, sácate la papa de la boca, te haces guaje, eres muy fresa, no es lo mismo Chana que Juana ni tuna que pitahaya, me importa un pepino, hace frijol, ¿qué te pasa calabaza?, estás más verde que un aguacate, vas a tronar como ejote, es puro melón y lo quieren vender como sandía, hijo de la guayaba* ('mothers scolding kids') and *me lleva la tostada* ('annoyance'). Most entries have no definition in the source. — [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/cultura/mejores-frases-con-frutas-y-verduras-que-usan-los-mexicanos-en-su-vocabulario/)
- TV Azteca refranes with food or nature: *A falta de pan, tortillas*; *Ahora es cuando, chile verde, le has de dar sabor al caldo*; *A todo puerquito le llega su San Martín*; *Cuando el tecolote canta, el indio muere* (dated, ethnically loaded); *Cuando hay para carne es vigilia*. — [TV Azteca](https://www.tvazteca.com/aztecauno/especiales-azteca/lista-38-refranes-y-dichos-mexicanos-que-todos-deberian-tener-a-la-mano)
- Speaking Latino: *dar atole con el dedo* (trick or fool), *llegar después del atole* (arrive late), *no son enchiladas* (not easy), *ni maíz* (no chance), *andar enchilado* (angry), *como agua para chocolate* (furious), *de lengua me como un taco* (yeah right), *saber de qué lado masca la iguana*, *cada chango a su mecate*. — [Speaking Latino](https://www.speakinglatino.com/mexican-spanish-phrases/)

### Inferences
- Several food idioms carry ethnic or class content and should be flagged or avoided in learning material, as dated or offensive: *alegre el indio y le das maracas*, *cuando el tecolote canta, el indio muere* (both in TV Azteca's list), and *naco*.
- Some of the user's food examples are unverified this session: *al nopal sólo se le arriman cuando tiene tunas*, *a darle que es mole de olla*, and *tener el nopal en la frente* (looking unmistakably Mexican).

### Gaps
- No fetched source covered nopal-, frijol- or tortilla-specific idioms such as *al nopal…*, *tener el nopal en la frente*, *se le voltea la tortilla*, *cuando hay para frijoles no hay para tortillas*, *echarse un taco de ojo* or *ponerse/estar como chile*. They are omitted from the sourced catalogue or marked unverified.
- Register and generational data (older vs younger speakers) for food idioms was not found.
