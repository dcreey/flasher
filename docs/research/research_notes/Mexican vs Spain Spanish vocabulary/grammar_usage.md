# Grammar and usage differences: Mexican (central) vs Spain (Peninsular) Spanish

Method note: rae.es (DPD, NGLE pages) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so DPD/NGLE positions below come from search-result snippets that quote those pages, or from secondary sources that quote them (Universidad de Piura's "Castellano Actual", academic papers). The Diccionario del español de México (DEM, El Colegio de México, dem.colmex.mx) was fetched directly and is the main primary source for Mexican usage. A University of Trieste course handout ("Características de la variante mexicana del español", units.it) was read in full; it is mostly copied from Spanish Wikipedia's "Español mexicano" article, so treat it as secondary. Items marked **[unverified]** are from the researcher's own linguistic knowledge and were not confirmed by a source in this session. The report writer should present them as such, or drop them.

## For each feature: how strong is the difference, and does a Spain-style sentence sound wrong, foreign, or fine in Mexico?

### Takeaway
The strongest, most consistent markers are: **ustedes for every plural "you" (vosotros/os/vuestro/-ad/-aos not used at all)**, the **preterite for today and recent events** ("hoy comí", "¿ya comiste?"), **lo, not le, for a male direct object**, "**hasta** = not until" ("abre hasta las 10" = opens at 10), **"qué tan"** for "how + adjective", plural-marked **"se los dije"**, and Mexican discourse items (**ahorita, ¿mande?, ¿bueno? on the phone, ándale/córrele, a poco, luego luego, nomás**). Spain forms like vosotros and "le vi" are understood in Mexico but heard as foreign (Spain). They are not "wrong". Spain's "he comido" for today's actions sounds odd or over-formal, and can be misread as "I have eaten (at some point / until now)".

### Cited Findings

**1. ustedes vs vosotros (os, vuestro, venid, sentaos)**
- The vosotros forms "carecen de uso en América" (are not used in the Americas) or in parts of southern Spain (Canarias, part of Andalusia). In most of Spain, vosotros is the informal plural — [RAE DPD "vosotros", via search snippet](https://www.rae.es/dpd/vosotros)
- In the Americas, "ustedes" is the only plural form of address, for informal and formal alike. Canarias has the same simplified system — [Diario de Avisos on Canarian usage](https://diariodeavisos.elespanol.com/2022/07/vosotrismo-habla-canaria/); [Univ. València, Aleza, formas de tratamiento](https://www.uv.es/aleza/Cap.%204.%20EA%20Formas%20tratamiento.pdf)
- In Mexico, vosotros is perceived as an archaism or a Spain form, "no se usan prácticamente en territorio mexicano" (practically unused in Mexico) — [units.it handout (from es.wikipedia "Español mexicano")](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- Consequences [unverified, but follow directly from the above]:
  - Spain "Venid aquí" → MX "Vengan acá".
  - "Sentaos" → "Siéntense".
  - "¿Vosotros queréis…?" → "¿Ustedes quieren…?".
  - "Os lo dije" → "Se los dije".
  - "vuestro coche" → "su coche / el coche de ustedes".
  - Mexican ustedes always takes third-person plural verbs and the pronouns se/les/los/las/su.

**2. Preterite vs present perfect**
- Peninsular Spanish uses the present perfect for events earlier today ("esta mañana he…"), and the preterite is required for prehodiernal events ("el año pasado") — [Moreno Burgos summary, eriac.univ-rouen.fr](https://eriac.univ-rouen.fr/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/10TMAp163MorenoBurgos.pdf). Even within Spain the choice varies by region (NW Spain, Canarias use the preterite) — [Moenia, USC](https://revistas.usc.gal/index.php/moenia/article/view/3137/3503)
- The Americas show a clear predominance of the simple preterite compared with Europe — [USC Minerva study](https://minerva.usc.gal/bitstreams/02bf1d4c-1eb0-437a-9160-b3978c5bc9b5/download)
- In Mexico, the compound perfect keeps a value tied to the present: an action that continues or is still relevant at the moment of speaking. Lope Blanch read a Mexican example ("ha procedido siempre") as imperfective/continuing. Henderson (2022, Cuadernos de Lingüística de El Colegio de México) argues that in both Spain and Mexico the form encodes a relation to the moment of speech — [Henderson, Cuadernos de Lingüística Colmex](https://cuadernoslinguistica.colmex.mx/index.php/cl/article/download/257/426/2588); [García Fajardo, Redalyc (NRFH)](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/602/60224200002.pdf)
- The NGLE (2009) treats the simple/compound opposition as more aspectual than temporal, building on Lope Blanch and Moreno de Alba — [revistas.nebrija.com](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/254/222) (via search snippet)
- Practical reading [unverified examples, consistent with the sources above]:
  - MX "Hoy comí tacos" = Spain "Hoy he comido tacos".
  - MX "¿Ya comiste?" = Spain "¿Ya has comido?".
  - MX "He comido mucho últimamente" = "I've been eating a lot lately" (continuing).
  - MX "¿Has comido chapulines?" = "Have you ever eaten…" (experience).
  - So "Hoy he comido" can sound to a Mexican like "today I have been eating (and still am)". "No he comido" is natural in Mexico for "I haven't eaten (yet, up to now)".

**3. Leísmo ("le vi" for a man)**
- Masculine personal leísmo ("le vi" for "lo vi" about a man) is the only leísmo the RAE admits. The DPD notes it is far more frequent in the singular. Leísmo with things is "desprestigiado" (stigmatized) — [RAE DPD leísmo (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/dpd/le%C3%ADsmo); [RAE Glosario "leísmo"](https://www.rae.es/gtg/le%C3%ADsmo); [Revista de Estudios Hispánicos, UPR](https://revistas.upr.edu/index.php/reh/article/view/18298/15739)
- American Spanish keeps the etymological lo/la: "A Juan lo vi" — [UDEP "¿Se dice lo vi o le vi?"](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/se-dice-lo-vi-o-le-vi/)
- Mexico: "ausencia de leísmo" (no leísmo), and le works as a "defective" dative. This gives three contrasting forms, with no "le" used for direct objects:
  - "No sé cómo hacerlo" (how to do this particular thing)
  - "No sé cómo hacerla" (how to do the task)
  - "No sé cómo hacerle" (I don't know what to do)
  — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- Caveat: Mexico and the rest of Latin America do use "le" with usted ("¿Le ayudo?", "Le espero"?) and with certain verbs (le ayudo, le llamo). The DPD discusses that variation. Not verified in this session.

**4. "Se los dije" (plural moved onto the direct-object pronoun)**
- The RAE Glosario: "Se lo dije (a ellos ~ a ustedes)" becomes "Se los dije", so that speakers can show plural number somewhere. The NGLE calls it "sumamente común en muchos países" (extremely common in many countries) but does not recommend it — [RAE Gramática "los pronombres dativos" (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/los-pronombres-dativos); [RAE DPD "pronombres personales átonos" (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pronombres%20personales%20%C3%A1tonos)
- Moreno de Alba (UNAM) documented it in Mexico "en todos los registros y niveles sociales" (in every register and social level). In Spain "no se dice ni mucho menos se escribe" (it is not said, still less written) — [UDEP "¿se los dijo o se lo dijo?" quoting Moreno de Alba](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-se-los-dijo-o-se-lo-dijo/)
- Example: "¡Esto iba a pasar!, se lo dije [a Juan]" vs "se los dije [a Juan y a Pedro]" — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- Strength: very strong in Mexican speech. It is still non-standard in careful writing, where "se lo dije" is normative.

**5. "Hasta" = "not until" (no negation)**
- In Mexico the "no" is dropped in sentences with hasta:
  - "Será publicado hasta fines de año" (Spain: "No será publicado hasta fin de año")
  - "Cierran hasta las nueve" (Spain: "No cierran hasta las 9")
  - "Hasta que tomé la pastilla se me quitó el dolor" (Spain: "Hasta que no tomé la pastilla no se me quitó el dolor")
  — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- Ambiguity risk: "Abre hasta las 10" is read in Mexico as "opens at 10 (not before)". A Spaniard reads it as "is open until 10". This is a real misunderstanding trap, not just a matter of style. The DPD entry "hasta" (it says the use is found in Mexico, Central America, and parts of Colombia/Ecuador) could not be fetched (403).

**6. "Qué tan" vs "cómo de"; "entre más" vs "cuanto más"**
- MX "¿Qué tan graves son los daños?" = Spain "¿Cómo de graves son los daños?". "¿Qué tan buen cocinero eres?" = "¿Cuán buen cocinero eres?" — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- Correlatives: MX "Entre más lo hagas, peor te va a ir" vs Spain "Cuanto más lo hagas, peor te va a ir" — same source
- "¿Qué tanto?" (= ¿cuánto?) and "se me hace" (= me parece) are listed as expressions current in Mexico but lost in Spain — same source

**7. Adverb "medio" agreeing ("media cansada")**
- NGLE §13.8d: adverb-to-adjective agreement is a living process in American Spanish, but not part of formal registers. It recommends "medio cansada". "Medio" is used as an agreeing adjective in Mexico and Central America — [RAE Gramática "adjetivo y adverbio" (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/adjetivo-y-adverbio)
- DPD: as an adverb "medio es invariable", and agreement found in popular speech should be avoided — [RAE DPD "medio" (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/dpd/medio); [UDEP "Medio cansada y no *media cansada"](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/medio-cansada-y-no-media-cansada/)
- Strength: common in informal Mexican speech, stigmatized in writing. A learner app should teach "medio cansada" and at most note "media cansada" as colloquial.

**8. Recién**
- DPD "recién" could not be fetched. [unverified] In the Americas, recién is used before finite verbs ("Recién llegó" = "he just arrived"). Spain mostly uses it only before participles ("recién llegado"), plus "acaba de llegar". Mexico uses "recién llegó" and "acaba de llegar". The Southern Cone also uses it to mean "only then / not until" ("recién mañana").

**9. Nomás / no más**
- DEM "nomás":
  1. "solamente, simplemente": "Estoy aquí nomás mirando", "Vive nomás con su madre y su hermana".
  2. "nomás que" = "en cuanto" (as soon as): "Nomás que tenga dinero, te invito a comer".
  — [DEM nomás](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/nom%C3%A1s)
- [unverified] Spain uses "solo / nada más" instead. "No más" in Spain means "no more" ("no más guerras").

**10. Ocupar = necesitar**
- Attributed to northern Mexico and spreading to the centre. Twitter data show it most in the Monterrey area. Plana Díez (2014) calls it colloquial and tolerable outside formal registers. The Academia Mexicana de la Lengua (2017) calls it colloquial and discourages it because it can be ambiguous — [Tovilla-Loza 2021, Borealis](https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/borealis/article/view/6627)
- The Diccionario de americanismos records it for Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica and Mexico — [UDEP "¿son sinónimos ocupar y necesitar?"](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-son-sinonimos-ocupar-y-necesitar/)
- Strength: regional and colloquial, strongest in the north. Not a central-Mexico default for a learner app.

**11. Luego luego**
- DEM: "luego luego" (popular) = right away / immediately after: "Recogimos el libro y luego luego nos fuimos". Also "Acaba luego; te espero en la puerta" (= soon) — [DEM luego](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/luego)
- The handout lists "luego" (in the sense "soon/immediately") as an archaism kept in Mexico — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)

**12. Ahorita (and diminutives)**
- DEM "ahorita" (popular) has three senses:
  1. right now
  2. in a little while: "Ahorita los alcanzo"
  3. whenever possible
  Examples: "Ahorita vengo", "Ahorita no está, regresa al ratito", "Ahoritita regreso" — [DEM ahorita](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/ahorita)
- [unverified] Diminutive -ito/-ita is very productive in Mexico, including on adverbs and greetings: ahorita, rapidito, cerquita, adiosito, tantito. Spain also uses -ito, but regionally uses -illo (Andalusia), -ín (Asturias) and -ico (Aragón/Murcia). The point to teach is the frequency, and that -ito attaches to adverbs and interjections.

**13. ¿Mande?**
- DEM: "¡mande!" is "Expresión de cortesía con la que uno contesta a alguien que lo llama" (a polite reply when someone calls you), also used to ask for a repeat: "—¡Raúl! —¡Mande!" — [DEM mande](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/mande)
- [unverified] Spain uses "¿Qué?", "¿Cómo?", "¿Perdón?" or "Dime" for this. In Mexico a bare "¿Qué?" can sound brusque, especially to parents and elders.

**14. "-le" intensifier on imperatives and interjections**
- "En buena parte del territorio mexicano se acostumbra añadir un -le al final de los verbos conjugados en modo imperativo" (much of Mexico adds -le to imperative verbs): "José, córrele porque se va el camión", "Muévanle (ustedes) bien a la harina" — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- "ándale" is cited as a non-indigenous mexicanismo — same source
- [unverified] Also "híjole", "órale", "pásale", "épale". Spain has no equivalent and uses "venga", "vamos", "anda".

**15. ¿A poco?**
- DEM: "¡A poco?" expresses admiration, surprise or doubt about what was said: "¡A poco no vas a venir?" — [DEM poco](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/poco)
- [unverified] Spain says "¿En serio?", "¿De verdad?", "¡No me digas!".

**16. Se me hace que**
- "se me hace" (= me parece) is listed as an expression current in Mexico but lost in Spain — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- Example [unverified]: "Se me hace que va a llover" = Spain "Me parece que va a llover".

**17. Phone and greetings**
- Mexico answers the phone with "¿Bueno?". The usual story traces this to early manual exchanges, where "bueno" confirmed the line was connected — [México Desconocido](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/bueno-la-historia-de-por-que-en-mexico-contestamos-asi-el-telefono.html)
- Spain uses "¿Diga?" / "¿Dígame?" / "¿Sí?". Colombia uses "¿Aló?" (DLE: "interjección para responder al teléfono") — [Billiken](https://billiken.lat/saber-mas/el-motivo-detras-del-famoso-alo-al-contestar-una-llamada/); [Portafolio (CO)](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/por-que-en-colombia-se-dice-alo-al-contestar-el-telefono-esto-dice-la-ia-630276)
- "¡Hasta luego!" = goodbye, equivalent to "¡Hasta pronto!", "¡hasta la vista!", "¡adiós!" — [DEM luego](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/luego)
- Note: the phone sources are popular or journalistic rather than academic.

**18. Articles and determiners**
- Mexico tends to drop the indefinite article ("Es ladrona" = "Es una ladrona"). It uses the definite article where Spain omits it ("Me salí de la casa" vs Spain "Me fui de casa") — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx)
- The same source contrasts MX "salirse" with Spain "irse" in "me salí de la casa".

### Inferences
- **Mexico (central default): what sounds wrong, foreign or fine**
  - Foreign (Spain), but understood: vosotros/os/vuestro and -ad/-aos imperatives; "le vi" for a man; "cómo de"; "cuanto más"; "¿diga?"; "vale".
  - Odd or misleading: "Hoy he comido" for a finished meal (sounds like "I've been eating"); "No abre hasta las 10" is fine, but a Spaniard reading Mexican "abre hasta las 10" gets the wrong meaning.
  - Fine in both: "se lo dije"; "medio cansada"; "acabo de"; "solo"; "¿qué?" (though "¿mande?" is politer in Mexico).
- For a learner app, the safest rule is to produce central-Mexican defaults:
  - ustedes
  - the preterite for completed past, including today
  - lo/la for direct objects
  - "qué tan"
  - "¿mande?" (polite) / "¿bueno?" (phone)
  - "ahorita", "nomás", "a poco", "-le" imperatives, but only in informal register
  - "se los dije" may be recognized but not required. Accept both "se lo" and "se los" for plural recipients.

### Gaps
- DPD entries for hasta, recién, leísmo (fine print on "le" with usted), se and vosotros could not be read directly (403). Regional lists, such as which other countries share "hasta" without "no", are unverified.
- No corpus frequencies were found for "he comido" vs "comí" in today's contexts in present-day Mexico City.
- Unverified (no source fetched):
  - "harto": common in Chile/Colombia, rare in Mexico.
  - "la María": an article before first names, colloquial in Spain (esp. Catalonia) and rural Mexico.
  - "cuadra" (Americas) vs "manzana" (Spain), for a city block.
  - "ir a + infinitive" being preferred over the synthetic future in Mexican speech.
  - tú/usted norms: more usted in Mexico with strangers, elders and service staff, vs Spain's broad tuteo.
- Pronunciation notes, all from general knowledge, unverified here:
  - Seseo: Mexico does not distinguish c/z from s, so learners misspell.
  - "x" is pronounced [x] in México/Oaxaca, [s] in Xochimilco, [ʃ] in some names.
  - -ado → -ao is a strong Spain colloquial feature and weak in Mexico.

## Which features does Apple/Google-style machine translation get wrong for Mexico? What should a translator rewrite?

### Takeaway
No independent evaluation of current Apple or Google output by variety was found. The rewrite list below rests on the grammar facts above plus industry and localization sources. A local test of the app's actual translation engine on the sample sentences is recommended.

### Cited Findings
- In Latin America "vosotros" is virtually non-existent, and "ustedes" covers formal and informal plural. Spain says "ordenador", Mexico says "computadora". Discourse particles differ: "Vale" (Spain) vs "Sale" (Mexico) vs "Dale" (Argentina). "Neutral Spanish" is typically based on a central Mexican standard — [Translated.com Spanish markets guide](https://translated.com/resources/english-to-spanish-translation-guide-20-markets); [Smartling](https://www.smartling.com/blog/5-common-challenges-of-translating-english-to-spanish-and-how-to-solve-them) (vendor sources)
- A 2025 MT Summit paper proposes using LLMs to adapt baseline NMT output into Mexican and Argentine variants. This implies baseline output is not variety-specific — [MT Summit 2025 paper (could not fetch; 404)](https://preview.aclanthology.org/mtsummit-25-ingestion/2025.mtsummit-2.7.pdf) (search snippet only)

### Inferences
Rewrite table: Spain-style output → central Mexican form. Grammar rows are based on the cited findings above; lexical rows are from general knowledge and should be checked against the separate vocabulary research.

## Where does Colombia match Mexico, and where does it differ?

### Takeaway
Colombia matches Mexico on the major grammar points: ustedes (no vosotros), the preterite for recent events, and no leísmo. It differs on discourse markers and address forms, such as ¿Aló?, the "usted" of intimacy (ustedeo), and harto.

### Cited Findings
- ustedes is the only plural form in all of the Americas, Colombia included — [RAE DPD vosotros (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/dpd/vosotros)
- Colombia answers the phone "¿Aló?", not "¿Bueno?" — [Portafolio (CO)](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/por-que-en-colombia-se-dice-alo-al-contestar-el-telefono-esto-dice-la-ia-630276)
- Academic work cites NGLE §16.15 on "ustedeo" (usted between intimates) in Colombia — [Gubitosi & Lifszyc, Glosas ANLE](https://glosas.anle.us/site/assets/files/1284/05_gubitosi-lifszyc.pdf) (search snippet)
- "Se los dije" is described as a Mexican hallmark that contrasts with "most other dialects", but the claim is flagged "cita requerida" (citation needed) in the source — [units.it handout](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/760127/mod_resource/content/1/Caracteristicas%20de%20la%20variante%20mexicana%20del%20espanol.docx). The RAE says the pattern is common "in many countries" — [RAE Gramática dativos (snippet)](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/los-pronombres-dativos). These two sources conflict on how exclusively Mexican it is.

### Inferences
- Unverified: ¿mande?, ahorita, the -le imperatives (ándale, córrele), a poco, luego luego and "qué onda" are Mexican-specific or at least far more Mexican. Colombia uses "¿qué más?", "¡Hágale!", "parce", "harto" (= mucho, very common in Bogotá) and "¿Sí o qué?". Colombia shares "qué tan" and, in some areas, "hasta" without negation.

### Gaps
- No Colombia-specific academic source was fetched. The Colombia-vs-Mexico comparison for hasta, se los, qué tan, harto and diminutives is unverified in this session.
