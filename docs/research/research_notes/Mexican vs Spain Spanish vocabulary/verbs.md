# Verbs and verb phrases: Mexican Spanish vs Spain (peninsular) Spanish

Scope: present-day usage, with central Mexico as the default. Researched 2026-11-07.

**How the sources were used, and how reliable they are**
- **DEM** = *Diccionario del Español de México*, El Colegio de México (dem.colmex.mx/Ver/&lt;word&gt;). This is the main primary source for the Mexican side. Its register labels are quoted as given: (Popular) = colloquial or common speech, (Groser) = coarse or vulgar, (Groser y Ofensivo). If an entry is missing from DEM, that is also evidence: "currar", "cabrear" and "chequear" all returned "Su búsqueda no tuvo resultados", and "molar" has only its noun sense (a molar tooth).
- **Wikt** = English Wiktionary, Spanish section (en.wiktionary.org/wiki/&lt;word&gt;#Spanish). It is used for the Spain side and for country labels (Spain, Mexico, Latin America, Colombia). It is crowdsourced, so it corroborates the Spain side; it is not authoritative.
- **LL** = the [Living Language table of Latin America vs Spain verbs](https://www.livinglanguage.com/blog/2015/07/29/spanish-latin-america-versus-spain-verbs). This is a learner reference with 14 pairs.
- **RAE DLE / DPD (rae.es, dle.rae.es) returned HTTP 403 for every request (WebFetch and curl), so no "Méx." mark in the DLE was checked directly.** Where the RAE's position is reported here, it comes through a secondary source, which is named. The ASALE *Diccionario de americanismos* could not be reached either.
- "Spain form in Mexico?" is mainly inferred from DEM: whether DEM has the Spain verb at all, and whether it gives the relevant sense. DEM records Mexican usage, so a Spain verb listed there exists in Mexico, but the entry says nothing about how often it is used.

## Q1. Verified table of common verbs and verbal expressions where Mexico differs from Spain

### Takeaway
85 rows follow. The Mexican side of every row is checked against DEM unless a row says otherwise. The biggest everyday contrasts are extrañar / echar de menos, manejar / conducir, rentar / alquilar, platicar / charlar, jalar / tirar, aventar / lanzar, enojarse / enfadarse, agarrar or tomar / coger, estacionar / aparcar, apurarse / darse prisa, pararse / ponerse de pie, prender / encender, rebasar / adelantar, reprobar / suspender, ponchar / pinchar, and correr / despedir. There are also many Mexican uses of jalar.

### Inferences
- When DEM lacks a Spain sense, the Spain verb is understood in Mexico but is not the default: no driving sense for *conducir*, no exam sense for *suspender*, *aparcar* only as a redirect, and *chequear*, *currar*, *cabrear* and verb *molar* absent altogether. Mexicans still see *conducir* in official phrases such as *licencia de conducir*; this is general knowledge, not sourced.
- *Jalar* is the most productive "Mexican verb" in the set. It means pull, work, function, join in, head off, flush, steal, and (vulgar) masturbate, so learners need it with its senses split out.

### Gaps
- The DLE could not be reached (403), so the "Méx." marks on these entries could not be quoted; the ASALE Diccionario de americanismos could not be reached either.
- No frequency data (corpus counts such as CORPES or CREA) was gathered. Words like "very common" are drawn from DEM labels and examples, not from counts.
- These were not verified: *echar los perros* = flirt in Mexico, *voltear a ver* = look at, *¿jalas?*, *no manches*, *dar lata*, *hablarle a alguien* = phone someone, *pedir aventón* vs *hacer autostop/dedo*, *hacer la parada*, *encabronarse*. They are omitted or flagged rather than asserted.

## Q2. Spain verbs that are vulgar, offensive or different in Mexico, and vice versa

### Takeaway
Verified traps: **coger** (sex in Mexico), **correrse vs venirse** (each country's word for orgasm), **pararse** (stop vs stand up, and *parársele* = erection), **pelar** (Spain *me la pela* is vulgar; in Mexico it means "pay attention"), **chingar** (in Mexico, "Groser y Ofensivo"), **jalársela**, **mamar**, **fregar** (washing up vs annoying), and **ocupar** (occupy vs need). **Pisar is not a Mexican trap.** Its sexual sense is labeled for Cuba, El Salvador and the Dominican Republic. **Pinchar** is not vulgar in Mexico; Mexicans simply say *ponchar*. Pinchar's sexual and flirting senses are Spanish colloquial.

### Cited Findings
- **coger**: DEM section V gives V.1 (Groser) "Lograr alguien que otra persona realice con él el acto sexual…" and V.2 "Hacer el acto sexual" ("¿Vamos a coger?"). DEM still lists neutral senses (take, catch an illness), and many are marked (Popular) — [DEM coger](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/coger). Wikt: "vulgar, chiefly Latin America" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/coger#Spanish). A news report cites the RAE for a sexual sense in Mexico, Argentina, Bolivia and Venezuela — [Uno TV](https://www.unotv.com/cultura/coger-o-cojer-como-se-escribe-correctamente-segun-la-rae/). The Academia Mexicana's Diccionario de mexicanismos uses coger as its example of a word that differs from Spain (via [Expansión](https://expansion.mx/entretenimiento/2010/11/30/el-espanol-mexicano-es-sexista-machista-alburero-y-querendon)). A Revista de la Universidad de México article dates the sexual sense to colonial New Spain — [Revista UdeM](https://mia.revistadelauniversidad.mx/downloads/ce8ddd08-04ab-4753-bfaa-61724424c853/rum-OCT.2009-art%c3%adculos-gramatica-del-deseo.pdf).
- **correrse / venirse / acabar**: Wikt correr (reflexive) = "to have an orgasm" (no region tag), and also "to move" or "walk away" — [Wikt correr](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/correr#Spanish). A learner dictionary describes correrse = orgasm as colloquial in Spain and not fit for formal contexts — [Speaking Latino](https://www.speakinglatino.com/word/correrse). In Mexico the coarse verb is **venirse**: DEM venir IV "Venirse prnl (Groser) Tener un orgasmo" — [DEM venir](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/venirse). DEM's *correrse* in Mexico = "extenderse… correrse la tinta" (ink running); *correr* = fire someone — [DEM correr](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/correr). Wikt *acabar* = orgasm is "Latin America, vulgar" — [Wikt acabar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/acabar#Spanish).
- **pisar**: Wikt "vulgar, Cuba, El Salvador, Dominican Republic" = have sex. **Mexico is not listed** — [Wikt pisar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pisar#Spanish).
- **pinchar / ponchar**: Spanish colloquial senses include flirt, wiretap, slip up, "to screw (have intercourse with)", pester and (GUI) click — [Wikt pinchar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pinchar#Spanish). Mexico uses ponchar for punctures — [DEM ponchar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/ponchar).
- **pararse**: Mexico = stand up or get up (DEM II.1). *Parársele* (Popular) = to have an erection (DEM I.4) — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pararse). Wikt tags the erection sense "vulgar, Mx, Central America, Caribbean, South America" — [Wikt parar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/parar#Spanish).
- **pelar**: Mexico "Ponerle atención o hacerle caso a alguien" (Popular) — [DEM pelar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pelar). Spain vulgar "me la pela" = not care — [Wikt pelar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pelar#Spanish).
- **chingar**: DEM labels the whole entry "(Groser y Ofensivo)": to harm or bother; to rape; *chingarse* = to sacrifice oneself or to fail — [DEM chingar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chingar). Wikt shows some senses (break, fail, work hard) also tagged ES — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chingar#Spanish).
- **fregar**: In Mexico, DEM I is the neutral "limpiar… los trastes", but DEM II (Popular) = cause harm or annoyance, ruin, steal; "¡No la friegues!" — [DEM fregar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/fregar). Wikt: "possibly vulgar, Latin America" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fregar#Spanish).
- **mamar**: Mexico (Groser) = to get drunk; oral sex; "No mamar" = don't talk nonsense, the root of *no mames* — [DEM mamar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/mamar).
- **jalar**: "Jalársela (Groser) Masturbarse"; "No te la jales" (Popular) = don't exaggerate — [DEM jalar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/jalar).
- **follar**: "vulgar, Spain" — [Wikt follar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/follar#Spanish). Not in the Mexican lexicon as the everyday word, since Mexico uses *coger* (inference).
- **cachar**: harmless in Mexico (catch). In Chile and Peru it is "vulgar slang" for having sex — [Wikt cachar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cachar#Spanish).
- **chupar**: also oral-sex slang (Wikt, no region); in Mexico it commonly means "to drink alcohol" — [DEM chupar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chupar).
- **ocupar** (need), **dar pena** (be embarrassed) and **rentarse** (Spain: be worth it) are innocent meaning traps; see table rows 51, 85 and 3.

### Inferences
- With coger, a Spaniard's everyday verb is the trap. With correrse/venirse, each country has its own coarse word, and the other country's word sounds innocent: in Mexico *se corrió la tinta* means the ink ran, but *me vine* is coarse.
- DEM still recording neutral *coger* senses means the verb is not unusable in Mexico, but learners should default to agarrar and tomar.

### Gaps
- The DLE "Méx." marks for coger, pisar, correrse and pinchar could not be checked (403).
- The adjective/insult *pinche* (related to pinchar) was not verified from a fetched source.

## Q3. Mexican verbs that are anglicisms, and how accepted they are

### Takeaway
*Checar* (from "check") and *rentar* (from "rent") are fully established in Mexico; DEM gives them as unlabeled, neutral headwords. *Textear* has also entered DEM. *Parquear* is in DEM but is mainly a broader Latin American and Caribbean word; central Mexico says *estacionar*. *Aplicar a (un puesto)* is a widespread Latin American anglicism that DEM does not record, and style authorities recommend *solicitar*. *Ordenar* (order food) is labeled LATAM.

### Cited Findings
- checar: unlabeled in DEM, with senses "comprobar…" and "checar la tarjeta de asistencia" — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/checar). DEM has no *chequear* at all — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/chequear). Wikt: "Mexico, alternative form of chequear" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/checar#Spanish). Per a Peruvian university language column, the Diccionario de americanismos and the DPD treat checar as usual in Mexico, and the column notes that Spain uses chequear — [UDEP](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/usted-chequea-un-documento-o-checa-un-documento/).
- rentar: unlabeled in DEM, used both for paying and for charging rent — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/rentar). Wikt tags rent = "Latin America" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/rentar#Spanish). (Whether rentar comes from English or is a revival of old Spanish *renta* was not settled by the sources.)
- textear: in DEM — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/textear).
- parquear: in DEM, glossed "Estacionar un vehículo" — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/parquear); "Latin America" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/parquear#Spanish).
- aplicar (a un puesto): Wikt "Latin America, intransitive, to apply (as a candidate) a/para" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/aplicar#Spanish). WordReference marks "aplicar a" as (AmL) — [WordReference](https://jann.wordreference.com/enes/apply). The Spanish Astronomy Society's terminology commission calls this use an English-driven extension spreading in Latin America, and recommends *solicitar, presentar una solicitud, optar a, presentarse a* — [SEA bulletin](https://www.sea-astronomia.es/sites/default/files/2024_12.pdf). The DEM entry for aplicar has no such sense — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/aplicar).
- ordenar = order a product or service, tagged "LATAM" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ordenar#Spanish).
- *Ponchar* (DEM; also the baseball strikeout sense) probably comes from English "punch", but this was **not verified** — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/ponchar).

### Inferences
- How accepted these words are can be ranked by whether DEM records them: checar, rentar ≈ fully normal > textear (recorded, newer) > parquear (recorded, regionally weaker) > aplicar a (not recorded; criticized by style guides).

### Gaps
- No Fundéu article on "aplicar a" or "checar" was found through search; Fundéu's site was not fetched.
- No usage-frequency data for textear compared with "mandar mensaje / whatsappear".

## Q4. Pronominal and phrasal differences, and notes on Colombia

### Takeaway
Mexico tends to use the pronominal form where Spain uses the plain verb or a periphrasis: *tardarse* ("no te tardes"), *enfermarse* (vs *ponerse enfermo*), *regresarse*, *demorarse*. It also has productive pronominal phrases: *echarle ganas*, *se me hace que*, *jalarle (al baño)*, *azotarse*, *aventarse*, *zafarse*, *cotorrearla*. Colombia shares many LatAm forms (*pararse*, *parquear*, *devolverse* = go back) but not Mexican-only words such as *chambear* or *ocupar* = need.

### Cited Findings
- tardarse: DEM example "No te tardes por favor" — [DEM tardar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/tardar). A secondary source reports the DPD's comment on impersonal *se tarda* — [club FAQ citing DPD](https://www.clubmitsubishiasx.com/faq/como-se-dice-cuando-algo-tarda-mucho) (weak source).
- enfermarse: DEM says the verb is "frequently pronominal" — [DEM](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/enfermar); LL pairs enfermarse with ponerse enfermo — [LL](https://www.livinglanguage.com/blog/2015/07/29/spanish-latin-america-versus-spain-verbs).
- echarle ganas: [DEM gana 6](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/gana); [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/echarle_ganas#Spanish).
- se me hace que: [DEM hacer 12](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/hacer).
- quedar de vs en: [DEM quedar I.11](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/quedar).
- darse cuenta: same in both countries (the task brief says so; no difference turned up).
- **Colombia**:
  - pararse = stand up is tagged for Co — [Wikt parar](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/parar#Spanish).
  - parquear is "Latin America" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/parquear#Spanish).
  - devolverse = go back is described as a Latin American pronominal use, where Spain uses volver or regresar — [SpanishDict compare](https://www.spanishdict.com/compare/regresar/devolver); [Los Andes](https://www.losandes.com.ar/volver-o-devolver).
  - dar la lata is tagged "Chile, Colombia, Spain" — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/dar_la_lata#Spanish).
  - chambear's tag list (CR, Guat., Hond., Mexico, Peru) leaves out Colombia — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chambear#Spanish).
  - ocupar = need is tagged "Central America, Mexico", not Colombia — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ocupar#Spanish).
  - botar = throw away or fire is general Latin American, and Colombia is plausible but not specifically tagged — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/botar#Spanish).
  - cachar = understand belongs to Chile, Bolivia and Argentina, not Colombia or Mexico — [Wikt](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cachar#Spanish).

### Inferences
- In Colombia, "devolverse" is the counterpart of Mexico's "regresarse"; a Mexican would hear "me devolví" mainly as "I returned (an item)". This is an inference from the sources above; it was not checked against Colombian usage data.

### Gaps
- *Regresarse* as a pronominal could not be verified in DEM (its entry shows only intransitive *regresar*).
- These Colombian items were not verified: *provocar* = feel like, *tanquear* (found only in a slang glossary snippet), *regalar* = give me (requests).
- No DPD text could be fetched directly on *tardarse* or *demorarse*.
