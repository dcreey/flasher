# Colombian vs Spain Spanish: everyday verbs and verb phrases

Scope: verbs and verb phrases where everyday Colombian Spanish (Bogotá, Medellín, Cali) uses a different word from Spain. These notes support an app that rewrites Spain-style machine translations into Colombian wording.

Source limits, read these first:
- **dle.rae.es returned HTTP 403** to every fetch, so no DLE entry was read directly. DLE content appears only where a secondary source quotes it (El Tiempo on *mamar gallo*, the RAE DHLE page for *emberriondar*).
- The Instituto Caro y Cuervo *Diccionario de colombianismos* (Dicol, 2018) is not freely online, so it was not consulted.
- The best primary source found is Gloria Viviana Nieto Martín, *Glosario de aprendizaje del español de Colombia* ("Hecho en Colombia", todoele.net, 2017). It is a Colombian ELE glossary that labels entries **Co.** (Colombia) or **Am.** (wider Latin America), with native-speaker example sentences. It is cited below as **[Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf)**.
- Mexico comparisons come from the sibling notes file `docs/research/research_notes/Mexican vs Spain Spanish vocabulary/verbs.md`, which checks each row against DEM (El Colegio de México). That file is cited below as "MX notes".

## Which Spain verbs sound wrong, odd or rude in Colombia?

### Takeaway
The headline finding reverses the Mexico rule: **coger is neutral in Colombia** ("coger el bus/la buseta" is normal), so the app should not swap it the way Mexico mode does. The clearest Spain-only verbs to replace are *aparcar → parquear*, *conducir → manejar*, *encender → prender*, *apetecer → provocar*, *echar de menos → extrañar* and *alquilar → arrendar* (housing). Spain slang verbs such as *molar*, *currar* and *flipar* had no Colombia-specific source.

### Cited Findings
- **coger: no sexual sense in Colombia.** es.wiktionary gives the neutral senses of *coger* (grab, take, catch, discover, trap) the "Ámbito" España, Perú, Colombia, Cuba. It gives the sexual sense (malsonante) **only** for Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, Venezuela, Mexico and Central America; Colombia is not listed — [es.wiktionary coger](https://es.wiktionary.org/wiki/coger)
- The en.wiktionary usage note says the vulgar sense is "entirely absent" in Colombia (as in Spain and Peru). It says *coger* "is best avoided" in Mexico, Central America, Bolivia and the River Plate, and that both senses exist in Venezuela — [en.wiktionary coger](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/coger)
- The Colombian glossary uses *coger* for transport in its own native examples: "Salgo a coger la buseta para irme para el centro" and "…a coger el autobús para el centro". It also lists the Colombian idiom *cogerle la maña [a algo]* (Co. inf.) = to get the hang of something — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf)
- **Conflict:** one search summary claimed Wiktionary marks the vulgar *coger* for Colombia. Both direct fetches above contradict that, so treat the claim as wrong. Generic learner sites tell readers to avoid *coger* in "Latin America" without naming countries, which is too broad for Colombia — [SpanishDict tomar vs coger](https://www.spanishdict.com/compare/tomar/coger)
- Dictionary.com names **pichar** as the Colombian regional variant of vulgar "coger" (to have sex). That is the word to avoid producing, not *coger* — [Dictionary.com coger](https://www.dictionary.com/e/slang/coger/)
- **regalar** sounds odd to a Spaniard in Colombian requests. "¿Me regalas un café?" is a polite request in Colombia even though the customer pays; in Spain *regalar* means to give something free — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/); [colombianspanish.co](https://colombianspanish.co/blog/colombian-spanish-learners-dictionary)
- **camellar / camellear:** in Colombia it means to work or sell; a Spaniard would think of drug dealing (*camello* = dealer in Spain) — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **amañarse** (Co.) = to feel at home or comfortable somewhere. A Spaniard may read it as "to rig, fix" — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf); [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **tirarse [algo]** (Co. inf.) = to ruin or break something: "¡Pare!, porque yo me tiro la cámara." This is a reverse trap: Spain *tirar* (throw away) is not wrong in Colombia, but the pronominal *tirarse algo* means "wreck it" — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf)
- **voltear** (Co. pop.) = to work hard running from place to place ("A nosotros nos toca voltear mucho") — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf)
- **molestar** (Co.) = (a machine or appliance) to break down or act up — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf)
- **vararse** (Co. inf.) = to give up ("El colombiano no se vara"), alongside the vehicle sense — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf); [colombianspanish.co](https://colombianspanish.co/blog/colombian-spanish-learners-dictionary) ("Un antioqueño nunca se vara")
- **mamar gallo** (to tease, pull someone's leg; also to stall or dodge a commitment). The RAE records it only as colloquial for Venezuela and Colombia; El Tiempo says it was once seen as vulgar but is now normal — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/colombia/otras-ciudades/que-significa-la-expresion-mamar-gallo-y-de-donde-viene-508172). SpanishDict flags a vulgar reading (oral sex) in some countries — [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/translate/mamar%20gallo)

### Inferences
- Do **not** swap *coger* → *tomar* in Colombia mode. Both are normal. Mexico mode's coger rule must not carry over. *Tomar el bus* is also fine in Colombia (the glossary has *tomar trago* = drink alcohol, Am.).
- *molar*, *currar*, *flipar* and *guay* are Spain colloquialisms (the MX notes cover them for Mexico). They are probably not native in Colombia, but this was **not verified** for Colombia; see Gaps.
- Spain *pillar* is not a problem for "to understand": the glossary records *pillar* = entender as Am. inf. ("¿Sí pilla?").

### Gaps
- No Colombia-specific source on how *molar*, *currar*, *flipar*, *follar*, *enfadarse* or *estropearse* sound to Colombians. dle.rae.es was blocked and the Dicol is not online.
- No usage-frequency data (CORPES XXI or Corpus del Español were not reachable within budget).

## What does Colombia use for each everyday meaning? (master table)

### Takeaway
Most solid swaps are pan-American and shared with Mexico: *manejar*, *prender*, *extrañar*, *apurarse*. The real Colombia vs Mexico splits are *parquear* (Colombia; Mexico prefers *estacionar*), *arrendar* (Colombia) vs *rentar* (Mexico), *provocar* = feel like (Colombia, Peru, Venezuela), and *coger*, which stays neutral in Colombia. Colombian slang verbs (*camellar*, *parchar*, *rumbear*, *embarrarla*, *mamar gallo*) are colloquial. They are best used as recognition vocabulary, not as automatic swaps of neutral Spain text.

### Inferences
- The safe auto-swaps for a Spain → Colombia rewriter are rows 1–4, 7 (housing context only), 9, 11 and 15, because each has a labelled dictionary or glossary source and a neutral register. Rows 19–30 are slang: offer them as glosses or recognition vocabulary, not automatic rewrites of neutral text.
- Rows 1, 2, 3 and 15 can probably share the Mexico-mode code path, except that the "park" target is *parquear* in Colombia and *estacionar* in Mexico.
- Row 5 needs an explicit "do not apply MX coger rule" exception.
- Since *dar* or *poner* in a request is ambiguous, row 6 (*regalar*) is best limited to 1st-person requests ("¿Me pones…?" / "¿Me das…?" → "¿Me regala…?").

### Gaps
No acceptable source was found within budget for the following. Several of them are widely reported anecdotally, but anything about them below is my own unverified knowledge, kept out of Cited Findings:
- **darse prisa → apurarse / afanarse** (and *afanado* = in a hurry). A search tool claimed the *afanado* sense without a source. The MX notes source *apurarse* for Mexico (DEM) only.
- **tardar → demorarse**, **volver/regresar → devolverse** (to go back), **ponerse de pie → pararse**, **colgarse** (to fall behind), **jartar** (to drink a lot / to eat), **recochar** (to horse around), **dañarse** (to break down), **charlar vs conversar**, **suspender → perder (una materia / el año)**. The search tool said "perder una materia" is habitual but gave no source.
- **pinchar / pincharse una llanta / estallarse**: no Colombia source. A search tool claimed Colombia says *se ponchó* more often. That looks like a Mexico feature, has no source, and should be **disregarded**.
- **emberracarse** (angry; also *berraco* = tough/great): not found. Only *emberriondarse* is sourced.
- **voltear = turn around**: Colombia has no source for this (Mexico does, per the MX notes). The only Colombian source gives *voltear* = to hustle at work.
- The DLE "Col." marks for all verbs could not be read (403), and the Dicol is not online.

## Which are colloquial vs neutral, and which are regional-only?

### Takeaway
Neutral, pan-Colombian and safe in any register: *parquear*, *manejar*, *prender*, *extrañar*, *arrendar*, *vararse* (vehicle), *coger* (kept). Colloquial but used nationwide: *provocar* (feel like), *ponerse bravo*, *regalar* in requests, *mamar gallo*, *rajarse*, *cuadrar*, *amañarse*. Slang: *camellar*, *rumbear*, *parchar*, *embarrarla*, *guerrearla*, *voltear* (hustle), *parar bolas*. Regional: *emberriondarse* (Antioquia).

### Cited Findings
- *provocar* = feel like is marked "Uso: coloquial" and shared by Colombia, Peru and Venezuela — [es.wiktionary provocar](https://es.wiktionary.org/wiki/provocar)
- *mamar gallo* is marked coloquial (Col., Ven.) by the RAE as reported; it was once considered vulgar but is now normalized — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/colombia/otras-ciudades/que-significa-la-expresion-mamar-gallo-y-de-donde-viene-508172)
- *emberriondarse* is Col., "más usual en Antioquia" — [RAE DHLE](https://www.rae.es/dhle/emberriondar)
- *ponerse bravo* is described as informal — [Yabla](https://espanol.yabla.com/lesson-The-meaning-of-bravo-in-Spanish-1273)
- The glossary's register labels: *manejar*, *parquear* and *prender* = Am. (unmarked register, i.e. neutral). *amañarse*, *desvararse* and *molestar* (break down) = Co. (neutral). *cuadrar* and *pillar* = Am. inf. *tirarse algo*, *guerrearla*, *dar la guerra*, *botarse* and *cogerle la maña* = Co. inf. *voltear* (work hard) = Co. pop. — [Nieto glossary](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf)
- The Colombian Spanish of different departments varies a lot, and many words persist despite "corrections" — [Canal Trece](https://canaltrece.com.co/editorial/y-cual-es-la-vaina-las-palabras-que-colombia-se-niega-a-soltar-aunque-algunos-insistan-en-corregirlas/)
- The *Diccionario de colombianismos* (Instituto Caro y Cuervo with the Academia Colombiana, 2018) has about 6,000 entries and 8,000 definitions, with regional and usage labels.
