- **[V]**: checked in a source cited inline.

## Which Colombian expressions are national vs regional?

### Takeaway
Most core slang a learner meets (chévere, bacano, parce/parcero, guayabo, plata, luca, pola, guaro, tinto, ¿qué más?, de una, qué pena, a la orden, vaina, sapo, paila) is national or close to it. Some items are clearly regional: "paisa" items (strongest use of parce, chimba, "pues"), "rolo"/Bogotá and Boyacá items (sumercé, chino/a for kid), and costeño items (yeyo, pelao, "ajá", "eche"). Where a word is used varies more than what it means. Rules should still be keyed to "Colombia" and carry an optional region note.

### Cited Findings
- The Instituto Caro y Cuervo's *Diccionario de colombianismos* (Dicol, 2018) has about 6,000 entries, 8,000 definitions and 4,500 examples. Each entry carries grammatical, **regional and usage marks**, so it is the main reference for regional labels. — [El Universal (Cartagena), 2018](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/colombia/2018/04/16/8000-definiciones-son-parte-del-nuevo-diccionario-de-colombianismos/)
- Radio Nacional lists Dicol entries: amañarse, bacano, chisga, corrientazo, chicharrón, darse garra, engallar, guayabo, hablar paja, intenso, jincho, lagarto, locha, mamar gallo, **ni de vainas**, ñapa, paila, rebusque, sardina, tacar burro, toche, yeyo. It also gives Dicol/Caro y Cuervo definitions, for example **camellar** "trabajar con esfuerzo para lograr una meta", **cacharrear**, **alcanzado** (short of money), **empelicularse** (imagining things that aren't real), **marca gato** (poor-quality, no-name product), **meter la ficha** (to make a big effort), **maluquearse** (to suddenly feel ill). — [Radio Nacional de Colombia](https://radionacional.co/cultura/historia-colombiana/los-colombianismos-una-riqueza-lexica-que-nos-une)
- **Parce**: "m. y f. coloq. Entre jóvenes, compañero y amigo" (a Dicol-style entry quoted secondhand). **[P]** — [Expat.com glossary citing Dicol](https://www.expat.com/es/guia/america-del-sur/colombia/13941-modismos-en-colombia.html)
- **Guayabo**: Dicol entry quoted in a linguistics article, "Malestar con el que se amanece después de haber bebido alcohol en exceso" (the hangover). **[V]** — [Revista de Lexicografía (UDC)](https://revistas.udc.gal/index.php/rlex/article/view/rlex.2018.24.0.5524/g5524_pdf)
- **Bacano**: colloquial, "excelente, muy bueno"; also used of a person, "amable, simpática". **[P]** — [Expat.com glossary citing Dicol](https://www.expat.com/es/guia/america-del-sur/colombia/13941-modismos-en-colombia.html)
- **Yeyo** comes from the Caribbean coast and means feeling unwell or about to faint. **Líchigo** is regional: in Antioquia and Caldas it names a small purse or pocketknife, in Bogotá a stingy person. — [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/nacion/las-10-expresiones-colombianas-que-no-se-encuentran-en-el-diccionario)
- **Paisa** means a native of the Medellín/Antioquia area; **rolo/a** or bogotano/a means someone from Bogotá; **guaro** (aguardiente) is used across Colombia; **sapo** (snitch) is used in Colombia and nearby countries. — [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/learn/spanish/spanish-tips/colombian-slang-expressions)
- **Chimba** is described as especially common in the Paisa region (Medellín). **[P]** — [Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/es/guia/america-del-sur/colombia/13941-modismos-en-colombia.html)
- **Sumercé** is still used in some parts of Colombia as a mark of respect (a holdover from colonial times). The Caro y Cuervo linguist notes "qué pena con sumercé" is still heard. — [Portafolio, quoting Gloria Viviana Nieto (Instituto Caro y Cuervo)](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/que-pena-con-usted-la-expresion-en-colombia-que-confunde-a-los-extranjeros-601258)
- Usage of tú, usted and vos varies by region, so "qué pena contigo" (tuteo regions), "qué pena con vos" (voseo regions, i.e. paisa and caleño) and "qué pena con usted" all occur. Usted is much more marked in Colombia. — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/que-pena-con-usted-la-expresion-en-colombia-que-confunde-a-los-extranjeros-601258)

### Inferences
- Region tags the app can use (several are [U]):
  - **Paisa** (Antioquia, Eje Cafetero): parce/parcero (now national among young people, but paisa in origin), chimba (strongest here), "pues" as a filler, "vos", "¿qué más pues?", "berraco/a", "amañarse".
  - **Rolo / cundiboyacense** (Bogotá and Boyacá): sumercé (mainly Boyacá and rural Cundinamarca), chino/a (kid), "qué boleta", "gomelo", "ala" ("¡ala, qué bueno!", dated and rolo).
  - **Costeño** (Caribbean): pelao/pelá (kid, young person), "ajá", "eche" or "¡eche, no joda!", "nojoda" (an interjection, not rude in practice), "bacano", "mondá" (vulgar, penis), "vale" (= friend, in "¿qué hubo, vale?"; not the Spain "OK"), "yeyo".
  - **Caleño** (Valle del Cauca): "vos", "mirá/vení" (voseo), "ve" (filler), "ñero", "oís", "pues sí".

## Colombian equivalents of Spain's guay, mola, flipar, tío, chaval, pasta, curro, vale, venga, hostia, joder, qué fuerte, ¡qué pasada!, es la leche, me la suda, pijo, cutre, chulo, majo, borde, gilipollas, etc.

### Takeaway
There is a reliable rewrite path for most items: guay/mola → chévere/bacano; tío/tía → parce, marica (only among close friends), man/vieja; resaca → guayabo; pasta → plata/lucas; curro/currar → trabajo/camello/camellar; vale → listo/dale/de una; me apetece → me provoca; de nada → con gusto/a la orden. Crude Spain interjections (joder, hostia, me la suda, gilipollas) have Colombian counterparts at a similar level of crudeness (jueputa, uy no, me vale/me importa un culo, huevón/güevón, gonorrea). They should be swapped like for like and never toned up.

### Cited Findings
- "¡Qué chévere!" = Spain "guay"/"mola"; Colombian "¿Qué más?" ≈ Spain "¿qué tal, tío?"; **marica** used affectionately ≈ Spain "tío"/"colega"; **guayabo** = "resaca"; **pelao** = any young person (in Spain "pelado" suggests bald); **amañarse** = to feel at home somewhere (in Spain "amañar" = to rig or fiddle); **patilla** = watermelon (Spain "sandía"); **pitillo** = drinking straw (Spain "pajita"; in Spain pitillo = cigarette); **chiva** = colorful open-sided rural bus or party bus; "¿me regalas un café?" is an ordinary polite request that gets paid for. **[V]** — [Matador Network (ES): 11 palabras colombianas que confundirán a un español](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **Camellar** = "trabajar con esfuerzo" (Caro y Cuervo, *Diccionario de oficios y afines*), so curro/currar → trabajo/camellar, camello. **[V]** — [Radio Nacional](https://radionacional.co/cultura/historia-colombiana/los-colombianismos-una-riqueza-lexica-que-nos-une)
- **Plata** = money; **billete** = money; **luca** = a thousand pesos (StoryLearning glosses it as "peso"; in practice "una luca" = 1,000 pesos, see Gaps); **pola** = beer (named after a discontinued brand); **guaro** = aguardiente; **tinto** = small black coffee; **chévere**/**bacano** = cool; **¡de una!** = yes, absolutely; **parche** = group of friends, **parchar** = to hang out, **desparchado** = with nothing to do; **vaina** = thing, also a nuisance; **jincho** = drunk; **tombo** = police officer (casual); **cucho/a** = old man/old woman, father/mother (affectionate, not disrespectful); **chino/a** = boy/girl; **llave** = friend; **dar papaya** = to make yourself an easy target; **hacer un catorce** = to do a favor; **¿quiubo?** = what's up; **vieja** = woman (late teens and up); **man** = guy; **sapo** = snitch; **rumbear** = to party. **[V]** — [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/learn/spanish/spanish-tips/colombian-slang-expressions)
- **Paila** = interjection for "too bad / you're in trouble" (often with a throat-cut gesture); **tragado** = madly in love; **pinta** = good-looking, well-dressed man; **guachafita** = mess or disorder; **achicopalado** = down, sad; **abeja** = someone who always takes advantage. — [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/nacion/las-10-expresiones-colombianas-que-no-se-encuentran-en-el-diccionario)
- **Berraco/verraco**: the Diccionario de americanismos senses for Colombia are "persona valiente o audaz", "persona que se desempeña muy bien", "difícil de resolver", "disgustado" (angry). Both spellings are accepted. In Spain, "verraco" is a breeding boar. — [UDEP "Castellano actual"](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-verraco-o-berraco/); [RAE DHLE "verraco"](https://www.rae.es/dhle/verraco); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/que-significa-la-palabra-berraco-en-otros-paises-es-diferente-que-en-colombia-609834)
- **Gonorrea**: in Colombia, an insult for a contemptible or loathsome person, "an extremely strong thing to say". It took hold as an insult in the 1990s under the influence of mafia, sicario and barrio-gang speech (parlache). Among friends it can be playful, depending on tone. — [El Espectador column "Del papayo a la gonorrea"](https://www.elespectador.com/opinion/del-papayo-la-gonorrea-columna-823284); [Worldcrunch: how violence shaped Colombian Spanish](https://worldcrunch.com/in-the-news/tamales-to-gonorrhea-how-violence-shaped-colombian-spanish/amp/); [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/learn/spanish/spanish-tips/colombian-slang-expressions)
- **Chimba**: Wiktionary gives Colombia, colloquial: "an enjoyable, pleasurable or attractive thing" ("Ese carro está muy chimba"), also as an adverb (nicely, pleasantly). StoryLearning says it "should be used cautiously" and can be "considered vulgar in some contexts". **[P]**. The vulgar base sense (vulva) and the opposite sense of **chimbo** (fake, bad) were not checked in a dictionary (see Gaps). — [Wiktionary "chimba"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chimba); [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/learn/spanish/spanish-tips/colombian-slang-expressions)
- **Marica** as a friendly vocative among friends: Matador. A rights-focused source notes it is still a homophobic slur in other contexts. — [Matador Network (ES)](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/); [Alturi: "Furia Marica"](https://alturi.org/news_items/furia-marica-the-meaning-of-faggot-and-lgbt-rights-in-colombia)

### Inferences
- When rewriting, do not raise the register. Map Spain C → Colombian C and Spain Cr → Colombian Cr. Treat chimba, marica, gonorrea, huevón and corroncho as Cr/O outputs only, never as defaults.
- The app's explanations should flag false friends that confuse Spain learners: tinto, pelado, mono, chino, pitillo, patilla, amañarse, vale (coast), pola vs polla, berraco/verraco, regalar (to ask for something, not to gift it).

- "Luca": StoryLearning glosses it as "peso". The common modern sense, a thousand pesos ("diez lucas" = 10,000 COP), was not checked in a source.

## Verification of "ni de fundas", the Colombian "no way", "ya mismo", "qué pena", "a la orden"

### Takeaway
- **"Ni de fundas"**: no source in this session places it in Colombia. Searches of Mexican idiom collections did not find it either (the results did not mention it). The researcher's understanding is that it is Mexican slang ("no way"). Either way, it should **not** be used as a Colombian rewrite.
- **Colombian "no way"**: the safest documented form is **"ni de vainas"** (a Dicol headword). Common unchecked alternatives, by register: "¡qué va!", "ni loco/a" (C); "ni de riesgos", "ni por el chiras" ([U]); "ni por el putas", "ni por la chimba", "ni mierda" (Cr).
- **"Qué pena"**: checked. It means "excuse me / sorry", a softening formula, because "pena" = embarrassment (not sorrow, as in Spain).
- **"A la orden"**: checked as a service and politeness phrase ("you're welcome", "can I help you?", "at your service").
- **"Ya mismo"**: not checked in a dictionary. The evidence points to "right away / right now" in Colombia (not "later", as in Ecuador). "Ahoritica" = immediate; plain "ahorita" is ambiguous.

- **Qué pena**: in Colombia and elsewhere in Latin America, "pena" = vergüenza, not aflicción as in Spain. It is a very common courtesy formula used before bad news, refusals and bureaucratic "no"s, to approach someone, soften tension, get attention or apologize. Variants: "qué pena con usted / contigo / con vos / con sumercé". There is no written record of its origin; it does not appear in Rufino José Cuervo's 1872 book on Bogotá speech. — [Portafolio, with Gloria Viviana Nieto (Instituto Caro y Cuervo)](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/que-pena-con-usted-la-expresion-en-colombia-que-confunde-a-los-extranjeros-601258)
- Qué pena = "excuse me" / "sorry", also said by waiters approaching a table. — [Transparent Language blog](https://blogs.transparent.com/spanish/?p=9715); also [Infobae Colombia, 2025](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2025/02/05/me-regalas-se-usa-para-pedir-algo-estas-son-las-tres-expresiones-que-turista-espanol-adopto-tras-su-viaje-por-colombia/)
- To say "what a shame" in Colombia, people use "qué lástima", not "qué pena". — [How to Learn Spanish](https://howlearnspanish.com/?p=4475)
- **A la orden**: polite phrase used by staff, meaning "yes, sir", "may I help you?" or thanks for your business. — [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/learn/spanish/spanish-tips/colombian-slang-expressions)
- **Ya mismo / ya**: Colombian "ya" = now. "Ya mismo" is suggested as an immediate alternative to "ahorita". Servientrega's express service, branded "Ya Mismo", promises pickup and delivery in under 3 hours. **[P]** (blogs and forum posts only). — [filmadventure blog](https://filmadventure.blogspot.com/2011/03/word-for-now-in-colombia.html); [italki thread](https://www.italki.com/en/post/question-440378)
- **Ahoritica** (double diminutive) in Colombia expresses absolute immediacy ("pasó ahoritica mismo"). — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/gente/por-que-en-colombia-ahorita-no-significa-ahora-la-palabra-que-confunde-a-los-extranjeros-3524161)
- **Regalar** as a polite request verb ("¿me regalas un café?" means "can I have a coffee", which is paid for). — [Matador Network (ES)](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/); [Infobae Colombia](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2025/02/05/me-regalas-se-usa-para-pedir-algo-estas-son-las-tres-expresiones-que-turista-espanol-adopto-tras-su-viaje-por-colombia/)

### Inferences
- Rewrite rules:
  - Spain "perdona/disculpa" (as an opener) → "qué pena" (optionally "qué pena con usted").
  - Spain "¡qué pena!" (what a shame) → Colombian "¡qué lástima!". **Do not** leave "qué pena" in place for this sense: Colombians would read it as "how embarrassing / sorry".
  - Spain "de nada" → "con gusto" (default) or "a la orden".
  - Spain "ponme / dame un..." in a shop → "¿me regala un...?".
  - Spain "¡ni de coña!" / "¡ni hablar!" → "¡ni de vainas!" (C) or "¡ni loco!". Crude only if the source is crude ("¡ni de coña!" is mildly crude in Spain, so "ni de vainas" fits).
  - Spain "ahora mismo / ya" → "ya mismo" / "ya" / "ahoritica"; never "ahorita" when the meaning is immediate.
- Other expressions from the brief, with status:
  - "¿qué más?" = how's it going ([V]).
  - "¿bien o qué?" = greeting, "all good?" ([U]).
  - "hágale" = go ahead / OK / do it ([U]).
  - "sumercé" = respectful you (Boyacá, Cundinamarca) ([V]).
  - "pilas" = watch out / be alert / get moving; "ser pilo" = to be smart or studious ([U]).
  - "de una" = sure, right away ([V]).
