# Colombian sayings, idioms and set phrases compared with Spain's

Source notes. dle.rae.es returned HTTP 403 and the Centro Virtual Cervantes refranero (cvc.cervantes.es) redirected to a maintenance page, so neither could be checked directly. The **Diccionario de americanismos** (ASALE, asale.org/damer, "DA" below) did load and is the main authority used here. DA country codes: Co = Colombia, Ve = Venezuela, Ec = Ecuador, Mx = Mexico, RD = Dominican Republic, Ho = Honduras, Pa = Panama, Pe = Peru, Ch = Chile, Co:N = northern Colombia. DA register labels: pop = popular; pop + cult → espon = popular and educated speech, spontaneous use; vulg = vulgar; desp = pejorative; juv = youth slang. The DA sometimes lists a locution under one headword without a definition and defines it under another headword (for example "mamar gallo" is listed under *gallo* but defined under *mamar*). Where I could not find a definition, I say so.

## Which idioms are distinctly Colombian, which are pan-Hispanic, and which are Spain-only?

### Takeaway
A core set is lexicographically confirmed as Colombian, or Colombian plus nearby Caribbean and Andean countries: mamar(le) el gallo, en la olla, valer huevo, echar los perros, echar cepillo, lambón, pelado (meaning a youngster), bacán, and hacer el dos (meaning "do a favor", northern Colombia). Dar papaya and "papaya puesta, papaya partida" are the best-known Colombian sayings in Colombian media, but the DA lists "dar papaya" without a definition. Many items on the brief's list, such as meter la pata, ponerse las pilas, hablar hasta por los codos and a otro perro con ese hueso, are pan-Hispanic. Spain-only status could not be verified from an authoritative source for any item (DLE and CVC were unreachable).

### Cited findings

**Distinctly Colombian, or Colombian plus a few neighbours (DA-confirmed)**
- **mamarle el gallo (a alguien)**: DA, marked Ho, Co, Ve, register pop + cult → espon. Sense 1: "burlarse de alguien o tomarle el pelo" (to mock someone or pull their leg). Sense 2, Co only: to string someone along with evasive excuses or false promises (synonym *caramelear*). The bare form "mamar gallo" is given for RD; Colombian media also use "mamar gallo" without the article. **This is the Colombian equivalent of Spain's "tomar el pelo."** — [DA, mamar](https://www.asale.org/damer/mamar); [El Tiempo, "Qué significa la expresión mamar gallo"](https://www.eltiempo.com/colombia/otras-ciudades/que-significa-la-expresion-mamar-gallo-y-de-donde-viene-508172); [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **Origin of mamar gallo**: El Tiempo reports that several Colombian and Venezuelan scholars trace it to cockfighting pits (*galleras*). It is a folk etymology with no consensus. — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/colombia/otras-ciudades/que-significa-la-expresion-mamar-gallo-y-de-donde-viene-508172)
- **(estar) en la olla**: DA, a locución adjetiva. Sense 1, RD and Co, pop: said of a person, short of money (synonym *en rines*). Sense 2, Co, pop: depressed, with no energy for anything. Psicología y Mente glosses "estar en la olla" as being in a very difficult financial or personal situation. — [DA, olla](https://www.asale.org/damer/olla); [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas)
- **valer huevo** (in everyday use, "me vale huevo"): DA, Co and Ec, **vulg**: "importar algo muy poco o nada a alguien" (to care little or nothing about something). The DA has no separate entry for the form "me vale huevo." **This is a Colombian equivalent of Spain's "me importa un pimiento / un bledo", at a much cruder register.** "Valer madre" and "valer verga" are marked for Mexico and Central America, not for Colombia. "Valer un culo" is marked only for Bolivia, with the sense "be worthless", so the DA does not support "me importa un culo" as a Colombian form. — [DA, valer](https://www.asale.org/damer/valer)
- **echar(le) los perros (a alguien)**: DA, marked Mx, Gu, Ni, Pa, PR, Co, Ve, Ch: "cortejar a alguien" (to court someone), with a rural label and register pop + cult → espon. In Honduras the same phrase means to insult or scold. "Tirar los perros" is listed under *perro* without a definition, and Psicología y Mente lists both forms meaning to flirt. — [DA, echar](https://www.asale.org/damer/echar); [DA, perro](https://www.asale.org/damer/perro); [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas)
- **echar cepillo**: DA, Co, pop: to flatter (cross-referenced to *cepillar*). **This is a Colombian counterpart to Spain's "hacer la pelota" (to suck up to someone).** — [DA, echar](https://www.asale.org/damer/echar)
- **lambón, -na**: DA. Sense 1: flatterer, marked Pa, RD, PR, Co, Ec, pop and desp. Sense 2, Co only: informer, a "sapo." **This is the Colombian word for Spain's "pelota / pelotero" (a suck-up).** — [DA, lambón](https://www.asale.org/damer/lamb%C3%B3n)
- **pelado / pelao**: DA, marked Ho, Pa, Co, pop: a young person or lad. Also marked Co, Ec, Bo, pop: a child or boy. Matador warns that in Colombia "pelao" means a young person, not a bald one. The DA gives the "broke" sense only in Ecuadorian and Honduran similes ("más pelado que pepa de huaba", "más pelado que rabo de chancho"). **So for Colombia, the DA-confirmed sense of "pelado" is "a kid", not "broke".** — [DA, pelado](https://www.asale.org/damer/pelado); [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **bacán, -na**: DA, marked Cu, RD, **Co**, Pe: "persona agradable, amigable" (a pleasant, friendly person), labeled juvenile and popular. The sense "excellent, cool" is marked Cu, Ec, Pe, Ch, **not Co**. The DA also records a Colombian use as a friendly term of address. Matador gives "bacán" as an alternative to "chévere", the Colombian equivalent of Spain's "guay" or "mola." — [DA, bacán](https://www.asale.org/damer/bac%C3%A1n); [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **hacer el dos**: DA, Co:N (northern Colombia): to do someone a favor. In Nicaragua, Cuba, Bolivia, Costa Rica, Peru and Mexico ("hacer del dos") it is a euphemism for defecating, so it is risky outside Colombia. Psicología y Mente also glosses it as "do someone a favor." — [DA, hacer](https://www.asale.org/damer/hacer); [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas)
- **echar(se) dedo**: DA, Co, pop: to hitchhike. — [DA, echar](https://www.asale.org/damer/echar)
- **de papaya**: DA, Co, pop, a locución adverbial meaning "oportunamente" (at the right moment, by luck). — [DA, papaya](https://www.asale.org/damer/papaya)

**Distinctly Colombian, from Colombian media and learner sources (not defined in DA or not checked)**
- **dar papaya / no dar papaya**: to expose yourself through carelessness so that someone takes advantage. It is mostly used as a warning in the negative ("no dé papaya"). Telemedellín calls the resulting saying a "mandamiento nacional." The DA lists "dar ~" under *papaya* but gives no definition. A low-quality Q&A site attributes Co/Ec/Ve marks to the DA; **treat those country marks as unverified**. — [Telemedellín](https://telemedellin.tv/dichos-colombianos-mas-populares/); [DA, papaya](https://www.asale.org/damer/papaya); [Le Petit Journal Bogotá](https://lepetitjournal.com/bogota/education/no-entiendo-guide-des-expressions-colombiennes-66775)
- **a papaya puesta, papaya partida**: the proverb built on "dar papaya" (if you leave it out, someone will take it). Lifeder gives the variant "a papaya partida, papaya servida." The origin is not documented. — [Telemedellín](https://telemedellin.tv/dichos-colombianos-mas-populares/); [Lifeder](https://www.lifeder.com/frases-palabras-colombia/)
- **hacer una vaca / hacer la vaca**: to pool money among several people, for a group outing or to help someone. The DA lists "hacer una ~", "hacer ~" and "hacerse la ~" under *vaca* without definitions or country marks. On the DA *hacer* page, Peru's "hacerse la vaca" means to skip class (student slang), so **"hacer la vaca" is ambiguous across countries; prefer "hacer una vaca" for Colombia.** — [Telemedellín](https://telemedellin.tv/dichos-colombianos-mas-populares/); [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas); [DA, vaca](https://www.asale.org/damer/vaca); [DA, hacer](https://www.asale.org/damer/hacer)
- **más perdido que el hijo de Lindbergh** (also spelled "Limber"): completely lost or clueless. It comes from the 1932 kidnapping of Charles Lindbergh's son. A USC folklore archive records it from Colombian informants, and Diario Libre treats it as Dominican. **So it is Caribbean and Colombian, not Colombia-only.** — [USC Folklore Archives](https://folklore.usc.edu/esta-mas-perdido-que-el-hijo-de-lindbergh/); [Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/actualidad/mas-perdido-que-el-hijo-de-lindbergh-popular-frase-del-espanol-dominicano-sobre-un-secuestro-CA24710031); [Speaking Latino](https://www.speakinglatino.com/word/estar-mas-perdido-que-el-hijo-de-lindbergh-limber)
- **Other Colombian dichos Telemedellín lists as popular**:
  - "más falso que billete de tres mil" (fake, untrustworthy)
  - "lo dejaron viendo un chispero" (left disappointed)
  - "cuentas claras y el chocolate espeso" (keep money matters clear)
  - "tengo un filo que si me agacho me corto" (I'm starving; paisa, i.e. from Antioquia)
  - "le falta pelo pa' moña" (too green or inexperienced for the task)
  - "colgó los guayos" (he died; *guayos* are football boots)
  - "¡serán guayabas!" (sarcastic disbelief)
  - "salió con un chorro de babas" (all talk, disappointing)
  - "la última Coca-Cola del desierto" (thinks he's God's gift)
  - "póngase trucha" (be alert)
  - "estar en la jugada" (be alert)
  - "pa' mañana es tarde" (it's urgent)
  - "le digo el milagro pero no el santo" (I'll tell the gossip but not who)
  — [Telemedellín](https://telemedellin.tv/dichos-colombianos-mas-populares/)
- **Colombian colloquial set phrases from learner and media lists**:
  - comerse el cuento (believe a lie)
  - parar bolas / parar bola (pay attention)
  - embarrarla (mess up; a Colombian counterpart of "meter la pata")
  - meter la cucharada (butt in)
  - dar lora (talk endlessly)
  - estar tragado / clavado (be head over heels)
  - poner los cachos (cheat on a partner; Spain says "poner los cuernos")
  - desayunar alacrán (wake up grumpy)
  - echarle mente (think about it)
  - ¡qué oso! / ¡qué boleta! (how embarrassing)
  - ¡qué vaina! / ¡qué embarrada! (what a shame)
  - ¡paila! (too bad, we're done for)
  - ¡pilas! / estar moscas (watch out)
  - hacerse el loco (pretend not to notice to dodge responsibility)
  — [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas); [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang); [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **hacerse el loco**: the DA records it with an Mx mark under "hacérsele al loco", and "hacerse el chivo loco" for Pa, Cu, RD, PR. Colombian lists include it as well, which **suggests it is pan-American rather than distinctly Colombian.** — [DA, hacer](https://www.asale.org/damer/hacer); [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas)

**Pan-Hispanic, or shared with Spain**
- **ponerse las pilas**: "look alive, get your act together." It is used across Latin America and in Spain. The short form "ponte pilas" and the interjection "¡pilas!" are typically Colombian, Ecuadorian and Peruvian. — [Univext](https://univext.com/es/resources/spanish/words/4525/ponerse-las-pilas); [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **Shared with Spain**: Telemedellín's "Colombian" list includes "habla hasta por los codos", "a otro perro con ese hueso", "Dios le da pan al que no tiene dientes", "lo que no mata, engorda", "apague y vámonos", "espere sentado", "de eso tan bueno no dan tanto" and "mear fuera del tiesto". My own knowledge (no source checked) is that these are general Spanish refranes also known in Spain. Their presence on the list shows that Colombian "dichos" lists mix in pan-Hispanic material. — [Telemedellín](https://telemedellin.tv/dichos-colombianos-mas-populares/)
- **Colombian speakers use more set phrases**: a Spanish creator living in Colombia said that people in Spain don't use as many dichos in daily life as Colombians do. This is a single anecdotal opinion. — [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/sociedad/un-espanol-se-enamoro-de-estos-dichos-colombianos-me-encantan-rs15)

**Spain-marked expressions (from sources about Spain, with no explicit statement about Colombia)**
- "estar como una cabra" (to be crazy) and "(no) importar un pimiento / un pepino / un rábano" (not to care at all) appear in guides to Peninsular Spanish idioms. Other Spain-flavoured items in the same guides: "estar hasta las narices", "comerse el coco", "ir a freír espárragos", "me la suda", "flipar". **None of the sources found states explicitly that these are unknown in Colombia.** — [donQuijote](https://www.donquijote.org/blog/spanish-idiomatic-expressions/); [Lingoda](https://www.lingoda.com/blog/es/40-expresiones-populares-en-espanol); [Psicología y Mente, Spanish expressions](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-espanolas)
- Matador Network says Spaniards in Colombia stop saying "guay" and "mola" and switch to "chévere." It also gives "qué tal, tío" as the Spain counterpart of the Colombian greeting "¿qué más?", and "tío / colega" as counterparts of the Colombian friendly "marica." — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/)
- **Words that change meaning between Spain and Colombia** (word level, but these words appear inside idioms):
  - *bruto*: rough or brusque in Spain; stupid in Colombia.
  - *pena*: sorrow in Spain; embarrassment in Colombia, as in "qué pena con usted", which is used as an apology.
  - *ahorita*: not used in Spain.
  - "córrete un poco": has an awkward sexual reading in Spain.
  — [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/gente/por-que-un-espanol-le-puede-decir-esto-a-otro-y-no-pasa-nada-colombiana-en-espana-3508533); [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2025/11/04/segun-influencer-estas-son-las-cosas-que-un-colombiano-no-puede-decir-en-espana-para-evitar-mal-entendidos/); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tendencias/sociales/que-pena-con-usted-la-expresion-en-colombia-que-confunde-a-los-extranjeros-601258)

### Inferences
- The DA-confirmed Colombian items, which are the strongest candidates for an app's "Colombia mode," are: mamarle el gallo, en la olla, valer huevo, echar los perros, echar cepillo, lambón, pelado (kid), bacán (nice person), echar dedo and hacer el dos. Dar papaya and hacer una vaca are confirmed by multiple Colombian media sources but not defined in the DA.
- Several of these are shared with Venezuela, Ecuador or the Caribbean, so "Colombian" in practice often means "Colombian and Caribbean/Andean."
- Register matters. "Valer huevo" is vulgar in the DA, while "me importa un pimiento" in Spain is mild, so a straight substitution raises the register. A milder Colombian rendering is probably needed; see Gaps.
- "hacer el dos" and "hacer la vaca" are false friends across Latin America (defecate, skip class), so an app should not show them as general "Latin American" phrases.

### Gaps
- DLE (dle.rae.es) returned 403 and the CVC refranero was in maintenance, so no item could be confirmed as Spain-only by an authority. Spain-only status rests on guides to Spanish idioms and on general knowledge.
- The DA defines "dar papaya" nowhere I could reach. The country marks attributed to it (Co, Ec, Ve) come from an unreliable site.
- I found no source for "el que peca y reza empata" as a regional saying. Only a Catholic.net commentary and a Colombian columnist's piece in Occidente.co (Cali) use it, which weakly suggests Colombian use but proves nothing ([Catholic.net](https://es.catholic.net/op/articulos/70035/el-que-peca-y-reza-empata.html); [Occidente](https://occidente.co/opinion/columnistas/el-que-peca-reza-y-empata/)). Its region and register remain unverified.
- No source was retrieved for these items on the brief's list: "camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente", "estar de malas", "la vida no es color de rosa", "eso es pan comido", "como Pedro por su casa", "ser uña y carne", "meter la pata". From general knowledge they are all pan-Hispanic, and "estar de malas" (to be unlucky) is very common in Colombia, but none of this is verified.
- "estar pelado" meaning broke: one dictionary glosses it as "to be broke" ([Tureng](https://tureng.com/en/spanish-english/estar%2520pelado)), but I found no source tying that sense to Colombia. The DA gives "pelado" in Colombia only as "kid." Flag as uncertain.
- I found no source on "hacer cola" (Spain) versus "hacer fila" (Colombia). This is general knowledge, not verified here.
- I found no source on "estar en el paro" (Spain) versus "estar desempleado / sin trabajo / sin camello" (Colombia). This is general knowledge, not verified here. "Camello" meaning work is attested for Colombia by Psicología y Mente, but not as part of a set phrase about unemployment ([Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas)). "Varado" meaning broke or stuck: not verified.
- I found no source on a Colombian equivalent of "estar como una cabra." Candidates not verified here: "estar loco / chiflado", "le falta un tornillo", "estar corrido".
- "me importa un culo" as Colombian: not supported. The DA's "valer un culo" is marked only for Bolivia.

## For Spain-only idioms that an app might produce, what would a Colombian say instead?

### Takeaway
For a handful of Spain idioms there is a sourced Colombian replacement:
- tomar el pelo → mamar gallo / mamarle el gallo a alguien
- hacer la pelota / pelota → echar cepillo / lambón
- me importa un pimiento → me vale huevo (vulgar)
- guay / mola → chévere / bacano
- poner los cuernos → poner los cachos
- meter la pata → embarrarla (meter la pata is also understood)
- hacer un fondo común → hacer una vaca
- resaca → guayabo

Most other replacements, such as hacer cola → hacer fila, estar en el paro → estar desempleado, and estar como una cabra → estar loco, are plausible but were not verified in this round.

### Cited findings (Spain form → Colombian form)
- **tomar el pelo → mamar gallo / mamarle el gallo (a alguien)**: DA, Ho, Co, Ve. The DA definition itself uses "tomarle el pelo." Related Colombian nouns "mamadera de gallo" and "mamagallista" were not found in the DA. — [DA, mamar](https://www.asale.org/damer/mamar); [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/colombia/otras-ciudades/que-significa-la-expresion-mamar-gallo-y-de-donde-viene-508172)
- **hacer la pelota / ser un pelota → echar cepillo / ser lambón**: DA, Co, pop (lambón is also marked desp). — [DA, echar](https://www.asale.org/damer/echar); [DA, lambón](https://www.asale.org/damer/lamb%C3%B3n)
- **me importa un pimiento / un bledo / me la suda → me vale huevo**: DA "valer huevo", Co and Ec, vulg. Use it only at a vulgar register. — [DA, valer](https://www.asale.org/damer/valer)
- **ligar / tirar los tejos → echar (o tirar) los perros, caerle a alguien**: DA "echar los perros", Co and others; "caer" is in BaseLang and Psicología y Mente. "Tirar los tejos" as a Spain idiom is general knowledge, not sourced here. — [DA, echar](https://www.asale.org/damer/echar); [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **¡qué guay! / ¡mola! → ¡qué chévere! / ¡qué bacano!** — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/). Note that the DA marks "bacán" as an adjective meaning "excellent" for Cu, Ec, Pe, Ch, not Co ([DA, bacán](https://www.asale.org/damer/bac%C3%A1n)). Matador's "bacán" for Colombia is a looser claim, and the common Colombian form "bacano" was not checked in the DA.
- **poner los cuernos → poner los cachos**: Colombian, per BaseLang. — [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **meter la pata → embarrarla** (also "¡qué embarrada!" for "what a shame"). — [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas); [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **estar sin blanca / estar a dos velas → estar en la olla**: DA, Co, pop. — [DA, olla](https://www.asale.org/damer/olla)
- **poner un bote / hacer un fondo → hacer una vaca**. — [Telemedellín](https://telemedellin.tv/dichos-colombianos-mas-populares/)
- **resaca → guayabo / estar enguayabado**. — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/); [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **¡qué vergüenza! → ¡qué oso! / ¡qué boleta!** — [BaseLang](https://baselang.com/blog/vocabulary/colombian-idioms-and-slang)
- **hacer autostop / hacer dedo → echar dedo**: DA, Co. — [DA, echar](https://www.asale.org/damer/echar)
- **qué tal, tío → ¿qué más?, parce / parcero**. — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/es/11-palabras-y-expresiones-colombianas-que-confundiran-un-espanol-si-o-si/); [Psicología y Mente](https://psicologiaymente.com/reflexiones/palabras-expresiones-colombianas)

### Inferences
- An app's Colombia mode can safely swap tomar el pelo → mamar gallo, hacer la pelota → echar cepillo, guay → chévere and resaca → guayabo, since they are close in register.
- "me importa un pimiento" → "me vale huevo" raises the register from mild to vulgar. A neutral option such as "no me importa para nada" would be a safer default, with "me vale huevo" shown as a colloquial or vulgar alternative. This is my recommendation, not a sourced finding.
- Pan-Hispanic idioms (meter la pata, ponerse las pilas, pan comido, ser uña y carne, como Pedro por su casa) probably need no substitution, but this is unverified.

### Gaps
- Unverified swaps:
  - hacer cola → hacer fila
  - estar en el paro → estar desempleado / sin trabajo / sin camello
  - estar como una cabra → estar loco / le falta un tornillo
  - estar en el quinto pino → (Colombian equivalent unknown)
  - mala leche (Spain: bad mood or ill will) → possibly "mala clase" or "mala leche" meaning bad luck in Colombia
  - ponerse las botas → (Colombian equivalent unknown)
  - flipar → (Colombian equivalent unknown)
  - estar hasta las narices → (Colombian equivalent unknown)

  None of these could be confirmed with a source in this round; they need checking against the DLE (marca "Esp.") and the DA, both of which should be retried when available.
- I found no academic paremiology study comparing Colombia and Spain directly. An Instituto Caro y Cuervo source was not reached.
