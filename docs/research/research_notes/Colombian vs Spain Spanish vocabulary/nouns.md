# Everyday nouns that differ between Colombian Spanish (Bogotá / Medellín / Cali) and Spain's Spanish

**How these notes were verified (read first).**
- **dle.rae.es returned HTTP 403** this session, so no DLE "Col." marks could be checked. The ASALE Diccionario de americanismos and the Caro y Cuervo *Diccionario de colombianismos* (2018) were not reachable as searchable text either.
- **ICC** = *Hecho en Colombia: Glosario de aprendizaje del español de Colombia*, by Gloria Viviana Nieto Martín. It was published by the Instituto Caro y Cuervo (Bogotá, 2017; ISBN 978-958-611-347-2) and read in full as text from the PDF mirror at [todoele.net](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-06/Hecho%20en%20Colombia-glosario.pdf). Its labels: **Co.** = "colombianismo: uso exclusivo de Colombia"; **Am.** = "americanismo usado en Colombia". This is the strongest source used. A **Co.** word is by the glossary's own definition not shared with Mexico.
- **WK** = English Wiktionary entries, read via the MediaWiki API (wikitext of the Spanish section, regional `lb` labels). Lookup pattern: `https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/<word>`. Wiktionary is a secondary, crowd-edited source.
- **TH** = De Checchi (2023), a Universidad de Padova MA thesis with a translation questionnaire ([PDF](https://thesis.unipd.it/retrieve/be868b08-c9be-48e6-8bb0-5387ed6d5776/DeChecchi_Janette.pdf)). **Caveat:** its four Colombian informants are 1 from Barranquilla and 3 from Cartagena. They are all **costeño**, not Bogotá/Medellín/Cali, and the sample is tiny. It is used only as supporting evidence. Counts are given as n/4.
- **EX** = [expat.com "Modismos en Colombia"](https://www.expat.com/es/guia/america-del-sur/colombia/13941-modismos-en-colombia.html), a travel guide (weak; used as corroboration only).
- **R** = the researcher's own knowledge, not verified by a source this session. R claims are kept out of the table and listed under Gaps.
- Strength codes: **W** = the Spain word means something else in Colombia, or is misread (must swap); **O** = understood but sounds Spanish or odd (should swap); **L** = both used, the Colombian word is just more usual (optional swap); **K** = keep (do not swap).
- MX column: compared against the sibling file `docs/research/research_notes/Mexican vs Spain Spanish vocabulary/nouns.md` ("MXN"). "=MX" means Colombia and Mexico share the word; otherwise the Mexican word is given.

## Which Spain → Colombia noun pairs hold, by domain?

### Takeaway
About 100 pairs have at least one dictionary-type source (ICC and/or Wiktionary). The firmest must-swap items are: ordenador→computador, móvil→celular, coche→carro, zumo→jugo, patata→papa, bombilla→bombillo, pajita→pitillo, calcetín→media, gafas (keep), biberón→tetero, bolígrafo→esfero/lapicero, grapadora→cosedora, aparcamiento→parqueadero, carné de conducir→pase, maletero→baúl, acera→andén, café solo→tinto, palomitas→crispetas, bocadillo→sándwich, resaca→guayabo, currículum→hoja de vida, DNI→cédula, mascarilla→tapabocas.

## What gender is each Colombian word, and where does the article change?

### Takeaway
Most pairs keep the gender, but about 25 flip it. The app's article logic must take gender from the Colombian word. Colombia also prefers masculine *-dor/-o* forms where Mexico uses feminine forms (el computador vs la computadora).

### Cited Findings
- Gender m→f (Spain m → Colombia f): calcetín→**la media** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/media)); frigorífico→**la nevera** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/nevera)); refresco→**la gaseosa** (ICC); guisante→**la arveja** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/arveja)); neumático→**la llanta** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/llanta)); intermitente→**la direccional** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/direccional)); currículum→**la hoja de vida** (ICC); DNI→**la cédula** (ICC); cubo→**la caneca** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/caneca)); microbús→**la buseta** (ICC); pantalón corto→**la pantaloneta** (ICC); estuche→**la cartuchera** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cartuchera)); carné de conducir→**la licencia** (if *pase* is not chosen).
- Gender f→m (Spain f → Colombia m): bombilla→**el bombillo** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bombillo)); pajita→**el pitillo** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pitillo)); acera→**el andén** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/and%C3%A9n)); sudadera→**el buzo** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/buzo)); gamba→**el camarón** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/camar%C3%B3n)); encimera→**el mesón** (ICC); lejía→**el blanqueador** (ICC); resaca→**el guayabo** (ICC); mascarilla→**el tapabocas** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tapabocas)); americana→**el saco** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/saco)); zapatillas→**los tenis** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tenis)); judía/alubia→**el fríjol** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fr%C3%ADjol)); tarta→**el ponqué** (if *ponqué* is chosen; *la torta* keeps f).
- Masculine Colombian words often assumed feminine: **el computador** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/computador), m; TH 4/4 CO vs "computadora en femenino" elsewhere); **el esfero**, **el lapicero** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/esfero)); **el bombillo**; **el pitillo**; **el tetero**; **el baúl**; **el celular** (ICC m.); **el pase**.
- Invariable singular/plural nouns (don't add -s): **el/los tapabocas**, **el/los lavaplatos**, **el/los lavamanos**, **los pasabocas** ([WK lavaplatos](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/lavaplatos); [WK tapabocas](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tapabocas)).
- Plural-only or usually plural: **las onces** (snack), **las crispetas**, **los tenis**, **los audífonos**, **las vueltas** (change).
- Gender-by-sense trap: **el parte** (traffic fine, ICC "parte m. Am. Multa… le ponen un parte") vs **la parte** (part).
- Source conflict: ICC prints "**caneca m.** Am. Contenedor cilíndrico de plástico" but Wiktionary gives *caneca* as **f** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/caneca)). ICC is probably a typo; usage is *la caneca* (R).
- Person nouns with m/f pairs: mesero/mesera, celador/celadora, niñero/niñera, plomero/plomera ([WK mesero](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mesero) "m|f=+").

### Inferences
- A rewrite rule should store the target article with each Colombian noun (e.g. `calcetín → la media`, `los calcetines → las medias`). Adjectives agreeing with the noun (*unos calcetines blancos* → *unas medias blancas*) must be re-inflected too.

### Gaps
- Gender of *bómper* (m, R), *bluyín* (m, R) and *chupo* (m, R) was not verified.

## Which Spain words are risky, misread or rude in Colombia?

### Takeaway
The main danger is not vulgarity but misreading. Spain words that exist in Colombia with another everyday meaning: *bocadillo* (guava paste), *coche* (baby stroller), *plátano* (plantain), *tinto* (coffee), *torta* (cake, not slap), *paro* (strike), *droguería* (pharmacy), *pitillo* (straw), *celador* (watchman), *piso* (floor), *chupete* (lollipop elsewhere in LatAm). A few Spain everyday words carry vulgar or crude senses in Colombia (*bollo*, *paja*, *turma*, *zorra*).

### Cited Findings
- **bocadillo**: Spain = sandwich; Colombia/Venezuela/Panama = guava paste ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bocadillo)); ICC "bocadillo ~ veleño Co. Dulce compacto de guayaba".
- **coche**: Spain = car; Colombia = baby carriage, as an ellipsis of *coche de niño* ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/coche)).
- **plátano**: banana in Spain, Mexico, Peru and others, but not labelled for Colombia, where *banano* = banana ([WK plátano](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pl%C3%A1tano); [WK banano](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/banano)).
- **tinto**: Colombia = black coffee (ICC "Co."; [WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tinto)). A Spain "¿un tinto?" (red wine) reads as coffee.
- **torta**: Spain = flatbread; (coll.) slap. Colombia = tart/pie/cake ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/torta)).
- **paro**: Spain = unemployment ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/paro)); TH: no American informant used it for unemployment.
- **droguería**: in Colombia = pharmacy (CCB document, search summary). In Spain it is a household goods shop (search-summary claim, R).
- **pitillo**: Spain coll. = cigarette, and skinny-fit trousers; Colombia = drinking straw ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pitillo)).
- **chupete**: Wiktionary marks "Canary, Latin America" = lollipop, beside the general pacifier sense ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chupete)).
- **celador**: watchman/guard, and porter/orderly ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/celador)).
- **bombillo**: Spain = lock cylinder; Colombia = light bulb ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bombillo)).
- **pimentón**: Colombia = bell pepper ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/piment%C3%B3n)); in Spain it is the paprika spice (R).
- Crude or vulgar Colombian senses of words that Spain uses neutrally (all ICC):
  - "**bollo** m. Co. vulg. Porción compacta de excremento". Spain *bollo* = bun/pastry, so never leave "un bollo" in Colombian output; use *pan dulce* / *pastel* (R).
  - "**paja** f. … Am. inf. Mentira". In both countries it is also a vulgar word for masturbation (sibling MXN).
  - "**turma** f. Co. pop. 1 Testículo. 2 Persona tonta".
  - "**zorra** f. Co. Carro de madera de dos ruedas tirado por un caballo". In Spain it is vixen / a vulgar insult.
  - "**arepera** f. Am. vulg. Lesbiana" (not a Spain word, but a trap for generated text).
  - "**joda** f. vulg. Co. Cosa".
- *Bombilla* has a vulgar colloquial sense (penis) in Wiktionary, region unspecified ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bombilla)). *Gamba* is Spain slang for a "butterface" ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gamba)).
- *Canguro* (babysitter) is Spain colloquial only ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/canguro)). In Colombia it reads as kangaroo or bum bag (TH 2/4 "cangurera", 1 "koala").

### Inferences
- A misread swap is worse than an untouched Spain word. The app should prioritise the W rows (bocadillo, coche, plátano, paro, canguro, piso-as-flat) even before O rows.
- *Bollo* should be treated as a must-replace in Colombian output, even though it is harmless in Spain.

### Gaps
- No source was found this session for the commonly cited Colombian taboo readings of *coger* (R: neutral in Colombia, unlike Mexico/Argentina), *cuca*, *chucha* (R: body odour in CO), *polla* or *concha*. They are left unclassified.
- Colombian use of *paro* = strike (*paro nacional*, *paro armado*) is R. It is very common in El Tiempo and Semana headlines, but no page was opened.

## Which swaps are sense-dependent and should be skipped, and which Spain words do Colombians also use?

### Takeaway
Skip blind word-level swaps for: piso, manzana, billete, matrícula, mando, carné, cocina, saco/jersey context, judía, pendiente(s), vaquero(s), lavabo, compra/mercado, batido, resaca, gasolinera (→bomba), plátano macho, medias, chapa, canguro. Keep: gafas, nevera, dinero (formal), multa, farmacia, rotonda, fontanero, habitación (hotels), cerveza, rubio, autobús (formal), maíz, fresa, piña, albaricoque, ensalada, falda, camiseta, portátil.

### Cited Findings
- *Piso* = flat only in Spain ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/piso)); it also = floor (EX "piso: suelo").
- *Manzana* = apple; block = *cuadra* in LatAm ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cuadra)).
- *Billete* = ticket only in Spain/Philippines ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/billete)); otherwise banknote.
- *Matrícula* = plate in Spain, but registration and tuition generally ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/matr%C3%ADcula)).
- *Mando* = remote only in Spain; also command ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mando)).
- *Carné* = identity card; driving licence only as an informal ellipsis ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/carn%C3%A9)).
- *Judía* = green bean / bean, and also Jewish woman ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/jud%C3%ADa)).
- *Pendiente* = earring (m), slope (f), pending (adj.) ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pendiente)).
- *Vaquero* = cowboy; jeans only in the plural ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/vaquero)).
- *Saco* is a suit jacket in LatAm ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/saco)) and a sweater in Colombia (EX), but also a sack.
- *Bomba* = petrol station in Colombia, but also firecracker, bomb, pump ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bomba)).
- *Gaseosa* is labelled for both Colombia **and** Spain ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gaseosa)), so Spain output "gaseosa" needs no change.
- *Fontanero* is labelled for Spain, Mexico **and Colombia** ([WK](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fontanero)), so it is not a firm swap.
- Spain words that Colombian (costeño) informants also chose (TH): *gafas* 4/4; *rotonda* 2/4; *fresa*, *piña*, *albaricoque* (all informants); *falda*; *papel higiénico*; *gasolina*; *dobladillo* 2/4; *paso de cebra* 2/4; *aparcamiento* 1/4; *nevera* (also Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia).
- *Mascarilla* is still used in Colombian retail (Falabella CO category "Mascarillas", [link](https://www.falabella.com.co/falabella-co/category/CATG32898/Mascarillas)), so it is not wrong, just less common than *tapabocas*.
- *Farmacia* is understood and used by chains, e.g. Farmatodo and Cruz Verde (search results; R). *Droguería* is the everyday shop word (CCB).
- *Dinero* is the formal word. *Plata* is "Am." colloquial (ICC); TH costeño speakers chose plata.
- *Cerveza* stays. *Pola* is "Co. inf." (ICC).

### Inferences
- Rules should be phrase-anchored. Examples: `carné de conducir → pase de conducción`; `judías verdes → habichuelas`; `a N manzanas → a N cuadras`; `billete de avión/tren/autobús → tiquete / pasaje`; `cocina de gas/eléctrica → estufa`; `estar en (el) paro → estar desempleado`; `piso + (alquilar|comprar|de N habitaciones) → apartamento`.
- When the MT output already contains a pan-Hispanic word (nevera, gaseosa, multa, rotonda, gafas), leave it.

### Gaps
- No corpus frequency (CORPES XXI country filter) was checked to quantify "less common" vs "odd". The strength codes are therefore judgments from the dictionary labels plus the small survey.

## How do the Colombian words differ from the Mexican ones?

### Takeaway
Colombia shares the pan-American words with Mexico (carro, celular, papa, jugo, durazno, camarón, cobija, clóset, tina, llanta, curita, aretes, brasier, tenis, control, audífonos, mesero, cuadra, direccional, marcador). On many everyday items it has its own word, or keeps the Spain one where Mexico doesn't. A Mexico-mode table must not be reused for Colombia.

- The ICC labels many of these **Co.** = "uso exclusivo de Colombia": tinto, cédula, aromática, perico, algo (snack), trasteo, pola, muchacha de servicio. By definition these are not Mexican.
- Wiktionary labels several as Colombia plus Venezuela/Ecuador only (tetero, pitillo, trancón, esfero), not Mexico.
- Mexican words that a Mexico-mode rewrite inserts, and that are wrong or odd in Colombia: *camión* (bus; in CO only truck, ICC "camión de trasteos"), *popote*, *foco*, *cajuela*, *banqueta*, *pluma*, *computadora* (f), *engrapadora*, *cruda*, *chícharo*, *ejote*, *cubrebocas*, *departamento* (in CO = an administrative region, ICC "departamento m. Co. Cada una de las treinta y dos divisiones del territorio colombiano").

### Inferences
- *Departamento* is the clearest case where a Mexico-mode rule is actively wrong for Colombia. Mexico's "apartment" word means "province/state" in Colombia (ICC). The same goes for *camión* = bus.
- Colombia and Mexico both differ from Spain on the same concepts, but often with different target words. So the Colombia table should be its own mapping from Spain, not derived from the Mexican one.

### Gaps
- The Mexican values come from the sibling notes, which are themselves partly R-level for some rows (e.g. ejote, pesero). They were not re-verified here.
- No source checked whether Colombians use *computadora* (f) as a variant. Speakers report both, but TH's 4/4 is costeño and very small.
