## 1. What do Butt & Benjamin (ch. 34), NGLE §29.8, DPD and Bosque & Demonte list? Where do they disagree?

### Takeaway
I could only partly confirm what the four reference works say. Butt & Benjamin cover the topic in **§34.14**: §34.14.2 gives the main uses of *para*, §34.14.2(d) is opinion or personal standard (*para mí*), and §34.14.8 is the main differences between *por* and *para*. The DPD (in its entry *estar*) says *estar para* + infinitive means 'about to', with *estar por* used for this in parts of Latin America. It says *estar por* + infinitive means 'still to be done'. I could not read the NGLE §29.8 text or Bosque & Demonte (GDLE) directly. So I cannot report any disagreements between the four works from their own wording.

### Cited Findings
- **Butt & Benjamin structure [S]:** an index of *A New Reference Grammar of Modern Spanish* lists §34.14 as the main *para/por* entry. §34.14.2 is the main uses of *para* (purpose, object, destination) and §34.14.8 is the key differences between the two. — [idoc.pub index, search snippet; page now dead](https://idoc.pub/documents/spanish-a-new-reference-grammar-of-modern-butt-benjaminpdf-2nv8gjqzeylk)
- **B&B §34.14.2(d) [S]:** *para* marks an opinion or personal standard (*para mí* 'in my opinion / for me'). Section numbers stay the same across editions; page numbers do not. The 6th ed. (2019) is by Butt, Benjamin & Moreira Rodríguez. — [WordReference thread citing B&B](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/too-expensive-for-me-para-por.3336128/); [dokumen.pub listing, 6th ed.](https://dokumen.pub/a-new-reference-grammar-of-modern-spanish-sixth-edition-9781138124004-1138124001-9781138124011-113812401x.html)
- **DPD, s.v. *estar* [S]:**
  - *estar para* + infinitive means 'to be about to / ready to', e.g. *¿Está para llover?* 'Is it about to rain?'. The DPD adds that some American countries also use *estar por* here.
  - *estar por* + infinitive has two senses in general Spanish. (1) Something is still pending, e.g. *La gran biografía de Rodolfo Usigli aún está por escribirse* 'The great biography of Rodolfo Usigli is still to be written'. (2) A future sense, e.g. *Es martes y está por llover* 'It's Tuesday and it's about to rain'. For sense (2) the DPD says *estar para* is the more general form.
  - Source: [DPD *estar*; 403 on fetch, quoted via search summary](https://www.rae.es/dpd/estar)
- **NGLE (RAE grammar pages) [S]:** treats *por* + infinitive with transitive verbs mainly as a passive-like construction, as in *asuntos que aún están por decidir* 'matters still to be decided'. It notes that in some American varieties *estar* + infinitive alternates between *por* and *para*: *El sol está {por ~ para} salir* 'The sun is about to come up'. — [RAE, "Infinitivos activos y pasivos (II)"; 403 on fetch, search summary](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/infinitivos-activos-y-pasivos-ii-otras-construcciones)
- **RAE general point [S]:** the RAE glossary entry *preposición* refers to NGLE §29, GDLE §§9.2 and 10, and DPD *preposición*. It says many non-spatial, non-temporal uses of prepositions are extensions of their physical meanings. — [RAE glosario, *preposición*](https://www.rae.es/gtg/preposici%C3%B3n)
- **GDLE (Bosque & Demonte), as quoted by a secondary article [S]:** *por* "expresa la finalidad cuando va acompañada de infinitivo" ('expresses purpose when followed by an infinitive'). — [search results listing, Messina/Glottodidattica article](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Other reference grammar [F, abstract only]:** Batchelor & San José, *A Reference Grammar of Spanish* (Cambridge), ch. 66, "Prepositions para and por". The abstract says "por, like para, may be used with the idea of intention". Its gap-fill text tests *para/por llegar antes* 'in order to arrive early' and *para que / porque no se me viera* 'so that it wouldn't be seen / because it wouldn't be seen'. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/reference-grammar-of-spanish/prepositions-para-and-porlas-preposiciones-para-y-por/581B27DF4D0C6F03BB423EBE2F57F23B)
- **Plan curricular (Instituto Cervantes) [S]:** a Ministry of Education document says the Plan curricular starts teaching prepositions at A1. A 2014 study analyses *por/para* in the Plan curricular at A1–A2. — [educacionfpydeportes.gob.es, preposiciones.pdf](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/dam/jcr:324ce58f-b768-4c7d-bf43-037d8e2d6af3/preposiciones.pdf); [Mendo 2014 PDF](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/dam/jcr:1eaab655-3fbb-49fa-bf92-087eba0805c7/2014-bv-15-04susanamendo-pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- The handouts mostly agree on one picture. *Para* points forward, to a goal, recipient, destination or deadline. *Por* points back or through, to a cause, means, route, exchange or agent. KU states this directly: "PARA is generally future-oriented … POR is retrospective". [KU][ku]
- Each handout splits the uses differently. For example, Bowdoin groups exchange, substitution and rate together, while Warwick lists "per" on its own. Treat the numbered list above as a merged list, not any one grammar's numbering.
- The only disagreement I can document between sources is on *estar por* 'about to'. The DPD accepts it but calls *estar para* more general, and the NGLE presents the *por ~ para* alternation as American. A forum claim that the RAE considers *está por llover* "incorrecta" is contradicted by the DPD wording itself. [WordReference thread](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/est%C3%A1-por-llover-est%C3%A1-para-salir.769682/) [S]

- I could not read the B&B §34.14 text (all sub-uses, the §34.14.8 list of contrasts, its notes on *por/para* + infinitive), NGLE §29.8 or GDLE ch. 9–10 (mirrors dead, rae.es 403). **So I cannot document any disagreements between these four works.**
- I could not read the Plan curricular inventory for *por/para* by level (site in maintenance).
- **[K] uses that are standard in reference grammars but did not appear in any fetched source. Verify before use:**
  - *quedar por* + infinitive 'remain to be done' (*Quedan tres capítulos por leer* 'Three chapters are left to read').
  - *por* in multiplication (*dos por tres son seis* 'two times three is six').
  - *por* for 'per person' distribution (*cien euros por persona* 'a hundred euros per person').
  - *ir a por* (Peninsular, 'go and get': *voy a por pan* 'I'm going to get bread').
  - Concessive *por* + adjective/adverb + *que* (*por mucho que insistas* 'however much you insist').
  - *por* + infinitive meaning 'because of not having done' (*por no estudiar* 'through not studying').
  - *por* for an unfinished task (*la casa está por barrer* 'the house hasn't been swept yet').
  - *para con* 'towards (a person)' (*amable para con todos* 'kind to everyone').
  - *para* + infinitive meaning an unexpected result (*salió para no volver* 'he left, never to return').
  - Imminence *para* without *estar* (*para las cinco faltan diez minutos* 'it's ten to five').
  - *no estar para bromas* 'not in the mood for jokes'.
  - *por* + an adjective of opinion or assumed identity (*lo dieron por muerto* 'they gave him up for dead').

## 2. Which uses allow only one preposition, and which allow both with a change in meaning? (minimal pairs)

### Takeaway
Most uses allow only one preposition. Agent, rate, multiplication, exchange, thanks and the fixed expressions take only *por*. Deadline, comparison with a norm, opinion and employer take only *para*. But many contexts allow both, each with a different meaning: *para* = goal, recipient or destination; *por* = cause, sake, route or substitution. Fetched sources confirm the pairs *compré esto por/para ti*, *recibimos dinero por/para la investigación* and *lo hice por/para ti*. The other pairs requested are not covered by a fetched source and are marked [K].

### Cited Findings
- *Compré esto por ti* 'I bought this because you wanted me to [motive]' vs *Compré esto para ti* 'I bought this to give it to you [destination]'. — [Bowdoin][bow] [L]
- *Recibimos dinero por la investigación* 'We got money for the research already done [exchange]' vs *Recibimos dinero para la investigación* 'We got money for research still to be done [purpose]'. — [Bowdoin][bow] [L]
- *Lo hice para ti* 'I did it for you (you are the goal/beneficiary)' vs *Lo hice por ti* 'I did it because of you / for your sake (cause, looking back)'. — [KU][ku] [L]
- *Trabaja para la OTAN* (employer) contrasts with *Trabajaba por la paz mundial* 'worked for (in support of) world peace'. These are two separate uses in the same handout, not a single minimal pair. — [Bowdoin][bow]
- *Salgo para Madrid* (destination, P6) vs *pasar por el parque* (route, R12). The handouts present these as separate uses, not as a pair on the same noun. — [Bowdoin][bow]; [KU][ku]
- *Está para llover* ≈ *está por llover* 'it's about to rain'. Here the difference is regional, not semantic: the DPD calls *para* more general and notes American *por*. Forum and learner sources add a possible nuance: *estar para* = ready or disposed, *estar por* = there are clear signs. — [DPD][dpd] [S]; [WordReference](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/est%C3%A1-por-llover-est%C3%A1-para-salir.769682/) [S]

### Inferences
- The two-way scheme in the handouts predicts the meanings in the table: *para* = forward-looking goal, recipient, deadline or destination; *por* = cause, sake, substitution, route or vague time. Only the first three rows come from a fetched source. The rest should be checked against B&B §34.14.8 before going into the app.
- Uses with only *por* (from the fetched catalogue): R2 thanks, R7 price, R8 *tomar por*, R9 rate, R10 duration, R12–R15 route and location, R16–R17 means, R18 agent, R23 fixed expressions. Uses with only *para*: P3 use, P5 employer, P7 deadline, P8 norm, P9 opinion, P10 good/bad for, P11 suitability. In this "only one" group, swapping the preposition is either ungrammatical or gives a different use, not a contrast in the same slot.

## 3. Where is the choice free or nearly free? (por/para + infinitive, para que vs por que)

### Takeaway
With purpose + infinitive, the reference grammars accept both prepositions. *Para* is neutral purpose. *Por* adds a sense of motive, desire or effort. The GDLE (as quoted) says *por* + infinitive expresses purpose. Batchelor & San José say *por*, like *para*, can express intention, and their exercise uses *para/por llegar antes*. For finite clauses the usual form is *para que* + subjunctive. *Por que* + subjunctive as purpose (as in *el chal … para que / porque no se me viera*) appears in Batchelor & San José's exercise, but I could not read their explanation.

### Cited Findings
- "por, like para, may be used with the idea of intention", with gap-fill items *para/por llegar antes*, *por/para salir pronto del trabajo* 'in order to leave work early', *por/para taparme el desgarrón* 'to cover the tear', and *para que/porque no se me viera el desaguisado* 'so that the mess wouldn't show'. — [Batchelor & San José, Cambridge][cam] [F, abstract only]
- GDLE (as quoted): *por* "expresa la finalidad cuando va acompañada de infinitivo". — [Messina article][mes] [S]
- Same subject: *para* + infinitive. Different subject: *para que* + subjunctive (*… para que el propietario les devuelva el dinero*). — unattributed search-engine summary of ELE guides (results included [spanishgrammar.info][fin], but which page holds the example is not verified) [S, weak]
- One pedagogical framing describes *para* as looking forward to a goal and *por* as looking back to a cause. This is not the NGLE's wording. — same unattributed search summary [S]; matches the [KU][ku] handout [L]

### Inferences
- **[K]:** The usual reference-grammar account (B&B, NGLE) is that *por* + infinitive of purpose marks a goal that is wanted, or the motive for the effort, rather than the planned outcome. Example: *lo hizo por ayudar* 'he did it out of a wish to help' vs *para ayudar* 'in order to help'. *Por* is also preferred after verbs of effort and desire: *esforzarse por*, *luchar por*, *hacer lo posible por*. Not confirmed from a fetched primary source.
- **[K]:** *Por que* + subjunctive as purpose is rare and literary, and not to be confused with causal *porque* + indicative. The DPD entry *porque/por que* is the place to check. It was blocked (403).

## 4. Time and space: *por la mañana*, *por dos horas* (vs *durante*), *para el lunes*, *para entonces*; *por aquí*, *pasar por*, *para allá*

### Takeaway
For time, *por* gives a vague part of the day (*por la mañana*) or a duration, often dropped (*(por) seis semanas*). *Para* gives a deadline or due date (*para el lunes*, *para las once*, *¿para cuándo?*). For space, *por* covers route, transit, area or vague location (*por la calle*, *pasar por*, *por aquí*, *por Brasil*). *Para* gives the direction or destination of movement (*salgo para Madrid*, *vamos para la oficina*).

### Cited Findings
- Duration "(often omitted)": *Estuvo en México (por) seis semanas.*; time of day: *Trabajaba por la mañana.* — [Bowdoin][bow]
- *He estudiado el español por más de 10 años* 'I've studied Spanish for more than 10 years'. — [KU][ku]
- *Por dos años* 'for two years'; *por la mañana (tarde, noche)*. — [Warwick][war]
- Deadline: *para el lunes*, *para las once*, *¿Para cuándo es…?*, *para las siete en punto*. — [KU][ku]; [Warwick][war]; [Bowdoin][bow]
- Space: *por la calle*, *pasar por mi oficina*, *pasear por el parque*, *entró por la puerta trasera*, *viajado por Brasil*, *por aquí / por allí*; destination *para Madrid*, *para la oficina*. — [Bowdoin][bow]; [Warwick][war]

### Inferences
- **[K]:** *durante* is the neutral 'for/during' for a stretch of time. *Por* + duration is common in American Spanish but many Peninsular speakers prefer *durante*, or no preposition at all. *Por* also gives an expected or planned duration (*me voy por dos semanas* 'I'm going away for two weeks'). Not confirmed by a fetched reference grammar. Bowdoin's "(often omitted)" partly supports it.
- **[K]:** *para entonces* 'by then'. *Para allá* 'over there, that way (direction)' vs *por allá* 'around there (vague location)'. These fit the scheme but no fetched source lists them.
- **[K]:** *por* + specific date or time gives an approximate time (*por Navidad* 'around Christmas', *por abril* 'around April').

## 5. Passive agent: *escrito por*; *por* vs *de* with *ser* passives

### Takeaway
The agent of the *ser* + participle passive is introduced by *por* (*América fue descubierta por Colón*). *Por* can also mark a cause with *estar* + participle (*Está afectada por la noticia*). I found no fetched source on when *de* takes over from *por*.

### Cited Findings
- "Por is used in the passive voice to indicate the active agent", with *América fue descubierta por Colón* and *Tres manifestantes fueron arrestados por la policía*. — [Warwick][war] [L]
- *Fue escrito por ella*; *Está afectada por la noticia*. — [Bowdoin][bow] [L]
- *Este libro fue escrito por mi autor favorito*. — [KU][ku] [L]

### Inferences
- **[K]:** Reference grammars (B&B ch. on the passive; NGLE §41) note that *de* marks the agent with participles of feeling, knowledge and accompaniment, and in older or literary usage. Examples: *conocido de todos* 'known to everyone', *rodeado de amigos* 'surrounded by friends', *acompañado de su mujer* 'accompanied by his wife', *temido de / por todos* 'feared by everyone'. *Por* is the rule with action verbs. I could not verify this; check before using.
