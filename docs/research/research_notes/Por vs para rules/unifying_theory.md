## 1. What do the RAE (NGLE §29.8) and the DPD say is the core meaning of each?

### Takeaway
I could not verify the NGLE or DPD wording; rae.es returned 403 for every page I tried. The academic sources I could read give the standard reference-grammar position: *por* = cause/motive, path, medium, exchange; *para* = purpose, destination, recipient. They also say the two values overlap in purpose clauses and are not synonyms there.

### Cited Findings
- The RAE's *Nueva gramática básica* has a section titled "Construcciones causales, finales e ilativas > Construcciones finales". It deals with *para* and *por* in purpose clauses, but its text was not retrievable (403). — [RAE, Gramática básica, construcciones finales](https://www.rae.es/gramática-básica/construcciones-causales-finales-e-ilativas/construcciones-finales)
- The NGLE syntax chapter 36 ("El complemento de régimen preposicional") has a subsection §36.10, "Complementos de régimen con las preposiciones para, por, sobre y tras". The snippet lists *luchar* among verbs with a *por* complement. The body was not retrievable (snippet only). — [RAE, NGLE §36.10](https://www.rae.es/gramática/sintaxis/complementos-de-régimen-con-las-preposiciones-para-por-sobre-y-tras)
- De Bruyne's chapter on prepositions in the Bosque & Demonte *Gramática descriptiva de la lengua española* (1999, pp. 667–703) ties *por* to "causalidad, intercambio y movimiento" (as summarised by D'Antonio et al.). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, Studi di Glottodidattica](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- A cognitive-linguistics paper says grammars disagree on how to characterise *por*. Some list its values without relating them; others reduce it to a single value such as [+causa] (abstract only). — [Funes 2020, repository record](https://repositorioslatinoamericanos.uchile.cl/handle/2250/4395458)
- Purpose with *por* is attested in reference-type examples such as *El niño se esfuerza por levantar la piedra* ("The boy strains to lift the stone"). One study concludes that *por* and *para* there are "intercambiables en el lenguaje, pero no en el concepto" (snippet only). — [munin.uit.no thesis "La finalidad con por/para"](https://munin.uit.no/handle/10037/7616)
- On *para* as a spatial preposition, it belongs with *a, hasta, hacia* to the directional (adlative) group, which introduce a path oriented toward a goal. Unlike *a* and *hasta*, *para* and *hacia* mark movement toward a goal that may stop short of reaching it, e.g. *Salimos para México* = "we're leaving for (heading toward) Mexico" (snippet only). — [Revista Philologia Hispalensis, US](https://revistascientificas.us.es/index.php/PH/article/download/16219/19858)

### Inferences
- The NGLE is a descriptive grammar. It lists the values of each preposition (place, time, cause, purpose, agent, exchange, and so on) and does not offer a single unifying test, so it should not be expected to supply "the rule". The reference grammars' "core" is the cause (*por*) vs purpose (*para*) pairing, plus the spatial pair "through/along" (*por*) vs "toward" (*para*).

### Gaps
- Exact NGLE §29.8 wording on the core meanings of *por* and *para*, and on purpose *por* (the NGLE treats final clauses in chapter 46). These need a library copy or Google Books; rae.es is blocked to fetches.
- What the DPD says. The DPD has no general *por/para* essay that I could confirm. Its entries mainly settle norm questions (e.g. *ir a por*, *estar por/para*); I could not verify their wording.

## 2. Cognitive and semantic analyses: what image schemas are proposed?

### Takeaway
Every cognitive account I could reach agrees on the same picture. *Por* profiles the path, process, medium or force that the event passes through or comes from. *Para* profiles the endpoint, goal or recipient the event is oriented toward. The newer polysemy-network work (Funes; Kissling and Tyler) keeps one spatial central sense per preposition and derives the other uses from it, rather than claiming one abstract feature predicts everything.

### Cited Findings
- **Delbecque (1996).** "Towards a cognitive account of the use of the prepositions por and para in Spanish", in Casad (ed.), *Cognitive Linguistics in the Redwoods*, Mouton de Gruyter, pp. 249–318. It is summarised as analysing *por/para* through construal, trajectory and force dynamics. For Delbecque, *para* introduces a goal or final recipient and is oriented toward a specific end. — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **The cognitive-grammar consensus**, as described by D'Antonio et al. *Por* highlights the route or process before a final result. It covers the spatial (*caminar por el parque*, "walk through the park"), the causal (*lo hizo por amor*, "he did it out of love") and the medium (*enviar un paquete por correo*, "send a package by mail"). *Por* is used when the event is viewed "desde el punto de vista del trayecto o proceso, más que desde el destino final". *Para* is a "proyección o dirección hacia un objetivo", often with a benefit or recipient sense: *Este regalo es para ti* ("This gift is for you"), *Estudia para ser médico* ("She studies to become a doctor"), *Trabaja para la empresa* ("He works for the company"). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Talmy's force dynamics** is invoked for the cause vs intention split: "La fuerza dinámica se relaciona con la causalidad o la intención en una acción" (citing Talmy 2005). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Meta-operational grammar (Adamczewski; Matte Bon 2016)** offers a different single rule. *Para* is "Fase I" (open paradigm): it introduces new information that comes after the event. *Por* is "Fase II" (closed paradigm): what it introduces already exists before or alongside the event, "por eso, se afirma que por expresa la causa, pues esta siempre precede al efecto". Their pair: *Lo han llamado por esa tarea* (cause: because of that task, which already exists) vs *Lo han llamado para esa tarea* (purpose: to do that task, which comes later). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Funes (2020)**, "Del espacio a la causa: El item polisémico de la preposición por", *Textos en Proceso* 6(1): 27–54. Using Lakoff's radial categories, *por*'s values form one organised polysemous item with [+Locativo] as the most prototypical attribute. Peripheral uses derive from it by losing, adding or changing attributes (abstract only). Funes (2022) treats *por* as a focaliser (title only). — [Funes 2020 record](https://repositorioslatinoamericanos.uchile.cl/handle/2250/4395458); [CONICET record](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/195582)
- **Kissling, Tyler, Warren & Negrete (2018).** "Reexamining Por and Para in the Spanish Foreign Language Intermediate Classroom: A Usage-Based, Cognitive Linguistic Approach", in Tyler, Huang & Jan (eds.), *What Is Applied Cognitive Linguistics?*, De Gruyter Mouton, pp. 229–256. They criticise the textbook list-of-uses format, which often amounts to English translations. Instead they organise the meanings as a "systematically connected polysemy network" whose central sense is spatial (snippet). — [Kissling et al. 2018, cited at core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/232791566.pdf); [Kissling, Benjamins author page](https://benjamins.com/catalog/persons/127116161)
- **Kissling & Arnold (2022).** Novice learners (n = 30) taught *por/para* with animated videos of principled polysemy networks made significant gains on controlled production tasks (snippet). — [Kissling & Arnold, Pedagogical Linguistics](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/pl.22005.kis)
- **A 2008 experiment** reported by the same search result found the following. Intermediate learners taught with a cognitive-linguistic description were less likely to confuse *por* and *para* in free writing than a group that memorised lists, whose accuracy fell (snippet; authors not shown). — [todoele bibliography](https://todoele.net/bibliografia/preliminary-evidence-applied-cognitive-linguistics-effective-novice-learners)
- **Lam.** Reported (secondhand, in a paper on English prepositions) as finding that L2 Spanish learners made "virtually no gains" in mastering *por/para* over four years of college Spanish (snippet only). — [search result citing Lam](https://core.ac.uk/download/232791566.pdf)
- **Llopis-García.** "Las preposiciones y la metáfora del espacio: aportaciones y potencial de la lingüística cognitiva para su enseñanza", *Journal of Spanish Language Teaching* 2(1): 51–68 (2015). Also "La gramática cognitiva: nuevas avenidas para la enseñanza de lenguas extranjeras", *Verba Hispánica* 19 (2011). D'Antonio et al. say Llopis-García (2011) warns that construal and trajectory are hard for beginners. — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Guerra Magdaleno (2014)**, "La imagen es la regla: cuestiones visuales en el tratamiento del contraste de las preposiciones por y para", is a visual-schema teaching proposal (reference only). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **A university pedagogical grammar** (KU *Acceso*) gives a one-line framing. *Para* "is characterized as being future-oriented, or the GOAL"; *por* "can be understood as more backward looking, or as describing the SOURCE". Minimal pair: *Lo hice para ti* ("I did it for you", you are the recipient or goal) vs *Lo hice por ti* ("I did it because of / for the sake of you"). — [KU Acceso, por y para](https://acceso.ku.edu/gramatica/unidad5/por_para.shtml)

### Inferences
- The proposals reduce to two versions of one asymmetry.
  - **(a) Spatial and cognitive version:** *por* = the path or medium travelled through (the trajectory, unbounded); *para* = the endpoint aimed at (a bounded goal).
  - **(b) Temporal and causal version:** *por* = something already in place that drives the event, i.e. the cause behind it, its source, or a force coming from behind; *para* = something not yet reached that the event points to, i.e. the goal ahead.
  - Matte Bon's "pre-existing vs new or subsequent" is version (b) put in discourse terms.

### Gaps
- I could not access the primary texts of Lunn (*Hispania*), Bull, Pinto & Rex, Zierer, Lunn & DeCesaris, Whitley (*Spanish/English Contrasts*), the Ruiz Campillo papers, or the *Gramática básica del estudiante de español* (Alonso Raya, Castañeda, Martínez Gila, Miquel, Ortega, Ruiz Campillo). Their specific formulations are unverified. My background knowledge, not confirmed this session, is that Bull and Whitley frame *por* as cause or motive vs *para* as purpose or destination, and that Lunn argues for a single meaning for each preposition. Treat this as unverified.
- Lam's original study: title, year and design are unverified.

## 3. Is there a defensible one-sentence rule? Test against hard cases

### Takeaway
Yes, with an honest scope. "**Por** names what the event comes from or passes through: its cause, motive, means, route, the thing exchanged, or the stretch of time or place it covers. **Para** names where the event is headed: its goal, recipient, destination, deadline, or the standard it is measured against." This predicts most of the core uses. The residue is a small set of lexicalised collocations (*luchar por*, *votar por*, *ir (a) por*, *estar por*) that the "motive behind" reading can explain after the fact but cannot predict for a learner. Those should be taught as exceptions.

### Cited Findings
- The literature itself says purpose and cause overlap: "existen muchos casos en los cuales la causa y el fin se rozan constantemente". Example: *Pasa por la ciudad por no pasar por la Vía de Cintura* vs *... para no pasar por la Vía de Cintura* ("He goes through town so as not to / out of not wanting to take the ring road"). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- *Trabajo por mi jefe* ("I work because of / on behalf of / in place of my boss": the boss as cause or force) vs *Trabajo para mi jefe* ("I work for my boss": the boss as goal or beneficiary). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- Pedagogical categories that fit the forward-goal reading of *para*:
  - Deadline: *Tengo que entregar el trabajo para el lunes* ("by Monday").
  - Comparison with a norm: *Para ser estudiante de SPAN 216, escribes muy bien* ("For a SPAN 216 student, you write very well").
  - Opinion: *Para mí, aprender... es difícil* ("To me...").
  - Employer: *Trabajo para la Universidad de Kansas*.
  - Destination: *Salimos para México mañana*.
  - For *por*: exchange (*Pago 25 dólares por el almuerzo*), duration (*por más de 10 años*), means (*por email*), route (*pasar por el parque*), cause (*No fuimos al concierto por el costo*), and fixed phrases (*por eso*, *por fin*). — [KU Acceso](https://acceso.ku.edu/gramatica/unidad5/por_para.shtml)
- *Luchar* is listed in the NGLE among verbs whose prepositional complement uses *por*. In other words, the RAE treats *luchar por* as a verb's governed complement (*complemento de régimen*), not a free choice of meaning (snippet only). — [RAE NGLE §36.10](https://www.rae.es/gramática/sintaxis/complementos-de-régimen-con-las-preposiciones-para-por-sobre-y-tras)

### Inferences (my analysis of the hard cases against the rule)

| Case | Gloss | Fits "por = behind/through, para = ahead/end"? | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| *gracias por la ayuda* | thanks for the help | Yes | The help already happened and is the cause of the thanks. |
| *por eso* / *para eso* | that's why / that's what it's for | Yes, a clean minimal pair | *Por eso* points back to a reason; *para eso* points forward to a use or purpose. |
| *por 10 dólares* | for $10 | Yes, as exchange | The money is what you go "through" to get the item (medium or substitute). Lexical, but easy. |
| *por la mañana*, *por aquí* | in the morning, around here | Yes | An unbounded stretch of time or space the event occupies. This is the path reading. |
| *para el lunes* | by Monday | Yes | A deadline is a temporal endpoint ahead. |
| *para mí, ...* | in my view | Yes, weakly | The statement is "directed to" a perspective as its endpoint. A learner can accept this but would not predict it. |
| *para ser tan joven* | for someone so young | Yes, weakly | The standard against which the judgement is measured. Same "reference endpoint" extension. |
| *trabajar para X* vs *por X* | work for (employer) vs for the sake of / instead of X | Yes, a clean pair | *Para*: X receives the product of the work. *Por*: X is the motive, or X's place is what you fill. |
| *lo hice por ayudarte* vs *para ayudarte* | out of wanting to help you / in order to help you | Yes, both possible | *Por* presents the wish to help as an already existing motive pushing the act. *Para* presents helping as a result not yet reached. |
| *luchar por la libertad* | fight for freedom | **Breaks the naive "goal = para" rule** | It is rescued only if freedom is construed as a cause or ideal that drives the fight. Governed by the verb (*complemento de régimen*), so teach it as a collocation. *Luchar para + inf.* exists for a concrete aim (*luchar para sobrevivir*). |
| *votar por X* | vote for X | Same as *luchar por* | X is the cause or side you back ("on behalf of"). It is a lexical verb-plus-preposition pairing. |
| *ir por agua* / Peninsular *ir a por agua* | go for (fetch) water | Breaks the rule (the water is the goal) | Explained historically: *por* as "in search of" (an object sought along a path). Teach as an exception. *Ir a por* is Peninsular and colloquial. |
| *estar por + inf.* vs *estar para + inf.* | be inclined to / be about to (also, in the Americas, "still to be done") | Partly | *Estar para salir* = about to leave (the endpoint is near). *Estar por hacer* = not yet done (still on the way); *estoy por irme* = I'm tempted to go. These are regionally variable. Teach as an idiom pair. |
| *esforzarse por*, *desvivirse por* | strive to | Gray zone | Effort verbs take *por* + infinitive for purpose, consistent with "the wish drives the effort". It is lexical. |

- Overall: the rule correctly predicts the free-choice contrasts: cause vs purpose, route vs destination, duration vs deadline, exchange, *trabajar por/para*, *hacerlo por/para ti*, *por eso/para eso*. It fails, or works only after the fact, on verb-governed *por* complements: *luchar, votar, preocuparse, interesarse, esforzarse, apostar por*. These are better taught as "verb + por" collocations, much as the subjunctive's assertion test leaves lexical triggers to be memorised.

### Gaps
- Corpus frequencies showing what share of real *por/para* tokens the one-line rule covers. Rubio (2015) did a frequency comparison, but I did not access its numbers. — [Rubio 2015, SECCLL](https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/seccll/2015/2015/29)
- Authoritative regional facts on *estar por* (Americas: "about to"; Spain: "tempted to / not yet done") and on the DPD's ruling on *ir a por* are not verified in a source this session.

## 4. Purpose vs motive overlap: por + infinitive vs para + infinitive

### Takeaway
When both are possible, the difference is how the speaker presents the aim. *Para* presents it as a neutral future result the act leads to. *Por* presents the same aim as a motive or desire that already exists and drives the act. *Por* is more subjective and justificatory, and is often negative or evaluative.

### Cited Findings
- *Me he quedado en la oficina por terminar mi trabajo con tiempo* ("I stayed at the office to get / because I wanted to get my work done in time"). With *por*, the speaker presents the reason as justification, a "more subjective" strategy than the neutral *para*. "Por nunca presenta el fin de forma contundente". Purpose with *por* and with *para* "no son asimilables" because they carry different pragmatic meaning. — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- *Te devuelvo el dinero ... para cumplir con mi promesa* ("to keep my promise": the result comes after) vs *... por cumplir con mi promesa* ("because of keeping my promise": the promise is the motor of the action, simultaneous with it). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- Mendizábal (1997) proposes three causes: logical, real and virtual. Virtual cause is purpose, so purpose is a forward-projected cause. — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- *Lo hice por amor* (cause) vs *Lo hice para ayudarte* (purpose) is the standard teaching pair. — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)

### Inferences
- An app can teach this as one test. Ask whether the infinitive or noun is something the subject already wants or has, which pushes the action (*por*), or a result the action is meant to produce (*para*). When either reading fits, both are grammatical, and *para* is the safe neutral default for learners.

### Gaps
- No corpus data on how often *por* + infinitive purpose occurs relative to *para* + infinitive, or on which verbs prefer it, apart from effort and struggle verbs.

## 5. Historical origin and whether it supports the unifying rule

### Takeaway
History supports the rule. *Por* continues Latin PRO and PER, which had merged phonetically and include "through" and "on behalf of / in place of". *Para* is a later Ibero-Romance compound of *por* plus *a* (from Latin AD, "to"), built specifically to mark the endpoint or direction. In effect, *para* is "por + toward", and the cause vs purpose split grew out of that.

### Cited Findings
- Per Corominas (2008), *por* (attested c. 938) comes from Latin PRO and once covered the values of today's *para* too. *Para* appears c. 1250, from an older *pora*. D'Antonio et al.'s summary attaches the oath form *par* (as in *pardiez*) to this etymology. The more usual reading is *pora* < *por* + *a*; see Brea below. — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- Brea (1985, *Verba* 12: 147–182) holds that unstressed PER and PRO reduced to a common *\*pr*, giving *per* in some regions and *por* in others. Ibero-Romance also formed *para* / *per a*, "cuyo segundo elemento remonta a AD, indicando así de un modo más claro el fin en el espacio, la dirección". Hence *para* came to express purpose and *por* cause, "aunque la distinción no se haya observado con un rigor absoluto". — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, quoting Brea 1985: 167](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- Latin PRO meant "delante de", "en lugar de" and "a favor de" (Chaves 2011). PER relates to Proto-Indo-European *\*per* "forward, through". — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- The standard monograph on the history of the split is Riiho (1979), *Por y para: estudio sobre los orígenes y la evolución de una oposición prepositiva iberorrománica*, Helsinki (reference only). — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- Catalan has the parallel *per* / *per a* split, which shows the same Ibero-Romance "+ a" strategy (the Institut d'Estudis Catalans has a dedicated seminar sheet on it; not read). — [IEC, per i per a](https://criteria.iec.cat/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/VIII-Seminari-Igualada-Full-de-suport-per-i-per-a-3.pdf)

### Inferences
- The hard cases look like leftovers of PRO's older "on behalf of / for the sake of / in place of" meanings: *luchar por, votar por, trabajar por, por ti, por 10 dólares* (substitution or exchange). *Para*, a later and more specialised form, took only the forward-endpoint meanings: destination, purpose, recipient, deadline, standard. This explains why *por* is the broad, messy one and *para* is the narrow, regular one.
- Teaching recommendation that follows: teach *para* as the marked choice ("Is there an endpoint the event is headed for: a destination, recipient, purpose, deadline or standard? Then use *para*") and *por* as the default for everything else (cause, motive, route, means, exchange, duration, agent). Then list the *por*-governed verbs as exceptions. This mirrors the assertion test, where one feature (assertion) selects the indicative and its absence selects the subjunctive.

### Gaps
- The etymology of *para* is attested here only through a secondary summary of Corominas. The summary is ambiguous between *par* + *a* and *por* + *a*. Check Corominas & Pascual (DCECH) or Riiho (1979) directly.
- No source I reached directly tests whether learners taught the "para = marked endpoint, por = default" framing outperform those taught a symmetric cause/purpose rule.
