# Por and para: fixed expressions, verb/adjective regimes, and regional variation (Mexico, Colombia, Spain)

Source-access note: rae.es (DPD, Ortografía) returned HTTP 403 and fundeu.es was blocked for direct fetching. DPD and Fundéu content below comes from search-engine summaries and snippets of those pages, or from secondary pages quoting them. These are marked "(via search snippet)". The Diccionario del español de México (dem.colmex.mx), Wiktionary and Universidad de Piura's *Castellano Actual* were fetched directly.

## Which regimes are fixed and which alternate with a meaning change (preguntar por vs para; estar por vs estar para)?

### Takeaway
Most regimes are fixed by the governing word. A few pairs change meaning with the preposition. The best-documented pair is *estar por* vs *estar para* + infinitive: both can mean imminence, but *estar por* also means "to be inclined to / still to be done", and the "about to" reading of *estar por* is mainly American. Several verbs that take "for" in English take no preposition in Spanish (buscar, esperar, pedir), and adding *por* to them is a recognised anglicism, with narrow exceptions.

### Cited Findings
- DEM (Mexico), *por* sense 14, going to fetch something: "Voy por agua", "Salió por pan", "Subió por el libro". This is the Mexican standard, with no *a* before *por*. — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- DEM *por* sense 10, support or favour, with the regime *brindar por*: "Brindo por tu felicidad" ("I toast to your happiness"). — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- DEM *por* sense 11, opinion, with the regime *tener por*: "Lo tiene por sabio" ("She considers him wise"). Sense 12 gives *aceptar por*: "Te acepto por amigo". — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- DEM *por* sense 15, pending action with *por* + infinitive: "un trabajo por entregar" ("a piece of work still to be handed in"). Sense 16 gives *acabar por* + infinitive, an outcome: "Acabó por llorar" ("He ended up crying"). — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- DEM *por* senses 17–18, repeated infinitive or noun: "hablar por hablar" ("talking for the sake of talking"), "el arte por el arte" ("art for art's sake"). — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- DEM *para* sense 5, function, the basis of the regime *servir para*: "¿Para qué sirve un martillo?" ("What is a hammer for?"). — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)
- DEM *para* sense 10, aptitude, the basis of *bueno/apto/listo para* and *tener ... para*: "Tiene oído para la música" ("He has an ear for music"). — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)
- DEM *para* sense 8, comparison relative to a standard: "Es alto para su edad" ("He's tall for his age"). Sense 7, point of view: "Para la ley, todos somos iguales" ("In the eyes of the law we are all equal"). — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)
- DPD (entry *estar*, via search snippet): *estar por* + infinitive meaning "estar a punto de" is frequent in some American countries, and *estar para* is the more general form with this meaning. The DPD also says *estar al* + infinitive is normal in Spain only with *caer* and *llegar* ("están al caer / al llegar"), while its use in America is much more general. — [DPD, estar](https://rae.es/dpd/estar)
- Burguera Serra (RILCE, 2013): *estar por / para / a punto de* + infinitive are often presented as synonyms. The paper argues *estar por* has modal, epistemic values (inclination, "I'm tempted to"), while *estar para* chiefly marks closeness in time. — [RILCE article](https://revistas.unav.edu/index.php/rilce/article/download/2888/2707)
- Pardo Llibrer (Revista Española de Lingüística, 2023) studies approximative uses of *estar por* + infinitive in peninsular Spanish and treats it as an imminence periphrasis under certain conditions. So the "about to" reading is not wholly absent in Spain. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/917cb644cd50414b8aa4f30bdc927806)
- Learner-resource commentary: *estar por* commonly means intention or temptation in Spain ("Estoy por irme" = "I'm inclined to leave"), while "about to" is more common in the Americas. This is a secondary source, not normative. — [Kwiziq Q&A](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/estar-a-punto-de-vs-estar-por)
- *Esperar* (Fundéu, via a secondary summary): when it means waiting for something hoped for, *esperar* takes no preposition, and *esperar por* is a growing calque of English "wait for". *Esperar por* is correct when *por* gives cause or duration. — [Panamá América, citing Fundéu](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/dia-d/esperar-algo-mejor-que-esperarporalgo-842881)
- A Mexican university study likewise treats *esperar por* as a calque of "to wait for". — [Jóvenes en la Ciencia, UGTO](https://www.jovenesenlaciencia.ugto.mx/index.php/jovenesenlaciencia/article/download/2929/2194/9848)
- DPD (entry *pedir*, via search snippet): *pedir por* is used in the Southern Cone to mean "reclamar / ask on someone's behalf". The DPD advises avoiding *pedir por* in the sense of *preguntar por* (asking after someone), probably an influence of English "ask for". — [DPD, pedir](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pedir)
- *Buscar* takes a direct object with no *por* or *para*: "busco mis llaves" ("I'm looking for my keys"). — [SpanishDict comparison](https://www.spanishdict.com/compare/look for/seek)

### Inferences
(The items below come from general grammatical knowledge. I did not verify them against a fetched source this session, so the writer should treat them as unverified or mark them accordingly.)
- Fixed *por* regimes, where the preposition is lexically selected:
  - preocuparse por ("worry about")
  - interesarse por ("take an interest in"; *interesarse en* also occurs)
  - optar por ("opt for")
  - votar por ("vote for"; *votar a* + person is common in Spain)
  - luchar por ("fight for")
  - apostar por ("bet on, back")
  - disculparse por ("apologise for")
  - felicitar por ("congratulate on")
  - brindar por ("toast to", cited above)
  - preguntar por ("ask after/about someone")
  - mandar por ("send for", American; Spain says *mandar a buscar* or *mandar a por*)
  - ir a por / ir por ("go and get")
  - acabar/terminar por + infinitive ("end up doing")
  - tomar por ("take for, mistake for")
  - dar por + participle ("consider as"): "lo doy por hecho" ("I consider it done")
  - Nouns: interés por/en, preocupación por, amor por, pasión por, respeto por (also *a*)
  - Adjectives: preocupado por, conocido por, famoso por, responsable de (the norm; *responsable por* appears in American usage)
- Fixed *para* regimes:
  - servir para ("be good for")
  - prepararse para ("prepare for")
  - alcanzar para ("be enough for"): "no alcanza para todos"
  - bastar para ("suffice")
  - Adjectives: bueno/malo para, apto para, listo para ("ready to"), capacitado para, necesario para, útil para, suficiente para
- Pairs where the preposition changes the meaning:
  - preguntar por alguien ("ask about someone") vs preguntar algo para + purpose ("ask in order to")
  - estar por ("be inclined to / be still to be done / about to" in America) vs estar para ("be about to; be in the mood for", as in "no estoy para bromas", "I'm not in the mood for jokes")
  - trabajar por (on behalf of, for a cause) vs trabajar para (for an employer)
  - hacerlo por ti ("for your sake / in your place") vs hacerlo para ti ("for you, as recipient")
  - votar por (choose) vs no hay para qué votar (purpose)
- Verbs that take no preposition where English uses "for": buscar, esperar, pedir ("pedir ayuda", "ask for help"), agradecer ("te agradezco la ayuda"; *agradecer por* is attested but considered non-standard by many guides; not verified), pagar ("pagar la cena", "pay for dinner"; *pagar por* = pay in exchange for or because of).

## What does the DPD say about "ir a por" and "por que / porque / por qué / porqué"?

### Takeaway
The DPD treats *ir a por* as an acceptable Spain-centred construction, a cross between "ir a [lugar]" and "ir por [algo]", with "no reasons to censure it". American speakers perceive it as odd and use plain *ir por*. The four forms *porque / por que / por qué / porqué* have distinct grammatical roles: conjunction, preposition + relative, interrogative, and noun.

### Cited Findings
- FundéuRAE (via an EFE note quoted in search results): with verbs of movement, both *por* and *a por* are admitted. *A por* appears mainly in Spain with the sense of fetching or seeking. In America it is perceived as anomalous and *por* is used in these contexts (citing the *Nueva gramática*). — [Al Navío, "¿Ir por agua o ir a por agua?"](https://alnavio.es/ir-por-agua-o-ir-a-por-agua-la-rae-resuelve-la-duda) (page itself returned 403; content via search summary)
- The DPD (as quoted in the same note) says the construction comes from crossing "ir a un lugar" with "ir por algo", and that there are no reasons to censure *a por*. — [Al Navío](https://alnavio.es/ir-por-agua-o-ir-a-por-agua-la-rae-resuelve-la-duda) (via search summary)
- DEM confirms the Mexican form *ir por* without *a*: "Voy por agua", "Salió por pan". — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- The four forms (Fundéu Guzmán Ariza and secondary sources quoting Fundéu):
  - **porque** is a causal conjunction meaning "because" (= puesto que, ya que). With the subjunctive it can mean purpose (= para que), and in that case *por que* written as two words is also valid.
  - **por qué** is the interrogative, in direct and indirect questions: "¿Por qué no viniste?", "No sé por qué lo hizo".
  - **porqué** is a masculine noun meaning "reason, motive". It takes a determiner and has the plural *porqués*: "el porqué de su decisión".
  - **por que** is the preposition + relative *que*. Test: an article can be inserted, as in "Ese es el motivo por (el) que decidió no ir" ("That's the reason why he decided not to go").
  - Common error: writing *porqué* for *por qué*.
  — [El Universal (Colombia)](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/educacion/aprende-los-usos-de-porque-por-que-porque-y-por-que-BL7580928); [Acento (Fundéu Guzmán Ariza)](https://acento.com.do/cultura/se-escribe-el-porque-no-el-por-que-9516030.html); [La Capital MdP](https://www.lacapitalmdp.com/por-que-no-es-lo-mismo-que-porque/) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- For an app, *ir a por* should be accepted as correct but tagged "Spain". *Ir por* should be the default form for a Mexico- or Colombia-focused learner. Marking *ir a por* wrong would contradict the DPD.
- *Por que* with a relative reading also occurs after verbs and nouns that govern *por*: "la razón por que", "luchar por que haya paz" ("fight for there to be peace"). This follows from the "por + que" rule above but was not separately sourced.

## What regional forms should an app accept for Mexico and Colombia that a Spain-centred grammar would mark?

### Takeaway
Accept these as correct or regional rather than errors: *ir por* (vs Spain's *ir a por*); "un cuarto para las ocho" and "diez para las ocho" (vs "las ocho menos cuarto"); *estar por* + infinitive = "about to"; *por mientras* = "meanwhile" (Mexico); *por si las dudas* = "just in case" (Mexico); and colloquial *pa'*, *pa'l* and *pa' que*, which are informal in all regions. Do not accept "un cuarto **a** las ocho", which the RAE rejects.

### Cited Findings
- Time-telling: the RAE *Ortografía* gives *y cuarto*, *y media* and *menos cuarto* as the standard fractions. It notes that in almost all of America the formula *un cuarto para…* is used instead of *menos cuarto*, and that "no debe decirse ⊗un cuarto a las ocho". — [RAE Ortografía, modelo de doce horas](https://www.rae.es/ortograf%C3%ADa/modelo-de-doce-horas) (via search snippet; page 403)
- DPD *hora*: *un cuarto para* is American usage, with examples from Peru, Mexico and Chile. The preposition must be *para*, not *a*. — [DPD, hora](https://www.rae.es/dpd/hora) (via search snippet)
- Agreement: *Castellano Actual* (Universidad de Piura) recommends "Es un cuarto para las cuatro", with the verb agreeing with the singular *un cuarto*. — [UDEP, Duda resuelta: es o son](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-es-o-son-3/) (via search snippet)
- DEM's example of the *para* + time model: "Faltan 10 minutos para las 5" ("It's ten to five"). — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)
- *Por mientras*: the *Diccionario de americanismos* (ASALE, 2010) records *por mientras* as an adverbial phrase "muy usada en México, Costa Rica, Perú, Bolivia, Paraguay y Chile", meaning roughly "meanwhile, for the time being". Colombia is not in that list. The same UDEP article says it does not recognise *por lo mientras*. — [UDEP, ¿Por lo mientras?](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/?p=2543)
- DPD *mientras* (via search snippet): advises against *por lo mientras*, located in Mexico and Central America, in educated speech. *De mientras* is considered popular. — [DPD, mientras](https://www.rae.es/dpd/mientras)
- An *Anuario de Letras* (UNAM) study analyses *por mientras* and *para mientras* as near-synonymous temporal relators of bounded simultaneity in contemporary varieties. — [Anuario de Letras, UNAM](https://revistas-filologicas.unam.mx/anuario-letras/index.php/al/article/download/1810/2569/4250) (via search summary)
- Mexican set phrases listed in DEM: *por lo pronto* ("for now"), *por ahora*, *por lo tanto*, *por más que*, *por poco* ("almost"), *por si acaso*, *por si las dudas* ("just in case"), *por si no*, *por ejemplo*, *por favor*, *por fin*. — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- DEM *para* phrases: *para colmo* ("to top it all"), *para su desgracia* ("unfortunately for him/her"), *para sí*. — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)
- DEM *por* sense 5, approximate time: "Regresa por Navidad" ("He's coming back around Christmas"). Sense 6, rate: *por hora*, *por kilo*, *por ciento*. Sense 7, distribution: *por cabeza*, *por niño*. Sense 8, price: "Lo vendió por $1 000". Sense 9, exchange: "Cambio pesos por tostones". — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)
- *Pa'*: Wiktionary labels *pa'* (also *pa*) a colloquial apocopic form of *para*, pronounced /ˈpa/, with derived forms *pa' lante*, *pa'l* and *pa'rriba*. It gives no regional label. — [Wiktionary, pa'](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pa%27)
- Spelling norm: the apostrophe is accepted in literary texts reproducing popular speech, as in "pa'". — [UDEP, Cuando los sonidos se suprimen](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/cuando-los-sonidos-se-suprimen/)
- *Estar por* = "about to" is frequent in some American countries (DPD, via snippet). — [DPD, estar](https://rae.es/dpd/estar)
- *Pedir por*, meaning to claim or ask on someone's behalf, is Southern Cone, not Mexico or Colombia (DPD, via snippet). — [DPD, pedir](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pedir)

### Inferences
(Unverified this session. Treat as working hypotheses.)
- *Pa'* and *pa' que* are pan-Hispanic colloquial forms, very frequent in Colombian and Mexican speech and song lyrics, and also common in Spain. An app should accept them in informal input, but normalise them to *para* / *para que* when correcting. "¿Pa' qué?" = "What for?"
- Colombia: *de a* + quantity or manner is common in Colombian and other American speech: *de a poquito* ("little by little"), *de a uno* ("one at a time"), *de a pie*. *Cada* + time with *por*/*a* (e.g. "dos veces por semana" / "dos veces a la semana", "twice a week") is general, not regional.
- Colombia: *mandar por* / *ir por* without *a* is normal, matching Mexico.
- Spain-only or Spain-marked forms an American-focused app may flag as "Spain" rather than wrong: *ir a por*, *menos cuarto*, *votar a* + candidate.
- *Responsable por* (vs *responsable de*) and *interés en/por* both occur in Latin American usage.
