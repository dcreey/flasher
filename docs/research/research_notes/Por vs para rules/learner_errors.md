# Por vs para: how it is taught to English speakers and what errors they make

Scope: learners whose first language is English. Examples are given in Spanish with English glosses. Source quality key: [primary] = peer-reviewed study or the study's own data as tabulated in a peer-reviewed review; [review] = secondary academic summary; [learner site] = commercial or learner-oriented website.

Main secondary source used: Jiménez, A. L. (2017), "Jerarquía de aprendizaje: un caso práctico con 'por' y 'para'", *Revista Nebrija de Lingüística Aplicada* 22. It re-tabulates the three classic English-L1 studies side by side: Guntermann (1992), *Hispania* 75(1):177-187; Lafford & Ryan (1995), *Hispania* 78(3):528-547; and Pinto & Rex (2006), *Hispania* 89(3):611-622. I could not reach the three original articles (JSTOR is paywalled). The numbers below come from Jiménez's tables, not from the originals. Jiménez URL: https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4

## 1. What acquisition research says about order of acquisition and which uses stay hard

### Takeaway
In English-speaking learners' speech, *para* makes up 64-68% of por/para tokens overall. At beginning level the split is about 50/50; *para* peaks at intermediate level (83% in Lafford & Ryan). There is no native baseline, so "overuse" is an inference. *Para* of purpose (para + infinitive) and *para* of recipient are acquired first and are the most accurate uses. *Por* is harder, especially duration, means/route ("through, along, by") and spatial uses. Jiménez describes a U-shaped accuracy curve (memorized chunks, then rule-testing errors, then recovery). His own tables support it only partly: Guntermann's *por* error rate falls steadily, and Lafford & Ryan's *para* error rate is flat.

### Cited Findings
- **Corpora.** Guntermann (1992) and Lafford & Ryan (1995) studied English-speaking study-abroad learners using oral interviews (OPI-style). Pinto & Rex (2006) studied classroom learners and included a fill-in questionnaire. [review] — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Share of por vs para.** Across all levels, *por* was 35% of tokens in Guntermann, 32% in Lafford & Ryan and 36% in Pinto & Rex; *para* was 64-68%. Native-speaker baseline frequencies were not given. In Lafford & Ryan, intermediate learners used *para* 83% of the time. [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tabla 1](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Error rates by level (Guntermann 1992).** *Por*: 83% wrong at beginning level, 32% at intermediate, 21% at advanced. *Para*: 0% wrong at beginning, 33% at intermediate, 16% at advanced. Guntermann found 95 obligatory contexts for *por* and 162 for *para*. [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tabla 2](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Error rates by level (Lafford & Ryan 1995).** *Por*: 40%, 48%, then 18% wrong. *Para*: 34%, 37%, then 33% wrong. Pinto & Rex (2006), read from the table's row order: *por* 35%, 21%, 14% wrong; *para* 53%, 46%, 59% wrong. The source table's labels are misaligned, so treat the Pinto & Rex row assignment with caution. [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tabla 2](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Native lexical hypothesis and the U-shape.** Beginners map *por* = "for" by spelling and function. Later they find that this fails and adopt *para* as the default form when in doubt. That produces the overuse of *para* until advanced level. Jiménez links this to Kellerman's (1983) U-shaped pattern and says both Guntermann and Lafford & Ryan show it for both prepositions. **His own Tabla 2 contradicts this in part.** Guntermann's *por* errors fall steadily (83% → 32% → 21%), and Lafford & Ryan's *para* errors are flat (34/37/33%). Only Guntermann *para* (0 → 33 → 16% errors) and Lafford & Ryan *por* (40 → 48 → 18%) show a U. [review] — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Intermediate stage.** "En el nivel intermedio de su IL los estudiantes anglófonos en general utilizan por en contextos donde se requiere para" (at intermediate level, English speakers generally use *por* where *para* is required). [review] — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Earliest and most accurate use.** *Para* of purpose was the most consistent use at every level in every study: 55% of correct *para* uses in Guntermann, 76% in Lafford & Ryan, 77% in Pinto & Rex. Together with recipient (*para ti*, "for you"), purpose made up 94% of *para* uses in Lafford & Ryan and 95% of correct *para* uses in Pinto & Rex. Lafford & Ryan argue that the "goal/objective" notion of *para* is transparent and so is acquired early. [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tabla 6](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Purpose para is partly a crutch.** Learners may favour *para* + infinitive because the pattern is salient. They then overuse it after motion verbs, where natives often use *a*: *Voy a la biblioteca para trabajar* vs *Voy a la biblioteca a trabajar* ("I'm going to the library to work"). [review] — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Early por uses are mostly chunks.** The *por* uses that appear early are lexicalized chunks (*por favor* "please", *por eso* "that's why", *por ejemplo* "for example"), cause (*gracias por*, "thanks for") and exchange (*por*, "in exchange for"). In Lafford & Ryan, 7 of 7 exchange tokens came at beginning level. Many cause tokens in Pinto & Rex were drawn out by *gracias por* items in the questionnaire. [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tablas 4-5](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Duration is not really acquired early.** Duration-*por* tokens looked early but were largely copied from the interviewer's question (*¿Por cuánto tiempo...?*, "For how long...?"). Jiménez also counts many of them as ungrammatical: *\*¿Por cuánto tiempo vives en Arizona? – Vivo por un año y medio* ("How long have you lived in Arizona? – I live for a year and a half"). Natives would say *Vivo aquí desde hace año y medio* or *Llevo año y medio aquí*. Guntermann and Lafford & Ryan note that not all natives accept duration *por* in every context, but Pinto & Rex and Guntermann accept *Estuve por tres semanas en Argentina* ("I was in Argentina for three weeks"). [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tabla 3](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Late uses.** "Through/along" (*tránsito*), means/manner, and vague location (*por aquí*, "around here") appear only at intermediate or advanced levels, in very small numbers. *Para* + *que* + subjunctive (purpose clause) appears only from intermediate level. *Para* of opinion (*para mí*, "in my opinion") is almost absent in English-speaking learners, because they say *creo que* ("I think") instead. [primary data via review] — [Jiménez 2017, Tablas 7-8](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Overall sequence.** Learners acquire notional uses first (purpose, recipient, cause, chunks, exchange), then temporal uses, then spatial uses. This is the reverse of L1 children, who start with spatial *por*/*para*. [review] — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **Ten weeks abroad.** One secondary summary says accuracy on por/para averaged about 65% after a ten-week study-abroad program. The same summary says chunks such as *para* + destination are central to these learners' repertoires. I saw this only as a search snippet and could not verify which study it refers to. — search summary pointing to [Lord 2010](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/glord/files/Lord-CombinedEffects-2010.pdf) and [Collentine 2004](https://jan.ucc.nau.edu/jgc/research/ssla_gram/collentine_ssla_2004.pdf) (not opened)

### Inferences
- For a learner-text checker, the base rate favours flagging *por* errors at beginner level and over-used *para* at intermediate level. *Para* of purpose is rarely wrong, apart from *para* + infinitive after motion verbs, which is stylistically marked rather than ungrammatical.
- Duration with *por* ("for three weeks") is the most contested area. Both native acceptability and learner accuracy are unstable here, so a checker should treat it as advice, not as an error.

- I could not read Guntermann (1992), Lafford & Ryan (1995) or Pinto & Rex (2006) directly. All figures are second-hand via Jiménez (2017). His Tabla 2 has misaligned row labels for Pinto & Rex and Jiménez.
- I found no published por/para error study on CAES or CEDEL2 for English L1 learners. A search turned up only a CEDEL2 study of Japanese learners' prepositions (error rate falling from 26.95% at A2 to 7.67% at C2; [Cuadernos CANELA](https://www.cuadernoscanela.org/index.php/cuadernos/article/download/300/149/908)) and a German-L1 study naming *por*, *a*, *para*, *en*, *de* and *con* as the hardest prepositions ([Redalyc/Signos](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92153510015/92153510015.pdf)). Neither is English L1. Someone could query CEDEL2 (cedel2.learnercorpora.com, free) directly for English-L1 por/para errors. I did not do this.

## 2. Teaching heuristics and mnemonics, and where each fails; English "for" sentences that take neither

### Takeaway
The standard rule list ("por = through, because of, in exchange for, duration; para = in order to, destination, deadline, recipient, opinion") and acronyms built from it all start from English "for". That is the very translation that causes the intermediate-stage errors. Many English "for" phrases take no preposition at all in Spanish (*buscar*, *esperar*, *pedir*, *pagar* + thing).

### Cited Findings
- **The "for" trap is the documented error source.** Beginners equate *por* with "for" (native lexical hypothesis), then over-correct to *para*. [review] — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- **List-based teaching.** Lam (2009) describes the traditional method as teaching each use as a separate item to memorise, "in a non-explanatory manner". [primary] — [Lam 2009, Language Awareness, DOI 10.1080/09658410802147345](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658410802147345)
- **Verbs whose meaning already contains "for".** *Buscar* ("look for"), *esperar* ("wait for") and *pedir* ("ask for") include "for", so adding *por* or *para* is wrong: *busco trabajo* ("I'm looking for a job"), *pedí ayuda* ("I asked for help"). *Preguntar por* means "ask about/after someone": *pregunté por su amiga* ("I asked about his friend"). [learner site] — [FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/spanish/blog/spanish-verbs-prepositions/)
- **Pagar.** *Pagar* normally takes the thing paid for as a direct object (*pagué la cena*, "I paid for dinner"). *Pagar por* exists but is a different construction ("pay [an amount] for", "pay because of"). *\*Pregunté por una cerveza* for "I asked for a beer" is a wrong-verb error; use *pedí una cerveza*. *\*Mirando para* as a calque of "looking at" is wrong (*mirar* takes a direct object). [learner site] — search summary of [123teachme common errors](https://123teachme.com/learn_spanish/common_errors_3), [Spanish411](https://spanish411.net/Spanish-Using-Por-Para.asp) and [SpanishNewYork](https://www.spanishnewyork.com/common-mistakes-in-spanish.html). These pages were not opened individually, so I cannot say which page made which claim.
- **Comparison against a norm uses para.** *Es bajo para su edad* ("He's short for his age"). [learner site] — same search summary as above

### Inferences (my own linguistic analysis, not taken from a fetched source; check against the RAE *Nueva gramática* §29.8 or the DPD before use)
- **"Por = cause, para = purpose" fails** where one situation can be framed either way. *Lo hice por ti* ("I did it because of you / on your behalf") vs *Lo hice para ti* ("I made it for you", as a gift). *Lucha por la libertad* ("fights for freedom") uses *por* even though freedom looks like a goal. The Spanish frames the cause as a motivating force, not a destination. *Por* + infinitive can also express motive-as-purpose (*Lo dije por ayudar*, "I said it to help / out of a wish to help").
- **"Por = duration" fails** because Spanish usually uses no preposition: *Estudié tres horas* ("I studied for three hours"), or *durante*. *Por* + duration is acceptable but marked, and varies by region (more common in Latin America). English "I've lived here for two years" is *Vivo aquí desde hace dos años* or *Llevo dos años aquí*; English-style "for" with *por* or *para* is wrong here.
- **"Para = deadline" vs "por = vague time."** *Para el lunes* ("by Monday") vs *por la mañana* ("in the morning"). Learners translate "for Monday" correctly, but "in the morning" gets *en la mañana* (accepted in much of Latin America) or *\*para la mañana*.
- **"Por = through" works for space** (*por el parque*, "through the park"; *por la ventana*, "through the window"), but learners rarely produce it before advanced level (section 1). They tend to use *a través de* or *en* instead.
- **PERDE / PODER-style acronyms** (por: Price, Exchange, Reason, Duration, Emotion...; para: Purpose, Opinion, Destination, Effect, Recipient...) inherit all of the failures above. They also give no way to handle the "neither" cases. Because they are lists, they are subject to the decay Lam (2009) found for list-taught groups.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch a primary source showing the exact letters of the PERDE / PODER / "POR: PERSON-TIME-..." acronyms, or of the StudySpanish, Kwiziq, SpanishDict and Lawless pages. The acronym expansions above come from general knowledge, and the versions on learner sites differ.
- There is no corpus frequency of "for"-calque errors (*buscar por/para*, *esperar para*) for English L1 writers. I found none.

## 3. Error patterns a checker could flag: always wrong vs context-dependent

### Takeaway
A small set of patterns is unambiguous and safe to flag. These are almost all "neither" verbs followed by *por*/*para* + a noun phrase, plus fixed expressions with only one acceptable preposition. Cause vs purpose and duration errors depend on context and should be soft suggestions.

### Cited Findings
- Learner sites document *buscar*, *esperar* and *pedir* followed by *por*/*para* as a common learner calque, and *pagar* followed by the thing paid for without a preposition. — [FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/spanish/blog/spanish-verbs-prepositions/); search summary of [123teachme](https://123teachme.com/learn_spanish/common_errors_3)
- *Gracias por* is the dominant elicited *por* (cause) item in Pinto & Rex. — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- *Vivo por un año y medio* is a documented learner error for present-perfect duration. — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)
- Learners avoid or misplace the subjunctive after *para que* (shown with an Italian-L1 example, *\*para que el autor justifica*). — [Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)

### Inferences: candidate patterns (my own linguistic analysis; the cited items above support only some rows; check every row against the RAE or DPD before shipping)

**General condition for the "neither" verbs.** *Buscar*, *esperar*, *pedir* and *pagar* + *por/para* are errors only when the preposition comes directly after the verb and introduces the thing sought, awaited, requested or paid for. Many valid strings look similar:
- *te lo pido por favor* ("I'm asking you please");
- *pedir por los enfermos* ("pray for the sick");
- *pido para todos* ("I'll order for everyone");
- *lo busqué por toda la casa* ("I looked all over the house for it");
- *buscar por internet* ("search online");
- *esperé para hablar con él* ("I waited in order to talk to him").

So each rule needs a narrow pattern: verb + *por/para* + determiner/noun. Exclude *por favor*, *por* + place/means words (*todo/toda*, *todas partes*, *internet*, *aquí*, *la casa*...), and *para* + infinitive.

**Likely always wrong (safe to flag with the narrow pattern):**

| Learner form | Correct form | One-line reason for the learner |
|---|---|---|
| *gracias para la ayuda* | *gracias por la ayuda* ("thanks for the help") | You thank someone *because of* something, so use *por*. |
| *gracias para venir* | *gracias por venir* ("thanks for coming") | Same: what you're thanking for is the cause, so *por*. |
| *espero para el autobús* (esperar + *para* + det + noun) | *espero el autobús* ("I'm waiting for the bus") | *Esperar* already means "wait for"; add nothing. |
| *busco para mis llaves* (buscar + *para* + det + noun) | *busco mis llaves* ("I'm looking for my keys") | *Buscar* already means "look for" (use *a* before a person: *busco a Ana*). |
| *pagué para la cena* (pagar + *para* + det + food/thing noun) | *pagué la cena* ("I paid for dinner") | The thing you pay for is the direct object of *pagar*. |
| *pagué 10 dólares para el libro* (pagar + amount + *para* + noun) | *pagué 10 dólares por el libro* ("I paid $10 for the book") | Price/exchange is *por*. |
| *preocupado/a(s) para* + det/noun | *preocupado por* ("worried about") | You worry *because of* something, so *por*. |
| *para favor* | *por favor* ("please") | Fixed expression. |
| *para supuesto* | *por supuesto* ("of course") | Fixed expression. |
| *para ejemplo* (sentence-initial or between commas) | *por ejemplo* ("for example") | Fixed expression. |
| *para que* + indicative in a purpose clause (*para que lo sabes*) | *para que* + subjunctive (*te lo digo para que lo sepas*, "I'm telling you so that you know") | Purpose clauses with *para que* always take the subjunctive. |
| *pregunté por un café/una cerveza* (preguntar + *por* + food/drink/object noun) | *pedí un café* | *Preguntar* is ask a question; *pedir* is ask for a thing. *Preguntar por* + person = ask after someone, which is fine. |

**Context-dependent (suggest, don't assert):**

| Learner form | Likely intended | Why it can't be auto-flagged |
|---|---|---|
| *estudié para dos horas* | *estudié dos horas* / *durante dos horas* | Very likely an error for duration ("for two hours"), but *para* + time can mean a deadline or planned stay (*voy para dos semanas*, "I'm going for two weeks", is acceptable in some regions). |
| *estudié por dos horas* | *estudié dos horas* | Grammatical but marked; preferred-style advice only. |
| *vivo aquí por dos años* | *vivo aquí desde hace dos años* / *llevo dos años aquí* | Wrong if meaning "have lived for (up to now)"; fine in a past or future framing (*viví allí por dos años*). Needs tense context. |
| *trabajo por mi padre* | *trabajo para mi padre* ("I work for my father", employer) | *Por mi padre* = "on his behalf / because of him / in his place" is also valid. |
| *lo hice para ti* vs *por ti* | either | Gift/benefit vs motive/on behalf: both are correct with different meanings. |
| *por* + infinitive for purpose (*fui a la tienda por comprar leche*) | *para comprar leche* ("to buy milk") | Usually an error from English, but *por* + infinitive is valid for motive (*lo hizo por ayudar*) and for "yet to be done" (*la casa está por limpiar*). Flag only when an infinitive follows *por* after motion and purpose verbs (*ir*, *venir*, *salir*, *usar*, *necesitar*). Never flag *ir por* + noun (*fui por leche*, "I went to get milk", standard in Latin America; *a por* in Spain). |
| *busco por* + noun / *pido por* + noun / *pido para* + noun / *espero por* + noun | *busco X* / *pido X* / *espero X* | Often a calque of "look/ask/wait for", but valid readings abound: *buscar por toda la casa* (place), *pedir por los enfermos* (pray for), *pedir para todos* (order on behalf of), and *esperar por* (Caribbean/US usage; "wait on account of"). Suggest, don't assert. |
| *para eso* | *por eso* ("that's why") if causal | *¿Para eso me llamaste?* ("You called me for that?") is valid. |
| *por que* + subjunctive | *para que* + subjunctive if purpose | Valid after *luchar/abogar/apostar/votar por* (*luchamos por que haya justicia*). |
| *salgo por Madrid* | *salgo para Madrid* ("I'm leaving for Madrid") | *Por Madrid* = "through/around Madrid" is valid. |
| *voy a la biblioteca para estudiar* | *a estudiar* is more natural | Grammatical; style only. |
| *para mí, ...* / *por mí, ...* | *para mí* = "in my opinion"; *por mí* = "as far as I'm concerned / I don't mind" | Both valid. |
| *luchar para* | *luchar por* ("fight for [a cause]") | *Luchar para* + infinitive ("struggle in order to") is valid. |
| *mirar para* | *mirar* + object ("look at") | *Mirar para* + direction ("look toward", common in Latin America and Galicia) is valid. |

### Gaps
- No source I fetched gives precision/recall figures for automatic por/para error detection. I found no grammatical error correction (GEC) work specific to Spanish por/para.
- The regional acceptability of *esperar por*, *por* + duration, and *para* + duration needs checking in the RAE/DPD or corpus (CORPES XXI). Not done here.

## 4. Cognitive ELE approaches (one image) and evidence that they work

### Takeaway
Cognitive approaches replace the list with one schema per preposition. In the usual cognitive analyses (Delbecque 1996, Ruiz Campillo's "gramática operativa"), *para* points ahead to a goal or end point, and *por* marks the path, area or cause the event passes through or comes from. The best English-L1 evidence, Lam (2009), shows cognitive instruction resists decay better than list instruction. Llopis-García's broader cognitive-pedagogical studies show advantages mainly on production tasks. The evidence base is small.

### Cited Findings
- **Lam (2009) study.** Intermediate university Spanish learners. Two experimental groups were taught *por*/*para* with a cognitive-linguistic description; a comparison group got a list of uses to memorise. Both groups improved on fill-in-the-blank and multiple-choice tests immediately after instruction. On the delayed post-test the experimental groups held or improved their accuracy, while the comparison group "decreased significantly". The experimental groups were also more confident and "less likely to confuse por and para in free writing". Lam names as confounds the treatment content and delivery method, learner cognitive styles, and problems applying cognitive-linguistic models to teaching. The fetched page did not show author or journal. The attribution to Lam (2009), *Language Awareness*, comes from the DOI and from the Kissling et al. citation. [primary] — [Lam 2009, DOI 10.1080/09658410802147345](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658410802147345)
- **Kissling et al. (2018).** They cite Lam (2009) as evidence that systematic organization helps retrieval. They describe the polysemy network as centred on a spatial relation between a focus element and a background element. [review] — via search summary; Kissling et al. 2018, in *What is Applied Cognitive Linguistics?* ([UEA eprint](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/63557/); not opened)
- **Llopis-García's evidence.** Llopis-García & Alonso-Aparicio (2024), a case study on Spanish past tenses, report that an earlier (2019) study favoured cognitive-pedagogical instruction only on a production task. This is not a por/para study. [primary, via search snippet] — [todoele.net node 372076](https://www.todoele.net/node/372076) (page returned 404 when fetched; the claim comes from the search snippet)
- **Llopis-García's thesis.** It applies Ruiz Campillo's pedagogical approach to cognitive grammar with German learners and argues that prototypes should organize grammar teaching. — [Columbia Academic Commons](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/9pk7-yv82); [Redalyc review](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92169591005/92169591005.epub)
- **Castañeda Castro.** "Gramática cognitiva y enseñanza de la gramática de ELE" argues for systematic use of images when presenting grammar. — via search summary ([core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/62907051.pdf), not opened)
- **Frago (2020, marcoELE).** "Un esquema, cuatro preposiciones" is a teaching proposal for *a*, *de*, *por* and *para* built on conceptual metaphor and image schemas. — via search summary ([Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92163387001/92163387001.pdf), not opened)
- **D'Antonio, Albiol-Martín & Priego-Casanova (2025).** "Por y para: enfoques y su didáctica" (*Studi di Glottodidattica* 10(1)) contrasts cognitive and meta-operational grammar for teaching por/para. It is a theoretical article with no empirical results reported in the abstract. — [Studi di Glottodidattica](https://ojs.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/view/2156)

### Inferences
- A "one image" explanation could be: *para* is an arrow pointing to a target (purpose, recipient, destination, deadline, standard of comparison); *por* is the path or force behind or through the event (cause, motive, exchange, route, means, vague time or place, agent). It covers cases the "for" rule cannot (*gracias por*, *pagar por*, *luchar por*, *por la mañana*). It still does not explain why *buscar*, *esperar* and *pedir* take no preposition; those need a separate "the verb already contains 'for'" note. This is a synthesis of the sources above, not a quote from Ruiz Campillo.
- On current evidence (one English-L1 experiment, Lam 2009, plus indirect evidence from other structures), the cognitive approach looks better for retention and free writing. It is not proven better on immediate test scores.

### Gaps
- I did not find a por/para-specific empirical study by Ruiz Campillo, Llopis-García or Castañeda, or their exact one-image wording for por/para. Reading Frago (2020) or Llopis-García, Real Espinosa & Ruiz Campillo, *Qué gramática enseñar, qué gramática aprender* (2012), would fill this.
- I did not obtain Lam (2009)'s sample size or effect sizes; only the abstract was available.
