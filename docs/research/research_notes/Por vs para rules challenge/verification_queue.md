## Items with sourced verdicts

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

**13. Purpose porque / por que + subjunctive: CONFIRMED, with detail (SN, DPD)**
- The DPD entry *porque* says *porque* is also used as a final (purpose) conjunction with the subjunctive, meaning *para que*. It calls this use normal in earlier periods but "hoy está decayendo" (now in decline). — [DPD, porque](https://www.rae.es/dpd/porque)
- One-word *porque* is accepted for purpose only when the verb is subjunctive and the clause can be read as purpose. — [DPD, porque](https://www.rae.es/dpd/porque)

**14. "luchar por que haya paz": MODIFIED, both spellings accepted (SN, DPD)**
- When *por* is required by a verb, noun or adjective, and it introduces a purpose clause in the subjunctive, the DPD accepts either *por que* or *porque*. — [DPD, porque](https://www.rae.es/dpd/porque)
- DPD examples: «Me preocupo por que los más desprotegidos estudien» ("I make sure the most vulnerable get an education"); «Rogué porque su agonía no fuera larga» ("I prayed that his agony would not be long").
- So "luchar por que haya paz" ("to fight for there to be peace") is correct, and "luchar porque haya paz" is also accepted. Separate *por que* is the transparent spelling. The DPD does not rule one form wrong.

**12. agradecer: CONFIRMED / MODIFIED (SN, DPD)**
- In general Spanish, *agradecer* is mostly transitive, usually with an indirect object too: *agradecer algo a alguien* ("to thank someone for something"). — [DPD, agradecer](https://www.rae.es/dpd/agradecer)
- "En el español de América se emplea también, con frecuencia, como intransitivo, seguido de un complemento con *por*": *agradecer a alguien por algo*. — [DPD, agradecer](https://www.rae.es/dpd/agradecer)
- Verdict: the DPD records *agradecer por* as a frequent American use and does not condemn it. The general norm is *le agradezco su ayuda* ("I thank you for your help"). A learner site calls *agradecer por el favor* a mistake ([Sublearn](https://sublearn.com/learn/es/vocabulary/agradecer), L). That is stricter than the DPD and should not be followed.

**20. estar por + infinitive: CONFIRMED for two meanings; "inclined to" (Spain) STILL UNVERIFIED (SN, DPD)**
- General Spanish meaning: something is still waiting for the action to be done to it. Example: *aún está por escribirse* ("it is yet to be written"); compare *quedan tres capítulos por leer*. — [DPD, estar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/estar)
- *Estar para* + infinitive means "about to, or ready to". The DPD adds "en algunos países de América, se usa también, en estos casos, *estar por*". Its example is Argentine: *Es martes y está por llover* ("It's Tuesday and it's about to rain"). The DPD says *estar para* is the more general form for this meaning. — [DPD, estar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/estar)
- No snippet showed the DPD giving the meaning "inclined to, in favour of" (*estoy por irme* = "I'm tempted to leave"). An RILCE article lists a modal reading, *estar por* = *estar a favor de*, alongside imminence and negation (*eso está por ver*, "that remains to be seen"). — [RILCE, Univ. Navarra](https://revistas.unav.edu/index.php/rilce/article/download/2888/2707) (SN)
- Keep the "inclined to" meaning, but grade it RK/SN, not DPD.

**21. esperar por: CONFIRMED (SN, DPD plus Fundéu as reported by newspapers)**
- The DPD prefers transitive *esperar* in educated use (*esperar a alguien*, "to wait for someone"). It says intransitive *esperar por* is not uncommon in much of America, especially the Caribbean, and may come partly from English "wait for". — [DPD, esperar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/esperar)
- Fundéu advises *esperar algo* over *esperar por algo* and calls the latter a calque from English. *Por* is fine when it means cause, duration or purpose: *esperó por tres horas* ("he waited for three hours"). — [Panamá América, Fundéu note](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/dia-d/esperar-algo-mejor-que-esperarporalgo-842881); [Acento, Fundéu Guzmán Ariza](https://acento.com.do/cultura/fundeu-guzman-ariza-esperar-algo-mejor-esperar-algo-8403414.html) (SN)
- Mexico is not singled out in any source found. Say "much of America, especially the Caribbean".

**22. ir a por: MODIFIED, exact DPD wording not obtained (SN plus F secondary)**
- The DPD entry *ir* refers *ir a por* to entry *a*, section 2 ("6. ir a por. → a2, 2"). — [DPD, ir](https://rae.es/dpd/ir) (SN)
- A University of Piura language column (fetched) cites the DPD 2005. It reports that *a por* after verbs of movement, meaning "en busca de" (to go and get), is felt to be odd by Latin American speakers, and that both forms alternate in parts of Spain. It also reports that educated usage prefers plain *por*: *Ve por ayuda* ("Go get help"). The column concludes that *a por* should not be censured. — [UDEP, Castellano actual: Duda resuelta: voy a por](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-voy-a-por/) (F, but it paraphrases the DPD rather than quoting it)
- The phrase "no hay razones para censurar" was not found verbatim.
- The current (2nd edition, 2023+) DPD wording was not obtained.

**23. pedir por vs preguntar por: CONFIRMED (SN, DPD)**
- "Debe evitarse en español el uso de *pedir por* con el significado de 'preguntar'" (avoid *pedir por* meaning "to ask about"). The DPD attributes that use to Catalan speakers and parts of America, probably via English "ask for". — [DPD, pedir](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pedir)
- The DPD accepts three uses of *pedir por*:
  - asking a price: *Le piden seis mil liras por un simple afeitado* ("They charge him six thousand lire for a simple shave");
  - praying for someone: *pedir por los enfermos* ("to pray for the sick");
  - in the Southern Cone, asking for something or on someone's behalf.
  — [DPD, pedir](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pedir)
- *Preguntar por* = "to ask about or after someone or something" (SpanishDict, L). The snippet of the DPD *preguntar* entry was cut off before its construction notes. — [DPD, preguntar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/preguntar); [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/compare/pedir/preguntar%20por)

**5. para con: CONFIRMED (SN, RAE Buen uso / NGLE)**
- RAE: "*Para con* expresa actitud favorable o desfavorable: *Fue buena para con sus padres*" ("She was good to her parents"). — [RAE, Las preposiciones (Buen uso del español)](https://www.rae.es/buen-uso-español/las-preposiciones)
- The NGLE notes that *con* and *para con* alternate in these contexts with very little difference in meaning: *amable con / para con todos* ("kind to everyone"). — [RAE Gramática, secuencias de preposiciones](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/secuencias-de-preposiciones)

**19. Etymology of para: MODIFIED (F, Wiktionary; DLE and DCECH not reached)**
- Wiktionary, Spanish *para*, Etymology 1: "From earlier *pora* (a combination of *por* and *a*, or from Latin *prō ad*) via assimilation". It also notes influence from archaic *par*. — [Wiktionary, para](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/para) (F)
- Wiktionary gives *pro ad*, not *per ad*. The DLE etymology line, which is believed but not verified to read "Del ant. *pora*, y este del lat. *pro ad*", could not be fetched. The Corominas (DCECH) article was not reached.
- Verdict: "old *pora* < *por a* / Latin *pro ad*" is supported. Drop "per + ad" as the stated source, or mark it as unverified.
- The same page confirms deadline *para*: *Necesito tu decisión para fin de mes* ("I need your decision by the end of the month"). It also confirms point-in-time *para*: *Para septiembre, me estaré quedando atrás* ("By September I'll be falling behind"). This is partial support for item 10 (*para entonces*, "by then").

**3. por mucho que / por más que: CONFIRMED as a teaching rule (SN, university handout plus L)**
- Indicative presents the obstacle as a real fact: *Por más que estudia, no aprueba* ("However much he studies, he doesn't pass"). Subjunctive presents it as a hypothesis or as not asserted: *Por más que estudie, no aprobará* ("However much he studies, he won't pass"). — [Masaryk Univ. handout, oraciones concesivas](https://is.muni.cz/el/fss/jaro2020/CJVS402/um/1_oraciones_concesivas.pdf?kod=SPp411) (SN)
- A learner site claims that indicative after *por más que* is wrong for a failed attempt. — [Colanguage](https://app.colanguage.com/spanish/grammar/las-concesivas) (L)
- That claim is contradicted by common use, e.g. *Por más que lo intenté, no pude* ("Try as I might, I couldn't"), and should not be used.
- No NGLE passage on *por más que* was retrieved.

### Inferences
- In every case where the DPD speaks, it is descriptive and regional, not prohibitive. *agradecer por*, *esperar por* and *estar por* ("about to") are labelled American uses, not errors. The only clear "debe evitarse" (should be avoided) found is *pedir por* meaning *preguntar*. App copy should read "in Latin America" or "in Spain", never "wrong".
- Item 14: the app should accept both *luchar por que* and *luchar porque*.

### Inferences (RK: researcher's knowledge, unsourced)
1. *quedar por* + infinitive is standard: *Quedan tres capítulos por leer* ("There are three chapters left to read"). It parallels the DPD's general sense of *estar por*, "pending" (item 20). Likely confirmed.
2. *Dos por tres* ("two times three") for multiplication is standard. Likely confirmed.
4. *Por no estudiar, suspendió* ("He failed for not studying") is standard *por* + infinitive for cause. Likely confirmed.
6. *Salió para no volver* ("He left, never to return") is a known use for an unexpected or subsequent result. Not verified against the NGLE.
7. *Dar por* + participle or adjective means "to consider". Examples: *lo dieron por muerto* ("they gave him up for dead"), *lo doy por hecho* ("I take it for granted"). Likely confirmed via the DLE *dar* entry (not fetched).
8. Agent with *de*: *conocido de todos* ("known to everyone"), *rodeado de amigos* ("surrounded by friends"). This is standard with verbs of state or feeling (B&B, NGLE). Not verified.
9. *Por dos semanas* ("for two weeks") is common everywhere. A preference for *durante* or no preposition in Spain is plausible. Fundéu treats duration *por* as correct (*esperó por tres horas*, item 21). This is the only sourced point here. *Voy para dos semanas* ("I'm going for two weeks") is unverified and should not be added.
10. *Por abril* ("around April") for approximate time; *por allá* ("around there") vs *para allá* ("toward there"). Likely standard; not verified.
11. Verb regimes: *optar por*, *apostar por*, *disculparse por*, *felicitar por*, *interesarse por*, *prepararse para*, *alcanzar para* and *bastar para* are all standard. Note *felicitar por* vs *felicitar a alguien por algo* (to congratulate someone on something). Not verified against the DPD.
15. *No estoy para bromas* ("I'm in no mood for jokes") is standard. Not verified.
16. The pairs (*hablo por ella* "on her behalf" / *para ella* "to her or for her"; *lo cambié por otro* "exchanged it for another" / *para otro* "for someone else"; *vino por el libro* "came to get the book" / *para* ...) are standard contrasts. *Estudiar para médico* ("to study to be a doctor") is believed to be accepted (it appears in the DLE under *para*?). Unverified. Do not present *por médico* as an option.
17. Colombian *de a poquito* ("little by little") and *de a uno* ("one at a time") are general American *de a*, not Colombia-only. *Mandar por* ("to send for") is unverified.
18. The fixed phrases are all standard: *por cierto* ("by the way"), *por lo visto* ("apparently"), *por completo* ("completely"), *por si las moscas* ("just in case"), *para siempre* ("forever"), *para nada* ("not at all"), *para variar* ("for a change"), *no es para tanto* ("it's not that big a deal"), *para que sepas* ("just so you know"). Not verified this pass.
