# Rival analyses of por vs para (challenge to "endpoint ahead -> para, otherwise por")

Grading key: **F** = fetched and read in full; **SN** = search-result snippet or abstract only; **L** = learner site; **RK** = unsourced background knowledge, unverified in this pass. A WebFetch rate limit hit partway through, so several sources stayed at SN level.

## 1. Which analyses propose a different core?

### Takeaway
The main source behind the earlier synthesis (D'Antonio, Albiol-Martín & Priego-Casanova 2025) does **not** frame por as the default. It gives two parallel images. In the cognitive one, por profiles the path or process and para the goal. In the meta-operational one (Adamczewski / Matte Bon), para gives a new, open datum (Phase I) and por a pre-existing, given one (Phase II). In final (purpose) contexts it calls **para "la forma neutra"** and por the subjective, speaker-involved choice. Separately, Funes gives por its own positive prototype, [+Locativo] (path through a place), and Kissling/Tyler give *each* preposition its own spatial central sense. None of these treats por as a semantically empty elsewhere case.

### Cited Findings
- **D'Antonio et al. 2025 is a position paper with no data.** Its first two authors teach at secondary schools (Liceo Statale Gaio Valerio Catullo; Colegio JABY), the third at Fu Jen Catholic University. Its evidence is "la experiencia de los autores" with Italian, Chinese-speaking and native students. The abstract says it presents differences "as a pretext" to explore teaching applications. (F) — [D'Antonio, Albiol-Martín & Priego-Casanova 2025, Studi di Glottodidattica 2025(1):57-68](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Cognitive image (attributed to De Bruyne 1999 and Delbecque 1996).** Por presents the event "desde el punto de vista del trayecto o proceso, más que desde el destino final" (from the path, not the final destination), for example *caminar por el parque* 'walk through the park', *lo hizo por amor* 'did it out of love', *enviar por correo* 'send by post'. Para introduces "una meta o destinatario final", for example *Este regalo es para ti* 'this gift is for you', *Estudia para ser médico* 'studies to become a doctor', *Trabaja para la empresa* 'works for the company'. (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §3](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
  - The paper treats Delbecque only briefly and secondhand, as general claims about construal, trajectory and force dynamics. It gives no page-level account of Delbecque's actual analysis. (F, same source)
- **Meta-operational image (Adamczewski 1991; Matte Bon 2016).** "Para está en la Fase I, porque propone un dato nuevo … es el paradigma abierto; en cambio, por está en la Fase II, es el paradigma cerrado … es preexistente al elemento que lo antecede; por eso … por expresa la causa, pues esta siempre precede al efecto." (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §4](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
  - Example pair: *Trabajo por mi jefe* 'I work because of / on behalf of my boss' (the boss as a prior cause; reading depends on intonation, thanks to or because of) vs *Trabajo para mi jefe* 'I work for my boss' (the boss as goal, "motor de la acción"). (F)
  - Example pair: *Lo han llamado por esa tarea* 'they called him because of that task' (A and B simultaneous, nothing new) vs *Lo han llamado para esa tarea* 'they called him to do that task' (B follows A, new element). (F)
  - Example pair: *Te devuelvo el dinero … para cumplir con mi promesa* 'I'm returning the money in order to keep my promise' vs *… por cumplir con mi promesa* 'because of / out of keeping my promise'. (F)
- **Markedness claim that inverts the earlier synthesis.** In *me he quedado en la oficina por terminar mi trabajo con tiempo* 'I stayed at the office (in order) to finish my work in time', the authors say por is a strategy "probablemente más subjetiva" and more persuasive "que la forma neutra para". They add: "Por nunca presenta el fin de forma contundente", and that por and para of purpose "conllevan un sentido pragmático completamente distinto". (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §4](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Cause and purpose overlap.** *Pasa por la ciudad por / para no pasar por la Vía de Cintura* 'he goes through town so as not to take the ring road' accepts both prepositions. (F, same source)
- **The authors' own conclusion is eclectic, not one-image.** Both frameworks are too abstract for beginners ("los estudiantes podrían beneficiarse más de reglas gramaticales simples"). Some students prefer lists of uses. They call for integrating both frameworks with traditional methods. (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §§6-7](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **They also name a three-way analysis of cause** (Mendizábal 1997): logical cause, real cause and virtual cause (= purpose), i.e. para as "virtual cause". (F, cited inside D'Antonio et al.; Mendizábal not checked)
- **Funes 2020: por has a positive locative prototype.** Its semantic values form one polysemous item whose "atributo más prototípico es el valor [+Locativo]". Funes 2022 treats non-prototypical por (por as focaliser) as derived by losing or adding attributes. The abstract also notes there is no agreement in the grammars on how to characterise por semantically. (SN) — [Funes 2020, Textos en Proceso 6(1):27-54, repository record](https://repositorioslatinoamericanos.uchile.cl/handle/2250/4395458); [Funes 2022, RLA](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/202416)
- **Kissling/Tyler et al. 2018: two polysemy networks.** Each is a "systematically connected polysemy network whose central sense is a spatial relationship", built with a usage-based, cognitive linguistics approach. Their review of nine popular intermediate textbooks found them consistent with the researchers' concerns about list-based teaching. (SN) — [Kissling, Tyler, Warren & Negrete 2018, in Tyler, Huang & Jan (eds.), What is Applied Cognitive Linguistics?, pp. 229-256, via core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/232791566.pdf) (the PDF itself returned 403/HTML, so this rests on the snippet only)
- **Delbecque 1996 exists:** "Towards a Cognitive Account of the Use of the Prepositions Por and Para in Spanish", in Casad (ed.), *Cognitive Linguistics in the Redwoods*, pp. 249-318. No source found describing its actual central images. The "involvement" label belongs to a different paper, Cornillie & Delbecque 2008, on speaker commitment. (SN) — [search results incl. Benjamins listings](https://benjamins.com/catalog/cagral.6.07del)
- **Bull (1965), "por = causal-motivational, para = final / directed".** Widely repeated in older pedagogy as para looking forward to a goal and por looking back to a cause or motive. (RK, not verified this pass)
- **Whitley (Spanish/English Contrasts).** Usually described as presenting para as a forward-looking goal and por as cause, path or exchange. (RK, not verified)
- **Ruiz Campillo, Castañeda Castro.** No por/para-specific text by Ruiz Campillo was found. The cognitive ELE volume's por/para chapter is by Susana Mendo Murillo. (SN) — [search results, todoele / RESLA](https://lwc1.benjamins.com/catalog/resla.15064.rom)
- **Guerra Magdaleno 2014, "La imagen es la regla".** A visual-image treatment of the por/para contrast, cited by D'Antonio et al. (F, citation only)

### Inferences
- The earlier synthesis's two-part claim ("one test" plus "por is the default") is **not** what its main cited source says. D'Antonio et al. give *two* images, endorse eclecticism, and in purpose contexts explicitly call **para** the neutral form. That makes "por = default" the synthesis's own addition, and for purpose clauses it runs against the source.
- "Endpoint ahead" fits the cognitive half (para = goal or recipient) well. But the meta-operational half adds a dimension the test lacks: **timing relative to the main event and speaker stance**. A purpose that already exists before the action and is presented as a motive can take por (*por terminar el trabajo* 'to get the work finished'). A single "is there an endpoint?" question cannot predict this. Both *lo han llamado por/para esa tarea* have a task "ahead" in some sense, yet both are grammatical, with different meanings.
- Funes and Kissling/Tyler give por its own positive core (a path through a place, a medium). Calling por "everything else" therefore misdescribes the cognitive literature, even if it is a useful classroom shortcut.

### Gaps
- Delbecque 1996 itself was not reached. Its actual proposal (whether it says por is more "subjective" or "involved", or gives a different schema) is unverified. The earlier synthesis's reliance on it via D'Antonio is weak, because D'Antonio paraphrases it only in general terms.
- Lunn, Pérez Saldanya, Real Espinosa, Castañeda Castro and Matte Bon's 2016 chapter were not reached. No claims are made about them.
- "Pinto & Rex" (thought to be a classroom acquisition study in Hispania, c. 2006) could not be located. A search returned nothing matching. (RK that it exists, unverified)

## 2. Markedness: which term is marked?

### Takeaway
No source examined argues outright that por is the unmarked, default member. Raw frequency favours por, but D'Antonio et al. call para the neutral form for purpose, and the cognitive accounts give each preposition its own core. The diachronic data point the same way: para took over purpose from por only from the 18th century. Treat "por = default" as a classroom heuristic, not a finding from the literature.

### Cited Findings
- **Frequency.** A list of most common Spanish words gives *por* 190,975 tokens (rank 12) vs *para* 126,061 (rank 15). The source corpus is not stated and is not CREA. (SN, Wikipedia via search) — [Most common words in Spanish](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Most_common_words_in_Spanish)
- **CREA** publishes lists of the 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 most frequent forms (written CREA v3.2: ~140,000 documents, ~154 million forms). The por/para rows were not retrieved. (SN) — [RAE CREA escrito](https://www.rae.es/banco-de-datos/crea/crea-escrito)
- **Para took over purpose from por late.** From the 18th century, para replaced por with infinitive complements and became the majority purposive variant. Allative para with a noun complement (person and place destinations) declined after the 14th century. (F, abstract) — [Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018, "Allative to purposive grammaticalisation", IHLL 16:195-221](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/)
- **Para as the neutral purpose marker** (*la forma neutra para*); por of purpose is more subjective and speaker-committed. (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **What "unmarked" means in markedness theory.** The unmarked member is more frequent and prototypical, and can cover the general case. (SN, general) — [Haspelmath, "Against markedness"](https://zenodo.org/record/815500/files/AgainstMarkedness(JL).pdf)

### Inferences
- The frequency gap (about 1.5:1 if the snippet is right) is consistent with por being unmarked by frequency. But much of por's count comes from fixed or grammatical uses: the passive agent (*escrito por Cervantes* 'written by Cervantes'), *por eso* 'that's why', *por favor* 'please', *por ejemplo* 'for example', *por ciento* 'percent', *por lo menos* 'at least'. Frequency does not show that por is the semantic "elsewhere" choice in a free-choice slot.
- In the one slot where both compete productively, purpose + infinitive, the history and D'Antonio both make **para** the default and por the marked, motive-flavoured choice. So in that slot the markedness runs **opposite** to the earlier synthesis's claim.
- "Neither is default" fits the cognitive literature best: two networks, each with a spatial prototype. Por's prototype is a path or medium, para's a direction or goal.

### Gaps
- No corpus count of por vs para in free-choice contexts was found.
- No L1 acquisition-order data was found. (No search returned child-language studies on por/para in this pass.)
- No typological study of cause/purpose markedness was located.

## 3. Does the etymology claim hold?

### Takeaway
Partly. Every account gives para (via older *pora*) a second element from Latin AD 'to, toward', so "para carries an allative, toward-the-endpoint element" is fair. But scholars disagree on the first element: PRO + AD, PER + AD, *per a*, or a fusion of Old Spanish *por + a*. And the endpoint meaning was originally *destination*. Para became the main purpose marker only from the 18th century. "Para was built to mark the endpoint" is a fair shorthand for destination and recipient, but overstated for purpose. D'Antonio et al. garble the Corominas point.

### Cited Findings
- **Standard reference view.** Para comes from *pora*, which comes from a fusion of Latin PRO and/or PER with AD (Penny 2006: 273; Torrens Álvarez 2007: 117). Alvar & Pottier (1987: 300) hold that the [a] of *para* requires an initial PER + AD. Riiho (1979: 195) says that, except in Portuguese, it is pointless to argue for PRO AD over PER AD. (F) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022, Scriptum digital 11:105-130, §2](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- **The pora > para vowel change.** It is explained by analogy with the oath formula *par* (Lapesa 1981: 214; Corominas & Pascual 1981, s.v. *para*; Penny 2006: 274), or by regressive vowel assimilation (Hanssen 1913), or by weakening of the unstressed vowel (Ueda 2015). (F) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- **Rival origin: *per a*.** Fernández-Ordóñez (2022) argues para does **not** come from pora but from *per a*, via [e] opening before [ɾ], spreading into central Spanish from Leonese *pera*. (F, as summarised) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- **Rival origin: fusion of por + a.** Torres Cacoullos & Bauman argue para arose by fusion of older *por* + *a* through usage-based chunking. Early para + person-destination still showed *a*'s separate meaning. (F, abstract) — [Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/)
- **Chronology.** Most studies date the generalisation of para to the late 13th century. Penny says pora "se ve eliminada" by then; Lapesa says para "no se extendió hasta la época alfonsí"; Riiho counts 85% para vs 15% pora in the second half of the 13th century. Hanssen puts the end of the change in the 14th century, Ueda in the 15th. Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias show the dating depends on how the abbreviation ꝑa is read; the early dates may come from editors expanding it as *para*. (F) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- **D'Antonio et al.'s version.** Per Corominas (2008, the *Breve diccionario*), por (from Latin *pro*) is first attested about 938 and carried the values of both modern por and para. Para appears about 1250, "plausible derivación de la antigua forma pora que, a su vez, provendría de par … y la preposición a". (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §2](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **Brea 1985 (quoted in D'Antonio).** PER and PRO reduced to a common *pr; in Iberian languages para / per a has a second element from AD "indicando así de un modo más claro el fin en el espacio, la dirección", so para expresses purpose and por cause, "aunque la distinción no se haya observado con un rigor absoluto". (F, quoted) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §2](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)

### Inferences
- D'Antonio et al. appear to garble Corominas. Lapesa, Corominas & Pascual and Penny invoke *par* to explain the vowel change pora > para, not as the source of pora itself (Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022). The earlier synthesis should not cite D'Antonio for etymology; it should cite Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias, Penny or Torres Cacoullos & Bauman directly.
- What holds up: the AD element in para signals direction toward an endpoint (Brea; all origin accounts). What does not: the implication that para has always been the purpose marker. Before the 18th century, por + infinitive was the majority purposive form (Torres Cacoullos & Bauman). Modern "por + infinitive = motive / pending action" (*está por hacer* 'it is yet to be done', *por no molestar* 'so as not to bother') is a remnant of that older system. It is a principled exception to the endpoint test, not noise.
- Brea's own caveat ("no … con un rigor absoluto") argues against presenting the test as exceptionless.

### Gaps
- The DCECH entry (Corominas & Pascual 1980-91, s.v. *para*) and Penny's *Gramática histórica* pages were not read directly; only the summaries above.
- The Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2014 chapter "Las preposiciones por, pora y para" was not reached.

## 4. Do classroom studies support one-image teaching?

### Takeaway
The studies support **cognitive-linguistics networks** (connected meanings with spatial prototypes for *both* prepositions), not a single binary test. The effects are positive but limited and task-dependent. One follow-up found an advantage on multiple-choice but not on fill-in-the-blank. Lam 2009 was later described, by Lam herself, as showing improvement only in specific aspects. No study found tests a one-question heuristic like "endpoint ahead?".

### Cited Findings
- **Lam 2009.** "Applying cognitive linguistics to teaching Spanish prepositions por and para", *Language Awareness* 18(1):2-18. Two experimental groups of intermediate university learners got a cognitive linguistic description; a comparison group got a list of uses to memorise. The comparison group's accuracy reportedly declined significantly; experimental learners were more confident and confused por/para less in free writing. The snippet's attribution to Lam's abstract was not confirmed. (SN) — [Taylor & Francis record](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658410802147345)
- **Lam's own later reading.** Lam 2018 (Canadian Journal of Applied Linguistics) describes the 2009 study as having found improvement in only specific aspects of learner performance. (SN) — [Lam 2018, CJAL 21(1)](https://releve.erudit.org/fr/revues/cjal/2018-v21-n1-cjal03922/1050808ar.pdf)
- **Kissling, Tyler, Warren & Negrete 2018.** First stage of a larger project. It compares explicit polysemy-network explanations with instruction that does not present meanings as a network. Outcome statistics were not retrieved. (SN) — [core.ac.uk record](https://core.ac.uk/download/232791566.pdf)
- **Follow-up study** ("More evidence that a usage-based, applied cognitive linguistics approach is effective for teaching the Spanish prepositions por and para", Benjamins, DOI 10.1075/pl.22005.kis). Intact intermediate classes in three conditions; only two group sizes appeared in the snippets: traditional list-based instruction in one lesson (n = 32), and incremental Principled Polysemy Networks over time (n = 34). "The usage-based CL approach was found to confer the most advantage for learners on a multiple-choice test, but not a fill-in-the-blank test." (SN; full text 403) — [Benjamins catalogue](https://benjamins.com/catalog/pl.22005.kis)
- **Kissling & Arnold 2022.** Preliminary evidence that the CL approach works for novice learners (prepositions and aspect). Design and sample size not retrieved. (SN) — [todoele bibliography entry](https://todoele.net/bibliografia/preliminary-evidence-applied-cognitive-linguistics-effective-novice-learners)
- **Critical review of polysemy-network teaching.** It argues a micro-level, sense-by-sense analysis is needed to show what learners actually gain. (SN) — [search result, Benjamins](https://benjamins.com/catalog/fol.00061.che)
- **Field review** (Review of Cognitive Linguistics): the evidence is "manifestly favourable", but questions remain about the scope of the approach and which properties produce its effects. (SN) — [Benjamins, RCL](https://benjamins.com/catalog/rcl.9.1.11boe)
- **Practitioners' doubts.** D'Antonio et al. report that cognitive-grammar and meta-operational teaching is too abstract for beginners, and that some students do better with lists. This is anecdotal, not experimental. (F) — [D'Antonio et al. 2025, §6](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)

### Inferences
- What has been tested is "connected networks beat disconnected lists", in small intact-class designs (about 30 per group), mostly at intermediate level. The advantage depends on the measure; production (fill-in-the-blank) is the measure closest to real use, and there it did not hold. That is thin support for promising learners that "one test works".
- A network with two spatial prototypes is closer to "neither is default" than to "por is the default".
- No null-result study was found beyond the fill-in-the-blank non-advantage. Publication bias in a small, enthusiastic research area is plausible but undocumented here.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes, delayed post-tests and full designs for Lam 2009, Kissling et al. 2018, the follow-up and Kissling & Arnold 2022 could not be retrieved (403s and a fetch rate limit).
- The author list of the follow-up was not confirmed (the "kis" DOI suggests Kissling as first author).
- No study comparing a single heuristic with full networks was found.

## Overall adversarial verdict (for the report writer)
1. **"Por is the default" is weakly sourced.** D'Antonio et al. say the reverse for purpose (para is neutral). History says the reverse for purpose (para took over from por from the 18th century). Cognitive accounts (Funes; Kissling/Tyler) give por a positive core. The only support found is raw frequency, which grammatical fixed uses inflate. Suggested revision: present it as a fallback heuristic ("if no endpoint, try por"), not as a fact about Spanish.
2. **"One test" understates the speaker-stance dimension** (Matte Bon / Adamczewski Phase I vs II; por's subjective, motive reading of purpose). Pairs such as *lo llamaron por/para esa tarea*, *por/para no pasar por la Vía de Cintura* and *me quedé por/para terminar* need a second clause in the rule: "por when the goal is presented as the motive behind the act."
3. **The etymology should be re-sourced and softened.** "Para contains AD 'toward'" is safe. "Para was built to mark the endpoint" is fair for destination and recipient, not for purpose. Drop D'Antonio as the etymology source; it misreads the *par* analogy.
4. **The classroom evidence supports networks, modestly**, not a one-image binary. Effects are task-dependent (multiple-choice yes, fill-in-the-blank no in the follow-up).
