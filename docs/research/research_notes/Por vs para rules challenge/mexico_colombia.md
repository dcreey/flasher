# Por and para in Mexico and Colombia: testing the earlier claims

## Are the earlier claims right for Mexico and for Colombia?

### Takeaway
Mexico: the DEM, a descriptive dictionary of Mexican Spanish built from a corpus, confirms *ir por* ('to go and get'), *estar por* ('about to'), *faltar ... para las* (telling time), *por si las dudas* and *pa* as everyday Mexican uses. It also lists duration *por* ("Salió por un mes"). The DEM does **not** list *por mientras* or *por lo mientras*, and it does not list *esperar por*. Colombia: one 2026 corpus study (snippet only) names Colombia as a country that **prefers *por mientras***. That contradicts the inference drawn earlier from the Diccionario de americanismos (DAm), which lists Mexico but not Colombia.

### Cited Findings

**1. *ir por* (Latin America) vs *ir a por* (Spain)**
- MEXICO, F: the DEM lists *por* sense 14 as "Señala lo que se va a buscar o a llevar de un lado a otro" (marks what you go to fetch). Its examples are "Voy por agua" ('I'm going for water'), "Salió por pan" ('He went out for bread') and "Subió por el libro" ('She went up for the book'). The DEM has no *a por*. — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/por)
- GENERAL AMERICA, SN: the *Nueva gramática*, as relayed by search summaries, says *a por* after verbs of motion is typical of Spain and is felt to be odd in America, where *por* is preferred. The DPD has a cross-reference entry for "ir a por" under *ir*. — [alnavio.es summarizing RAE](https://alnavio.es/ir-por-agua-o-ir-a-por-agua-la-rae-resuelve-la-duda); [DPD, ir](https://rae.es/dpd/ir) (blocked; seen only in search results)
- Castellano Actual (Universidad de Piura, Peru), SN, says that per the DPD both forms alternate, but educated usage prefers *por*. — [UDEP, "voy a por"](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-voy-a-por/)
- COLOMBIA: I found no source specific to Colombia. RK: *ir por* is the norm there too, and *a por* sounds peninsular.
- **Verdict:** confirmed for Mexico (F). Assumed for Colombia from the pan-American statement (SN/RK).

**2. "un cuarto para las ocho" for telling time**
- MEXICO, F: the DEM lists *para* sense 3, "Señala un plazo determinado" (marks a deadline or set time), with the example "Faltan 10 minutos para las 5" ('It's ten to five'). That shows the *para las + hour* frame is standard Mexican usage. The DEM entry for *cuarto* has "cuarto de hora" but no worked example of "cuarto para las". — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/para); [DEM, cuarto](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/cuarto)
- COLOMBIA: two searches turned up no source. RK: Colombians use both "un cuarto para las ocho" and "las ocho menos cuarto", and *para* is the more colloquial of the two. **Unverified.**
- **Verdict:** Mexico supported (F, through "faltan ... para las"). Colombia is a gap.

**3. *estar por* + infinitive = 'about to' in America vs 'inclined to' in Spain**
- MEXICO, F: the DEM lists *estar* sense 7, "Estar por… Haber una persona o encontrarse a punto de comenzar algo, o alguna cosa a punto de suceder" (to be about to start something, or for something to be about to happen). Its examples are *estar por comer* ('to be about to eat') and *estar por terminar una novela* ('to be about to finish a novel'). The DEM gives no 'inclined to' sense for the phrase and no register label. — [DEM, estar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/estar)
- DPD, SN only: according to the search summary, in Spain the 'about to' sense is normal only with *caer* and *llegar* ("está por llegar", 'he's about to arrive'), while several American countries use *estar por* + infinitive freely to mean 'about to'. — [DPD, estar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/estar) (403 when fetched)
- COLOMBIA: no source. RK: the 'about to' sense is common, as in "está por llover" ('it's about to rain').
- **Verdict:** confirmed for Mexico (F). "A tendency, not a boundary" fits the DPD's Spain exception for *caer* and *llegar* (SN).

**4. *por mientras* is Mexican, not Colombian**
- DAm, SN only, relayed by Castellano Actual: the DAm lists *por mientras* (locución adverbial, 'mientras tanto, durante un breve periodo', meaning 'meanwhile, for a short while') for Mexico, Costa Rica, Peru, Bolivia, Paraguay and Chile, but **not Colombia**. The same page says *por lo mientras* was unknown to the column's authors. A Mexican commenter there describes *por lo mientras* as colloquial speech, not formal register (that comment is grade L). — [UDEP, "por lo mientras"](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-por-lo-mientras/)
- **Contradicting evidence, SN:** Florencio del Barrio de la Rosa, "por mientras / para mientras", *Anuario de Letras. Lingüística y Filología* (UNAM), 2026. According to the abstract and snippets, *por mientras* "corresponde a la variante privilegiada en Honduras y **Colombia**" (is the preferred variant in Honduras and Colombia), *para mientras* stands out in Guatemala and Venezuela, and Chile and **Mexico** have both forms side by side. — [Anuario de Letras PDF](https://revistas-filologicas.unam.mx/anuario-letras/index.php/al/article/download/1810/2569/4250) (404 when fetched); [IRIS Ca' Foscari record](https://iris.unive.it/handle/10278/5107429) (not fetched)
- MEXICO, F, a negative finding: the DEM entry for *mientras* lists *mientras tanto*, *mientras que* and *mientras más... menos*, but **neither *por mientras* nor *por lo mientras***. — [DEM, mientras](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/mientras)
- **Verdict:** the earlier claim is **refuted or needs revising for Colombia**. Leaving Colombia out of the DAm list is weak evidence that the form is absent. A corpus study names Colombia as a country that *prefers* *por mientras*. For Mexico, the form is real but competes with *para mientras*. *Por lo mientras* looks colloquial, and the DEM does not even include it.

**5. *por si las dudas* is Mexican**
- MEXICO, F: the DEM lists "Por (si) las dudas, por aquello de las dudas: Por si acaso" (just in case), with the example "Por si las dudas, compra tu boleto de una vez" ('Just in case, buy your ticket now'). It is also listed among the fixed phrases under *por* (sense 19), with no "Popular" label. — [DEM, duda](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/duda); [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/por)
- RK: *por si las dudas* is also used in Argentina (as *por las dudas*), Colombia and elsewhere, so "Mexican" means "very much at home in Mexico", not "only Mexican". No Colombian source found.
- **Verdict:** confirmed as normal, unmarked Mexican usage (F). The DEM cannot show the phrase is *exclusive* to Mexico.

**6. *pa'* is colloquial everywhere**
- MEXICO, F: the DEM entry *pa* is a preposition, "(A veces se escribe pa') (Popular) Para", with examples "Vente pa' cá" ('Come over here'), "Pa' que te levantes temprano" ('So you get up early') and "Uno pa mí y otro pa ti" ('One for me and one for you'). The DEM labels it **Popular**, meaning informal or vernacular. — [DEM, pa](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/pa)
- COLOMBIA: no source. RK: *pa'* is everywhere in Colombian speech ("pa' dónde va?", 'where are you headed?', and "pa' que sepa", 'just so you know').
- **Verdict:** confirmed for Mexico and labeled informal (F). Colombia is RK.

**7. *esperar por* is a calque of English "wait for"**
- MEXICO, F, a negative finding: the DEM entry *esperar* builds the verb transitively ("esperar al administrador" 'wait for the manager', "esperar el camión" 'wait for the bus') and does not list *esperar por*. — [DEM, esperar](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/esperar)
- COLOMBIA, SN: a column by Andrés Hurtado García in *El Tiempo*, "Hablamos, a veces, demasiado mal", opens by criticizing *esperar por*. It quotes "Carlos esperó por Julio" ('Carlos waited for Julio') and "el gobierno espera por los delegados" ('the government is waiting for the delegates'), and says such sentences grate. That shows the form occurs in Colombia and that purists reject it. The snippet does not show whether he calls it an anglicism. I could not fetch the column. — [El Tiempo column](https://www.eltiempo.com/opinion/columnistas/andres-hurtado-garcia/hablamos-a-veces-demasiado-mal-columna-de-andres-hurtado-809925)
- Calque origin: the DPD entry *esperar* could not be fetched, and I found no source calling it a calque. RK: the DPD treats *esperar por* as a construction heard in the Caribbean and in Spanish in contact with English, and it is not considered standard. This is **unverified**.
- **Verdict:** in both countries the form exists but is outside the norm (Mexico, F by its absence from the DEM; Colombia, SN from a prescriptive column). Whether it comes from English is **not supported by any source I fetched**.

**8. Duration "por dos semanas"**
- MEXICO, F: the DEM gives *por* sense 5 as "Señala aproximación a cierto tiempo o fecha" (approximation to a time or date), with the example "**Salió por un mes**" ('He left for a month') alongside "Regresa por Navidad" ('He's coming back around Christmas'). So duration *por* sits in the descriptive dictionary of Mexican Spanish as unmarked, ordinary usage. — [DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/por)
- COLOMBIA: no source. RK: "por dos semanas" is normal there too.
- **Verdict:** normal in Mexico (F). Whether it is "more American" than peninsular is still untested, since I found no corpus comparison.

### Inferences
- The DEM is built from the *Corpus del español mexicano contemporáneo*. When it includes a phrase without a regional or "Popular" label (*ir por*, *estar por*, *por si las dudas*, duration *por*), that is good evidence the phrase is ordinary Mexican usage. When it leaves a phrase out (*por mientras*, *por lo mientras*, *esperar por*), that is weak evidence: either the phrase is rare in the corpus or the editors judged it substandard.
- The earlier finding about *por mientras* came from a dictionary (the DAm country list). The 2026 study is a corpus study and points the other way for Colombia. The corpus study should win, though it is only SN-grade until someone reads the full text.

- For Colombia I have no fetched source for telling time with *para*, *estar por*, *pa'* or duration *por*.

## Other por/para features specific to either country

### Takeaway
Mexico: the DEM confirms *pa'* (Popular), *nomás* ('only' and 'as soon as'), and the full set of everyday *para* senses. Most of the Colombian candidates (*regalar*, *a la orden*, *de a*, *por allá*) could not be sourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- MEXICO, F: the DEM entry *nomás* gives sense 1 as "Nada más, simplemente, exclusivamente" (just, only), with "Estoy aquí nomás mirando" ('I'm just looking'). Sense 2 is "Nomás que" = 'as soon as', with "Nomás que tenga dinero, te invito a comer" ('As soon as I have money, I'll take you to lunch'). It has no regional label because the DEM describes Mexican Spanish itself. — [DEM, nomás](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/nomás)
- MEXICO, F: the DEM entry *para* lists "¿Para qué sirve un martillo?" ('What is a hammer for?') under its sense for an object's function. My checks of the DEM *nada* and *luego* pages found **no** *para nada* or *para luego* subentry. — [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/para); [DEM, nada](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/nada); [DEM, luego](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/luego)
- COLOMBIA, SN only: a search on Colombian courtesy turned up studies of address forms in Bogotá and of diminutives used for politeness ("me puedes prestar platica", 'can you lend me a little money'). None of them treats *me regala* or *a la orden* directly. — [todoele.net PDF](https://www.todoele.net/sites/default/files/2025-05/me_puedes_prestar_platica.pdf); [Universidad de Antioquia thesis](https://bibliotecadigital.udea.edu.co/handle/10495/34987)

### Inferences
- RK, unverified and to be treated only as leads: Colombian "¿Me regala un tinto?" ('Could I have a coffee?', with *regalar* used for buying, not giving away). "A la orden" is said by shopkeepers and means 'at your service' or 'you're welcome'. *De a* is distributive or manner, as in "de a dos" ('two each', 'in twos') and "de a pie" ('on foot'). "Por allá" is a vague 'over there' or 'some time back'. In Mexico: "para nada" as an emphatic 'not at all', "¿para cuándo?" ('by when?'), "¿para qué es bueno?" ('what is he good at?'), with *para* marking aptitude, in line with DEM *para* sense 10, "Tiene oído para la música" ('He has an ear for music'). None of these is regional to *por* or *para* themselves except *por mientras* and *pa'*.
