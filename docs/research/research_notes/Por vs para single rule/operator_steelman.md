# Operator / invariant-value accounts of por vs para: steelman and stress test

Grades: **F** = verified in a primary text I read in full or in part (quote given). **SN** = from a secondary source summarising the original, or a search snippet; not checked against the original. **L** = my inference from F/SN material. **RK** = from my own recall; not verified this round, so treat as a lead only.

## 1. What exactly does each invariant-value account claim?

### Takeaway
The strongest single-rule account I found and could verify is Francisco Matte Bon's metaoperational one (built on Adamczewski's "Phase I / Phase II"). It says **para** presents what follows as *new*, proposed now, and less tied to what comes before it. **por** presents it as *prior to* (presupposed by) what comes before it, "hence por expresses cause: causes are what comes before." Matte Bon calls the opposition "extraordinariamente sencilla" (extraordinarily simple). The structuralist invariants (Morera, Trujillo, Pottier) are also single-meaning accounts, but I could get them only second-hand. The ELE grammars (GBE, Patin) and the cognitive accounts (Delbecque, Funes, Kissling & Arnold) are polysemy networks or lists of uses, not single features.

### Cited Findings
- **F** Matte Bon's formulation, verbatim: "una oposición sobre la que se ha vertido tanta tinta, como PARA / POR resulta ser extraordinariamente sencilla: PARA presenta un elemento como nuevo, y por tanto menos ligado al elemento que antecede, mientras que POR lo presenta como previo a ese elemento: de ahí que se diga que POR expresa la causa: las causas son lo que hay antes." (English: "an opposition on which so much ink has been spilt, PARA / POR, turns out to be extraordinarily simple: PARA presents an element as new, and so less tied to what precedes it, while POR presents it as prior to that element. That is why people say POR expresses cause: causes are what comes before.") Source: Matte Bon, "Cómo construimos las relaciones en la interacción: preposiciones, conjunciones, marcadores", in *Geométrica explosión. Estudios … en homenaje a René Lenarduzzi*, Edizioni Ca' Foscari, pp. 289–312, at p. 310 — [PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-080-8/978-88-6969-080-8-ch-19.pdf)
- **F** Matte Bon deliberately writes the pair as "PARA / POR" (Phase I first) and says the reversal is intentional and "no se trata, pues, de un error" ("so it is not a mistake") (n. 11, p. 297) — [same PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-080-8/978-88-6969-080-8-ch-19.pdf)
- **F** The underlying binary, after Adamczewski 1983 as translated by Matte Bon:
  - Phase I (rhematic) is a "perspectiva en la que no se presupone y por tanto la elección paradigmática es abierta … asertividad fuerte" ("a perspective with no presupposition, so the paradigm choice is open … strong assertiveness").
  - Phase II (thematic) is a "perspectiva en la que se presupone y por tanto la elección paradigmática es inexistente, cerrada … asertividad débil o nula" ("a perspective with presupposition, so there is no paradigm choice, it is closed … weak or zero assertiveness").
  - Gabilan's labels are rendered as "ESTATUS DE ELEMENTO PROPUESTO / PRESUPUESTO" ("status of proposed / presupposed element") (p. 300) — [same PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-080-8/978-88-6969-080-8-ch-19.pdf)
- **F** Two caveats that bear on whether the account can be tested:
  - "'viene de antes' en la interacción. Esto no tiene nada que ver con el calendario extralingüístico" ("'comes from before' in the interaction. This has nothing to do with the real-world calendar") (n. 15).
  - "No debe cometerse el error de pensar que cuando un dato ya ha aparecido … se pasa automáticamente a los operadores de FASE II, ni que lo que se codifica como FASE II necesariamente ha aparecido explícitamente en el contexto anterior" ("One must not think that once a piece of information has appeared … the speaker automatically switches to Phase II operators, nor that what is coded as Phase II has necessarily appeared explicitly in the earlier context") (p. 301) — [same PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-080-8/978-88-6969-080-8-ch-19.pdf)
- **F** Table 6 (p. 309) arranges the prepositions in a nested I/II tree in the order "hacia, para, hasta, a, con, sin, en, por, desde, de". The text adds that *para Madrid* (toward/for Madrid) presents Madrid "como más adquirido / asumido" ("as more acquired / taken for granted") than *hacia Madrid* (toward Madrid). It also says *le he comprado un libro para Pablo* (I bought a book for Pablo) presents Pablo as newer than *a Pablo* (to Pablo), which explains why the clitic *le* can double *a Pablo* but not *para Pablo* — [same PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-080-8/978-88-6969-080-8-ch-19.pdf)
- **L** So para is "new" only *relative to por*. Relative to *hacia*, para is the given member. The feature is a position in a nested opposition tree (Matte Bon's "árbol de las muñecas rusas", tree of Russian dolls), not a fixed semantic content. The extracted table layout is garbled, so the exact tree shape is an inference.
- **F** D'Antonio, Albiol-Martín & Priego-Casanova summarise it this way: "para está en la Fase I, porque propone un dato nuevo, menos relacionado con el elemento que antecede … por está en la Fase II, es el paradigma cerrado, porque no introduce ningún dato nuevo, es preexistente al elemento que lo antecede; por eso, se afirma que por expresa la causa, pues esta siempre precede al efecto." ("para is in Phase I because it proposes a new piece of information, less related to what precedes it … por is in Phase II, the closed paradigm, because it introduces nothing new and pre-exists what precedes it; that is why por is said to express cause, since cause always precedes effect.")
  - Their teaching gloss uses *armario abierto / cerrado* (open / closed cupboard) and a "doble teclado" (double keyboard).
  - Source: "Por y para: enfoques y su didáctica", *Studi di Glottodidattica* 2025, 1, 57–68, at pp. 60–61 — [PDF](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **F** The same paper restates the account as simultaneity versus posteriority, with the "doble teclado con su simultaneidad/posterioridad" (double keyboard with its simultaneity/posteriority) (p. 64):
  - *Lo han llamado por esa tarea* ("They called him because of that task / on account of that task"): "A y B son simultáneos … armario cerrado, no se introduce ningún elemento nuevo" ("A and B are simultaneous … closed cupboard, no new element is introduced").
  - *Lo han llamado para esa tarea* ("They called him to do that task"): "el segundo es sucesivo, armario abierto" ("the second is subsequent, open cupboard") — [PDF](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **SN** Morera Pérez (1988, *Estructura semántica del sistema preposicional del español moderno*; Canarian school, Trujillo's line) gives each preposition a single, abstract, spatially rooted invariant. On the secondary summary I saw:
  - *de, desde, hacia, para, a, hasta* express a direction marked from outside the point named by the preposition's object.
  - *por* places its meaning in concomitance with that point. That is why *por* is excluded from *los barcos arrancaron ___ el puerto* ("the boats set off ___ the port").
  - para is directional without contact with the goal.
  - Source: search snippets of Morera's work — [search hit: Morera, "El concepto de variante"](https://raco.cat/index.php/Elies/article/download/350484/441789/0); [Morera, "Origen y evolución de … hacia"](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/91881.pdf)
- **SN** Morera holds that the semantic form "no es de naturaleza espacial, sino de carácter abstracto como todos los valores" ("is not spatial in nature but abstract, like all values") — quoted in [memoireonline thesis on Luces de bohemia](https://www.memoireonline.com/10/10/3980/m_Estudio-de-las-preposiciones-en-Luces-de-bohemia-de-Valle-Inclan11.html). This conflicts with the other snippet's "naturaleza espacial" (spatial in nature); I have not resolved it.
- **SN** Trujillo, "Notas para un estudio de las preposiciones españolas", *Thesaurus* 26(2), 1971, 234–279, argues that each preposition has a differential content against the others; prepositions are not empty words. A secondary source says his criterion would make *para* the usual preposition with verbs of definite orientation (*salir, volver, venir*: leave, return, come) — [Patin 2016, marcoELE](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92153510015/92153510015.pdf); search snippet of Llitera on Trujillo.
- **SN** Pottier (1968) gives certain prepositions a spatial, a temporal and a "notionnelle" (notional) value of one underlying meaning, illustrated by *Voy por París / Voy para París* ("I'm going through/around Paris / I'm heading for Paris") — quoted in [Patin 2016](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92153510015/92153510015.pdf)
- **F** Cognitive-grammar summary (after Delbecque 1996) in D'Antonio et al.: por "resalta el recorrido o proceso antes de llegar a un resultado final" ("highlights the path or process before reaching a final result"); para is "una proyección o dirección hacia un objetivo" ("a projection or direction toward a goal") (p. 60) — [PDF](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **SN** The GBE (Alonso Raya, Castañeda, Martínez Gila, Miquel, Ortega, Ruiz Campillo) treats "Para, por" as a unit (§ at p. 207, revised A1–B2 edition) with a separate index entry "por: la causa (¿por qué?)" ("por: the cause (why?)") — [search snippet of Difusión sample PDF](https://difusion.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/gramatica-basica-ed-revisada-1.pdf). I did not retrieve the actual text or image.
- **RK** The GBE / Ruiz Campillo "operativo" image, as I recall it: *por* is what is behind or in the middle (cause pushing, path traversed, means, exchange), and *para* is what is ahead (goal, destination, deadline, recipient). This is a two-image account (back/through versus ahead), not a strict single feature. Unverified this round.

### Inferences
- **L** Matte Bon's claim is the strongest candidate for "one invariant per preposition". It is a single binary: presented-as-prior/presupposed versus presented-as-new/proposed. It is explicitly about how the speaker codes the information, not about time or space in the world.
- **L** "Endpoint ahead → para" is close to Morera's "direction marked from outside, without contact with the goal". Matte Bon instead moves the opposition from the world to information status. That move is what lets it absorb cases that broke the endpoint rule.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact invariant formulas of Trujillo 1971, López 1970 (*Problemas y métodos en el análisis de las preposiciones*), Bello, Alarcos (1994 §279 gives only the general definition of a preposition) or Cuervo's DCR entries for *por/para*. Full texts were not online or were not reached.
- Matte Bon's *Gramática comunicativa del español* (1992/1995) section on por/para was not retrieved, so I cannot say whether it already contains the Phase I/II formulation or still lists uses.
- Matte Bon 2015a (Pisa UP, "el double clavier y el principio de ciclicidad") is said to hold the fuller treatment; not retrieved.
- I found no feature notation such as [±término] for por/para in an online source. I could not confirm that anyone proposes exactly that binary.

## 2. Does "por = already given / prior; para = new / projected" handle the hard cases?

### Takeaway
It handles the motive-type *por* cases cleanly: *luchar por, gracias por, votar por, ir por pan*. That is a real gain over "endpoint ahead". The misses are *estar por + infinitive* (not yet done), *por si acaso*, *para mí* (opinion) and the *por/para* doublets (*por siempre / para siempre*). It covers these only because "prior" is defined as how the speaker codes the information, independent of time ("nothing to do with the calendar") and of prior mention. That same definition makes the rule hard to falsify, and a learner cannot apply it ahead of time. The rule's own advocates say it is not enough on its own.

### Cited Findings
- **F** Even its advocates say it is not enough: "no basta con proporcionar algunas construcciones enunciativas para que los estudiantes aprendan a diferenciarlas solo con el auxilio de la teoría de la GM" ("giving a few utterances is not enough for students to learn to tell them apart with the help of metaoperational grammar alone") (p. 62).
  - They also concede that "existen muchos casos en los cuales la causa y el fin se rozan constantemente, siendo posible su intercambio" ("there are many cases where cause and purpose constantly touch, and the two can be swapped"), e.g. *Pasa por la ciudad por/para no pasar por la Vía de Cintura* ("He goes through the city so as not to take the ring road").
  - Source: [D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **F** Their treatment of purpose *por*: *me he quedado en la oficina por terminar mi trabajo* ("I stayed at the office to finish my work"). Here "Por se usa en este caso en que el fin es obvio" ("por is used here because the goal is obvious") and "Por nunca presenta el fin de forma contundente" ("por never presents the goal forcefully"), a pragmatic/persuasive choice — [same](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943). **L** This is how the account absorbs goal-*por*: the goal is coded as already obvious or presupposed.
- **SN** Interchangeable purpose infinitives in the descriptive literature:
  - *Trabajo por/para ganar el pan* ("I work to earn my bread"): por gives "inmediata" (immediate) finality, para "mediata" (mediated). Morera Pérez 1988, p. 301.
  - Blocked cases: *Me duele la cabeza por (*para) haber estudiado tanto* ("My head hurts from having studied so much"); *Es necesario que estudies para (*por) que mañana te ganes el pan* ("You need to study so that tomorrow you can earn your bread").
  - Source: [Patin 2016](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92153510015/92153510015.pdf)
- **SN** Uses that a "given/prior" rule must cover:
  - *Estar por + inf* means "be tempted to" (*Estoy por irme al cine* "I'm thinking of going to the cinema").
  - It can also mean "still to be done" (*La cocina está por barrer* "The kitchen is still to be swept").
  - In some varieties it means "about to" (*El tren está por salir* "The train is about to leave").
  - *Para mí* is opinion ("in my view"); *por mí* means "as far as I'm concerned" (*Por mí, ya puedes irte* "As far as I'm concerned, you can go").
  - Source: [Patin 2016](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92153510015/92153510015.pdf)
- **F** The text says "las causas son lo que hay antes" ("causes are what comes before"). Its examples are *trabajo por mi jefe* ("I work because of / on account of my boss") versus *trabajo para mi jefe* ("I work for my boss", i.e. the boss as goal or beneficiary) — [D'Antonio et al.](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)

Case-by-case stress test (all **L**, my application of the verbatim F formulation):

| Case | Gloss | Fit with "por = presupposed / prior to the preceding element; para = new, proposed" | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ir por pan* | go and get bread | The bread is the already-present motive of the going; no new element is projected. Contrast with *ir para la panadería* ("head for the bakery"), where the place is proposed as a destination. | Fits, though after the fact |
| *luchar por la libertad* | fight for freedom | Freedom is the value already in place that drives the fight. *luchar para conseguir la libertad* ("fight in order to win freedom") recodes it as a new aim. | Fits well |
| *votar por X* | vote for X | X is in the choice set before the vote (closed paradigm). | Fits |
| *loco por ella* | crazy about her | She is the pre-existing cause of the state. | Fits |
| *gracias por el regalo* | thanks for the gift | The gift is given, before the thanks. | Fits very well |
| *por 10 dólares* | for 10 dollars (price) | The counterpart is set up with the deal ("viene de antes en la interacción", "comes from before in the interaction"), not before in time. | Fits only through the information-status reading |
| *escrito por García Márquez* | written by García Márquez | Matte Bon covers this with the process/verb versus noun argument (*por* vs *de*), not with "prior". | Covered by a separate argument |
| *para siempre* | forever | The span is projected, proposed. But *por siempre (jamás)* ("for ever and ever") also exists; the rule must say the same span is coded as given there. | Fits *para*; the doublet needs an extra step |
| *para mí, es un error* | in my view, it's a mistake | "I" am hardly new information. The rule needs "the judgement is proposed relative to me", i.e. the evaluation is new. *Por mí* (as far as I'm concerned) = my already-held stance. | Works only if "new" is read as "the evaluation is new" |
| *para ser niño, habla bien* | for a child, he speaks well | The standard of comparison is proposed. | Arguable |
| *la cocina está por barrer* | the kitchen is still to be swept | The sweeping is in the future and not yet done, yet it takes *por*. The rule must say the pending task is presupposed (on the to-do list). | Strain; this is the strongest counterexample |
| *por si acaso* | just in case | It anticipates a possible future, yet it is *por*. The "prior" reading: the possibility is the pre-existing motive. | Fits only through the motive reading |
| *vendré para / por Navidad* | I'll come by / around Christmas | *para* = deadline (proposed limit); *por* = approximate time inside which the event sits. | Fits loosely |

- **L** Net result: once "prior" means "presented by the speaker as presupposed, a motive or setting already in place", and "new" means "proposed now, open choice", all the hard cases from the challenge round can be described. But the rule assigns the feature after the fact from the form the speaker chose. Matte Bon himself says Phase II material need not have appeared before and is unrelated to real time. So the rule cannot predict, in advance, the choice in *estar por barrer*, *por siempre* or idioms such as *por cierto* ("by the way") or *por favor* ("please"). Patin, following García Yebra, treats these as lexicalised norm rather than system.
- **L** Where it predicts better than "endpoint ahead":
  - It explains *por* of sake or goal (*luchar por, votar por, ir por*) and *gracias por* without exceptions.
  - It explains the *por / para* + infinitive doublets as a choice between a presupposed motive and a proposed aim. That fits the native-speaker intuitions reported above ("immediate" versus "mediated" purpose; por "never presents the goal forcefully").

### Gaps
- I found no source in which Matte Bon himself applies the account to *estar por*, *para mí*, *para siempre / por siempre* or *por + price*. The table above is my extrapolation.
- I found no corpus-based or judgement-task test of whether native speakers' choices follow the information-status feature.

## 3. Was any of this tested with learners?

### Takeaway
I found no controlled learner test of the metaoperational (given/new) account. Its proponents report only classroom experience in upper secondary school and list limits. Cognitive-linguistics teaching of *por/para* has been tested: Lam 2009 (mixed or weak results, per secondary reports) and Kissling & Arnold 2022 (gains with novices). The approach tested there is a polysemy network, not a single invariant. Error data from French-speaking learners shows the residual errors cluster on the cases the given/new account explains best.

### Cited Findings
- **F** D'Antonio et al. report no experiment. They rely on "nuestra práctica docente" ("our teaching practice") and say metaoperational grammar may be too complicated for beginners. They also write "no es infrecuente encontrar estudiantes con los que las estrategias basadas en la GM no resultan eficaces" ("it is not unusual to find students for whom strategies based on metaoperational grammar do not work"). They conclude in favour of an eclectic approach (pp. 64–66) — [PDF](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)
- **SN** Lam, Y. (2009), "Applying cognitive linguistics to teaching the Spanish prepositions por and para", *Language Awareness* 18(1), 2–18:
  - Design: two intermediate groups were taught with a cognitive-linguistic description; a comparison group was given a list of uses to memorise.
  - The abstract flags confounds: the treatment's content and delivery, learner cognitive styles, and "problems with applying cognitive linguistic models of prepositions to language pedagogy".
  - I did not see the numeric outcome. A secondary paper says Lam found that L2 learners made "virtually no gains" on *por/para* over four years of college Spanish.
  - Source: [DOI page](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658410802147345); search snippets.
- **SN** Kissling, E. M. & Arnold, T. (2022), *Language Teaching Research*, doi:10.1177/13621688221139626:
  - 30 novice learners were taught with animated videos presenting "Principled Polysemy Networks" for *por* and *para*.
  - They made significant gains on controlled production and performed about as well as more advanced learners from earlier studies.
  - Outcomes did not correlate with working memory, executive function, motivation or learning style.
  - Source: [todoele abstract page](https://todoele.net/bibliografia/preliminary-evidence-applied-cognitive-linguistics-effective-novice-learners)
- **F** Patin 2016 (French L1, university learners), error shares in third year:
  - *Ir por algo* ("go and get something", "*Aller pour le pain*"): 15% of errors.
  - *Luchar por vivir* ("fight to live", cause/purpose ambiguity): 20%.
  - *Optar / votar / estar para* instead of *por* ("opt / vote / be for", in favour of; learners "perciben finalidad", "perceive purpose"): 23%.
  - *Estar por / para*: 12%.
  - Patin concludes that the binary "resulta inoperante" ("stops working") from B2 upwards.
  - Source: ["Por" y "para", ¿una oposición necesaria?, marcoELE 22, 2016, pp. 189–202](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92153510015/92153510015.pdf)
- **F** Guerra Magdaleno (2014), "La imagen es la regla: cuestiones visuales en el tratamiento del contraste de las preposiciones por y para" ("The image is the rule: visual issues in treating the por/para contrast"), is cited as a cognitive/visual teaching proposal (title only seen) — reference list in [D'Antonio et al.](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)

### Inferences
- **L** The errors that persist at advanced level (*ir por*, *votar / optar por*, *luchar por*) are exactly the "por of sake/goal" class. "Por = pre-existing motive / closed choice set" plausibly targets these, and "por = cause, para = purpose" and "endpoint ahead" do not. This is a reason to test the given/new account, not evidence that it works.
- **L** The one positive controlled result (Kissling & Arnold) comes from a network of related senses per preposition, not a single feature. The evidence so far favours "one core image plus a motivated network" over "one binary feature that settles every choice."

### Gaps
- No experimental or quasi-experimental study of the Adamczewski / Matte Bon Phase I/II account for *por/para* was found.
- The full results and effect sizes of Lam 2009 and Kissling & Arnold 2022 were not retrieved (paywall or 403).
- The GBE's por/para section and images, Ruiz Campillo's own writing on prepositions, and Delbecque 1996 were not read directly.
