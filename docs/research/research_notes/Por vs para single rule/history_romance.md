## 1. How did the split develop, use by use? Which uses were para's from the start, which did it take from por, and which did por keep?

### Takeaway
*Para* began as an allative, a "to(wards)" marker built from a preposition plus *a* 'to'. Its oldest firm use is destination, including a person as destination. Purpose before infinitives belonged to *por* and moved to *para* only from the 18th century (F). So para's "terminus" changed from a place to an abstract goal. The sources I could reach give no use-by-use dates for recipient, deadline or standard.

### Cited Findings

**Form and etymology: three positions.** Whichever is right, the compound is always "preposition + allative *a*".
- Standard references derive *para* from older *pora*, a fusion of Latin PRO/PER with AD 'to' (Penny 2006: 273; Torrens Álvarez 2007: 117). (F, reported by C&EA, p. 108) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- Alvar & Pottier (1987: 300) hold that the *a* in *para* is only possible from PER + AD. (F, reported) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- Riiho (1979: 195), as quoted by C&EA: except in Portuguese, where the two simple forms stayed apart until fairly late, it is pointless to argue whether PRO+AD or PER+AD is the etymon. (F, second-hand quotation of Riiho) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)
- Fernández-Ordóñez (2022) argues that *para* does not come from *pora* at all. She derives it from *per a*, with the vowel opening before [ɾ], and places the variants geographically:
  - *pora* in the centre of the peninsula;
  - *per a* in Catalan (east) and Galician-Portuguese (west), and more widely in Leonese than usually accepted.

  On her account, the *para* that replaced central *pora* came from Leonese *pera*. (F, reported) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)

**Dating of pora > para.** The dates depend on method.
- Lapesa (1981: 214) says *para* "did not spread until the Alfonsine period" (F, reported).
- Penny (2006: 273) says *pora* was eliminated by the end of the 13th c. (F, reported).
- In Riiho's (1979: 232) counts, *para* is at 85% against 15% *pora* in the second half of the 13th c. (F, reported).
- Ueda (2015) dates *para*'s majority to the 15th c. (F, reported).

C&EA's point is that these dates turn on how the scribal abbreviation *pa* (p-with-stroke + a) is resolved. Older editions silently expanded it as *para*. *Pora* survived in Aragon until the 15th c. — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)

**Use-by-use history.** The 2018 abstract is the backbone of this section.
- *Para* arose from *por* + *a* by usage-based chunking. In early allative uses with a person as destination, the *a* still contributes its own meaning. (F) — [Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018, abstract](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/)
- After the 14th c., allative (destination) uses of *para* with a noun complement decline, for both person and place destinations. (F) — [Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018, abstract](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/)
- From the 18th c., *para* replaces *por* before infinitive complements and becomes the majority purposive form. The authors call the whole change "allative-to-purposive grammaticalisation", seen in both para's spread across contexts and its variation with *por*. (F) — [Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018, abstract](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/); bibliographic listing: [Benjamins](https://benjamins.com/catalog/ihll.16.09tor)
- Earlier Spanish treatments exist that I could not read: Torres Cacoullos & Bauman (2014), "Las preposiciones por, pora y para", in Company (ed.), *Sintaxis histórica*, 3rd part; Bolinger (1945), "Purpose with Por and Para", *Modern Language Journal* 28(1): 15–22. (SN) — [Benjamins reference list](https://benjamins.com/catalog/rllt.9.03bau)
- Medieval Asturian kept a three-way *per / por / pa(ra)* system. A study citing Riiho describes semantic specialisation of each form (cause, instrument, direction). (SN) — [Prieto Entrialgo, Lletres Asturianes](https://lletresasturianes.alladixital.org/pdf/1397116014Artículu%203-Clara%20Elena%20Prieto%20Entrialgo-Per,%20por%20y%20para%20nel%20asturianu%20medieval.%20guía%20pa%20nun%20se%20perder.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Para's original core (F-based):** motion or orientation to a terminus. Examples:
  - *fue pora Burgos* 'he went to(wards) Burgos';
  - *enbió cartas pora el rey* 'he sent letters to the king'.

  The *a* is the allative; *por/per* contributes "through, by way of, on behalf of". These example sentences are RK illustrations, not corpus citations.
- **Taken from por (F-based):** purpose with an infinitive. Example: *lo hizo por ganar dinero* 'he did it (in order) to earn money', the older type, became *para ganar dinero*. This is the historically documented transfer.

  Residues of the older layer are still in modern Spanish (RK):
  - *por no molestar* 'so as not to bother';
  - *lo hice por ayudarte* 'I did it to help you / out of wanting to help you';
  - *estar por hacer* 'to be still to do'.

  Here purpose with *por* is colored as motive.
- **Kept by por (RK):** path, means, cause/motive, exchange/price, agent, substitution, and "in favour of" (*luchar por*, *votar por*). Nothing I fetched says *para* ever competed for these.
- **Abstract terminus (RK, but forced by the F data):** because purpose moved to *para* while concrete noun-destination uses declined after the 14th c., any single modern rule must define para's "terminus" abstractly, as an endpoint the action is aimed at. A spatial definition will not do.
- **Recipient, deadline, standard (RK):** recipient follows naturally from person-destination, as in *pora el rey* 'to/for the king'. Deadline (*para el lunes* 'by Monday') and standard (*para su edad* 'for his age') look like extensions of "endpoint of reference". The sources I reached give no dates for these.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gives use-by-use dates for beneficiary/recipient, deadline (*para el lunes*), standard (*para ser niño*) or opinion (*para mí*).
- Riiho's monograph, the TC&B 2014 chapter and Lapesa/Penny on semantics were not reachable. I found no source stating "para = por + direction to a terminus" as a principle. TC&B's "allative-to-purposive" label is the closest.
- I could not check whether *por*-purpose survived longer in some construction types, such as *por que* + subjunctive or after particular verbs.

## 2. Portuguese, Catalan, French and Italian: what do they do with the same meanings?

### Takeaway
- **Portuguese** went through the same change as Spanish, purpose moving from *por* to *pera/para*, but its formal written texts flip about three centuries earlier, in the 15th–16th c. (F).
- **Catalan's** norm fuses *per* and *per a* in exactly the contexts where purpose is intention-like: purpose infinitives, orientation and opinion. It keeps them apart where the contrast is agent/cause versus recipient/destination (F).
- **Italian** uses one *per* for nearly all the meanings Spanish splits (F).

### Cited Findings

**Portuguese: history**

Bomfim's purpose counts, as tabulated in a PUC-Rio master's thesis chapter (F) — [PUC-Rio thesis, ch. 4](https://www.maxwell.vrac.puc-rio.br/14737/14737_5.PDF):

| Text | Date | *por* | *pera/para* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Livro de Linhagens | 13th c. | 74% | 26% |
| Demanda do Santo Graal | 13th/14th c. | 90% | 10% |
| Crónica Geral de Espanha | 1344 | 56% | 44% |
| D. Duarte, Livro da Ensinança | 15th c. | 30% | 70% |
| Esmeraldo de situ orbis | c. 1505–27 | 42% | 58% |
| Fernão de Oliveira's grammar | 1536 | 9% | 91% |
| Barros, Décadas | 1552 | 16% | 84% |
| Os Lusíadas | 1570 | 27% | 73% |

- The thesis concludes that purpose in archaic Portuguese is expressed "preferentially by *por*", and that formal *por*-purpose declines from the 15th c.
- **Inconsistency:** the prose gives 48% *por* / 57% *pera* for *Lusíadas* Canto I, which sums to 105%; the table gives 27/73.
- In the 15th-c. cookbook of Infanta D. Maria, purpose is marked by *pera* + noun or infinitive (9 of 14 cases). *Por* survives only as *por que* + subjunctive (3 cases), plus 2 cases of bare *que* + subjunctive.

  Example: *por q não fure o fundo do pastel* 'so that it does not pierce the bottom of the pastry'.
- Archaic Portuguese had *per* (< PER) and *por* (< PRO) as separate forms until about the 16th–17th c., when *per* gave way to *por* and *pera* to *para*. One study says *per* had lost its sense of direction already in Latin and was reinforced with AD, giving *pera*. (SN) — [Bomfim 2000, UFC repository](https://repositorio.ufc.br/bitstream/riufc/46668/1/2000_art_ermbonfim.pdf); [Medievalis, per/por in Auto da Barca do Inferno](https://revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/medievalis/article/view/45398)
- This matches Riiho's remark that Portuguese kept the two simple forms apart until late. (F, second-hand) — [Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)

**Portuguese: modern grammar**
- Secondary sources quote Cunha & Cintra's *Nova Gramática* as follows (SN, not checked against the book) — [Cunha & Cintra as cited, core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/489471402.pdf); [Signum/UEL](https://ojs.uel.br/revistas/uel/index.php/signum/article/download/4203/4468/17756):
  - movement in general = "tendency toward a limit, purpose, direction, perspective";
  - *para*'s predominant sense is movement, the endpoint of a path;
  - *por* marks a path through an extent between limits, or duration;
  - *para* versus *a*: movement with a stay at the destination versus without one.
- Learner sites give the usual list (L) — [Mango Languages](https://www.mangolanguages.com/resources/learn/grammar/brazilian-portuguese/how-to-use-por-vs-para-in-brazilian-portuguese); [Caminhos Languages](https://caminhoslanguages.com/blog/por-para-in-portuguese):
  - *por* = reason, duration, approximate time, means (*por telefone* 'by phone'), exchange (*comprou uma caneta por R$2* 'bought a pen for 2 reais');
  - *para* = purpose, deadline.

**Catalan**

The GIEC 2016 statement on *per* / *per a* (F) — [IEC, GIEC presentation of new features, 2016](https://aoe.iec.cat/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/GIEC-Presentacio%CC%81_MostraDeNovetats.pdf):
- Speech differs by dialect:
  - Valencian, Tortosa Catalan and the westernmost northwestern varieties distinguish *per* and *per a*;
  - the other dialects use only *per*.
- Formal language keeps the distinction, but in certain contexts both are accepted interchangeably in every register:
  - (a) before a noun phrase indicating orientation or intention:
    - *Estudia per / per a metge* 'He's studying to be a doctor';
  - (a) before a noun phrase giving an opinion or point of view:
    - *Per / Per a tu, permetre'ls-ho seria un escàndol* 'For you, allowing it would be a scandal';
    - *Per / Per a l'Església el matrimoni és un sagrament* 'For the Church, marriage is a sacrament';
  - (a) in a concessive adjunct:
    - *Per / Per a l'edat que té, es conserva molt bé* 'For his age, he's well preserved';
  - (b) in lexicalised phrases:
    - *no n'hi ha per / per a tant* 'it's not that big a deal';
    - *per / per a sempre* 'forever';
  - (c) in purpose infinitive constructions:
    - *Ho fa per / per a viure* 'He does it to live / for a living';
    - *Serveix per a / per tallar* 'It's used for cutting';
    - *Ja som prou gent per / per a pintar la tanca* 'There are enough of us to paint the fence'.
- Infinitive modifiers of nouns "with a sense of purpose or destination" also take *per / per a*: *menjar per / per a emportar* 'food to take away'.

The traditional criterion (F) — [UOC language guide](https://www.uoc.edu/ca/serveis/llengua-estil/criteris/gramatica/preposicions/altres-sistemes):
- *per* when the purpose clause depends on an action verb with an agent: *L'Amadeu treballa moltes hores per poder pagar-se els estudis* 'Amadeu works long hours to pay for his studies';
- *per a* when it depends on a noun, an adjective or a non-action verb: *Necessita diners per a poder pagar-se els estudis* 'He needs money to pay for his studies'.

The alternative is always *per a* before a purpose infinitive. This follows the dialects that distinguish, with exceptions for verbs that govern *per* (*optar per* 'opt for').

Badia (VilaWeb), on Fabra's norm and how speakers feel the difference (F) — [Badia, VilaWeb 2019](https://www.vilaweb.cat/noticies/per-per-a-jordi-badia/):
- Fabra's norm keeps *per a* wherever purpose is expressed, except after a verb of voluntary action. Coromines favoured *per* always before an infinitive; Bibiloni proposed another system.
- *Per a* nearly always carries purpose, destination or receiving.
- Minimal pair:
  - *un dibuix fet per alumnes novells* 'a drawing made by beginning students';
  - *un dibuix fet per a alumnes novells* 'a drawing made for beginning students'.
- *Per* covers cause, agent, means, passage, time (*pel maig* 'in May'), substitution and "as" (*va agafar un bastó per espasa* 'he took a stick as a sword').
- *Per a* covers destination (*enviar menjar per a l'Índia* 'send food to India'), use or benefit (*és bo per a tothom* 'it's good for everyone') and target time (*deixem-ho per al dia 10* 'let's leave it for the 10th').

**French**
- *par* < Latin PER (SN) — [Etymonline, par](https://www.etymonline.com/fr/word/par)
- Old French *por/pur* (modern *pour*) < Late Latin *pōr* < PRO, glossed 'for, in order to' (SN) — [Kaikki/Wiktionary, Old French por](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Old%20French/meaning/p/po/por.html); [Etymonline, pur-](https://www.etymonline.com/fr/word/pur-)

### Inferences
- **The same drift in Spanish and Portuguese (F-based).** In both, an allative compound (*pora/para*, *pera*) gradually took over purpose from *por*. The timing differs a great deal: 15th–16th c. in Portuguese formal texts, 18th c. for Spanish infinitives.

  The counts are not directly comparable:
  - Bomfim counted all purpose expressions in formal texts;
  - TC&B counted infinitive complements.

  So "Portuguese was about three centuries earlier" is suggestive, not established.
- **Catalan's zone of indifference (RK).** The contexts where the IEC lets *per* and *per a* alternate are purpose infinitives, orientation/intention, opinion and standard/concession. These are roughly where Spanish *para* arrived late (purpose) or where the "terminus" is most abstract.

  Where the contrast is concrete, both Catalan *per a* and Spanish *para* are firm:
  - agent versus recipient: *fet per* / *per a alumnes* 'made by / for students';
  - destination: *enviar per a l'Índia* 'send to India';
  - use: *bo per a tothom* 'good for everyone'.

  This suggests a gradient: the more concretely the endpoint is a recipient or destination, the more firmly it takes the compound.
- **Catalan's verb-type rule (RK).** The rule is *per* after a volitional action verb, *per a* after nouns, adjectives and non-action verbs. Read as semantics, it says that purpose felt as the agent's motive goes with the simple preposition, and purpose felt as the destination or function of a thing goes with the compound.

  Spanish residues parallel this:
  - *lo hice por ayudarte* 'I did it to help you / out of wanting to help you' (motive);
  - *sirve para cortar* 'it's for cutting' (function).
- **Italian as a baseline (RK).** Italian shows that a single preposition can carry all of these meanings, including destination, deadline, beneficiary, support, price, cause and purpose. The Ibero-Romance split is therefore a later local overlay; nothing in the general Romance inventory required it.
- **French (RK, unsourced).** French split along a different line, PRO > *pour* versus PER > *par*:
  - *pour* covers purpose, beneficiary, destination (*partir pour Paris* 'leave for Paris'), price (*pour 10 euros* 'for 10 euros') and support (*voter pour* 'vote for');
  - *par* covers path, means and agent.

  So French groups "for/on behalf of/in exchange" with purpose, while Spanish puts them under *por*. I did not verify these usage claims from a source.
- **Portuguese counterexamples (RK, unverified).** These look parallel to Spanish:
  - *ir buscar pão* 'go and get bread', where Spanish has *ir por pan*;
  - *lutar por* 'fight for';
  - *votar em* 'vote for', using *em* 'in' rather than *por*;
  - *trocar por* 'exchange for'.

  No grammar or Ciberdúvidas page was fetched to confirm them.

### Gaps
- I found no Ciberdúvidas, Bechara or verbatim Cunha & Cintra text. Whether any Portuguese grammar states a single rule is unknown, beyond Cunha & Cintra's "limit/direction" versus "path between limits" as reported second-hand (SN).
- I did not reach the full GIEC text (§§ 19.3.5.4, 29.3.3), so I could not check whether the IEC states an underlying semantic distinction in its own words. The presentation gives only the alternation contexts.
- French and Italian historical semantics of pour/par and per are unsourced beyond etymology and the Treccani sense list.

## 3. Does the historical record support one modern rule: "para marks a terminus the action is directed to; por marks the ground the action stands on or passes through", with por-of-goal as an older layer?

### Takeaway
The record partly supports it. It is F-based that *para* entered as a marker of direction to an endpoint and later absorbed purpose from *por*. That makes "por-purpose residues are an older layer" historically sound for infinitival purpose. However, no source I found states the single rule itself. The explanation for the "por of goal" class (*luchar por*, *votar por*, *ir por pan*) as an older PRO layer rests on an SN etymology and an RK French parallel, not on any source that makes the link.

### Cited Findings
- *Para*'s change runs from allative to purposive. Early person-destination uses show the *a* contributing its meaning, and *para* takes purposive infinitives from *por* starting in the 18th c. (F) — [Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018, abstract](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/)
- Portuguese *por* was the main purpose marker in the 13th c. (74–90%) and lost that role to *pera* in the 15th–16th c. (F) — [PUC-Rio thesis, ch. 4](https://www.maxwell.vrac.puc-rio.br/14737/14737_5.PDF)
- Portuguese grammar, as reported, describes *para* as tendency toward a limit or endpoint and *por* as path through an extent. This is the closest sourced statement of the proposed rule. (SN) — [core.ac.uk, citing Cunha & Cintra](https://core.ac.uk/download/489471402.pdf)
- Spanish *por* continues both Latin PER 'through' and PRO 'for, on behalf of, in front of'. (SN; a generic etymology source, not a scholarly one) — [BuenoSpanish etymology](https://buenospanish.com/dictionary/por/etymology)
- In Catalan, *per a* nearly always carries purpose, destination or receiving, while *per* carries cause, agent, means and passage. (F) — [Badia, VilaWeb 2019](https://www.vilaweb.cat/noticies/per-per-a-jordi-badia/)

### Inferences
- **What para was "added" to mark (F-based):** a direction toward an endpoint, *a* = 'to', which in early texts was often a person. What *por* kept is everything without that endpoint: path, means, cause/motive, exchange, agent, and acting for or on behalf of someone.
- **The infinitive-purpose layer (F-based).** The contrast *lo hice por ayudarte* versus *para ayudarte* ('I did it out of wanting to help you' / 'in order to help you') is a historical residue of the older *por*-purpose. The modern meaning contrast, motive versus aim, is what is left after *para* took over the neutral purpose role.
- **The "por of goal" class (RK).**
  - *Luchar por la libertad* 'fight for freedom', *votar por X* 'vote for X' and *hacerlo por ti* 'do it for your sake' look like the PRO 'on behalf of, in favour of' sense. That sense never had a terminus, so *para* never competed for it.
  - *Ir por pan* 'go for (to get) bread' fits the same old *por*-of-objective use, which survives where the goal is an object to be fetched or obtained.
  - Under this reading, these are not exceptions to the rule but a pre-*para* layer. They are a stable layer, not a dying one, because they mean "for the sake of / in pursuit of" rather than "toward an endpoint".
- **A single rule the history would support (RK synthesis):** "*para* adds an endpoint the action is aimed at (a place, recipient, deadline, purpose or point of reference). *Por* is the older, general 'through / by / on account of / on behalf of', used wherever no such endpoint is in view." Two caveats:
  - by the 18th-c. evidence, the endpoint must be abstract, not spatial;
  - the Catalan data (F) show that the boundary is weakest exactly at abstract purpose and point of view, so learners should expect the most native variation there.
- **Counter-pressure (RK).** The persistence of *por* + infinitive as motive (*por no molestar* 'so as not to bother') shows the split never became fully complementary. Even after the change, "the ground the action stands on" (motive) and "the endpoint aimed at" (aim) can both describe the same situation.

### Gaps
- No source states the proposed single rule in these terms. TC&B's abstract does not describe *por*'s residual meaning.
- No historical source confirms that *luchar/votar/ir por* continue PRO specifically, as opposed to PER.
- Riiho (1979) probably addresses which senses each form took over, but I could not reach its content.
- I could not check whether Torres Cacoullos & Bauman (2014) give dates for beneficiary, deadline or standard uses.
