# Cognitive-linguistics steelman: one rule for por vs para

Source-access note: as in earlier rounds, Delbecque (1996), Kissling et al. (2018), ResearchGate, core.ac.uk and two Redalyc articles all blocked fetching (403 or 502). The one new full text I read is **Frago (2020), "Un esquema, cuatro preposiciones"**, *monografías marcoELE* 31 (Univ. of Helsinki), PDF read end to end. Frago quotes Delbecque, Melis (1992), Riiho (1977), Company & Flores (2014), Torres & Bauman (2014) and Heine & Kuteva (2002). Those quotes are **secondhand**: they are verbatim in Frago, but I did not check them against the originals.

Grades: **F** = fetched page; **SN** = search snippet or summary only; **L** = learner site; **RK** = my own reasoning, unsourced.

## What the cognitive authors propose as the schema of each preposition

### Takeaway
Two kinds of core image exist. The **spatial-network** camp (Lunn, Lam, Kissling/Tyler, Funes, Frago) gives por a "path through" core and para a "path oriented toward a limit, without contact" core. **Delbecque** goes more abstract: por = *coincidence* / "conditioning, conceptual precedence", para = *separation* / "projection". Delbecque's version can stretch to cover the goal-like uses of por. It is also the one critics call too abstract to predict anything.

### Cited Findings
- **Delbecque 1996**, conceptual level: "por representa 'condicionamiento' o 'precedencia conceptual' y para representa 'proyección' (Delbecque, 1996) o 'virtualidad' (Riiho, 1977), de ahí su valor temporal de futuro o que sea la preposición elegida para introducir las oraciones finales" ('por represents conditioning or conceptual precedence and para represents projection or virtuality; hence para's future-time value and its use in purpose clauses'). (F, secondhand via Frago 2020, p. 13) — [Frago 2020, marcoELE](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Delbecque, base schemas**: por evokes an abstract relation of *coincidencia* (coincidence) and para one of *separación* (separation). Prototypical conceptual and discursive meanings are layered on top of these. Delbecque is said to differ from Lunn, Lam and Negrete et al., who posit spatial basic meanings. (SN, search summary of a Redalyc review; the page itself returned 502) — [Redalyc 92154321015, "Modelos de análisis lingüístico de las preposiciones… por y para"](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/921/92154321015/html/)
- **Criticism of Delbecque**: her basic meaning of por is said to be too abstract to predict the remaining uses. (SN) — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332872144_Towards_a_cognitive_account_of_the_use_of_the_prepositions_por_and_para_in_Spanish)
- **Delbecque 1996, bibliographic record**: "Towards a cognitive account of the use of the prepositions por and para in Spanish", in Casad (ed.), *Cognitive Linguistics in the Redwoods*, Mouton de Gruyter, pp. 249–318. (SN) — [Benjamins listing](https://benjamins.com/catalog/cagral.6.07del)
- **Por, base schema (Frago)**: from Latin *per* and *pro*, por "indica trayecto y movimiento" ('indicates path and movement'). Her drawing deliberately lets the line touch both ends of the path, "ya que esto permite explicar usos como el de intercambio o medio" ('since this explains the exchange and medium uses'). (F) — [Frago 2020, p. 9](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Por is atelic**: "La preposición por tiene un valor aspectual atélico, de proceso" ('por has an atelic, process aspectual value'). That is why *Corrió por la playa* 'ran along the beach' is fine and *\*Corrió por la maratón* is not. (F) — [Frago 2020, p. 10](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **The left/right axis**: "en la línea espacial-temporal-lógica, de y por miran hacia la izquierda, el pasado o la causa; en cambio a y para miran hacia la derecha, el futuro, la consecuencia o la finalidad" ('on the spatial-temporal-logical line, de and por look left, to the past or the cause; a and para look right, to the future, the consequence or the purpose'). (F) — [Frago 2020, p. 11](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Para, base schema**: para comes from *pro/pre + ad*, a "trayecto orientado hacia un límite" ('path oriented toward a limit', quoting Melis 1992: 76). "En este caso no hay contacto con la meta, sino direccionalidad" ('there is no contact with the goal, only directionality'). (F, Melis secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 13](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **How para's meanings developed (Melis 1992: 85, via Frago)**: spatial path, then temporal path, then the path of a transfer (recipient or beneficiary), and finally "el trayecto de la orientación estática" ('the path of static orientation'). Static orientation covers *Para la edad que tiene, es muy alto* 'For his age he's very tall' and *para mí* 'in my opinion'. (F, secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 13](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Para mí**: Company (2006) treats it as subjectification, "un cambio metonímico de meta espacial a meta mental valorativa" ('a metonymic shift from spatial goal to evaluative mental goal'). (F, secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 13](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Kissling, Tyler, Warren & Negrete 2018**: each preposition is a polysemy network with a spatial central sense, ordered in a cognitive-linguistics-inspired way. Their survey of nine intermediate textbooks found that the books treat por and para as translations of English prepositions. (SN) — [Richmond repository](https://scholarship.richmond.edu/lalis-faculty-publications/148); [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/232791566.pdf)
- **Lam 2009**: tested a cognitive description of por/para against memorised lists of uses. (SN) — [ERIC EJ861993](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ861993)
- **Funes 2020**: por is one radial category with [+Locativo] as its prototype. (SN; covered in earlier round) — see `../Por vs para rules/unifying_theory.md`

### Inferences
- Only Delbecque's level (coincidence/precedence vs separation/projection) can unify both prepositions under a single contrast. The spatial cores ("through" vs "toward") are two separate images, and they leave the goal-like uses of por as metaphorical leaps. (RK)

### Gaps
- Delbecque's own wording, diagrams and her treatment of *ir por*, *luchar por*, *estar por* were not reached. Every Delbecque claim above is secondhand.
- Lunn, Taylor, Rubio 2015, Real Espinosa, Castañeda Castro and Llopis-García: no por/para schema text was reached this round. Rubio 2015 is abstract-only (see earlier round). No claims are made about the rest.

## The goal-like uses of por (ir por pan, luchar por, votar por, morir por)

### Takeaway
The cognitive and historical literature has a ready answer: the "goal" in these uses is coded as a **mover**, not a destination. Melis calls por "meta y móvil al mismo tiempo" ('goal and motive at the same time'), and Company & Flores say *ir por algo* introduces "la causa del desplazamiento" ('the cause of the movement'). This makes the steelman workable. It is still a *construal*, though, and nothing in the scene forces it.

### Cited Findings
- **Favour and choice come from substitution**: because por covers the whole path it "pone en contacto ambos extremos" ('puts both ends in contact'), which gives equivalence and substitution: *Voy a la reunión por él* 'I'm going to the meeting in his place'. From substitution come 'in favour of' and then choice. Riiho: choosing between alternatives "no es, en realidad, más que una abstracción extrema de la idea de favor o defensa" ('is really just an extreme abstraction of the idea of favour or defence'). This covers *votar por* 'vote for' and *optar por* 'opt for'. (F, Riiho secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 11](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Both prepositions at once**: *Trabajamos por y para la justicia* 'We work for (por) and toward (para) justice'. The beneficiary (para) is "también inspiración o motivo (por) del sujeto" ('also the subject's inspiration or motive'). "Por expresa un valor causal final, es decir, meta y móvil al mismo tiempo, valor que ya estaba presente en la preposición latina pro (Melis, 1992: 73)" ('Por expresses a final-causal value, goal and motive at once, a value already present in Latin pro'). (F, Melis secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 11](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Ir por algo**: "Otro ejemplo de por con valor causal es la expresión ir (a) por algo donde la preposición introduce la causa del desplazamiento" ('another causal por: *ir (a) por algo* "go for something", where the preposition introduces the cause of the movement'). On peninsular *ir a por*: "el hablante codifica y pone de relieve primero la meta del movimiento, mediante a, y después el trayecto, mediante por" ('the speaker first codes and highlights the goal with a, then the path with por'; Company & Flores 2014: 1254). The construction spreads to verbs of seeking: *preguntar por* 'ask after', *pedir por* 'ask for / pray for' (Torres & Bauman 2014). (F, secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 12](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)
- **Cause and concession**: because there is no explicit endpoint, "por focaliza hacia el pasado, por lo tanto, desde la perspectiva de la lógica se sitúa en el campo de la causa y de la concesión" ('por focuses toward the past, so logically it sits in the field of cause and concession'). Examples: *Estudia español por amor* 'studies Spanish out of love'; *Por mucho que lo intentes, no lo conseguirás* 'however hard you try, you won't manage it'. This cites Heine & Kuteva (2002: 40): spatial and temporal markers commonly grammaticalise into cause and concession markers. (F, Heine & Kuteva secondhand) — [Frago 2020, p. 12](https://marcoele.com/descargas/31/01.frago.pdf)

### Inferences
- The bread in *ir por pan* 'go for bread' is a desire that exists *before* the trip and pulls it along. It sits upstream, like *por amor* 'out of love'. *Ir a por pan* is the evidence: Spain adds *a* precisely to supply the destination that por does not code. (RK, supported by the Company & Flores quote)
- *Luchar por la libertad* 'fight for freedom' frames freedom as a value the fighter already holds, so it sits upstream. *Luchar para ser libres* 'fight in order to be free' frames freedom as a result still to be produced, so it sits downstream. The alternation is real and meaningful, and the minimal pair supports the axis rather than breaking it. (RK)

### Gaps
- No source tested experimentally whether native speakers construe the por-goal class as motive.

## Typology: is there one "before vs after the act" axis?

### Takeaway
Croft's causal-chain typology supports the axis: oblique markers split into antecedent and subsequent roles. Croft also identifies reason or motive as the one role that can fall on either side. That is the strongest available justification for *luchar por / luchar para*. Schmidtke-Bode 2009 exists but I reached nothing on cause–purpose syncretism in it.

### Cited Findings
- **Croft's split**: obliques divide into antecedent roles (Instrument, Manner, Means, Comitative, Cause) and subsequent roles (Benefactive, Recipient, Result). Case syncretisms occur within each class but rarely across the divide. (SN, search summary of excerpts, not Croft's text) — [Croft abstracts](https://www.unm.edu/~wcroft/WACabst.html); [Levin LSA 2009 handout](https://web.stanford.edu/~bclevin/lsa09causapp.pdf)
- **Reason is the exception**: reason is mixed. It can be an antecedent cause or a subsequent purpose, so a subsequent marker can extend purpose, then reason, then cause. Turkish and Konda reportedly use one marker for both. Croft 2012's linking hierarchy: Antecedent Oblique > Object > Subsequent Oblique, in order along the force-dynamic chain. (SN; the summary itself warned to verify against the book) — [LINGUIST List review of Croft 2012](https://linguistlist.org/issues/23/4022/); [Levin handout](https://web.stanford.edu/~bclevin/lsa09causapp.pdf)
- **Schmidtke-Bode 2009**, *A Typology of Purpose Clauses* (TSL 88), 80-language sample; it has a chapter on purpose clauses in the "conceptual space of complex sentences". Nothing I retrieved addressed cause–purpose syncretism in it. (SN) — [Benjamins TSL 88](https://lwc1.benjamins.com/catalog/tsl.88)
- **Haspelmath 2023**: semantic maps are better called "coexpression diagrams". Nothing I found was specific to cause and purpose. (SN) — [Frontiers in Psychology 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1236853)

### Inferences
- English *for*, French *pour* and Catalan *per* each cover both motive and purpose, so they ignore the split. Spanish draws it with two forms, and it puts *motive* on the por (antecedent) side. That is exactly the slot Croft calls ambiguous. On this view the por-goal class is not an exception. It is Spanish choosing the antecedent construal for the ambiguous role. (RK, building on Croft SN)
- History is consistent with this. Por once covered purpose, and para took over the purpose slot, as covered in the earlier round. The surviving por-goal verbs (*ir por, luchar por, votar por, preguntar por, morir por*) would then be layered residue of the older antecedent construal. If so, *which* verbs keep por is a historical fact, not something the image predicts. (RK; history claim from the earlier round, `../Por vs para rules challenge/rival_theories.md`)

### Gaps
- Croft 1991 (*Syntactic Categories and Grammatical Relations*) was not read directly. The Turkish/Konda examples and the "reason is mixed" claim are SN only.

## The best single rule, and the full coverage test

### Takeaway
Best single rule, in one sentence (RK, built on Delbecque's coincidence/separation plus Croft's antecedent/subsequent):

> **Por puts the noun inside or behind the action: something the action runs through, happens within, trades against, comes from, or is driven by. Para puts the noun out ahead of the action: where it is aimed but which it does not contain or reach.**

This replaces "endpoint ahead" from earlier rounds with "ahead vs not-ahead *relative to the action's own span*". It treats a desired object as a force already acting on the doer.

### Coverage table (all RK unless marked)

| Example | Gloss | Verdict | Why |

| *por teléfono* | by phone | fits | medium the action runs through (Frago p.11, F) |
| *escrito por Laforet* | written by Laforet | fits | initiator upstream; Frago: the agent sits on the "left" (F) |
| *gracias por venir* | thanks for coming | fits | the thing thanked for precedes the thanks |
| *cambiar X por Y*, *por 10 dólares* | swap X for Y, for $10 | fits, via the "inside" half | the counter-value is the other end of the same transaction path (Frago: "pone en contacto ambos extremos", F), not something ahead |
| *luchar por la libertad* | fight for freedom | fits, by construal | freedom as motive or value (Melis "meta y móvil", F) |
| *morir por la patria*, *loco por ella* | die for the homeland, crazy about her | fits | motive or cause of the state |

| *para ser tan joven* | for someone so young | partial | the standard the judgment is aimed at; same static-orientation extension |
| *estar para salir* | to be about to leave | fits | the event is just ahead |
| *estar por salir* / *estar por algo* | to be about to leave (Am.) / to be in favour of | mixed | 'in favour' fits as motive; the 'about to' sense does not |
| *la casa está por limpiar* | the house is yet to be cleaned | **fails** | the cleaning is ahead, yet the preposition is por |

### Inferences: where the single rule still fails (RK)
1. **Pending por** (*está por hacer* 'it's still to be done', *queda mucho por hacer* 'there's a lot left to do'): the action is ahead, yet por. A rescue ("the task is still inside an unfinished process") is possible but ad hoc.
2. **Por si** (*llévalo por si llueve* 'take it in case it rains'): refers to a future, hypothetical event. It can be rescued as "the present possibility is the motive", but the rescue looks after the fact.
3. **Construal is free, so the rule doesn't predict the choice.** For *luchar*, *trabajar* and *esforzarse* both prepositions are grammatical. The rule explains the difference in meaning, but it cannot tell a learner which one a native speaker *will* say. *Votar por* and *preguntar por* are fixed. Nobody says *votar para*, and the image doesn't say why.
4. **Which verbs take por-goal is a matter of lexicon and history.** *Ir por* (Americas) vs *ir a por* (Spain), *pedir por*, *preguntar por*, *optar por*: the image motivates these verbs but does not generate the list. This matches the earlier "test plus lexicon" conclusion, but only for this one narrow class.
5. **"Inside or behind" is really two cases joined by "or".** Exchange and medium are "inside"; cause, motive and agent are "behind". Delbecque's *coincidencia / precedencia* covers both only at a level abstract enough that critics call it unpredictive (SN). For learners, the effective test is the negative one: "is the noun *ahead* of the action, not yet reached? → para; otherwise por".
6. **Para mí and para ser joven** need the extra step of "static orientation". They are motivated by the image, not derived from it.
7. **Fixed expressions** (*por fin* 'finally', *por supuesto* 'of course', *para colmo* 'to top it all', *no es para tanto* 'it's not that big a deal') are lexicalised and outside any rule.

### Overall verdict (RK)
The user's intuition is partly right. One axis does cover the por-of-goal class, provided the class is read as **motive** (antecedent) rather than destination. Melis, Company & Flores, and Croft's "reason is ambiguous" all independently support that reading. What the axis cannot do is *predict* construal for verbs that allow both, or produce the fixed por-goal verb list and pending *por + infinitive*. The honest framing: one rule, with three predictable "construal" zones (motive, choice, viewpoint) and a short closed list (*estar/quedar por + infinitive*, *por si*, the seeking verbs, idioms). That beats "no single rule, only a test plus a lexicon", but it is not a rule with zero exceptions.

### Gaps
- No corpus figure for what share of tokens falls in the failure zones (Rubio 2015 numbers not online).
- Delbecque's own treatment of *estar por* and *por si* is unknown. She may cover them, and that would strengthen the case.
