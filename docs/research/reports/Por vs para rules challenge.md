# Por chases goals; para delivers them

No single feature predicts *por* vs *para* the way the assertion test predicts indicative vs subjunctive, and the earlier rule ("is there an endpoint ahead? yes → *para*, otherwise → *por*") does not survive adversarial testing as stated. It fails in three ways. First, a whole productive class of *por* names a goal that is plainly ahead: the thing fetched, fought for, voted for, chosen, desired or done for someone's sake (*voy por pan*, *luchar por el título*, *optar por*, *morir por la patria*). This class is open-ended, and the NGLE itself describes these verbs as expressing "tendencia o inclinación hacia algo", so it cannot be filed as a short list of exceptions. Second, the rule's claim that *por* is the unmarked default is backwards in the one slot where the two compete freely: before an infinitive of purpose, *para* is "la forma neutra" and *por* is the marked, motive-framing choice, and *por* was Spanish's main purpose marker until *para* displaced it from the 18th century. Third, "endpoint" had to be stretched to include "standard of judgement" and "recipient" to catch *para mí* and *para su edad*. At that width it predicts nothing *por* could not also claim (*gracias por*, *cambiar por*), and *para siempre* contradicts its "duration → *por*" clause outright. The rule is worth keeping, but in revised form. **Check the lexicon first (governed prepositions, fixed phrases, "for"-verbs with a bare object). Then use *para* when X is where the action is delivered or measured (destination, deadline, recipient or user, function, norm, viewpoint), and use *por* when X is what the action goes after (the thing sought or championed, or the person for whose sake) or what drives or carries it (cause, route, means, exchange, span, agent).** Purpose with an infinitive stays a zone where both are correct: *para* is the default and *por* frames the aim as a motive. A caveat on evidence: rae.es, the DPD and Fundéu blocked every direct fetch, so all Academy wording here comes from search snippets or from pages quoting them, and no corpus measures how much real text each branch covers.

## The endpoint rule breaks on a productive class, not a short list

The earlier rule treated *luchar por*, *votar por*, *preocuparse por*, *esforzarse por* and *ir (a) por* as a handful of lexical exceptions. The challenge pass shows they are sample members of one regular sense of *por*: the thing an action goes after, supports or competes for. The fetch sense is open across verbs of motion. The Universidad de Piura's language column, citing the DPD, gives *ir por* as "en busca de" and the preferred educated form (*Ve por ayuda*, "go get help"), with *a por* accepted mainly in Spain ([UDEP, Castellano Actual](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-voy-a-por/)). The Fundéu summary extends it to *ir, venir, bajar…* + *por* ([Al Navío](https://alnavio.es/ir-por-agua-o-ir-a-por-agua-la-rae-resuelve-la-duda)), and the Mexican DEM lists it as a sense of its own, "lo que se va a buscar", with *Salió por pan* and *Subió por el libro* ([DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/por)). In each case the bread or the book is literally where the trip ends.

The choice-and-support verbs make the same point more sharply, because the Academy's own description puts an endpoint on them. The NGLE section on governed complements says *por* heads the complements of verbs of "tendencia o inclinación hacia algo" ("tendency or inclination toward something"), naming *votar, apostar, decantarse, decidirse, inclinarse, interceder, optar*, and contrasts *optar a* ("apply for") with *optar por* ("choose") ([RAE, Gramática, complementos de régimen](https://www.rae.es/gramática/sintaxis/complementos-de-régimen-con-las-preposiciones-para-por-sobre-y-tras), snippet). The DPD glosses *apostar por* as "opt for" or "show confidence in" someone ([DPD, apostar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/apostar), snippet), and a Fundéu-style column offers *apoyar, optar, decidirse, confiar, abogar, favorecer, preferir, promover* as alternatives to it ([El Caribe](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/opiniones/espanol-urgente/apostar-pero-tambien-apoyar-optar-decidirse-confiar-abogar/)). Purpose *por* also survives outside these verbs: *Se casó por dinero* ("he married for money") is cited as a purpose reading ([Bakken 2014, UiT](https://munin.uit.no/handle/10037/7616), snippet), and a Spanish Ministry of Education document lists *por* + infinitive as purpose in *Viene al cine con nosotros por distraerse un poco* ([redined](https://redined.educacion.gob.es/xmlui/bitstream/handle/11162/72352/00820093000027.pdf?sequence=1), snippet). Beyond these sourced members, the researchers' own judgement places contest nouns (*la carrera por la vacuna*), desire nouns and adjectives (*afán por*, *ansioso por verte*) and "for the sake of" uses (*morir por la patria*, *rezar por los enfermos*, *brindar por los novios*) in the same class. That judgement has no corpus count behind it, but the pattern accepts new nouns freely, which a closed list would not.

History explains why the class exists. Torres Cacoullos and Bauman show that *para* arose from older *por* + *a* by chunking. From the 18th century *para* replaced *por* before infinitives and became the majority purpose marker, through a long period of variation rather than a clean split ([Torres Cacoullos & Bauman 2018](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/allative-to-purposive-grammaticalisation-a-quantitative-story-of-/)). **"Por = no endpoint" is therefore historically backwards** for this layer. Old purpose *por* survives where a sought or championed entity is named, while *para* won the free purpose clause. The endpoint rule can absorb these cases only by relabelling the goal as a "motive" after seeing the answer, and that move could relabel any goal.

The *para* side holds up better but exposes a different weakness. Its leftovers are a short, mostly closed set of idioms: *para colmo* (attested in the [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/para)), *para nada*, *no es para tanto*, *para mí que*, *salió para no volver*. That supports "para is narrow". But the rule fits *para mí* ("in my view") and *para ser tan joven* ("for someone so young") only because "standard" was written into the definition of "endpoint" to catch them. Once "endpoint" means "any reference point", it should also cover *gracias por el regalo* (the gift is the reference point of the thanks) and *lo cambié por otro* (the replacement is where the swap lands), which take *por*. *Para siempre* ("forever") is a duration with *para*, a direct hit on the "duration → *por*" clause. It is standard Spanish, though no fetched source in this pass attests it. Scholars noticed the slippage long ago: Bolinger devoted an article to "Purpose with por and para" in 1945 ([citation via search](https://linguisticsandlanguageteaching.blogspot.com/2011/02/clearing-up-native-english-speaking.html)), and the literature notes that some *¿por qué?* questions can be answered with *para* phrases and vice versa ([core.ac.uk, citing Pavón Lucero 2012](https://core.ac.uk/download/233941398.pdf)).

## "Por is the default" inverts what the sources say about purpose

The earlier synthesis claimed that *para* is the marked preposition and *por* the residue. No examined source says this, and the main source it leaned on says the opposite. D'Antonio, Albiol-Martín and Priego-Casanova (2025) is a position paper with no data, written by two secondary-school teachers and a university lecturer from classroom experience. It gives two parallel images rather than one test. In the cognitive image, *por* profiles the path or process and *para* the goal or recipient. In the meta-operational image (Adamczewski; Matte Bon 2016), *para* introduces a new, open datum and *por* a pre-existing one, which is why *por* reads as cause. In purpose contexts the paper calls ***para* "la forma neutra"** and *por* "probablemente más subjetiva", adding that "por nunca presenta el fin de forma contundente". Its own conclusion is eclectic: beginners may do better with simple rules or lists ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). The paper paraphrases Delbecque (1996) only in general terms, so the earlier report's attribution of the path/goal image to Delbecque rests on second-hand wording.

The cognitive literature gives *por* a positive core, not an empty elsewhere slot. Funes treats *por* as one polysemous item whose most prototypical attribute is [+Locativo], a path through a place, and notes that the grammars disagree on how to characterise *por* at all ([Funes 2020](https://repositorioslatinoamericanos.uchile.cl/handle/2250/4395458); [Funes 2022](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/202416)). Kissling, Tyler, Warren and Negrete build **two polysemy networks, each with a spatial central sense** ([Kissling et al. 2018](https://core.ac.uk/download/232791566.pdf), snippet). Raw frequency does favour *por*, at 190,975 tokens to 126,061 in one frequency list whose corpus is not stated ([Most common words in Spanish](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Most_common_words_in_Spanish)). But much of that count comes from the passive agent and fixed phrases (*por favor*, *por eso*, *por ciento*), which says nothing about which preposition wins a free-choice slot.

The etymology also needs re-sourcing. All accounts give *para* (via older *pora*) a second element from Latin AD "to, toward", so the allative core is safe. The first element is disputed: PRO + AD or PER + AD (Penny; Alvar & Pottier; Riiho calls the choice pointless outside Portuguese), *per a* spreading from Leonese *pera* (Fernández-Ordóñez 2022), or fusion of *por* + *a* (Torres Cacoullos & Bauman). The vowel change *pora* > *para* is explained by analogy with the oath word *par* or by assimilation ([Cadiñanos & Enrique-Arias 2022](https://scriptum.uab.cat/article/download/v11-cadinamos-enrique-arias/124-pdf-es/164)). D'Antonio et al. garble this, presenting *par* + *a* as the source of *pora*. Wiktionary gives "earlier *pora* (a combination of *por* and *a*, or from Latin *prō ad*)" ([Wiktionary, para](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/para)). Brea's caveat, that the purpose/cause split "no se haya observado con un rigor absoluto", argues against presenting any test as exceptionless ([D'Antonio et al. 2025, quoting Brea 1985](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)).

Classroom evidence supports connected networks, not a single binary question, and supports them only modestly. Pinto and Rex (2006), a cross-sectional study of classroom learners at four levels, argue that textbook explanations presenting the two prepositions **as opposites** may not match learners' difficulties or the prepositions' functions in discourse ([Pinto & Rex 2006, Hispania](https://researchonline.stthomas.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Acquisition-of-the-Spanish-Prepositions/991015163652703691)). A one-question rule is the most extreme form of that framing. Lam (2009) found intermediate learners taught a cognitive description confused the two less in free writing than a list-taught group ([Lam 2009](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658410802147345)), but Lam later described the result as improvement in only specific aspects ([Lam 2018](https://releve.erudit.org/fr/revues/cjal/2018-v21-n1-cjal03922/1050808ar.pdf)). A follow-up with intact classes (list instruction n = 32; incremental polysemy networks n = 34) found the network approach "confer[s] the most advantage … on a multiple-choice test, but not a fill-in-the-blank test" ([Benjamins, pl.22005.kis](https://benjamins.com/catalog/pl.22005.kis)). Fill-in-the-blank is the measure closest to production. Kissling and Arnold (2022) report preliminary gains for novices ([todoele](https://todoele.net/bibliografia/preliminary-evidence-applied-cognitive-linguistics-effective-novice-learners)). No study tests a single heuristic against full networks. The University of Kansas reports that the prospective/retrospective framing "really clicks" with some learners ([KU Acceso](https://acceso.ku.edu/gramatica/unidad5/por_para.shtml)), which is a teaching testimonial, not evidence.

## A revised rule: one lexical check, then three branches

The repair is to promote the pursuit class from the exception list into the rule and to replace "endpoint" with a sharper contrast. ***Para* marks where the action is delivered or measured. *Por* marks what the action goes after, or what drives or carries it.** The difference between *voy por pan* and *voy para casa* is not whether something lies ahead, since both do. It is whether X is the thing the trip goes after (*por*) or the place it delivers the traveller to (*para*). The same contrast separates *trabajo para mis hijos* (they receive the result) from *trabajo por mis hijos* (they are what I'm working for the sake of), and *votar por un candidato* (the choice gone after) from *votar para presidente* (the office the vote fills).

| Step | Question | Answer | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Lexicon | Does the word before pick the preposition, is it a fixed phrase, or does the verb already mean "for"? | Use the lexicon | *preocuparse por*, *servir para*, *por favor*, *para colmo*, *buscar trabajo*, *esperar el bus* |
| 1. Delivered or measured | Is X the destination, deadline, recipient or user, intended function, or the norm or viewpoint the judgement is made against? | ***para*** | *sale para Madrid*, *para el lunes*, *es para ti*, *taza para café*, *alto para su edad*, *para mí* |
| 2. Gone after | Is X the thing fetched, sought, fought, voted or opted for, desired, or the person or cause for whose sake the act is done? | ***por*** | *voy por pan*, *luchar por la libertad*, *optar por*, *ansioso por verte*, *lo hice por ti* |
| 3. Driven or carried | Otherwise: is X the cause or motive, route or area, means, price or exchange, rate, time span, or agent? | ***por*** | *por la lluvia*, *por el parque*, *por correo*, *por cinco euros*, *por hora*, *por la mañana*, *escrito por* |
| Overlap | Purpose + infinitive or *que*-clause after effort, desire or benefit predicates | both; *para* neutral, *por* = aim framed as motive | *se esfuerza por/para levantar la piedra*; *me quedé por/para terminar* |

Two short paraphrase tests make the branches usable. If X can be glossed "con destino a", "antes de", "destinado a" or "en comparación con", it is branch 1. If it can be glossed "en busca de" or "a favor de", it is branch 2; "a causa de", "a través de" or "a cambio de", branch 3. The overlap zone is a construction, not a verb list. Nebrija authors call *por* and *para* interchangeable when the difference becomes almost imperceptible ([Nebrija, "El desafío preposicional en ELE"](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/226/195/)). *Pasa por la ciudad por / para no pasar por la Vía de Cintura* accepts both, and *Lo han llamado por esa tarea* ("because of that task") vs *para esa tarea* ("to do it") shows both prepositions with a task "ahead" in some sense ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). A checker must never flag *por* + infinitive of purpose.

The revision is no longer a one-question rule, and it should not pretend to be. The assertion test works because mood is grammaticalised on one semantic feature, and its trigger lists are consistent with that feature. *Por* and *para* are two polysemy networks layered by history, and the same situation (a wanted goal ahead) is coded both ways depending on whether it is presented as delivered-to or gone-after. The revised rule still decides some cases only after the fact. *Trabajar por / para el bien común* is natural both ways, and so are *hacer lo posible por / para llegar a tiempo* and *rezar por / para que llueva* (*rezar por los enfermos* takes only *por*). Those are judgements from the challenge notes, not sourced data. What the revision buys is that its residue is genuinely lexical, while the old rule's residue was a whole meaning.

The minimal pairs below are the ones the revised rule should anchor in lessons. The first four are attested as pairs in sources. The rest are built from separately attested halves.

| R26 | Concessive (*por más/mucho que*) | *Por más que estudia, no aprueba* (fact) / *estudie* (hypothesis) | [Masaryk Univ. handout](https://is.muni.cz/el/fss/jaro2020/CJVS402/um/1_oraciones_concesivas.pdf?kod=SPp411) |

The lexicon has three parts. **Governed prepositions** fix the choice regardless of meaning. Sourced *por* regimes are the inclination verbs the NGLE names, *luchar, preocuparse, preguntar, brindar, tener, aceptar* and *acabar por*; sourced *para* regimes are *servir para* and aptitude with *tener … para* ([DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/por); [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/para)). After a *por* regime, a purpose clause can be written *por que* or *porque* with the subjunctive. The DPD accepts both, gives «Rogué porque su agonía no fuera larga», and notes that purpose *porque* is "hoy … decayendo" outside these contexts ([DPD, porque](https://www.rae.es/dpd/porque), snippet). *Agradecer* is mostly transitive (*le agradezco su ayuda*), but the DPD records *agradecer a alguien por algo* as frequent in American Spanish and does not condemn it ([DPD, agradecer](https://www.rae.es/dpd/agradecer), snippet), so learner sites that mark it wrong are stricter than the Academy. **"For"-verbs** take a bare object. The DPD prefers transitive *esperar* and says *esperar por* is common in much of America, especially the Caribbean, partly through English "wait for" ([DPD, esperar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/esperar), snippet). Fundéu calls it a calque but accepts *por* for cause or duration ([Panamá America, Fundéu](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/dia-d/esperar-algo-mejor-que-esperarporalgo-842881)). The only clear "debe evitarse" found is ***pedir por* meaning *preguntar por***, which the DPD attributes to Catalan speakers and parts of America. The same entry accepts *pedir por* for price, for praying for someone, and in the Southern Cone for asking on someone's behalf ([DPD, pedir](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pedir), snippet). **Fixed phrases** sourced in the DEM, Bowdoin and Warwick are *por fin, por ejemplo, por desgracia, por supuesto, por eso, por lo menos, por favor, por primera vez, por casualidad, por otra parte, por lo tanto, por lo general, por lo pronto, por ahora, por poco, por si acaso, por si las dudas* and, with *para*, *para colmo, para su desgracia, para sí* ([DEM, por](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/por); [DEM, para](https://dem.colmex.mx/Ver/para); [Bowdoin](https://learn.bowdoin.edu/spanish-grammar/newgr/ats/43.htm)). Common phrases with no fetched source include *por cierto, por lo visto, por completo, por si las moscas, para siempre, para nada, para variar, no es para tanto, no estoy para bromas* and *para que sepas*. They are standard but should be checked before they become hard checker rules. The spellings split four ways: *porque* (conjunction), *por qué* (interrogative), *porqué* (noun) and *por que* (preposition + relative, which passes the inserted-article test *el motivo por (el) que*) ([El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/educacion/aprende-los-usos-de-porque-por-que-porque-y-por-que-BL7580928)).

## Learners overproduce para, so checkers should flag only lexical errors

English-speaking learners produce *para* in **64–68% of por/para tokens**, rising to **83% at intermediate level** in Lafford and Ryan's data, as re-tabulated by Jiménez (2017) from Guntermann (1992), Lafford and Ryan (1995) and Pinto and Rex (2006). *Por* error rates in Guntermann fall from **83% to 32% to 21%** across levels. Purpose and recipient make up **94% of *para* uses** in Lafford and Ryan, and early *por* is mostly chunks (*por favor*, *gracias por*). Duration *por* is a typical error: *\*Vivo por un año y medio*, where natives say *Llevo año y medio aquí* or *desde hace*. Learners also acquire notional uses before spatial ones, the reverse of children, so route and area *por* need explicit teaching ([Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)). No native baseline was given, so "overuse" is an inference. Error research on other L1s points the same way: a UAB thesis on Chinese learners finds *por* the top error source and *para* the next ([UAB thesis 2019](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/tesis/2019/hdl_10803_670098/jigu1de1.pdf)).

The revised rule targets these errors better than the old one. Branch 2 gives learners a positive reason for *por* in exactly the cases where English "for" pulls them toward *para* (*luchar para*, *votar para*, *ir para pan*). It is fair to keep "make every *para* earn its place" as a teaching stance, given the overuse data, as long as it is not presented as a fact about Spanish markedness.

For an automated checker, the safe error tier is lexical. It covers *gracias para*, *para favor / para ejemplo / para supuesto*, price with *pagar … para*, *preocupado para*, *para que* + indicative in a purpose clause, and *esperar/buscar para* + determiner + noun. Everything that depends on meaning belongs in a suggestion tier: *esperar por*, *pedir por* + person, *vivo aquí por dos años*, *trabajo por* + person, *luchar para* + infinitive, and *salgo por* + city. The checker must never flag *por* + infinitive purpose, *ir por* + noun, *agradecer por* (American norm), *luchar porque* + subjunctive (DPD-accepted), or *por más que* + indicative, which presents the obstacle as fact (*Por más que lo intenté, no pude*). A learner site claims the indicative is wrong there ([Colanguage](https://app.colanguage.com/spanish/grammar/las-concesivas)), and that claim should not be followed. Error rules need narrow patterns, because valid look-alikes are common: *te lo pido por favor*, *pedir por los enfermos*, *lo busqué por toda la casa*. No precision or recall figures exist for Spanish por/para error detection.

Mexico is the best-sourced variety, because the DEM, built from a corpus of contemporary Mexican Spanish, was fetched directly. Spain comes through DPD and Fundéu snippets. **Colombia remains thinly sourced**, and the one new Colombian finding overturns the earlier report.

Three corrections to the earlier picture stand out. First, the earlier report inferred that Colombia lacks *por mientras* because the *Diccionario de americanismos* lists Mexico, Costa Rica, Peru, Bolivia, Paraguay and Chile but not Colombia. A 2026 corpus study names **Honduras and Colombia as the countries that prefer *por mientras***, Guatemala and Venezuela as favouring *para mientras*, and Mexico and Chile as using both ([del Barrio de la Rosa 2026, Anuario de Letras](https://revistas-filologicas.unam.mx/anuario-letras/index.php/al/article/download/1810/2569/4250)). The study is known only from its abstract, but a corpus finding outranks an omission from a dictionary list. Second, *esperar por* is not specifically Mexican. The DPD places it in much of America, especially the Caribbean, and the DEM leaves it out entirely. Third, the notes disagree on a detail of the DPD *estar* entry. The challenge pass's snippet says Spain allows "about to" *estar por* only with *caer* and *llegar*. The earlier pass's snippet attaches that restriction to *estar al* + infinitive (*está al caer*). The earlier reading matches the known Spanish idiom, but neither was read in full, so the point needs a library check. The "inclined to" sense of *estar por* (*estoy por irme*, "I'm tempted to leave") has only an RILCE mention of a modal "estar a favor de" reading behind it, with no DPD support ([RILCE](https://revistas.unav.edu/index.php/rilce/article/download/2888/2707)). Across these features, the DPD is descriptive and regional, never prohibitive. App copy should say "in Latin America" or "in Spain", never "wrong", and *ir a por* and *menos cuarto* should be tagged "Spain" rather than corrected. "Un cuarto **a** las ocho" is the exception: the RAE says it "no debe decirse" ([RAE Ortografía](https://www.rae.es/ortograf%C3%ADa/modelo-de-doce-horas)).

## Conclusion

The adversarial pass changes what kind of object the rule is. The old rule treated *por* as the absence of a feature, so every *por* with a visible goal became an exception, and the exceptions turned out to be a productive meaning with a thousand-year history. The revised rule gives each preposition a positive content: *para* delivers to or measures against, and *por* goes after, drives or carries. Its residue is then genuinely lexical (regimes, fixed phrases, *estar por/para*, "for"-verbs), which is the same shape as the subjunctive's trigger lists. The analogy to the assertion test holds only at that level: a semantic decision procedure plus a lexicon, not one binary feature. For the app, this means three data structures rather than one list. A three-branch decision tree drives explanations, a regime and phrase lexicon drives deterministic checks, and an overlap flag on purpose-infinitive slots tells the checker to accept both.

What remains unknown matters more than any further theory. No corpus figure says what share of real *por/para* tokens fall in each branch, the lexicon or the overlap zone, so the claim that the residue is small is untested. The pursuit class's membership beyond the NGLE-named verbs rests on native judgement. Every Academy statement here was read through a snippet. The cheapest next steps are a CORPES XXI count of the branch-2 frames (*ir/venir por* + noun, *luchar/optar/apostar por*, desire nouns + *por*) against branch-1 *para*, a full reading of the 2026 *por mientras / para mientras* study, and a library check of the DPD *estar*, *porque* and *a* §2 entries before any regional or normative rule ships as an error.
