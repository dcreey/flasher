# Para points ahead; por covers the rest

There is a usable high-level rule, but it is a synthesis rather than a rule any one grammar states. **Ask "is there an endpoint ahead that the event is headed for: a destination, recipient, purpose, deadline, or standard of judgement?" If yes, use *para*; otherwise use *por*, which names what the event comes from or passes through (cause, motive, route, means, exchange, duration, agent).** Cognitive grammar (path vs goal), pedagogical grammar (forward-looking vs backward-looking), meta-operational grammar ("comes later" vs "already exists") and the history of the two words (*para* was built with Latin AD, "to", to mark the endpoint) all point to the same asymmetry. The rule works like the assertion test in one respect: one feature (an endpoint ahead) picks out the marked form and its absence gives the default. It differs in another. A closed set of verb-governed *por* complements (*luchar por*, *votar por*, *preocuparse por*, *esforzarse por*, *ir (a) por*) can only be explained after the fact, so it has to be memorised as a list, much as lexical triggers are for the subjunctive. Making *para* the marked choice is supported by learner data: English speakers fall back on *para* when unsure, so a rule that makes learners justify each *para* targets the most common intermediate-level error. What share of real *por/para* tokens the rule covers is unknown, because no corpus study in the notes measured it. One caveat on sources: rae.es (NGLE, DPD, Ortografía), the Butt & Benjamin full text and the Instituto Cervantes Plan curricular were all unreachable (HTTP 403, dead mirrors or a maintenance page). Every RAE or DPD point below comes from a search snippet or from a secondary page quoting it, and should be checked against the books before it becomes a hard checker rule.

## One asymmetry, four independent derivations

The reference grammars do not offer a unifying test. The NGLE is descriptive: it lists the values of each preposition and puts verb-governed *por/para* complements in a separate subsection (§36.10) on *complementos de régimen* ([RAE, NGLE §36.10](https://www.rae.es/gramática/sintaxis/complementos-de-régimen-con-las-preposiciones-para-por-sobre-y-tras)). De Bruyne's chapter in Bosque & Demonte ties *por* to "causalidad, intercambio y movimiento" (causality, exchange and movement) ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). Grammarians themselves disagree on whether *por*'s values can be related to one another at all ([Funes 2020](https://repositorioslatinoamericanos.uchile.cl/handle/2250/4395458)). The single rule has to be built from other traditions, and four of them agree.

The **cognitive** account, going back to Delbecque (1996), has *por* profile "el trayecto o proceso" (the route or process) of an event "más que el destino final" (rather than its final destination): walking *por el parque* ("through the park"), acting *por amor* ("out of love"), sending *por correo* ("by mail"). *Para* is a "proyección o dirección hacia un objetivo" (a projection or direction toward a goal) ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). **Pedagogical grammar** puts the same split in time: *para* "is characterized as being future-oriented, or the GOAL", while *por* is "more backward looking, or as describing the SOURCE" ([KU Acceso](https://acceso.ku.edu/gramatica/unidad5/por_para.shtml)). **Meta-operational grammar** (Matte Bon 2016) puts it in discourse terms. What *por* introduces already exists before or alongside the event, which is why *por* reads as cause, since a cause always comes before its effect. What *para* introduces is new and comes after. Hence *Lo han llamado por esa tarea* ("they called him because of that task") vs *Lo han llamado para esa tarea* ("they called him to do that task") ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). **History** fits too. Brea (1985) argues that Latin PER and PRO merged into one form, and that Ibero-Romance then formed *para* / *per a* with a second element going back to AD, "indicando así de un modo más claro el fin en el espacio, la dirección" (marking more clearly the endpoint in space, the direction). That is how *para* came to mark purpose and *por* cause, "aunque la distinción no se haya observado con un rigor absoluto" (although the distinction has never been observed with absolute rigour) ([D'Antonio et al. 2025, quoting Brea](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). The exact etymology of *para* (*por* + *a* vs *par* + *a*) is ambiguous in the summary of Corominas and should not be stated as settled. Catalan shows the same "+ a" strategy in its *per* / *per a* split ([IEC](https://criteria.iec.cat/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/VIII-Seminari-Igualada-Full-de-suport-per-i-per-a-3.pdf)).

The history explains why the rule is lopsided. Latin PRO meant "in front of", "in place of" and "in favour of", so *por* inherited a broad, mixed set of meanings (on behalf of, in exchange for, for the sake of). *Para*, the later and more specialised word, took only the forward-endpoint meanings. **So *para* is narrow and regular and *por* is broad and messy**, and a decision procedure should test for *para* first. This "para is marked, por is the default" framing is the report's own inference. No source states it in those words, and no study has tested it against a symmetric cause/purpose rule.

Tested against the hard cases, the rule handles every free-choice contrast. *Gracias por la ayuda* works because the help is already done and is the cause of the thanks. *Por eso* / *para eso* is a clean pair ("that's why" vs "that's what it's for"). *Para el lunes* is a temporal endpoint, *por la mañana* an open stretch of time. *Para ser tan joven* and *para mí* are "reference endpoints" the judgement is measured against: acceptable once taught, though a learner would not predict them. The rule breaks on *luchar por la libertad*, where freedom looks like a goal. The NGLE treats *luchar* as a verb that governs *por* ([RAE, NGLE §36.10](https://www.rae.es/gramática/sintaxis/complementos-de-régimen-con-las-preposiciones-para-por-sobre-y-tras)), and *luchar para* + infinitive survives for concrete aims (*luchar para sobrevivir*, "to fight to survive"). It also breaks on *ir por agua* ("go for water"), where the water is plainly the goal. The honest answer to the question is therefore **one rule plus one list**. The rule decides open choices. The list covers verbs whose *por* is fixed and that can only be explained after the fact as "the cause or ideal that drives the action".

The tables show the useful structural fact for an app. **Most uses take only one preposition**, so swapping it is either ungrammatical or produces a different use rather than a nuance. Only *por* is possible for thanks, price, rate, means, agent, route and the fixed expressions. Only *para* is possible for deadline, norm, opinion, use, suitability and good/bad-for. Real competition happens in a handful of slots: cause vs recipient with a person, route vs destination with a place, motive vs purpose with an infinitive, and imminence with *estar*. For a checker, the one-preposition uses are deterministic rules and the competing slots need meaning, which in practice means the checker should only suggest.

## Minimal pairs and the narrow zone of free choice

Only three minimal pairs are attested whole in fetched sources. *Compré esto por ti* ("I bought this because you wanted me to") vs *para ti* ("as a present for you"). *Recibimos dinero por la investigación* ("payment for research already done") vs *para la investigación* ("funding for research still to come") ([Bowdoin](https://learn.bowdoin.edu/spanish-grammar/newgr/ats/43.htm)). *Lo hice por ti* ("because of you, for your sake") vs *Lo hice para ti* ("you as recipient") ([KU](https://acceso.ku.edu/gramatica/unidad5/por_para.shtml)). The second pair is the best teaching item because the rule's time axis carries it directly: the same noun, with *por* pointing back to work done and *para* pointing ahead to work to come. A fourth pair comes from the theory literature: *Trabajo por mi jefe* (the boss as cause, or the person whose place I fill) vs *Trabajo para mi jefe* (the boss as beneficiary or employer) ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). Other pairs an app will want, namely *salgo por/para Madrid* (via vs bound for), *caminar por/para el parque* (through vs toward) and *por/para la tarde* (in vs by the afternoon), are built from halves attested separately (R12/P6 and R11/P7). They follow from the rule but have no source as pairs.

The one zone of near-free choice is **purpose with an infinitive**. Batchelor & San José note that "por, like para, may be used with the idea of intention" and test *para/por llegar antes* ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/reference-grammar-of-spanish/prepositions-para-and-porlas-preposiciones-para-y-por/581B27DF4D0C6F03BB423EBE2F57F23B)). The GDLE, as quoted, says *por* "expresa la finalidad cuando va acompañada de infinitivo" (expresses purpose when followed by an infinitive) ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). The difference is how the speaker presents the aim. In *Te devuelvo el dinero para cumplir con mi promesa* ("to keep my promise"), the result comes after the act. With *por cumplir con mi promesa*, the promise is the motor of the act and is simultaneous with it. *Por* is the more subjective, justifying choice: "Por nunca presenta el fin de forma contundente" (*por* never presents the aim forcefully), and the two purpose readings "no son asimilables" (cannot be equated) ([D'Antonio et al. 2025](https://ojs.cimedoc.uniba.it/index.php/glottodidattica/article/download/2156/1943)). The same source shows cause and purpose rubbing against each other in *Pasa por la ciudad por / para no pasar por la Vía de Cintura*. For Learn lessons, the test is "is this something the subject already wants that pushes the act (*por*), or a result the act should produce (*para*)?", with **para as the safe default for learners**. A checker should never mark *por* + infinitive as purpose wrong outright.

Imminence with *estar* is the other alternation, and the sources are less tidy than learner sites suggest. The DPD (via snippet) gives *estar por* + infinitive two general-Spanish senses: something still pending (*La gran biografía de Rodolfo Usigli aún está por escribirse*) and a future sense (*está por llover*). For "about to" it calls *estar para* the more general form and *estar por* frequent in some American countries ([DPD estar](https://www.rae.es/dpd/estar)). The NGLE presents *El sol está {por ~ para} salir* as an American alternation ([RAE NGLE, infinitivos](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/infinitivos-activos-y-pasivos-ii-otras-construcciones)). Burguera Serra argues *estar por* carries a modal sense of inclination ("I'm tempted to"), while *estar para* marks closeness in time ([RILCE](https://revistas.unav.edu/index.php/rilce/article/download/2888/2707)). Pardo Llibrer documents an "about to" reading of *estar por* in peninsular Spanish too ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/917cb644cd50414b8aa4f30bdc927806)). "Spain = tempted, Americas = about to" is therefore a tendency, resting mainly on learner sources such as [Kwiziq](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/questions/view/estar-a-punto-de-vs-estar-por), and not a boundary. A forum claim that the RAE calls *está por llover* "incorrecta" is contradicted by the DPD's own wording ([WordReference](https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/est%C3%A1-por-llover-est%C3%A1-para-salir.769682/)). *No estoy para bromas* ("I'm not in the mood for jokes") is a further *estar para* idiom that was not sourced.

## Where the rule runs out: governed verbs, "for"-verbs and fixed phrases

Three kinds of item fall outside the rule, and each needs its own data structure.

**Verb, noun and adjective regimes.** Here the governing word fixes the preposition. Sourced *por* regimes include *luchar por* ([NGLE §36.10](https://www.rae.es/gramática/sintaxis/complementos-de-régimen-con-las-preposiciones-para-por-sobre-y-tras)), *votar por*, *preocuparse por* and *preguntar por* ([Bowdoin](https://learn.bowdoin.edu/spanish-grammar/newgr/ats/43.htm); [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/arts/languagecentre/about/staff/salvador/spanishculturalgrammar/para_and_por_and_the_passive_voice_v.2.pdf)), and *brindar por*, *tener por*, *aceptar por* and *acabar por* + infinitive ([DEM por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)). Sourced *para* regimes are *servir para* and aptitude with *tener … para* ([DEM para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)). Effort verbs (*esforzarse por*, *desvivirse por*) are the gray zone where the rule's "the wish drives the effort" reading fits but does not predict. The NGLE treats these as *complementos de régimen*, which is the justification for a "verb + por" list in the rules file rather than a semantic rule.

**"For"-verbs that take no preposition.** *Buscar* ("look for"), *esperar* ("wait for") and *pedir* ("ask for") already contain the English "for", so the thing sought, awaited or requested is a direct object: *busco trabajo*, *pedí ayuda* ([FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/spanish/blog/spanish-verbs-prepositions/); [SpanishDict](https://www.spanishdict.com/compare/look for/seek)). Fundéu, as summarised, calls *esperar por* a growing calque of "wait for", correct only when *por* gives cause or duration ([Panamá América, citing Fundéu](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/dia-d/esperar-algo-mejor-que-esperarporalgo-842881)), and a Mexican university study agrees ([UGTO](https://www.jovenesenlaciencia.ugto.mx/index.php/jovenesenlaciencia/article/download/2929/2194/9848)). The DPD (via snippet) advises against *pedir por* in the sense of *preguntar por* (asking after someone), probably an influence of English "ask for". It records *pedir por* = "ask on someone's behalf" as Southern Cone ([DPD pedir](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pedir)). *Pagar* takes the thing paid for as a direct object (*pagué la cena*), while *pagar X por Y* is price ([FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/spanish/blog/spanish-verbs-prepositions/)). The single rule says nothing about these verbs. They need a separate "the verb already means 'for'" lesson.

**Fixed expressions.** Fetched sources attest the following. With *por*: *por fin*, *por ejemplo*, *por desgracia*, *por supuesto*, *por eso*, *por lo menos*, *por favor*, *por primera vez*, *por casualidad*, *por otra parte*, *por lo tanto*, *por lo general* ([Bowdoin](https://learn.bowdoin.edu/spanish-grammar/newgr/ats/43.htm); [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/arts/languagecentre/about/staff/salvador/spanishculturalgrammar/para_and_por_and_the_passive_voice_v.2.pdf)), plus *por lo pronto*, *por ahora*, *por más que*, *por poco* ("almost"), *por si acaso*, *por si las dudas* and *por si no* ([DEM por](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/por)). With *para*: *para colmo* ("to top it all"), *para su desgracia* and *para sí* ([DEM para](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/para)). The written forms of *por* + *que* split four ways. *Porque* is the causal conjunction (with the subjunctive it can mean purpose, where *por que* is also valid). *Por qué* is the interrogative in direct and indirect questions. *Porqué* is the noun ("el porqué de su decisión"). *Por que* is preposition + relative, which passes the test of inserting an article: "el motivo por (el) que" ([El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/educacion/aprende-los-usos-de-porque-por-que-porque-y-por-que-BL7580928); [Acento, Fundéu Guzmán Ariza](https://acento.com.do/cultura/se-escribe-el-porque-no-el-por-que-9516030.html)). For a learner, *por eso* / *para eso* is the cleanest single illustration of the rule, and that pair should anchor the fixed-phrase lesson.

## Learners lean on para, and the checker should exploit that

The acquisition data, re-tabulated by Jiménez (2017) from Guntermann (1992), Lafford & Ryan (1995) and Pinto & Rex (2006), show English speakers produce *para* in **64–68% of por/para tokens**, rising to **83% at intermediate level** in Lafford & Ryan ([Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)). No native baseline was given, so "overuse" is an inference. Error rates in Guntermann fall for *por* from **83% to 32% to 21%** across beginning, intermediate and advanced levels, while *para* goes from 0% to 33% to 16%. In Lafford & Ryan, *por* goes 40% → 48% → 18% and *para* stays flat at 34/37/33%. Jiménez describes a U-shaped curve for both prepositions in both studies (memorised chunks, then over-generalisation, then recovery), but **his own table shows a U in only two of the four series**. The Pinto & Rex figures are omitted here because the table's row labels are misaligned. The story behind the numbers is consistent: beginners map *por* to "for", find that this fails, and then fall back on *para*. Jiménez notes that at intermediate level learners "utilizan por en contextos donde se requiere para" (use *por* where *para* is required), alongside the general drift toward *para* ([Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)).

The order of acquisition matters for sequencing Learn lessons. *Para* of purpose is the most stable use at every level (55–77% of correct *para* uses), and purpose plus recipient makes up **94% of *para* uses** in Lafford & Ryan. Early *por* is mostly chunks (*por favor*, *por eso*, *gracias por*) and exchange. Duration tokens that look early were largely echoed from the interviewer's *¿Por cuánto tiempo…?*, and many were ungrammatical: *\*Vivo por un año y medio*, where natives say *Vivo aquí desde hace año y medio* or *Llevo año y medio aquí*. Route, means and vague location appear only late. *Para mí* as opinion is almost absent because learners say *creo que*. Learners overall move from notional uses to temporal to spatial ones, **the reverse of L1 children**, who start with spatial uses ([Jiménez 2017](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/1/3/4)). Lessons should therefore introduce the spatial *por* (route, through, around) deliberately rather than leave it for learners to pick up.

On teaching method, Lam (2009) found that intermediate learners taught a cognitive-linguistic description held or improved accuracy on a delayed post-test, while the list-taught group "decreased significantly". The cognitive groups were also "less likely to confuse por and para in free writing". Lam names confounds in treatment content and delivery ([Lam 2009](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658410802147345)). Kissling & Arnold (2022) report gains for novices taught with animated polysemy networks (n = 30, snippet only) ([Pedagogical Linguistics](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/pl.22005.kis)). Two further claims, "virtually no gains over four years" attributed to Lam and an unnamed 2008 experiment, appear only as search snippets and are not counted as evidence here. The evidence base is small, but it points one way: **one image per preposition beats a memorised list for retention**, and acronym lists (PERDE/PODER) built on English "for" give learners no way to handle the verbs that take no preposition.

For the checker, the split into an "error" tier and a "suggest" tier below is this report's design. The rows marked as sourced rest on the citations above; the patterns are proposals.

| Tier | Pattern | Correction | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Error | *gracias para* + noun/inf. | *gracias por* | Sourced (R2; Jiménez) |
| Error | *para favor*, *para supuesto*, *para ejemplo* | *por …* | Sourced fixed phrases |
| Error | *pagar* + amount + *para* + noun | *por* (price) | Sourced (R7) |

| Error | *esperar/buscar* + *para* + determiner + noun | direct object | Sourced verbs; narrow pattern is design |
| Error | *pregunté por* + food/drink/object | *pedí …* | Learner-site summary |
| Suggest | *esperar por* + noun | *esperar* + object | Fundéu calls it a calque; acceptable for cause/duration |
| Suggest | *pedir por* + person (= ask after) | *preguntar por* | DPD advises against; Southern Cone *pedir por* is valid |
| Suggest | *vivo aquí por dos años* | *desde hace* / *llevar* | Sourced learner error; past/future framing is fine |
| Suggest | *estudié por/para dos horas* | no preposition or *durante* | Duration *por* contested; *para* may be a deadline |
| Suggest | *ir/salir* + *por* + infinitive (purpose) | *para* + inf. | *Por* + inf. valid as motive; never flag *ir por* + noun |

The narrow patterns matter because valid look-alikes are common: *te lo pido por favor*, *pedir por los enfermos* ("pray for the sick"), *lo busqué por toda la casa*, *buscar por internet*, *esperé para hablar con él*. Each "error" rule must require the preposition to sit directly after the verb and introduce the object, and must exclude *por favor*, place and means nouns, and *para* + infinitive. No published precision or recall figures exist for Spanish por/para error detection, and no English-L1 error study on CEDEL2 or CAES was found. Tune these thresholds on real learner text.

Two normative points apply everywhere. "Un cuarto **a** las ocho" is wrong: the RAE says "no debe decirse" (must not be said), and the American formula must use *para* ([RAE Ortografía](https://www.rae.es/ortograf%C3%ADa/modelo-de-doce-horas)). With *un cuarto para*, the verb agrees with the singular: *Es un cuarto para las cuatro* ([UDEP](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/duda-resuelta-es-o-son-3/)). The apostrophe in *pa'* is accepted in texts reproducing popular speech ([UDEP](https://www.udep.edu.pe/castellanoactual/cuando-los-sonidos-se-suprimen/)). The checker should accept *pa'* in casual input and normalise it to *para* only when correcting toward the formal register. For an app targeting Mexico and Colombia, *ir a por* and *menos cuarto* should be tagged "Spain", not marked wrong. Marking *ir a por* as an error would contradict the DPD.

## Conclusion

The subjunctive comparison holds better than expected, but in reverse. The assertion test picks out the indicative as the marked mood. The por/para test picks out *para* as the marked preposition, the one that needs an endpoint to justify it, and leaves *por* as the residue. The learner data give this asymmetry a practical payoff the theory alone does not. English speakers already over-produce *para*, so a rule that makes every *para* earn its endpoint attacks the dominant error, while a symmetric "cause vs purpose" rule leaves learners guessing in the cases where cause and purpose overlap. The parts the rule cannot cover are also well defined. They are lexical: a short list of *por*-governing verbs, a separate class of "for"-verbs that take a bare object, and the fixed phrases. That suggests three separate structures for the rules file (a semantic decision tree, a regime lexicon and a phrase table) rather than one list of uses.

The biggest open risk is the evidence, not the theory. The core normative sources (NGLE, DPD, Butt & Benjamin) were read only through snippets, the rule's coverage of real text is unmeasured, and Colombia is close to a blank. The fastest ways to close these gaps are a library check of B&B §34.14.8 and the DPD entries *estar*, *pedir* and *porque*; a CORPES XXI query on *esperar por* and duration *por* split by country; and a pass over CEDEL2 English-L1 essays to tune the checker tiers. Until those are done, the "error" tier should stay limited to the fixed phrases and the gratitude, price and "for"-verb patterns, and everything involving meaning should only be suggested.
