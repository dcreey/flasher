# The two-step por/para test on 100 common sentences

The test, applied literally:

1. Is the noun the target the action is aimed at (where it's going, who gets it, when it's due, what it's for, what it's
   measured against)? No → **por**.
2. If yes: is it something someone *wants* (to get, win, choose, protect)? Yes → **por**. No → **para**.
   Before a verb, purpose → **para**.

Pass = the test gives a correct answer (where both work, either counts). The sentences and the scoring are one person's
judgement (Claude's). No native speaker checked them. Verbs that take no preposition (*buscar, esperar, pedir*) are left out,
because the test only chooses between por and para.

**Result: 87 of 100 pass.** Leaving out the 16 set phrases, 75 of 84 pass (89%).

## The 13 misses

- **Step 2 overreaches (3):** *ahorro para un carro, bueno para la salud, estudia para médico*. The noun is wanted, but
  it's what the action is *for*, not what it goes after.
- **Desire + verb (2):** *loco por verte, se esfuerza por mejorar*. A verb follows, so the test says para, but these take por.
- **Still to be done (2):** *está por limpiar, quedan por leer*.
- ***Estar para* (2):** *está para salir* (about to), *no estoy para bromas* (mood).
- **Set phrases with para (4):** *para colmo, para siempre, para nada, no es para tanto*.

## Without step 2

The 11 wanted things (*por* of sake or seeking: 51–54, 66–69, 71, 96, 100) would fail, and the three step-2
misses would pass: 79 of 100. Step 2 adds 8 points net.

## Rescored with a sharper question 2

> **2. Are you going after it (going to get it, fighting for it, voting for it, choosing it, doing it for someone's sake)
> → por. Or is it what this is for → para.**

Only sentences where question 1 says "target" change. The three step-2 misses now pass: *ahorro para un carro*,
*bueno para la salud*, *estudia para médico* are what the action is for. All 11 *por* sentences about going after
something or someone's sake still pass. Two of them are a judgement call: *se interesa por la música* (does interest
count as going after?) and *compré flores para mi mamá* (is it for her sake, or what the flowers are for?). They were
scored as passes.

**Result: 90 of 100 pass. Leaving out the 16 set phrases, 78 of 84 pass (93%).**

The 10 misses:
- **Wanting + a verb (2):** *loco por verte, se esfuerza por mejorar*.
- **Still to be done (2):** *está por limpiar, quedan por leer*.
- ***Estar para* (2):** *está para salir, no estoy para bromas*.
- **Set phrases with para (4):** *para colmo, para siempre, para nada, no es para tanto*.

## Best two questions so far (scored on the same 100)

> **1. Is it a target the action is aimed at: where it's going, who gets it, when it's due, what it's for, what it's
> measured against, or what you're ready for (*estar para*)?** No → **por**.
> **2. Or is it something you're going after: going to get it, fighting, voting, choosing, craving or striving for it,
> or doing it for someone's sake?** Yes → **por**. No → **para**.
> These apply to a verb after the gap too, not just a noun.

Two changes from the version above:
- *Estar para* is read as *estar listo para*. This fixes 14 and 15.
- Question 2 also applies before a verb. This fixes 65 and 97. Purpose verbs still pass, because they're "what it's for":
  *estudio para aprender, te llamo para invitarte, trabajo para vivir*.

**Result: 94 of 100. Leaving out the 16 set phrases, 82 of 84 (98%).**

Still missed: *está por limpiar*, *quedan por leer* (still to be done), and *para colmo, para siempre, para nada, no es para
tanto*. The questions were tuned on these same 100 sentences, so they need a fresh set to confirm the score.

## Blind test on 200 new sentences

One agent wrote 200 common sentences with their answers, without seeing the rule. A second agent applied the two
questions above literally, without seeing the answers. A script compared them. Files are in [por-para-200/](por-para-200/).

**Result: 187 of 200 (93.5%). Without the 53 set phrases: 141 of 147 (96%).** Set phrases: 46 of 53. The second agent
marked 42 rows "unsure", and 29 of those were still right.

The 13 misses:
- **Set phrases with para (7):** *para siempre, para colmo, para mí que, para variar, no es para tanto, para nada,
  para bien o para mal*.
- ***Estar por* = about to (2):** *estoy por terminar, está por llover*. The rule says para (ready for). In Mexico these
  are usually por.
- **Cause that looks like a purpose (2):** *dejé de fumar por mi salud, viajo mucho por trabajo*.
- **Pasar por = stop by (1):** *pasé por tu casa*.
- **Planned length of stay (1):** *un cuarto para dos noches*.

Limits: the sentences, the answers and the rule's application are all model judgements, made by different agents. No
native speaker checked them.

## Real sentences: 500 from Tatoeba

500 random Tatoeba sentences, each with exactly one por or para, from the manythings.org spa-eng export (CC-BY 2.0 FR).
They split 316 por and 184 para. The correct answer is what the writer used. One agent marked where the other preposition
also works (6), the set phrases (207) and the faulty sentences (6, left out). A second agent, blind to the answers,
applied the revised rule: two questions, the built-in checks, the verb list and *estar por/para*. Set phrases had no list
to look them up. Files are in [por-para-500/](por-para-500/).

**Result: 469 of 494 (94.9%).** Set phrases: 204 of 207 (most are *por qué / por favor / por la mañana*, where por is the
default). Everything else: 265 of 287 (92.3%). Por 303 of 315, para 166 of 179. The scorer marked 46 rows unsure, and 23
of those were right.

The 25 misses:
- ***Razón / motivo para* + verb (6):** *no hay razón para quedarnos*. The "because of" check wrongly turns these into
  por. A reason *to do* something takes para.
- ***Hacer algo por alguien* (4):** *¿qué puedo hacer por ti?* The rule reads you as the one who gets it. Doing something
  for someone is on their behalf: por.
- **Feelings toward someone (4):** *cariño por, sentimientos por, desesperado por escapar, loco para* (the last means
  "you'd have to be crazy to…", which is para).
- **Good or bad at, fussy about, taking time to (4):** *malo para el tenis, mejor para tocar, tiquismiquis para comer,
  se toma su tiempo para todo*.
- **Still to be done (2):** *quedan por escribir, trabajo por hacer*.
- **Others (5):** *espera por mí, fue por allí, por si quieres, para nada / para tanto*.

## The simplest wording, on all three sets

> **Para → where it ends up:** the place it's headed, the person who gets it, the deadline, the purpose.
> **Por → what drives it, or what it passes through:** the reason, the thing you're after, the route, the means, the
> price, the time.
> Memorise: *para siempre, para nada, no es para tanto*.

Applied blind by a fresh agent on each set, with the same answers and scoring as before. Predictions are in
[por-para-simple/](por-para-simple/).

| Set | Simple wording | Two questions | Unsure (simple / two q.) | Right when sure (simple / two q.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| My 100 | 91 | 94 (scored by me) | 35 / – | 64 of 65 / – |
| 200 written by an agent | 188 | 187 (original version) | 63 / 42 | 137 of 137 / 158 of 158 |
| 500 real (Tatoeba) | 468 of 494 | 469 of 494 (revised version) | 59 / 46 | 425 of 435 (97.7%) / 446 of 448 (99.6%) |

Accuracy is about the same. The simple wording loses on certainty. It's unsure more often, and on the real sentences it
was confidently wrong 10 times, against 2. The confident misses: *hacer algo por alguien* (5: ¿qué puedo hacer por ti?,
nos encargaremos por usted), *por hacer* (2), *por si*, *¿por qué?* and *por allí*.

## The rules engine (Check a sentence)

`Flasher/Data/PorParaEngine.swift` is a rules engine with no model behind it. It reads the words around each por, para or
___ and applies the simple rules (the five P's for para; reason, the thing you're after, someone you do it for, route,
means, price and time for por; the memorised phrases). It reports the rule, why, and how sure it is. The app shows it on
the Por vs para guide as "Check a sentence".

It was tuned on the 100, 200 and 500 sets above, where it scores 98%, 98% and 97%. Those numbers are flattering, because
the patterns were written after seeing those sentences. The honest score is from 500 further Tatoeba sentences, held out
before tuning and run once ([por-para-engine/](por-para-engine/)):

**Held out: 467 of 500 (93.4%). When it says it's sure: 227 of 231 (98.3%). Probably: 91.7%. Edge case: 75%.**

That set was scored strictly (only the writer's word counts, and no sentences were removed), so 93.4% is a floor. Several
misses are acceptable Spanish, for example *¿para qué has venido?* and *lo hizo para ti*. To rerun:
`swiftc -O Flasher/Data/PorParaEngine.swift docs/research/por-para-engine/harness.swift -o ppe && ./ppe --data=<folder> heldout`.
The harness expects the same file layout as the scratchpad: heldout/truth.tsv.
